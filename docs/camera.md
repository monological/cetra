# Cameras and walkers

What moves a camera (`camera_rig`), the app helpers that feed it, and a first-person walker's gait.

## Modules

### `app.c/h`

App helpers, and since 12.19 NEITHER is a camera: `CameraDrag` turns a pointer into what a `camera_rig.h` rig is told (a drag is an aim, a shift-drag an anchor, the wheel and keys a distance), and `PlaneDrag` does the same for a plane under an orthographic camera while keeping its own arithmetic (it was `CanvasController` until spec 14.0, renamed so the 2D canvas could have the name). Plus a three-point light rig and input gating. **`MouseDragController` was DELETED from this public header** -- a camera named after a mouse, and being named for its input rather than its job is why three apps wrote their own follow cameras instead of finding it. `apps/pcb` builds against `app.h` from its own repository (spec 11.105), so that deletion is a break this tree cannot see

### `camera_rig.c/h`

What MOVES a camera, and what the CONTROLS mean under it (spec 12.19). A rig is an ANCHOR, an ARM and an AIM -- `look = anchor + look_lift*Y`, `eye = look + eye_lift*Y - dir(yaw,pitch)*dist` -- with NO mode enum, because a follow is an anchor that moves, a viewer orbit one that does not, first person a distance of zero, and a pinned pose derives all three. `eye_lift` raises the eye and not the aim, which is the look-down bias gametest's follow camera frames the player through. Layered on it: an arm RESPONSE (a 0..1 signal the rig does not interpret, smoothed with asymmetric rates, so gametest's fall and a speed-reactive framing are the same field), an occlusion PROBE seam (how much of the arm is clear; the rig shortens it and the AIM is untouched, which a floor clamp on the eye does not manage), a pose BLEND rather than a parameter one, a decaying SHAKE whose zero scale is BIT-IDENTICAL to none, and a Catmull-Rom RAIL through cglm's `glm_smc` so a whole spline costs no new arithmetic. **`steers_controls` is the roadmap row's actual complaint**, defaulting to false: a rig that steers publishes the yaw its input is read in, and 12.13/12.17's flip is a named field that reddens an arm by 104.85 degrees when wrong. **A stated pose is KEPT rather than re-derived** -- decomposing one into an angle and an arm and putting it back loses 0.002 world units at `aerial_fixture`'s 20,196-unit framing, which was 885 px of a restored session, and the error grows with the arm. The player's look preference is `look_scale`/`invert_pitch` beside the authored rates and never onto them, because settings apply on every edit and scaling in place compounds (12.15's idempotence lesson, in a second subsystem). NO GL, NO PHYSICS, NO CLOCK and no `game/` type, so five of its seventeen arms need no window at all

### `gait.c/h`

A first-person walker's gait, as its eye and its ears know it (spec 13.47): the stride, the footfalls, the eye's lag on the ground's height, the head's bob and sway, and the pace a person keeps on a climb, for a walker with no body to animate. Four things about it are easy to get backwards. **The stride runs on GROUND COVERED**, never on time or on the speed asked for: a walker held against a wall stops walking, and how frames fall cannot change how many steps a path takes -- `gait-split` holds three splits of one path to the same count. A level step is `0.40 + 0.25 * speed` (120 a minute walking, 163 jogging); on a flight it is Blondel's 2R + G of 0.63 m, one a tread on any stair built to the rule whatever it collides as. **It runs at TWO RATES**: `gait_step` once a fixed step on the step's exact move (the grade, how much of it is a flight, the pace, a landing), `gait_frame` once a rendered frame on the drawn body's move and the render time (the stride, the footfalls, where the eye goes) -- so nothing the simulation reads depends on the frame rate. Both take DISPLACEMENTS, so neither cares where the origin is. **A footfall is at the bob's TROUGH**, as a heel strikes, never at the phase's wrap: there it lands at the top of the head's rise, half a step out, which reads as a limp (`gait-trough`). **The bob is a MOVE, never a turn**: lowest as a heel strikes, highest over the standing foot, swayed toward it, about half a body's own excursion, and no pitch or roll, which is what makes a bob sickening; `bob_scale` 0 is bit-identical to none. The eye trails the body's height on a critically damped spring, clamped tighter above than below so a low ceiling over a flight down is never put through. NO GL, NO PHYSICS, NO CLOCK and no `game/` type, as `camera_rig.h`, so all ten of the `gait` group's arms run on moves `gametest --gait-probe` makes up
