# Spec 11.81 — the day/night cycle

## Context

Roadmap row 46 / **B11**, now unblocked: it depended on the stars (11.79) and on the floor
landing first (11.80), and both shipped. The ask that started the whole night stack —
"as the sun goes down the stars become more and more visible" — happens live: stand on
tree's island, watch the sun set, twilight roll through, the stars come out over the
floor-lit sea.

The blocker is priced: a sun move runs `sky_update_sun` = view LUT + env cube + IBL
re-bake, ~0.11 s, ~9 fps if animated naively. The GUI slider survives it because a drag
releases; a cycle never releases.

**Exploration correction that reshapes the sketch (recorded here because the roadmap row
has it wrong):** the cost is INVERTED from "one cube face per frame, one prefilter mip per
frame". `sky.c:703`'s own log splits the bake as view+env ≈ 10 ms against IBL ≈ 100 ms —
the six 256² env faces are ~1.7 ms each, while 78 of the 91 draws are the GGX (7 mips × 6
faces) and Charlie (6 × 6) prefilter chains, and mip 0 outweighs mip 6 by 4096×. A
one-mip-per-frame schedule would spike ~40 ms on two frames of every cycle. The slicer
therefore schedules by WEIGHTED WORK ITEMS, not by stage. Also: the 0.11 s citation chain
all traces to `config_snapshot.c:303-314`, which measures the restore CHAIN (update_sun +
scene_environment_changed), and its comment falsely claims transmittance/multiscatter
re-bake on sun moves (they are startup-only, `luts_baked` set once at `sky.c:527`) — that
comment gets corrected in this spec.

## The slicer — a weighted work list with an atomic swap

**The work list** (one full rebake, in today's exact order so quiescence is byte-identical):
1. bake the slicer's own FROZEN view LUT at the latched sun (one item),
2. six env-cube faces into a SHADOW cube, sampling the frozen LUT (six items),
3. `glGenerateMipmap` on the shadow cube (one INDIVISIBLE item — a mip chain from a
   half-drawn cube is garbage),
4. irradiance: six 32² faces into a shadow cube (one item — negligible),
5. GGX prefilter: (mip, face) pairs into a shadow chain — mip 0 sliced BY FACE, coarse
   mips batched (weights below),
6. Charlie prefilter: same shape,
7. SWAP: assign the four shadow handles into `IBLResources` (env, irradiance, prefilter,
   charlie) **together with `max_reflection_lod`** (`ibl.c:603`/`ibl.h:77` — the one
   non-texture field that must move atomically with `prefilter_map`), delete the old
   handles, `gi_volume_mark_dirty` if a GI volume exists, re-latch the sun, restart.

**Scheduling:** each item carries a texel weight (draw resolution² × face count); the tick
consumes items until a per-frame budget is spent. The budget constant is set by a
MEASUREMENT step during implementation (per-item `glfwGetTime` logs, recorded in the
spec), targeting no slice frame above ~2-3 ms and a full cycle in roughly 15-25 frames.

**Why the shadow handles are safe to swap:** verified — every consumer re-dereferences
`ibl->field` per frame (`bind_ibl_textures` at `render.c:553`, `water.c:1974`, the skybox
read at `render.c:1363`); no GL name is cached anywhere. The env cube is today DELETED at
the top of the atomic bake (`sky.c:631`), which is exactly why in-place slicing is
impossible and shadows + swap is the design.

**Two view LUTs, deliberately:** the background and cloud march keep sampling the LIVE
per-frame LUT (`sky->sky_view_lut`, re-baked cheap every tick so the on-screen sky tracks
the sun continuously), while the six env-face items sample the slicer's FROZEN copy — all
six faces of one cube must see ONE sun or the cube seams between faces. The frozen LUT is
a slicer-owned texture baked as item 1 from the latched sun.

**Byte-identity discipline (the 11.79 lesson — one arithmetic, not a parallel copy):**
the slicer does not reimplement the bake. `sky_bake_ex`'s face loop and
`ibl_prefilter_cubemap`'s mip loop are refactored into resumable pieces (`bake_env_face(i,
target)`, `prefilter_slice(chain, mip, face_range, target)`) that BOTH the atomic path and
the slicer call — the atomic path becomes "run the whole work list in one call". Each
slice re-establishes its GL preamble (FBO/RBO re-spec, program, units, uniforms — the
existing code already re-specs the shared `capture_rbo` per stage, so per-slice re-spec is
the established pattern, `sky.c:637`/`ibl.c:299`).

## The clock, and the sun's path through the stars' own sky

**The tick lives in the ENGINE loop, not the app render callback — correcting the
roadmap's sketch.** `engine.c:2553-2573`, after the origin shift and BEFORE the GI sweep
and the shadow pass: `sky_apply_sun_to_light` rewrites the key light the shadow pass then
fits, and the swap must land before the frame's first `bind_ibl_textures`. Tree's
"frame callback" (the roadmap's suggestion) is the RENDER hook at `engine.c:2679` — inside
the scene render, after shadows — and a driver there ships a one-frame shadow/sky mismatch
plus a mid-frame swap. The engine calls `sky_cycle_tick(sky, ibl, engine, frame_dt)` when
a scene has a sky; the tick is a structural no-op when the cycle is off.

**The sun rides the stars' celestial frame.** 11.79 already defined latitude (pole
altitude) and an hour angle; the sun at equinox (declination 0) rides that frame's
celestial equator: with solar hour angle H (0 = noon),
`sin(elevation) = cos(H) * cos(latitude)` — noon altitude 90°−lat, sunrise/set at
H = ±90°, a real east→south→west azimuth sweep — and `stars_hour_deg` advances with the
SAME H, so the star field wheels in sync with the sun instead of being a second unrelated
animation. One clock drives the whole sky. (`sky_sun_path(lat, hour, *el, *az)` in sky.c;
the gate holds it against a Python twin, the `roads`/`wind_bounds` pattern.)
`stars_latitude_deg`'s comment ("only the star frame reads them") goes stale here and is
updated: the sun path is a celestial-frame computation too.

**Per tick (cycle on):** advance `cycle_hour` (double, accumulated from the frame clock —
headless is fixed-step, so frame N's hour is a pure function of N and two runs are
byte-identical); derive el/az from the path; `sky_update_sun_dir` (sun_dir + the zenith
march — both cheap CPU); re-bake the live view LUT; `sky_apply_sun_to_light`; advance the
slicer by one budget's worth. The aerial volume already rebuilds per frame from live
`sun_dir` (`engine.c:1810`) and needs nothing. The cloud-shadow cache keys on `sun_dir`
(`sky_clouds.c:275`) so it re-marches 256² per frame under a cycle — zero for tree/render
(no cloud noise), measured and recorded for `--clouds`, not fixed.

**State on `SkyAtmosphere`:** `cycle_enabled` (bool), `cycle_day_seconds` (float — real
seconds per 24 h day; 0 = frozen: the clock holds, the slicer idles once converged;
**defaults 0**, which is what keeps config-perturb's automatic `cycle=true` perturbation
round-trippable — an enabled-but-frozen cycle moves nothing during the perturb render),
`cycle_hour` (double, 0-24, solar noon at 12). Plus the slicer's phase struct (work index,
shadow handles, latched sun, frozen LUT).

**Three exactness requirements, found in plan review and load-bearing for the arms:**
- **`sky_sun_path` computes in DOUBLE** (C libm), converting to float only at the end.
  The quiesce arm demands the path-derived sun and the flag-passed sun land on
  bit-identical floats; CPython's `math` calls the same platform libm, so a double-domain
  formula printed at `repr` precision and recovered through `atof` reproduces exactly —
  a float32-domain formula does not, and a 1-ulp sun is ≥1 px.
- **The initial hour is applied at STARTUP, before the atomic bake**: the apps compute
  el/az via `sky_sun_path` when `--time-of-day` is given and seed the sun with it, so
  frame 0 is already correct and a frozen cycle's tick is a TRUE structural no-op (it
  acts only when the hour changes) — quiesce identity by construction, not by re-baking
  identical content every frame.
- **`--time-of-day` wins over `--sun-elevation`/`--sun-azimuth`** when both are given
  (applied after, the CLI-wins ordering's own last word); one stderr note when it
  overrides explicit angles.

## Predictions, written before implementing

1. **All 27 goldens 0 px, no rebake** — cycle off is a structural no-op: no shader
   changes on the default path, the tick returns immediately, the atomic bake is the same
   arithmetic re-expressed. This is the strongest identity claim of the three night specs
   and anything above literal 0 px (not even PAE movement is expected — no default-path
   shader text changes) is a wrong diagnosis, stop.
2. `stars` and `night-floor` groups byte-identical — nothing they run enables the cycle.
3. `cycle-slice` reads 0 px. If it does not, the resumable refactor changed arithmetic
   somewhere and the fix is in the refactor, never in the bar.
4. Per-slice cost lands under ~3 ms with the measured budget; the mip-0 face slices are
   the peak. Recorded per-item in the spec, not guessed.
5. The GUI drag path (`sky_update_sun`) is untouched and stays atomic — dragging a slider
   mid-cycle is prevented by the disabled controls, not by new logic.

## Verification

- Noise floor first: one `--day-cycle 60` run rendered twice, 0 px, before anything else
  is quoted (this is the FIRST moving-sun configuration the suite has ever had — its
  determinism is an assumption until measured).
- `./build.sh`; goldens 27/27; `--only cycle,stars,night-floor,config,gate-docs` during
  development; full suite at the end on a quiet machine (`--bin-dir out/release/bin`).
- Falsify all four arms by hand with the named mutations; record readings in the spec.
- The measurement step: per-item ms table from the slicer instrumentation goes in the
  spec beside the chosen budget constant.
- A sunset strip for the user: `tree --day-cycle 120 --screenshot-every` tiled — the
  original ask, watched happening. This is the A/B that matters.

## Deliberately out of scope (booked, not forgotten)

- **The moon (B13)** — lands after this and slots into the cycle as a second slow light;
  the slicer needs no changes for it.
- **Purkinje (B14)** — after night frames exist in motion.
- **Probe sets under a cycle** — hold capture-time lighting by design
  (`scene_environment_changed` refuses set relight); env-only probes are also NOT
  refreshed per swap (a prefilter-chain walk per swap is real cost for zero visible gain
  at cycle rates). Both booked.
- **Cloud-shadow per-frame re-march under `--clouds` + cycle** — measured and recorded,
  not optimized; zero for the shipping defaults.
- **GUI sun sliders during a cycle** — disabled, not reconciled.

## As built

Eight commits. The plan's core survived — the LUT/slice insertion points, the shared clock,
the celestial frame, the engine placement — and every one of the four predictions held. What
did not survive was my own confidence: **six review agents found eight real defects and three
false claims in code I had already declared green**, and two of the six returned a block
verdict. The list below is theirs, not mine.

### Defects found in review, all fixed

1. **GUI edits reverted mid-cycle.** The Night Floor and cloud controls call the atomic bake;
   an in-flight slicer then swapped its own chain — baked from the frozen LUT taken *before*
   the edit — over the top. Fixed at the cause: an atomic bake now CANCELS a slice in flight,
   which covers the GUI, the config restore and `--cycle-rebake-at` together. Disabling the
   sliders would have been the bandaid.
2. **The clouds predicate had two copies and they disagreed** — the slicer's omitted
   `with_clouds`, so after any GUI drag (which goes through `sky_bake(..., false)`) it replaced
   a cloudless env cube with a clouded one. No arm could see it; `aerial_fixture` has no clouds.
3. **The frozen LUT read the LIVE sun**, correct only because item 0 happened to run in the
   same tick as the start. The one-sun-per-cube property was enforced for the six faces and
   left to a timing coincidence for the seventh.
4. **A missing shader program was swapped in as a black cube.** The slices early-return
   silently, so an unrendered shadow chain replaced a good live one where the atomic path
   returns −1 and installs nothing.
5. **`--day-cycle 0` teleported the sun.** Seeding sat inside the `--time-of-day` branch, so
   arming a frozen cycle without an hour let the first tick derive from the default 12 — tree
   off its authored 0.8°. This is exactly what `cycle-quiesce` asserts, so the arm would have
   been written against broken behaviour.
6. **The budget did not bound the frame it named**: it was larger than the heaviest item, so a
   tick always ran at least two mip-0 faces and the real ceiling was ~2× the constant.
7. **The star hour could stall** — it was assigned inside the sun-angle comparison, and near
   noon at low latitude both sun derivatives approach zero.
8. **An unscoped perturb exception** silently excused every light in every scene: the diff
   walker recursed into list elements without carrying their identity.

### Claims I made that were false

- **The `CFG_DOUBLE` reason.** I wrote that the double exists for the gate arm's bit-identity.
  Every hour INPUT narrows through a float on the way in; what the double actually buys is the
  ACCUMULATOR (a float32 ulp near hour 24 is half a per-frame increment on a 24-real-hour day).
  Wrong in three places, all corrected.
- **A measurement that did not exist.** `SKY_SLICE_BUDGET`'s comment cited "spec 11.81's timing
  table". There was no table. The measurement has now been taken (below) and the comment
  carries it.
- **`stars_latitude_deg`'s "only the star frame reads them"** — false the moment `sky_sun_path`
  took a latitude. The spec scheduled that correction and the first pass skipped it.

### The measurement, finally taken

Through the `sky cycle` profiler scope at 800x500, `aerial_fixture`, `--day-cycle 20`:

    budget 120000 (shipping)   4.74 ms GPU on an average slice frame
    budget 1 (one item/frame)  1.87 ms GPU, ~43 ticks instead of ~20

**The gap is not headroom.** A mip-0 prefilter face is ONE item and cannot be split at face
granularity, so the per-frame PEAK is that face either way; lowering the budget only spreads the
cheap items thinner and lengthens convergence. The plan's "no slice frame above ~2-3 ms" was
simply wrong for this schedule — beating it needs sub-face slicing, a schedule change rather
than a constant. Recorded rather than quietly re-targeted.
