# 13.5 -- The glare moves light, and only as far as a lens does

## Context

Spec 13.4 ported Clearwater's diffraction glare, and on a sunset sea it is too much. Every glint
past the threshold gets a whole star, so the glitter path's hundreds of glints add up to a broad
veil with long diagonal streaks. Two things make it heavier than physics.

1. **The far field is boosted 8×.** `_glare_build_psf` multiplies the pattern by
   `1 + 7 * clamp((r - 3) / 30)`, which is Clearwater's imitation of a phone lens. It is not part
   of an aperture's diffraction. A real aperture's power spectrum falls off far faster away from
   the core. The boost is most of the veil and all of the streaks.
2. **It adds light rather than moving it.** The tonemap keeps the frame as it is and adds
   `glare_strength` times the starred light on top. A lens spreads a glint's light round it, so
   the glint loses what its halo gains. The star is normalised to carry its source's light once,
   so the added term is new light, about 0.9 of everything above the threshold. 13.4's
   `glare-energy` arm compares the star with its source, not the frame with the frame, so it could
   not see this.

A/B on the tree sunset (scratchpad `GLARE_3way.png`): without the boost, the streaks and the veil
are gone. Redistribution on top of that trims the glints slightly. The user chose both.

## Plan

### 1. No far-field boost
- Delete the boost from `_glare_build_psf`. The aperture's power spectrum, summed over the eight
  bands and faded to its inscribed circle (13.4), is the pattern.
- Update the header and `glare.c` comments that describe the phone-lens lift, and 13.4's docs.

### 2. The glare moves light
- The tonemap subtracts `glare_strength` times the pixel's own light above the threshold, the
  same thresholding `glare_source_frag` applies, and adds `glare_strength` times the starred
  light. At strength 1 a glint's light above the threshold is entirely redistributed; below 1,
  part of it stays where it was.
- `glareThreshold` becomes a tonemap uniform beside `glareStrength`.
- **The threshold rule has one home.** The source pass and the tonemap both need "the light
  above the threshold", so it goes in a shared include, `include/glare_threshold.glsl`, and both
  call it.
- A known approximation, stated in the code: the source is thresholded after a 4-tap downsample
  and the subtraction at full resolution, so the two agree on average rather than per pixel.

### 3. Gates
- `glare-energy` keeps the star-against-source check.
- **New arm `glare-conserves`:** the mean frame radiance with the glare against `--no-glare`, on
  the sunward water, before the tone curve. The instrument is `--glare-probe` extended with the
  frame's summed working-space light, with and without the glare terms. It must agree to within
  what spills past the frame edge.
- `glare-live` and `glare-dark` are re-measured, and their floors re-set if the weaker star falls
  under them.

### 4. Look, and goldens
- **Shown to the user before any re-bake:**
  - the tree sunset at the user's framing;
  - `flare_fixture`;
  - `clearcoat_fixture`;
  - `cornell_point`;
  - `dof_fixture`.
- Re-bake the goldens it moves once the look is approved.

## Risks
- **At normal brightness the glare becomes nearly invisible.** That is what a good lens does. If
  the user wants more, the honest dial is `glare_strength`, not the far-field boost.
- **The redistribution can darken a glint's core below what it was**, where the downsampled
  source and the full-resolution subtraction disagree. The A/B has to be looked at close up.
