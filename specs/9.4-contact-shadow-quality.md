# Contact shadows, done right — full-res, canonical thickness march

## Context

Screen-space contact shadows (spec 9.3) ground objects, but on hard-edged geometry
viewed close-up they draw a **self-shadow artifact tracing silhouettes** — a dark
streak where a ray marching toward the light grazes the object's own silhouette
edge (on a curved surface the same failure shows as a dark crescent along the
terminator). This is the worst case for the technique.

This spec is the "do it right" rebuild: implement the **canonical SSCS algorithm**
(UE4 ContactShadows / Frostbite / Bend Studio), tuned with its **standard
parameters** — not bespoke heuristics reverse-engineered against one screenshot.
All of it is pure fragment-shader gather work (the compute/RT ceiling only blocks
Virtual Shadow Maps / RT shadows, out of scope). Target: clean, correct, UE4-era
contact shadows. The honest ceiling is *clean but subtle* — contact shadows
sharpen the last pixels of a seam the CSM texel is too coarse for; the CSM draws
the bold shadow. They are not a dramatic ground pool at any setting.

## What was tried and rejected (so it is not tried again)

- **Coherence gate (require N consecutive same-depth hits).** The original plan.
  It was a bespoke mechanism with no basis in any real implementation, built to
  defeat the stipple. It converted the *dashed* stipple into a *solid* streak —
  no better — because a ray grazing its own surface is *maximally* coherent, so
  the gate confirmed it. **Removed.** Overfitting, exactly the trap to avoid.
- **Thickness bound bolted on top of the coherence gate.** Killed grounding to
  0 px while barely denting the streak — the two gates fought. The lesson was not
  "thickness fails" but "thickness is the canonical mechanism and the coherence
  gate had to go first."
- **The old premise "grounding and silhouette overlap in depth, only coherence
  separates them"** was wrong about *what contact shadows are for*. A short-range
  contact shadow is a supplement to the shadow map; a "dark band under a floating
  cube" is shadow-map/AO behaviour, not contact-shadow behaviour. Chasing that
  band is what forced the thickness huge and made it look unworkable.

## The canonical algorithm

March from the shading point toward the light; take the FIRST sample where a
scene surface sits between the ray and the light — **in front of the ray by more
than `bias` and by less than `thick`**:

```
delta = sceneZ - rayZ                       // both view-space negative; >0 = surface in front of ray
occluder = sceneZ < SKY_Z && delta > bias && delta < thick
```

- `bias` (near bound) rejects self-contact quantization.
- `thick` (far bound) is THE mechanism that stops a ray grazing its own
  silhouette from reading the far side of a depth cliff as an occluder. It is
  core to the algorithm, not a hack. `thick = THICK_FRAC * csDistance`.
- First blocker wins; the term is binary + short-range, then distance/edge faded
  and N·L culled.

## The self-shadow residual and the canonical fix (B)

The clean thickness march removes the streak but a **grazing self-shadow** remains
(sphere terminator crescent, hard-edge rim): the ray skims its own surface at
grazing angles. The canonical fixes — both standard, physically motivated, neither
content-specific:

- **Slope-scaled (N·L) start bias.** Lift the ray off the surface proportional to
  how much the light grazes it (`offset ∝ 1/max(N·L, k)`). Grazing rays — the ones
  that self-shadow — lift a lot; face-on rays — the ones that must catch a real
  contact — lift little. The shadow-map slope-bias idea applied to the march; the
  principled resolution of the fixed-offset tension (small offset self-shadows,
  large offset skips real contacts).
- **Terminator N·L fade.** Fade the term to 0 as the surface turns from the light,
  ramp tied to self-shadow onset. Direct light is ~0 there anyway, so nothing
  legitimate is lost.

The offset tension is inherent to screen-space SSCS: it is *reduced* by making the
offset adaptive, not *eliminated*. This is documented, not fixed.

## Fixture (rework)

The old cube fixture is a poor test: gaps 7–37% of the object (shadow-map scale,
not contact scale), hard silhouettes filling the frame (worst-case self-graze),
and its "grounding bands" were themselves the large-depth artifact. Replaced with
the classic **matte objects resting on a matte plane, grazing side-lit**, that
isolate a genuine near-contact seam. Curved / rounded-edge geometry so the
silhouette does not self-graze. `gen_contact_fixture.py` regenerates it; sibling
`.cscn` ships the side sun + 3/4 camera. The two content-facing knobs — reach
(`csDistance`) and strength (`cs_strength`) — are tuned to this scene.

## Full-res plumbing — `postfx.c`

Contact shadows are a sharpness feature, so the pass runs at full internal res
(no half-res option):

- `postfx_ensure_contact_targets`: `cs_fbo[2]/cs_texture[2]` (R8) + `cs_history`
  (R16F) at `fx->width/fx->height` (was `ssao_width/height`). No `postfx.h` field
  changes — the fields already exist.
- Run block: viewport `fx->width/height`; `run_temporal_accum(..., &fx->cs_history,
  fx->width, fx->height, ...)`.
- Blur `texelSize` is now per-draw (the contact blur at full res, the GTAO AO blur
  at AO res — the `run_atrous`/fog precedent), since the two consumers differ in
  res and the contact block runs first.

## Composite — unchanged

Tonemap folds `cs` into `aoFactor` (a direct-light-occlusion multiplier). Honest
limitation: it darkens ambient/IBL too — benign (a real gap *is* ambient-darker)
and subtle at `csStrength` defaults. True direct-only would need a separated
direct-light buffer the forward renderer does not produce; not worth it.

## Cost & honest residuals

- **Cost**: full-res + 16 steps is ~8× the per-pixel march of the old half-res
  8-step pass. Correct for a sharpness feature; default-off.
- **Grazing self-shadow is reduced, not eliminated** — inherent to the offset
  tension in screen space.
- **Subtle by design** — the honest ceiling. The CSM draws the bold shadow.
- **Direct/ambient inseparable** in a post multiply. Inherent; benign.
- **Ceiling**: UE4-era-clean. UE5/VSM detail needs compute/RT we do not have at
  GL 4.1 — out of scope.
