# Area-light shadows, and the horizon term the LTC integrator never had

## Context

Two defects, found together because one was hiding the other.

`0f6ea74` merged the photometric-lighting work and left `cornell_box` rendering
a black room with lit box faces. That looked like a lighting bug and was one --
but the *shadow* work that follows was only findable once the light behaved,
and the light bug was only findable once the shadow story stopped explaining
it away. They are recorded together for that reason, not because they share a
mechanism.

## The shadow half (done)

### What was outstanding

`d4bd275` fixed a lit line along every contact for point and spot lights and
said, in its own message, that area panels kept the old policy and *"their
contact-line cost is still outstanding"*. `cornell_box` is lit by an area
panel. The line was invisible there only because the panel was contributing
almost nothing.

### Two claims in that commit are wrong

**Its diagnosis.** It read the leak as a depth tie: a caster's far side is
coplanar with whatever it rests on, so occluder and receiver compare equal and
the comparison reads lit. Tested directly -- a negative epsilon in the shader,
then a negative polygon offset in the depth pass -- and neither moved the line
by a pixel. A tie would have flipped under either.

The real mechanism: a caster's far side near its silhouette is a sliver that a
downward map sees almost edge-on. A sliver misses texel centres, those texels
keep the clear value, and comparing depth against *nothing* reads lit. No bias
of any sign reaches a texel with no data in it. Near-side storage puts the
caster's TOP in the map -- a full-coverage quad over the same silhouette -- so
the texels exist.

**Its measurement.** It rejected near-side for panels because `cornell_box`
went "near-black over 79% of frame". That was measured while the panel's LTC
response was inverted, so the surfaces it reported losing were the ones the
inversion had lit. Re-measured with the light behaving, near-side does not
darken the room.

### What landed

- **Near-side storage for every punctual type** (`97deaeb`). One policy, no
  per-type split.
- **A world-space receiver bias.** The NDC-z epsilon it replaces is worth
  ~d²/near world units, so it was only ever right at one distance and one
  scene scale -- a caveat the file already carried. The bias is now a
  displacement along the light ray, sized in shadow texels at the receiver's
  distance, which needs a per-layer texel scale (a point light's 90° face and
  a panel's 120° cone do not cover the same ground at the same distance).
- **A grazing fade.** Below NdotL 0.2 a single perspective map is not
  answering the question asked of it: the receiver compresses into a sliver
  where its own silhouette sits inside the kernel, and the true answer for an
  area source is "half the panel", which a binary test cannot express. No-op
  on point/spot, whose radiance already carries the same NdotL.
- **A penumbra the panel's own size** (`540e477`). The staircase on a wall was
  a filter-width problem, not a comparison problem -- measured pixel-identical
  across the near-side switch. The punctual path now runs the cascades' PCSS
  pair with the panel as emitter. Perspective needs two things ortho does not:
  blocker separation from inverting the projection rather than a lerp, and the
  emitter's UV footprint from the per-layer texel scale.

### Measured

Against `0f6ea74`: `contact_debug`, `froxel_fog`, `aerial_fixture`,
`cornell_leak`, `backface_dark` all **0 px**; `cornell_point` **94 px of
1.92M** at the terminator. All four scale-invariance gates pass. Inert
everywhere but the area path, which is the scope.

## The LTC half (partial -- see Open)

### The defect

`_ltcIntegrate` was missing the reference algorithm's horizon conditional
(Heitz/Dupuy/Hill/Neubelt, `ltc_quad.fs`):

```glsl
bool behind = dot(points[0] - P, cross(points[1]-points[0], points[3]-points[0])) < 0.0;
...
if (behind) z = -z;
```

The edge-vector sum is a *signed* area, so its tilt comes out negated for a
fragment looking at the back of the winding -- and the corners are wound along
`+dir`, the side the panel lights, so that is every fragment the plane test
admits. Without the conditional the horizon clip floors the form factor at
zero for a surface facing the panel and passes the grazing ones instead: the
response is inverted.

### Why it survived since 9.2

It does not look like a sign error. An inverted panel still produces
*something* that reads as lighting -- it lights the far side of an object
rather than the near side. Measured on `cornell_box` before the fix, the floor
directly under the ceiling panel returned **exactly 0**, and what remained lit
was the vertical faces of the boxes. A hardcoded 3%-of-white ambient filled in
the surfaces it blanked, so the room was dim rather than obviously wrong.

The decisive test is the one with no aesthetic ambiguity: **a panel directly
overhead must light the top of a sphere.** Before the fix it lights the
bottom.

### What the goldens record

`roughness_sweep`, `guard_thin_panel` and `cornell_box` were all generated
against the inverted response, so they are references for the bug. Their
movement is not evidence of a regression, and they cannot be regenerated until
the item below is closed.

Separately: **six goldens already fail at `0f6ea74`**, before any of this work
-- pre-existing staleness on master, unrelated and worth its own pass.

### Dead ends, recorded so they are not re-run

- **Flipping the corner winding.** Repairs `cornell_box` and breaks the sphere
  fixtures. CORRECTED (spec 10.5 era): "breaks the sphere fixtures" was
  measured against fixtures whose geometry was inside-out (below), so the
  conclusion is void. Measured with sound fixtures, the flip is equivalent to
  the shipped `behind` conditional: given `ltcPanel`'s plane test admits only
  the `+dir` side, `behind` is true for every admitted fragment (verified: an
  unconditional negation differs by 4 px in 921,600, PAE 1/255 -- last-bit
  noise). The conditional is kept as the reference's per-fragment form, per
  the comment in ltc.glsl.
- **"The horizon table fetch is broken."** It is not. The vendored
  `LTC_AMP_TABLE` and the GPU fetch agree; the apparent mismatch was a bug in
  the probe script, which matched `LTC_AMP_TABLE` in the file's *header
  comment* and read the MAT table instead.
- **Normal-offset receiver bias for the shadow acne.** Loses the same way it
  lost on the cascade path (recorded at `pbr_frag.glsl`): at a silhouette it
  pushes the sample *into* the caster, so it needs a displacement large enough
  to detach shadows everywhere else before it clears anything.

## Open -- ALL CLOSED

1. **The tilted/offset panel case: CLOSED -- it was never an LTC bug.** The
   crescent came from the fixtures, not the integrator: five generators wound
   their spheres inside-out (`[a, b, a+1]` under their phi/theta sweep faces
   every triangle inward), so back-face culling hid the near hemisphere and
   the camera shaded the INSIDE of the far one -- every visible normal pointed
   away from the viewer, and a panel on the camera's side sat below every
   fragment's horizon. A CPU transcription of `_ltcIntegrate` predicted the
   offset panel lights the spheres correctly; regenerating the fixtures with
   outward winding confirmed it on the GPU. The "vsum.z changes sign" claim
   was an artifact of comparing different surfaces, not the same one. Fixed
   in `e675ebe` (all five generators + a winding audit across every fixture).
2. **Goldens: regenerated** against corrected fixtures across `e675ebe` and
   spec 10.5's branch.
3. **Fixture aim: no re-aim needed.** With sound geometry the authored panel
   shows exactly what the sweep exists to show -- a sharp rectangular
   highlight at 0.15 broadening to a wash by 0.95, over a ground plane that
   renders for the first time.
