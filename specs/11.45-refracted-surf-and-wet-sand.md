# Waves that know where the shore is, and sand that remembers where they went

## Context

Two defects, and they are in that order because the second depends on the first.

**The surf does not refract.** `ocean.glsl:407-408` says so out loud:

```glsl
// Residual angle off the wind, radians, after refraction has turned each train shoreward.
const float OCEAN_TRAIN_ANGLE[3] = float[3](0.0, 0.38, -0.26);
```

Refraction is *assumed to have already happened* and frozen into a constant offset from the
**wind** bearing, with `OCEAN_SURF_OBLIQUE = 0.3` as a global "angle the crest makes with the
shore". So every train arrives from one compass direction everywhere in the world. On tree's
**round island that is wrong on most of the shoreline** — real waves wrap around a headland
and arrive very nearly shore-normal on every aspect, and right now the far side of the island
gets surf running *along* the beach instead of into it. The surf's *timing* is already
bed-driven (`tau = 2√h/(β√g)`, `:526`); only its *direction* is not.

**And the sand does not answer the surf.** `ground_beach_color` (`ground.c:147`) paints
`GROUND_SAND_WET` below a fixed 0.18 m and dry sand above, baked once into vertex colour at
mesh build (`ground.c:281`, `GL_STATIC_DRAW`). The tide climbs a beach whose wet line never
moves. `ground.h:143` already says what is missing, in the file's own words: *"what is wet
ABOVE it at any moment is the swash's business, not a band's."*

**Refraction is local; diffraction and reflection are not.** A wave turns because the end of
its crest in shallower water moves slower — that only asks what the depth gradient is *here*,
so a closed form gets it right (the WKB / eikonal limit; "Snell's law refraction" is a named
coastal-engineering method, not a hack). Diffraction and reflection ask where the energy came
from, which is non-local by construction and is what wave packets (SIGGRAPH 2017) and surface
wavelets (SIGGRAPH 2018) buy with a transport solver and its state. **We are not buying that**,
and a slope-0.09 sand beach is *dissipative* — its reflection coefficient is genuinely small,
so skipping those two is physically defensible here. It stops being defensible the day we add
a cliff or a seawall.

**Outcome:** surf that wraps the island and comes in facing the beach, breaking the way its own
slope and sea state say it should; and sand that darkens where the swash just reached, saturates
where every wave hits, and dries out behind it.

  `waterSurfHeight`, `waterSurfOmega`, `waterBeachSlope` (`:348-354`), `waterExtent` (`:65`);
- `OCEAN_GRAVITY` and the train / bore / swash / cusp / group constants (`:379-452`);
- `oceanBoreWave`, `oceanSurfTrains` (`:461-496`);
- `shoreRunup(...)` returning `{ float edge; vec2 dEdge; float runup; }` — the Stockdon R2%,
  capture, cusp, group and swash arithmetic now inline at `:552-597`.

`ocean.glsl` gains `#include "shore.glsl"` and `oceanSurf` calls it; `pbr_frag` includes only
`shore.glsl`. **Zero new samplers**, and the `_Static_assert` unit ledger at `render.c:34-61`
is untouched. The include expander is already include-once with chunk-to-chunk support
(`cetra/gen_shader_header.py:12-40`), so `water_frag` pulling both is safe.

**The one contract that matters, and it is what couples the two halves.** `oceanSurfTrains`
takes the shoreward direction as a **parameter** rather than reading the bed, because the bed
is a sampler `pbr_frag` cannot have:

- the water passes `-normalize(bed.dColumn)` — the bed's own downhill;
- `pbr_frag` passes `-normalize(Normal.xz)`, the **geometric** normal (`pbr_frag.glsl:4`, used
  as `Ng` at `:1008`), which on a beach face *is* the shore normal — smooth, free, and not the
  ripple-mapped one.

Two readings of the same surface, so the sand and the sea agree without the sand seeing the bed.

# Part A — the wave field

## A1 — refraction from the bed gradient

Replace the constant `OCEAN_TRAIN_ANGLE` residual and `OCEAN_SURF_OBLIQUE` with the real thing.

- Shoreward direction `s = -normalize(bed.dColumn)` (`dColumn` grows with depth, so its negative
  points to shallower water). Computed **once**, shared by all three trains.
- Snell on the shallow-water celerity `c = √(gh)`: `sinθ_local = sinθ_deep · √(h_local/h_deep)`,
  so θ → 0 at the waterline. Take `h_deep` as `OCEAN_SHOAL_FULL_M`, already the depth at which
  the file considers the field whole.
- Each train keeps its own deep-water angle off the wind; the rotation carries it toward `s`.
- **One field serves all three trains**: shallow water is non-dispersive, so every frequency
  refracts identically — the same gift the file already exploits to share one `tau` (`:401-403`).

Guards, both real: `dColumn ≈ 0` (flat bed) must fall back to the wind direction rather than
normalize a zero; and the effect is bed-gated, so with `bedAvailable == 0` it degenerates to
*exactly* today's arithmetic.

**That degeneracy is the safety argument.** Both water goldens are Gerstner with **no bed**, so
A1 must move them **0 px**. The corpus gap that made three foam fixes move zero golden pixels
works in our favour here.

The dome-bed arms (`water-shoal`, `water-surf`, `water-shore-foam`) *will* move and should — the
dome is a surface of revolution, so every train turns radially inward. They read ratios and
thresholds rather than pixels, so they should still pass; if one does not, that is a finding
about the arm.

## A2 — refractive focusing (judge after A1, not before)

Where contours converge the ray tube narrows and amplitude rises: headlands get bigger surf than
bays. Two ways in, and **A1 should be looked at before either is built**:

- **Runtime proxy** — sample the shoreward direction at two nearby points and take its
  convergence. Two extra bed fetches in the vertex stage.
- **Baked** — `_water_bake_bed` (`water.c:488`) already sweeps 256² on the CPU. Trace rays
  inward there properly, and store the gain per texel. The bed texture is `GL_RGBA32F` with
  height in `.r` and gradient in `.g/.b`, so **`.a` appears free** (confirm before relying on
  it) — no new sampler, zero runtime cost, and correct for curved contours where the local rule
  is not. Requires re-baking on **wind change**, which the current invalidation
  (`water.c:496-498`, level / extent / units) does not cover.

Both need a **caustic clamp**: where rays cross, the gain diverges. Geometric optics has no
answer there and neither will we — clamp and say so.

## A3 — the breaker type is the Iribarren number, not two literals

`OCEAN_BORE_SKEW 0.6` and `OCEAN_SWASH_SKEW 0.85` are look constants standing in for a physical
parameter we can already compute. ξ = tanβ / √(H/L₀): spilling below ~0.5, plunging 0.5–3.3,
surging above. `waterBeachSlope` is tanβ, `waterSurfHeight` is H, and `l0` is already computed
at `ocean.glsl:553`.

**Calibrate the mapping so tree's and the fixture's present ξ reproduce ~0.6 / 0.85.** Then the
change is a generalisation that pins to the current look at the current sea state, moves 0 px on
every existing frame, and only diverges when the sea state or the beach does — which is the
whole point, and the same discipline 11.42 used when the far-field roughness became an absolute
slope that collapses to the authored value where nothing is filtered.

# Part B — wet sand with a memory

## B1 — the model, in `pbr_frag`

March backwards in taps of `dt = T/3` (`T = 2π/waterSurfOmega`) over about three primary periods:

```
h = WorldPos.y - waterLevel                        // elevation above still water
for k in 0..N-1:
    e     = shoreRunup(WorldPos.xz, time - k*dt, -normalize(Normal.xz)).edge
    cover = 1 - smoothstep(-s, +s, h - e)
    wet   = 1 - (1 - wet)  * (1 - cover * decaySlow(k))   // (7) accumulate, do not max
    film  = 1 - (1 - film) * (1 - cover * decayFast(k))
wet = max(wet, waterTable(h))                              // (9)
```

**Item 7 is the one that makes history legible.** Accumulating rather than taking the max means
the lower beach — hit by every wave — saturates dark, while the upper beach, hit by one wave in
twenty, stays barely damp. That gradient is the memory; a max gives a step.

**Item 9** floors `wet` near the still line so the lower beach cannot flicker dry between waves —
the beach has a water table and sand below it never fully dries. One line.

**The shading (items 4–6), from Lagarde rather than from my guesses:**

- **(4) Porosity darkening.** `Kd *= (1 − α·Gp)`, α = sand's open porosity ~0.35–0.45, inside
  Lagarde's stated 25–50% band for natural materials. Replaces a tint multiply.
- **(6) `F0 → 0.02` and flatten the normal** toward `Ng` as the film fills the ripples. The
  surface reads shinier because it goes *smooth*, not more reflective — F0 goes **down**, toward
  water's, not up. The normal flattening is what makes a drained sheet read as a sheet.
- **(5) The hue shift — and one correction to make before writing it.** Beer-Lambert at water's
  `α = (0.0035, 0.0004, 0)` per cm over a film 0.1–1 mm thick is `exp(-0.0007) ≈ 0.9993`:
  **that term computes to nothing.** This session already shipped and reverted exactly that
  mistake once (the Cox-Munk capillary slope term, which measured to zero). So: **compute the
  number first, and if it is negligible, say so and use the mechanism that actually causes the
  shift** — Lekner–Dorf internal reflection, which raises the *effective* path through the
  substrate's own pigment. `a_wet ≈ a_dry^p`, p > 1: one line, correctly saturating (a channel
  the dry sand already absorbs gets absorbed more), and it composes with (4).
- **(8) Two-phase drying.** Fast film drain (seconds), slow pore evaporation (tens of seconds).
  Our two constants are already the right *shape* per the TVBRDF work; I do **not** have Sun et
  al.'s fitted numbers without reading the paper, so these start eyeballed and are flagged as
  such.

**Cost control.** Two things keep this off every other fragment:

- Analytic ceiling: `climb ≤ 1.35`, `env ≤ 1.4`, `swash ≤ 1.0`, `runup = r2/1.4·upm`, so
  `edge ≤ 1.35·r2·upm` — a uniform-computable scalar. Everything above it is dry with no taps run.
- Taps **share their train evaluations**: `swPrev(t_k)` is `sw(t_k − T)`, which at `dt = T/3` is
  `sw(t_{k+3})`. N taps cost N+3 evaluations of the three-train sum, not 2N.

**Gating, and the 0-px safety argument.**

- `Material` gains `float shore_wetness;` defaulting to 0, in the shape of `wind_response`.
  Uploaded in `_update_program_material_uniforms` (`render.c:145-270`), the per-material site.
- The shore floats are a per-frame global and go where the cloud shadow goes: a new
  `water_bind_shore(Water*, ShaderProgram*)` from the program-switch block at `render.c:407-422`,
  publishing what `water.c:1403-1429` already computes. No `Water`, or `surf` off → publish
  `waterSurfHeight = 0` and the term is dead.
- `pbr_frag` already gets `time` from `engine->render_time` (`render.c:348`) — the **same clock**
  `water.c:1251` gives the sea, so the sand cannot describe a different instant. Headless is
  frame-index accumulation, so determinism holds.
- Fade out past `waterExtent`, so an opted-in material is not wetted by geometry that merely sits
  below the run-up ceiling a kilometre inland.
- `bool wetness` on `Water` + `--no-water-wetness` (in `apps/render`'s parser and tree's own) —
  the bisect lever and the exact pre-change frame, mirroring `--no-water-caustics`.

**Nothing in the corpus sets `shore_wetness`, so all 24 goldens must stay 0 px.**

## B3 — the fixture can see it, and three arms

`beach_fixture.gltf` authors the dome **emissive over a black base colour**
(`gen_beach_fixture.py:132-142`), so an albedo change on it is invisible.

- Regenerate with a lit sand `baseColorFactor` **alongside** the emissive: the emissive keeps the
  underwater bed authored rather than lit (the docstring's stated reason) and the added lit term
  is what wetness moves. Free to change — no arm reads this fixture yet.
- `beach_fixture.cscn` sets `shoreWetness` through the `materials` block, which already records
  params generically by name and shape (`cscene.c:512-541`); the key needs one row in
  `cscene_apply.c`'s table.

Arms in `scripts/gates.py`, shaped like `water-surf` (`:4857-4892`):

- **`beach-wet`** — the swash band is darker with wetness than without, **and the dry crown box is
  0 px**. The second half is the claim that matters: wetness that darkened the whole beach would
  be a brightness knob wearing a beach's name.
- **`beach-wet-history`** — brightness rises **monotonically** across three boxes going up the
  beach. A band gives two levels; a memory gives a ramp. This is the arm that fails against the
  code being replaced.
- **`beach-wet-moves`** — px between frames 30 and 44 above a floor. A wet line that stood still
  is the painted band again.

`--no-water-wetness` reaching the off frame at 0 px is the reference `beach-wet` renders, so it is
asserted rather than assumed.

Note `gen_beach_fixture.py:21-22` already promises a `beach-shoreline` arm that does not exist —
**11.44's P5 arms are still owed** and are not in this plan's scope.
