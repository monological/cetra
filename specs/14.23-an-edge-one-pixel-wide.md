# cetra 14.23: an edge one pixel wide

## Context

pcb 7.1's board modal draws a board's cross-section as the rows of a scrolling pane, one a layer,
each drawing its slice of a via with `ui_draw_quad`. Where two rows meet, a faint line crosses the
via: one a row, the via drawn as a stack of pieces rather than one hole. The user found it looking
at the drawing (2026-11-05).

**Why it happens.** The UI's fill, `ui_frag.glsl:66-70`:

```glsl
// One texel of screen-space falloff. fwidth on the distance itself rather
// than a fixed epsilon, so the edge stays one pixel wide at any DPI and
// under any future UI scale.
float aa = max(fwidth(d), 1e-5);
float inside = 1.0 - smoothstep(-aa, aa, d);
```

`smoothstep(-aa, aa, d)` runs from a pixel inside the edge to a pixel outside it: two pixels wide,
not the one its comment says. A quad lying exactly on the pixel grid covers the pixel inside its
edge by 84% and the one outside by 16% (smoothstep at d = -0.5 and +0.5 pixels, 0.156). Two quads
meeting on a pixel boundary each paint both pixels of it part way, and 13% of what is under them
shows through each, a line across. The border's inner edge, `ring` (line 80), has the same two
pixels.

The rest of cetra already has it right: the glyph path above halves its width
(`sw = fwidth(dist) * 0.5`), and `alpha_coverage.glsl:51` and the water's edge
(`water_frag.glsl:1362`) use the one-pixel ramp, `clamp(x / fwidth(x) + 0.5, 0.0, 1.0)`.

Seen in pcb: the rows' heights made whole points, then each row snapped to whole points (whole
pixels at the window's 2x), left the line as it was; a crop of the via shows it at every row's
edge, and a faint darker line along every block's edge where it meets the gap beside it.

**What this fixes, and what it does not.** Shapes meeting on a pixel boundary meet with nothing
between them, and a shape on the grid fills its pixels whole. Two shapes meeting part way into a
pixel each still cover it in part, and what is under them shows there by up to a quarter (f times
1 - f): that is any per-shape coverage's, which no edge rule removes, and an app wanting a clean
join puts it on a pixel. Placing the layout's elements on whole pixels is not this spec's.

Branch and spec: `14.23-an-edge-one-pixel-wide`, off cetra's master (`b88d862b`).

## The design

One rule in `ui_frag.glsl`, the fill's edge and the border's inner edge:

- **The edge is one pixel, centred on it**: the box filter's coverage of a straight edge, the form
  `alpha_coverage.glsl` uses.

  ```glsl
  float aa = max(length(vec2(dFdx(d), dFdy(d))), 1e-5); // d's change across one pixel
  float inside = clamp(0.5 - d / aa, 0.0, 1.0);
  ...
  float ring = border_w > 0.0 ? clamp(0.5 + (d + border_w) / aa, 0.0, 1.0) : 0.0;
  ```

  A pixel whose centre is half a pixel inside an edge is covered whole, one half a pixel outside not
  at all, and an edge through a pixel's centre covers it by half. The derivatives' length rather than
  `fwidth`'s sum of them, so a rounded corner's edge at 45 degrees is one pixel too, not 1.4.
- **Its comment says what it does**: a one-pixel ramp centred on the edge, so a shape on the pixel
  grid fills its pixels whole and two meeting there leave nothing between them.
- Glyphs are untouched.

What changes to the eye: every UI edge a pixel crisper. A rectangle on the grid is hard-edged; a
rounded corner's ramp narrows from about two pixels to one; a border's inner edge likewise. Text is
as it was.

## Tests

A case in `apps/canvas`, beside `--ui-borders` (spec 14.12), and an arm in the `canvas` group of
`gates.py`, its docstring in the list:

- **`--ui-seams`** -> `canvas-ui-seams`. On an opaque backdrop of one colour, boxes of another, each
  printed by the probe as its rect in pixels:
  - `stack`: a column of quads, each a whole number of pixels high, meeting edge to edge on pixel
    boundaries, as pcb's rows: every pixel from the column's top to its bottom is the quads' colour,
    within 2 codes.
  - `alone`: one quad on whole pixels: every pixel inside it is its colour and every one outside the
    backdrop's, within 2 codes: its edge is inside it, one pixel.
  - `half`: a quad whose edge falls half way across a column of pixels: those pixels are half way
    between the two colours, within 4 codes.
  - `round`: a box with rounded corners: along a corner's diagonal, no more than two pixels are
    neither the box's colour nor the backdrop's.
- `canvas-ui-borders` and `canvas-under-ui` pass as they are: the border's outer edge is the fill's,
  and both change alike.

## Phases

0. **Spec**: this plan into `specs/` on the branch, committed alone; a task list.
1. **The fix**: the rule in `ui_frag.glsl` and its comment; `--ui-seams` and its arm. Build with no
   warnings; `scripts/gates.py --only canvas,ui,gate-docs`, nothing else rendering. **Falsified**:
   the old `smoothstep` put back, `stack` and `alone` fail. **Look**: `apps/canvas --ui-borders` and
   `--ui-seams` headless, master's and the branch's, each rendered twice first for the noise floor,
   crops side by side for the user; and a screen of the UI at its densest (`apps/gametest`'s menu),
   before and after. Every golden whose app draws a UI over it is found and rendered again; an edge
   moving by its one pixel is the change this spec makes, shown, not a decision. As built,
   committed.
2. **Docs**: `docs/ui.md`, what an edge is (one pixel, centred on it; a shape on the grid filled
   whole); `docs/eda-roadmap.md`, a 14.23 bullet under "Not among the nine" and the smaller specs'
   line to 14.23. Committed.

**Then, with the user's say-so**: cetra's master fast-forwards and the branch is deleted; pcb moves
`extern/cetra` to it, and its board modal puts its rows on whole pixels (pcb's side).
