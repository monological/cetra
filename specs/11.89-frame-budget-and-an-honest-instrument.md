# Spec 11.89 — a frame budget, and an instrument honest enough to hold it

## Context

The AAA rendering roadmap is finished. Fifty-one of its fifty-four rows are DONE, SHIPPED, or
closed with a measurement; of the three left, one (E7 occlusion culling) the roadmap argues against
itself, one (F2 asset cook) is a build-system project, and one (G2 alpha distribution) is a texture
pre-process.

**That document cannot see what is actually wrong, because it is a feature list and a feature list
has no denominator.** It was written to answer "what does this renderer not have", and the answer is
now "very little". It was never asked "how long does a frame take", so it has no row for the largest
remaining gap between Cetra and a shipping renderer: **the engine is not real-time on its own
flagship scene.**

`apps/forest`, release, M1 Max, 1600x900 window (3200x1800 Retina framebuffer), TAA path, 120
headless frames:

- **133 ms/frame wall — 7.5 fps.**
- CPU-timed 124.6 ms against GPU-timed 115.5 ms, so the frame is **CPU-bound**.
- Shadow (33.1 ms) and opaque (60.0 ms) are **81%** of GPU time. The entire post chain is 19%.

A shipping game has 16.6 ms. Forest is 8x over.

This spec does three things: it writes the budget down, it establishes the evidence base that the
next five specs are built on, and it repairs the instrument — because three of the numbers the
profiler currently prints are wrong or missing, and a performance programme measured against a
lying instrument is the hack-and-measure loop this one exists to avoid.

---

## The evidence base

Every later spec in this series cites this section rather than re-deriving it.

**Noise floor first**, per `docs/verification.md`. Three baseline runs: total GPU 115.2 / 115.5 /
115.5 ms; opaque 60.0 / 60.0 / 62.1 ms. Totals are good to ~0.3%, the opaque row to ~3.5%.

| config | GPU total | opaque | shadow GPU | shadow CPU | shadow draws | shadow tris | opaque tris |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 115.5 | 60.0 | 33.1 | 33.1 | 7,630 | 163.6M | 25.5M |
| quarter pixels (`-W 800 -H 450`) | 71.6 | 31.2 | 33.2 | 30.9 | 7,630 | 163.6M | 25.5M |
| 2.25x pixels (`-W 2400 -H 1350`) | 191.0 | 105.5 | 34.0 | 33.4 | 7,630 | 163.6M | 25.5M |
| `--lod-bias 0.5` | 118.1 | 56.5 | 39.0 | 43.5 | 9,916 | 128.4M | 18.6M |
| `--no-lod` | 129.8 | 80.2 | 29.8 | **11.9** | **1,621** | 165.9M | 25.9M |

### The opaque pass is two costs, and the split is measured over a 9x pixel range

Fitting `opaque = fixed + k·megapixels` to the 1.44 / 5.76 / 12.96 Mpx points gives
**21.6 ms fixed + 6.67 ms per megapixel**, which predicts all three within 2.5% (31.2 / 60.0 / 108
predicted against 31.2 / 60.0 / 105.5 measured).

So at the shipping framebuffer the pass is ~21.6 ms of geometry and ~38.4 ms of shading.
**A fragment-shader optimisation addresses at most 64% of the pass and 33% of the frame**, which is
worth knowing before anyone spends a month in `pbr_frag`.

The fixed term is 25.5M triangles against 5.76M pixels — **4.4 triangles per pixel.** That is the
micropolygon regime, where a sub-pixel triangle still costs a full 2x2 quad of fragment
invocations. The cost scales with triangles rather than pixels, which is exactly why it lands in
the fixed term and why the `GL_SAMPLES_PASSED` counter cannot see it.

### LOD currently costs more than it saves

At the default bias it removes **1.5%** of opaque triangles (25.9M → 25.5M) and adds 251 opaque
draws and **6,009 shadow draws**. Pushing it harder makes the frame slower: `--lod-bias 0.5` buys
21% fewer shadow triangles and pays 2,286 more draws for them, and the total goes up.

### CPU cost is per-draw, at ~3.5 µs

From the two shadow points: (33.1 − 11.9) ms / (7,630 − 1,621) draws. That is very high for a draw
call, and §2 below says where it goes.

## Nine root causes

Established by reading the code against the measurements above. Each is cited by the spec that
fixes it.

### 1. Cascade 1 and cascade 2 cover almost the same ground

`shadow.c:1399-1421` uses a practical (Zhang) split with **`lambda = 0.75` as a literal** at
`:1412`, and `shadow_dist = fminf(ss->far_plane, camera->far_clip)` at `:1414` — **there is no
shadow distance separate from the camera far clip.** Forest's camera far clip is 2000, so the
splits land at 172.7 / 427.9 / 2000.

At fov 58° and 16:9, `k2 = 1.278 > 1`, so the `zc > slice_far` clamp at `shadow.c:342` always
fires, `dz = 0`, and `radius = 1.1306 · slice_far`. The outermost cascade is not a slice fit at all
— it is the classic whole-scene fit (`shadow.c:1461-1467`) at `2 × ortho_size`:

| cascade | ortho box width | texel @2048 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 390.5 | 0.191 |
| 1 | **967.8** | **0.473** |
| 2 | **1000.0** | **0.488** |

**Cascade 1 and cascade 2 are within 3% of each other in texel density and cover nearly the same
ground.** Cascade 1 costs a full whole-scene traversal — roughly a third of the shadow pass — and
buys almost nothing. Compounding it, `pbr_frag.glsl:825` unions the fragment's own cascade and
every wider one, so a near fragment samples both near-identical maps.

### 2. Every instanced draw uploads 12 KB of uniform block

`instance_chunk_upload` (`render.c:729-753`) ends with `ubo_upload(ubo, chunk, sizeof(*chunk))`, and
`InstanceChunk` is three `mat4[64]` arrays — **12,288 bytes** (`render.h:186-188`, `ubo.h:59`).
`ubo_upload` (`ubo.c:44-46`) orphans the whole buffer and re-uploads it. Across 8,558 draws that is
roughly **105 MB of buffer traffic per frame**, and it happens on depth draws too, which fill only
`model` and leave the other two arrays carrying whatever the last batch left there
(`render.c:737-738`).

**This does not contradict spec 11.28.** `ubo.h:229-235` records that a *partial write after the
orphan* measured 4.3 ms/frame slower, and explains why: the orphan hands back a fresh block, where a
prefix leaves the driver owing an answer for the rest. That reasoning is correct, and it is an
argument against partial writes — not against removing the orphan.

### 3. Runs must be contiguous, so culling and LOD shatter batching

`draw_run_can_join` (`draw_list.c:327-329`) requires the same mesh, the same LOD level, and
visibility; `_visible_run` (`render.c:702-713`) and `_visible_caster_run` (`shadow.c:633-645`) then
walk **contiguous** items only, because `instance_chunk_upload` indexes `list->items[first + k]`
with raw indices and cannot skip. One culled item, one lane change or one LOD change ends a run.
Mean batch size in the shadow pass is **2.79 of a possible 64.**

LOD is selected from the **camera** (`render.c:722`), so instances form concentric LOD rings around
the camera, and every ring boundary crossed in graph order ends a shadow run — even where the
cascade sees those instances identically.

**Spec 11.28's reason for camera-eye selection is sound and stays** (`draw_list.h:62-65`): a caster
drawn at a coarser level than the camera shades it with has a silhouette that disagrees with its own
surface, which reads as acne. That is an argument against *coarsening a drawn caster*. It is not an
argument for the shadow pass inheriting the camera's *batch fragmentation*, which is what happens
today.

### 4. The shadow pass re-uploads `model` even for instanced runs

`shadow.c:703` calls `uniform_set_mat4(u, "model", ...)` unconditionally. The camera path guards
exactly this at `render.c:899`, with the reason at `:894-898`: *"a batched draw reads the transform
from the instance block, and writing `model` anyway dirties the program's whole uniform block and
costs a copy of it at submission. This pass exists to be cheap; it should not re-buy that."* The
pass that issues more draws than any other in the engine does not have the guard — roughly 5,000
dead `glUniformMatrix4fv` per frame.

### 5. Nothing about shadows is cached, staggered, or skipped

No dirty flag, no sun-moved test, no frame parity, no static/dynamic caster split. Every cascade of
every caster is cleared and fully re-rendered every frame, and each punctual face costs a whole
scene traversal — `shadow.c:1556-1557` says so in its own comment.

**The outermost cascade is the natural first cache target and carries the largest caster set.** Its
matrix comes from `compute_directional_light_space_matrix(light->direction, scene_center,
ortho_size, near_plane, far_plane)` (`shadow.c:1462`), which in forest depends on nothing that
changes. Skipping `begin_shadow_pass` also skips its `glClear`, so content persists correctly.

Two things block naive caching and both are tractable. **Wind** displaces every tree every frame
(`wind.glsl:79-128`), and `object_position.glsl:1-24` establishes that the shadow pass must displace
identically to the shading pass — but `wind_max_offset` (`wind.c:89-125`) is already a closed-form
conservative envelope, so a cached map can be validated against it. And **movement detection needs
no new storage**, because `prev_global_transform` / `prev_valid` already exist for motion vectors
(`scene.h:51-57`).

### 6. The cluster-DAG builder emits bands that reduce nothing

`--cluster-probe` over forest's 20 prototypes:

```
mesh=0  (trunk)  band0=16344 band1=967   band2=241  band3=59
mesh=1  (canopy) band0=12740 band1=12740 band2=6370 band3=5495
mesh=12 (rock)   band0=1280  band1=1280  band2=1280 band3=640
```

Trunks simplify 277x. **Canopy band 1 reduces nothing at all**, and rocks have **three identical
bands**. A degenerate band costs duplicated index memory in the EBO *and* a distinct value in the
batch key, so two instances drawing literally the same triangles cannot share a draw.

`lod.c` already refuses exactly this — `LOD_MIN_SHRINK 0.85f` (`lod.c:32`), applied at `:95-96`
with the comment *"a level that saves nothing is worse than no level."* The DAG builder
(`cluster_build.cpp:184-212`) has only an empty-selection guard and no shrink test, while
`finalize_mesh` (`forest.c:311-338`) treats the two builders as interchangeable. **The lesson was
learned in one builder and not carried to the other.**

### 7. One uber-shader for everything, with dead-code elimination defeated

`glShaderSource` is called from a single place with `count = 1` (`shader.c:88`). There is no define
injection and no permutation system; the program cache is one program per name
(`engine.c:1532-1566`), and forest's four materials all point at the same `pbr` program.

So every leaf-card fragment runs a shader that also contains decals, IES profiles, LTC area lights,
clearcoat, sheen, SSS, POM, transmission, four-layer triplanar terrain and a 16-tap GI walk. The
shader's own comment (`:1558-1566`) explains why the driver cannot drop any of it: **the terminal
`FragColor` write at `:2439` is unconditional, which defeats DCE for the whole body.** And
`sampleLayeredSurface` is called *unconditionally* at `:1066` — the `layerCount <= 0` test is inside
it — so a leaf card links the entire four-layer body.

**The calibration for what that costs is already in the tree** (`pbr_frag.glsl:1146-1153`), and it
is the most useful number in this spec:

> Hoisting that pair up to this point to serve both — which is the obvious way to write it, and how
> this shipped first — cost **11% of the opaque pass on a scene with no decals in it**, against 3.1%
> for the whole rest of the feature. Two vec3s live across the entire shader body is register
> pressure every fragment pays whether or not it takes this branch.

Two `vec3`s = 11% of the opaque pass. Roughly 95 scalars are live entering the light loop, among
them `layerSurf` (11 floats), `decalSurf` (10) and `ltcMinv` + `ltcAmp` (11).

Separately: **every shader in the tree is `#version 330 core` against a 4.1 context**, so
`textureGather` is available and unused. The 3x3 PCF kernel is 9 fetches where 3 gathers would do,
and PCSS is 32.

### 8. The CPU column is not a measurement after the first heavy pass

`profiler_scope_begin/end` (`profiler.c:391-419`) takes plain wall clock around the entire scope
body. `profiler.h:21-29` states the consequence: a draw that blocks on a full command queue charges
GPU time to the CPU column.

**So SSR at 10.4 ms CPU and atmosphere at 10.6 ms are backpressure, not work** — they are fullscreen
passes with a few dozen GL calls between them. Only the shadow row has real submission in it. The
GPU column *is* trustworthy: a 4-frame query ring, availability-checked and never waited on
(`profiler.c:152-204`).

**And `draw_list_build` is outside every profiler scope.** It runs at `shadow.c:1267`, before the
`"shadow cascades"` scope opens at `:1526`, doing a full recursive DFS with a per-item
`aabb_transform` over 9,052 items — every frame, because the stamp folds in `total_frames`
(`render.c:725`). Its cost is in no row.

### 9. Smaller, independently true

- **`glGetError` runs 23 times per frame in `postfx.c`**, unconditional in release
  (`util.c:38-43`). On a GL-over-Metal driver each is a round trip.
- **The light cluster build has no zero-light early-out** (`light_cluster.c:574-648`). With forest's
  1 directional and 0 clusterable lights it still memsets ~25 KB, runs 6,144 loop iterations, and
  uploads a 12 KB all-zero grid — and again for each probe-capture face.
- **`_item_bounds` is recomputed about seven times per item per frame** (`draw_list.c:272-315`) —
  once per cascade layer, twice for the prepass sweeps, once to shade — with no caching.
- **Shadows read last frame's transforms.** `render_shadow_depth_pass` (`engine.c:2607`) runs before
  `apply_transform_to_nodes` (`forest.c:2434`). Already known; what was not recorded is that **LOD is
  therefore selected from stale positions too**, and that this blocks cascade caching.
- **100 MB of shadow VRAM is dead.** The array is sized `MAX_SHADOW_LIGHTS × cascade_count` = 9
  layers at 2048² (`shadow.c:187`); forest uses 3. Deliberate (`shadow.c:962-965`) but real.
- **Foliage `discard` forfeits early-Z on the shadow map**, and every foliage draw is two-sided
  (`shadow.c:737-738`), so each leaf card rasterizes both faces into the map. In a canopy scene this
  is the dominant shadow-map fill cost.
- **Auto-exposure blocks for a measured 5.25 ms** against 0.033 ms for PBO+fence
  (`postfx.c:1887-1899`). The comment rejects the PBO **deliberately**, to keep headless runs
  deterministic, and that trade stands. Forest pins exposure (`forest.c:2186`) so it is not in the
  numbers above — but `render` and `tree` pay it every frame.

## What this spec builds

Phase 0 of six. It adds no optimisation and must move no pixel; it exists so the five specs after it
are measurable.

1. **A real wall-clock frame row.** Both tables print a placeholder `FRAME (wall) 100.000 ms`
   against a measured 133 ms frame. Every ratio in a performance spec has this as its denominator,
   and right now the denominator is a constant.

2. **The CPU column's caveat, printed where the column is read.** `profiler.h:21-29` has it; the
   stdout table does not. That gap is why SSR's 10.4 ms reads as work. A reader of the table should
   not have to open the header to know the column is a ceiling.

3. **`draw_list_build` inside a scope.** A full scene flatten per frame, currently invisible and
   currently attributed to nothing.

4. **The budget, written down.** `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md` gains the target and this
   six-spec programme as rows, so the next person asking "what is left" finds it where they look.

## The programme this opens

Run in order, each its own spec and branch:

| spec | phase | what |
|---|---|---|
| **11.89** | 0 | this — the budget and the instrument |
| 11.90 | 1 | the shadow pass: causes 1, 3, 4, 5 |
| 11.91 | 2 | the instance arena: cause 2 |
| 11.92 | 3 | the LOD band shrink guard: cause 6 |
| 11.93 | 4 | shader permutations and `textureGather`: cause 7 |
| 11.94 | 5 | the small independent wins: cause 9 |

**Defaults change and goldens rebake.** A cascade split that makes cascade 1 redundant with
cascade 2 is wrong for every app, not only forest, so it gets fixed rather than hidden behind an
opt-in flag. The obligation that comes with that: every golden that moves is diffed, looked at, and
its move explained in the spec that moved it. A rebake is not a way to make a regression quiet.
Phases 0, 2, 3 and 5 must be 0 px on their own — they change submission mechanics and band
selection, not shading or fit, so movement there is a bug. Only Phases 1 and 4 are expected to move
pixels, and each names its fixtures and its reason before rebaking.

## What this programme will not do

- **E7 occlusion culling.** The roadmap already argues against it and this data agrees: depth
  complexity is 1.81, and the cost is shading and submission, not hidden-object overdraw.
- **Chase the SSR / atmosphere / spec-occ CPU numbers.** Cause 8.
- **Turn the depth prepass on.** Specs 11.30/11.31 measured it as a substitute for the sort rather
  than a complement, and the loser on the clock.
- **Raise `--lod-bias`.** Measured above: it makes the frame slower.

## Verification

- Noise floor before any number, per `docs/verification.md`. Established here at ~0.3% on the frame
  total and ~3.5% on the opaque row.
- `scripts/gates.py --only <group>` scoped to the blast radius; the whole suite once at the end.
- `scripts/goldens.py` — **all 29 at 0 px.** This spec adds instrumentation only, so any movement is
  a defect in the instrumentation.
