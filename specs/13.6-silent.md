# Spec 13.6 -- silent: a grimy kitchen and its foggy street

## Context

The user wants a new app inspired by (not a copy of) a screenshot of a grimy horror-game kitchen:
- a grimy kitchen lit by fluorescent tubes;
- a first-person player;
- a window onto a foggy street of houses, not a cityscape.

Answers already given:
- **The look is PS2 era**: low-poly boxy models, low-res procedural textures, smooth filtering, heavy fog, grain.
- **The street is walkable**: a door leads out onto it.
- **Extras**: a flashlight, a flickering tube, and ambient audio.

Everything is procedural (meshes and textures) and built in C, like tree, forest and gametest. The reference screenshot is not itself low-poly; it is a dense, photoscan-like capture. The PS2 look comes from four things:
1. few polygons, flat-shaded;
2. a low texel density (about 128 px/m, textures 128-256 px);
3. baked-looking dirt (edge grime, drips, stains in the albedo rather than detail in the geometry);
4. fog and darkness hiding the draw distance.

**No engine change is required for any of that.** Engine additions are only an optional later phase, if the look review asks for them.

## Decisions (mine, stated)

- **App name** `apps/silent`, branch `13.6-silent`, spec `specs/13.6-silent.md`.
- **Night by default**, to match the reference: lit house windows, street lamps in fog, flashlight. `--day` gives a white overcast fog world.
- **Textures and props are app-local** (`apps/silent/src/`), not in `cetra/src/procedural/`: nothing else consumes them, and that keeps the public API unchanged.
- **Every prop is described once**: a list of boxes drives both the mesh and the static Jolt collider. This is gametest's `grotto_box` pattern (`gametest.c:843`), so what you see and what you bump into cannot drift apart.

## Reused

- **Player**: the game framework, Jolt `CharacterVirtual`, input actions, mouse capture, and a `CameraRig` at distance 0, all copied from forest's structure:
  - action table `forest.c:276-289`; cursor capture `forest.c:301`;
  - physics and character init `forest.c:2524-2622`;
  - move `forest.c:2780-2840`; look `forest.c:2919-2964`.
- **Meshes**:
  - `mesh_builder.h` (`mb_init`/`mb_vertex`/`mb_tri`/`mb_transfer`), with a normal per face vertex for flat shading;
  - `mesh_generate_box` (already flat, 24 vertices).
  - Never rely on `mesh_compute_normals`, which smooths across shared vertices.
- **Textures**:
  - `veg_fbm2_tiled` / `veg_worley2_tiled` (seed right before each bake, on one thread);
  - `noise_perlin3_tiled` with its own `NoisePerm`;
  - `texture_load_memory_owned` with `texture_desc(is_srgb)` and `TEXTURE_USE_NORMAL`;
  - the packed layout of `terrain_layer_maps`: albedo+height, and normal.xy / roughness / AO.
- **Lights**:
  - `LIGHT_AREA` tubes (LTC, with shadows), following gametest's worked example `gametest.c:1850-1882`;
  - a spot light for the flashlight, with shadows;
  - spot lights for the street lamps.
  - Budget: at most 8 punctual shadow layers.
- **Fog**: global froxel fog at a low density for indoor haze, plus up to 8 dense feathered `FogVolume`s over the street (`scene_add_fog_volume`), and `postfx_set_fog_ambient` for the colour. Volumes only add density, so the house interior stays clear.
- **Glass**: `transmission` about 0.9, `thickness` 0, with a grime opacity/albedo map.
- **Post**: grain, vignette, dither, and optionally the film look (`postfx_apply_film_look`) and render scale 0.5.
- **Audio**: `AUDIO_SOURCE` components carrying procedural tones; `audio_system_read_pcm` for headless runs.
- **Headless args**: the hand-rolled parse in `tree.c:937-1070`: `-x -f -S -W -H --screenshot-every`, plus gametest-style `--cam-eye/--cam-target` so a framing can be photographed twice.

## Layout

```
 street (x along road, ~70 m, houses both sides, lamps every ~15 m, fog volumes)
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   sidewalk | front yard + porch
            | [front door]--hallway--[kitchen 4.6 x 3.6 x 2.6 m]
            |                         window on the street-facing side wall
```

The player starts in the kitchen. The hallway turns the view, so the street is seen first through the window. The house the kitchen sits in is one of the procedural houses, with a real interior.

## Phases

Each phase ends with:
- `./build.sh`;
- a headless screenshot at pinned framings, **shown to the user** (the app draws, so it gets looked at every phase);
- a commit, and the spec's as-built section updated.

### 0. Setup
- Branch off master and copy this plan into specs. Commit it on its own.

### 1. Skeleton: walk a room
- `apps/silent/CMakeLists.txt` (`add_cetra_app(silent)`) and `src/silent.c`: args, `main`, the game hooks.
- `src/player.c/.h`: forest's walker, trimmed down to WASD, mouse look, sprint, crouch-less.
- `src/kit.c/.h`, the prop kit:
  - `KitBox {centre, half, rot_y, material id, collide}`;
  - `kit_build(...)` emits the flat-shaded mesh and the static box body.
  - **UVs are world-scaled** (u,v = metres × texels-per-metre / size), so texel density is the same on every surface. This is the thing that makes it read as PS2.
- The kitchen shell: floor, ceiling, walls with a window opening and a door opening, and one placeholder light.
- **Check**: walk into a wall and stop.

### 2. Procedural textures (`src/tex.c/.h`)
- One height/grime field per kind, and all maps derived from it (the bark pattern). 256² unless stated. Kinds:
  - **Floor tile**: 30 cm ceramic, grout, cracked tiles, a wet sheen held in the roughness.
  - **Backsplash tile**: 15 cm.
  - **Plaster wall**: stains, damp, peeling patches.
  - **Ceiling**: water stains.
  - **Cabinet enamel**: off-white, with chips down to rust.
  - **Metals**: stainless steel (sink, hood), brushed steel.
  - **Wood**: table, chair, doors.
  - **Lino/rug**.
  - **Outside**: asphalt with a centre line, concrete sidewalk, clapboard siding (3 tints), shingle roof, brick, fence wood.
  - **Window grime**: opacity.
- **A shared grime pass**: an AO darkening toward the bottom edge, vertical drip streaks from the seeded noise, and blotchy stains. The dirt is authored here, not in the geometry.
- **Shown**: a swatch sheet PNG, plus the kitchen with textures on.

### 3. Kitchen props (`src/kitchen.c`)
- An L of lower cabinets with a counter, doors and handles.
- Upper cabinets.
- Stove and oven with knobs, and a range hood with a duct.
- Fridge with magnets and paper notes.
- Sink with a faucet.
- Table and two chairs.
- An open shelf with jars and bottles (6-8 sided cylinders, tinted glass-ish).
- Plates, pots, a towel on the oven handle, a floor mat.
- Tube fixtures.
- Seeded clutter placement; `--seed` changes it.
- Everything walkable collides; small clutter doesn't.

### 4. Lights and the flashlight
- **Two ceiling/window tubes and the hood strip** as `LIGHT_AREA` with emissive tube meshes. The colour is a cool green-white, about 4100 K tinted toward green.
- **Flicker**: one tube follows a seeded pattern (mostly on, bursts of stutter). Light intensity and `emissive_strength` are driven from one value, so they cannot disagree. `--no-flicker` turns it off.
  - The pattern is a function of the frame index (sim time), so a headless run is deterministic.
- **Flashlight**:
  - a spot light with shadows, at the camera with a small offset, lagging the view slightly;
  - `F` and pad Y toggle it;
  - `--no-flashlight`, and `--flashlight` to start with it on (it starts off indoors).
- **Exposure is pinned to a stated EV** in the app, so the dark reads as dark and the meter doesn't open it up.
- **Shown**: the reference-like framing, the flashlight on and off, and flicker frames.

### 5. The street (`src/street.c`)
- **Road**: asphalt, curbs, sidewalks, and grass-less dirt yards.
- **House generator** (seeded): 1-2 storeys, a box body with a gable roof prism and eaves, a porch with posts, door and windows.
  - Windows are frames plus glass. About 25% are lit: emissive, warm, with a curtain-shaded albedo, and no light object (cost).
  - Siding tint and variation.
- **Also**: fences, street lamps (spot lights, the nearest 3-4 with shadows by budget, the rest emissive only), telephone poles with low-poly wires, a boxy parked car, a mailbox.
- **Colliders**: house boxes, fences, poles and the car. The street ends in fog and an invisible wall, where the fog is thick enough to hide it.
- **The player's own house**: the hallway and front door connect the kitchen to the porch.
- **Fog**:
  - global haze density about 0.01;
  - 4-6 street volumes at density about 0.25, feathered 3 m, not overlapping the house;
  - ambient grey-blue at night, white by day.
  - The distance to full fog is about 25 m by day and 15 m at night. That is judgment, to tune with the user.
- **`--day`**: an overcast sky or a dim sun, white fog ambient, no lamps, windows unlit.
- **Shown**: from the window, from the porch, and mid-street, at night and by day.

### 6. Glass and the look pass
- The window pane and house windows: a transmissive grime pane.
- **Post**: grain about 0.06, vignette, dither on, a mild green-grey grade (a small generated `.cube`, or the film look), and bloom kept low.
- **Try `render_scale` 0.5** for the PS2 softness, under TAA in a window.
- **Tuning**: fog and exposure together with the user, from A/B crops.

### 7. Ambient audio
- **Fridge hum**: about 60 Hz plus a harmonic, positional at the fridge.
- **Fluorescent buzz**: about 120 Hz, quiet, at each tube; the flickering tube's buzz follows its intensity.
- **Outside**: faint wind noise, if the procedural source supports noise; otherwise skipped and said so.
- `--mute`, and `--audio-probe` for headless offline renders.

### 8. Optional engine additions (only if the phase 6 review asks)
- `TextureDesc.filter`: `LINEAR` (today's trilinear + 8x aniso, the default, so nothing moves) or `NEAREST` / `BILINEAR` (no aniso). It is honoured in `texture_set_default_sampler_state` (`texture.c:100`).
- A `render_scale` floor of 0.25 with a nearest upscale on the non-TAA blit (`postfx.c:3593`).
- Each addition gets its own commit, and goldens must stay at 0 px because the defaults are unchanged.

### 9. Wrap-up
- **No gate arms and no golden for this demo** (the user's call). Verification is by looking: headless screenshots at pinned framings, plus a windowed run.
- **Docs**: AGENTS.md's app table and headless list, and `docs/cli-reference.md` (silent flags).
- **Reviews**: thermo-nuclear and /simplify, in the background. Wait for all of them before applying anything.
- **Suites**: none for the app itself. Only if phase 8 touches the engine, run `goldens.py` once to confirm the defaults didn't move.

## Risks
- **Punctual shadow budget (8)**: the flashlight, 3 tubes and 3-4 lamps is at the limit. Nearer lamps take shadows; the rest go without.
- **Fog volume cap (8)**: the street has to fit in 4-6 boxes.
- **LTC area tubes at 5 cm wide** can alias in speculars. The hood strip can fall back to a spot if it does.
- **Game-framework apps and TAA headless**: headless screenshots run with TAA off and render scale 1, so an A/B crop compares like with like.
- **The look is judgment**: every phase shows crops before moving on.

## As built

### 1. Skeleton
- **The app**: `apps/silent/src/`:
  - `silent.c` (args, hooks, sky, post);
  - `player.c` (forest's walker cut down: walk, hurry, look, no jump);
  - `kit.c` (flat-shaded batched geometry plus colliders, world-metre UVs);
  - `mats.c` (one kit slot per surface, flat colours for now);
  - `house.c` (the shell), `street.c` (road, kerbs, yards, the world's edge), `layout.h` (every dimension).
- **Sky**: a night sky from apps/tree's setup: the Hillaire atmosphere with the sun at -18 degrees, the moon, stars, the night floor, and the IBL baked from it.
- **Added, not in the plan: a GI probe volume over the house.** The first frame had the ceiling lit navy: the sky's IBL irradiance reaches every closed room, because nothing occludes it.
  - The DDGI volume (12x3x9 probes, converged in one sweep at load) replaces that irradiance with what each probe actually sees, so a room is lit by the tube's bounce and not the sky.
  - The grid is placed so no probe centre lands in a wall.
  - Outside the grid, queries clamp to the front-yard probes.
- **For looking at it headless**: `--pad-script` (the engine's scripted pad on slot 0) and `--trace-player`.
- **Collision**: walking straight at the window stops the capsule at z = 10.39: the wall's inner face at 10.09, plus the 0.28 radius and the skin.

### 2. Surfaces: photo textures, not procedural
- **The procedural bake was rejected on look, and the user chose photo textures.** Noise cut off at hard thresholds read as camouflage and clip art. The reference's dirt is photographic: low contrast, soft and fine.
- **The set**: 17 CC0 sets from Poly Haven, fetched by `apps/silent/tools/fetch_textures.py`. They are cut from 1k down to 256 square and committed under `assets/textures/silent/` (4.4 MB). The script records every regrade it makes: saturation, gain, and one quarter-turn for the horizontal siding.
- **The backsplash is the floor set at a 0.6 m repeat**, which gives the reference's 15 cm dirty square tiles.
- **Two conventions the maps must meet, both found by rendering:**
  - Rows are stored bottom first. The loader uploads a PNG's first row as v = 0, and the kit points V up every wall.
  - Roughness is stored as grey RGB. `pbr_frag` reads a roughness map's green channel (glTF packing), so a one-channel file read as zero roughness and every wall came out a mirror.
- **Floor wetness** is the material's roughness factor (0.55 on the kitchen tile), not a separate map.

### 3-4. The kitchen, and its light
- **Laid out after the reference:**
  - an L of cabinets with the sink under a cross-framed window;
  - the stove under a stainless chimney hood, with the fridge beside it on the left wall;
  - uppers either side of the window;
  - shelves of glass jars over a sideboard on the right;
  - a table and ladder-back chairs, a rug, a towel on the oven bar, and seeded clutter.
- Placed in `KitFrame`s against each wall: along, up, out.
- **The tubes are flat emissive strips, and the engine's emissive-light pass derives their LTC panels.** The flicker changes the material's strength and the panel follows. Irradiance captures leave the strips out, so the GI doesn't count them twice.
  - Range and shadows are set on the derived lights by name each frame. Left derived, a 6000-nit panel claims every cluster.
  - A one-frame cluster-overflow warning remains at frame 0, before the panels exist to be bounded.
- **Flashlight:** the scene's first spot, so it is the one the fog beams.
- **GI volume and reflection probes** (kitchen, hall) install on frame 2, after the panels cast. A volume's first sweep is the only full-weight one, and the probes allocate the atlas the volume shares.
- **From ambientCG** (CC0), where Poly Haven has no scan: brushed steel `Metal009`, paper `Paper003`, and the glass smear `Smear008`.
- **Glass containers:** a transmissive shell with an opaque fill inside, part-filled so the wall shows through the empty glass.
- **Engine bug found and fixed (`706ab25b`): glass took the exposure twice.** Transmission samples the resolved scene, which is already pre-exposed, and `pbr_frag` multiplied it by `preExposure` again.
  - Invisible at exposure 1, which is the render app's fixture. At this app's 0.02, glass rendered black.
  - `water_frag` already kept the term outside the product.
- **Goldens:** `menu` and `menu_focus` dropped at the user's request (`56d12867`).

### 5. The street
- `houses.c`: a gable roof over a box (our house wears it too), and a seeded neighbour: foundation, clad body, chimney, porch or step, door, and windows in rows. Some windows are lit (a warm curtain glow), some have blinds, a few are boarded.
- `street.c`: ten neighbour houses, fences, seven mercury street lamps (the two nearest the house cast shadows, one is dead), utility poles with wires, a parked car and a mailbox.
- **Fog:** four volumes that overlap by exactly one feather, so their ramps sum level and no clear seam runs between them. They leave the house interior clear and are 40 m tall, so the sky fogs out.
  - The fog's colour is a set dim grey-green, not the sky's navy: fog lit by the night sky swallowed the street into black instead of reading as a veil.
- **Kitchen and front-room windows:** real panes now, thin grimy glass.
- **The black outlines round everything in the street** were 4x MSAA, which headless runs used, meeting dense fog.
  - The fog composite takes one depth for a multisampled edge pixel. Where half its samples are sky, that depth is too near, so every silhouette came out traced in an unfogged dark line. One sample (left) and the window's TAA path (right) both clear it.
  - The app now renders one sample with TAA headless, as it does in the window, so a screenshot is what a player sees; `--no-taa` gives the exactly repeatable frame.
  - **Engine flaw, left open:** the fog composite resolving one depth per MSAA pixel. It shows in any app that combines MSAA with fog this thick.

### 6. The look pass (in progress)
**Street lamps are point lights now, not spots.**
- The fog scatters point lights, and of spots only the scene's first, which is the flashlight. A spot lamp lit its pool on the road and left the air around it dark: no halo, and in fog the halo is most of what a lamp looks like.
- They cast no shadows, because a point light's map is six layers of a pool of eight. That drops phase 5's two shadowed lamps.
- Their range stays 12 m. At that range they add about 4% of a tube's light at the middle of the kitchen through the wall, which is small enough to leave.

**A colour grade:** `assets/lut/silent_grade.cube`, written by `tools/make_grade.py`.
- It keeps 0.68 of the saturation, tints green-grey, lifts the blacks, and adds a gentle S-curve.
- The film look's warm highlight gain is reset to neutral, so the LUT is the only tint.
- `--no-grade` gives the A/B.

**Render scale defaults to 0.5**, the engine's floor. `--render-scale F` overrides it, and it applies only when TAA is on, since the upscale rides on it.
- Headless TAA is now jittered, as it is in the window. Without it, headless TAA integrated one sample position: no antialiasing, and the engine refused any render scale.
- `--no-taa` is the exactly repeatable frame.
- **Measured:** at 0.5 the frame is softer and the tile and cabinet texture goes to mush in a 1:1 crop, but at viewing size the difference is small. A Retina window at 0.5 is still about 960x540, where the consoles it imitates rendered about 512x448.

**The lamps are cones, not blobs**, through an IES profile on each point light (`assets/ies/silent_street_lamp.ies`).
- The fog already applies a point light's profile (spec 11.57), so the lit air and the pool on the road agree.
- The profile is authored, not measured: brightest straight down, half by about 42 degrees, nothing from 80 up.
- **A wider, more realistic throw was tried first:** peaking 30 degrees off the nadir, half by 60. It cut the upward half of the blob but left a glow under the head, not a cone. The narrow profile is what reads.
- **It also fixed the kitchen window:** the pane had been washed white by the halo of the lamp across the street.

**`--day` is parked, not finished.** It has a pinned exposure of 0.0015 (night is 0.02) and no indoor haze. That makes the street a white fog world, but the rooms come out dark.

Four passes each fixed one view and broke another. Underneath them:
- **Two brightness scales.** The engine's sky is on a relative scale (the sun is intensity 3), while the tubes, the flashlight and the day fog colour are physical. At night the sky is next to black and the lights are the scale, so it doesn't matter. By day, the 400-nit fog ambient was hundreds of times brighter than any sunlit surface.
- **Converting the app's lights onto the sky's scale** made the street legible, but at a guessed 20,000 nits per unit.
- **Taking the fog colour from the sky** turned the fog into a clear, sunny day's: hard shadows and dirty grey fog, not an overcast white.
- **The automatic exposure meters the brightest 30% of the frame,** so indoors it exposes for the window and the room goes black.

What day wants is an overcast sky in the engine, with a physical brightness scale. That is the next spec after this one closes. The indoor haze is off by day regardless: the ambient that lights it is not blocked by walls, so at daylight's level it filled the rooms like smoke.

### Engine: the light-cluster index pool, bytes not halfwords
**Symptom:** the kitchen went nearly black when the street lamps became point lights, but only in views that looked out through the window.

**Cause:** the cluster index pool held 6144 slots for 3072 froxels, an average of two lights each.
- A light whose sphere holds the camera claims every tile of every slice out to its range. Under this app's 0.05 m near plane, nine of the 24 slices sit inside the first 1.2 m.
- So each tube costs about 1900 froxels, and the three of them spent the pool before any lamp was counted.
- The froxels that starved were the kitchen's own.

**Fix:** a light index is now a byte, not a halfword. The light cap is 128, so the halfword wasted half the block. The block is now GL 4.1's guaranteed 16 KB, which gives 16384 slots, about five per froxel.
- Changed: one GLSL decode (`lightIndexAt`), the C mirror, and the size constant.
- Two static asserts guard it: the light cap fits a byte, and the pool fits the grid word's 20-bit offset field.

**Measured steady demand**, against 16384:

| View | Slots |
|---|---|
| kitchen towards the window | 10,896 |
| hall out of the front door | 10,093 |
| street | 12,832 |
| kitchen diagonally down the street | 11,267 |

**Still over, then fixed:** only GI capture faces were still over, by at most 600 slots.
- Every one of those builds carried the flashlight while it was switched off. It has an authored range of 18, and `light_cull_radius` returned an authored range before it looked at the intensity, so a zero-intensity light cost about 3000 froxels a build and lit nothing.
- The intensity test now comes first, and a light that is off culls to nothing whatever its range.
- The kitchen run no longer overflows anywhere. Its first build dropped from 13,504 indices to 11,385.

**Considered and not done:** the Unreal-style near offset on the slicing. It halves an enclosing light's cost but doubles a distant one's, so it redistributes the budget rather than growing it. The fog volume has its own slicing and would not have moved.
