# 11.68 — roads as splat-weight overrides, composited through the layers VT

## Context

11.66/11.67 built the composite cache and its paged near field, and on today's content pages are
a measured 0 px identity — the roadmap's D10 explicitly books "CONTENT: roads and decals" as what
remains. This spec ships the first real content: **roads/trails on layered terrain**, and with
them the first frame where the pages visibly out-resolve the fallback. Two exploration passes and
one design pass verified every claim against the source.

**The core decision:** a road is a **procedural splat-weight override evaluated inside
`sampleLayeredSurface`** (`layers.glsl`) — world-XZ polylines, capsule SDF min-distance over
segments, mask = smoothstep over (halfWidth, halfWidth+feather), weights reshaped toward the
road's layer (one of the material's own 4) **before the height blend**:

```glsl
vec4 w = layerWeights(arr, layerSplatUV(worldPos, uv1), n);
w = roadReshapedWeights(w, tilePos.xz, n);   // tilePos = authoredPos(worldPos), hoisted up
```

Why this is the judo move: `layers_vt_bake_frag.glsl` calls `sampleLayeredSurface` UNCHANGED (its
stated hard constraint — "a caching of the existing path, not a second implementation"), so the
fallback bake, the page bake (at 4× density, automatically), and the `--no-layers-vt` per-texel
path all inherit roads with **zero new bake code**; the road shoulder height-interlocks like every
painted layer boundary; the dominant index and detail term follow through the reshaped weights;
the existing cached-vs-per-texel identity arms keep comparing equal; and
`w' = w(1−m) + unit(L)·m` preserves the convex-weights invariant the file demands. Positions are
AUTHORED (`authoredPos`), so origin shifts are correct. Lane select via
`vec4(equal(ivec4(0,1,2,3), ivec4(L)))` — no dynamic local component write (the file's
local-indexing rule). Loops constant-bounded with early breaks over UBO data (the
`iesProfile` precedent). Everything is a structural no-op at road count 0 (zero-filled UBO).

Rejected: painting roads into the splat texture (loses the 4× page-density edge — the whole point
of pages is content the fallback over-resolves); a decal pass into the atlas FBO (a second
implementation of the blend, and `--no-layers-vt` goes lossy).

## Bake-key invalidation — raw fields, not a digest

`MaterialLayersVtKey` gains the road fields raw (+580 B: points, counts, width/feather/layer per
road, road_count), copied in `vt_make_key` **only when `roads_armed`** (the key is what the bake
read). Raw beats a digest: the struct's contract is "everything the bake read, by VALUE"; a
digest adds a second serialization whose forgotten-field failure is silent, and collisions turn
"re-bake missed" from impossible into possible. memcmp of ~710 B/frame is noise.

## Authoring

**.cscn** (parse in `cetra/src/cscene.c`, apply in `apps/render/src/cscene_apply.c`):

```json
"roads": [{"points": [[-2.0, 1.35], [2.0, 1.35]], "width": 0.4, "feather": 0.4, "layer": 2}]
```

Required: points (≥ 2 well-formed 2-arrays — the format's FIRST array-of-arrays, parsed manually
with per-element `cJSON_IsArray` + size-2 + numbers, malformed vertex refuses the whole road),
width (> 0), layer. Defaulted: feather 0. `parse_material_roads` follows `parse_fog_volumes` +
`parse_material_layers`, with the machine-checked `known[] = {"points","width","feather","layer"}`
formatting EXACTLY (gates.py `_cscn_key_sets` scrapes the literal). Note: cscene.c has NO
`get_int` — parse layer via `get_float` + cast (or add `get_int` beside it). Wire-up: call beside
`parse_material_layers`; add `"roads"` to the generic-walk skip chain (cscene.c:796-798); add
`road_count` to the usability gate (:851). cscene.h: `CSceneRoad` + fixed arrays on
`CSceneMaterialOverride` (flat-POD contract holds). Apply beside the layer block: copy to
`m->roads[]`/`road_count`; warn by name on layer ≥ layer_count, roads on an unlayered material,
or a non-world-XZ splat. Roads do NOT dirty the texture array.

**`--road-width-at <frame:value>`** (render app, the `--layer-blend-at` idiom): render_args
fields + parse + apply at the render.c:1882 schedule site (set width on every road, log). One
stroke exercises both mechanisms: UBO re-upload (pack+memcmp) and key staleness (unreachable from
a fresh process — the argument transplanted verbatim).

**Material:** `roads[4]`, `road_count`, `roads_armed` after the layer block; MATERIAL_DEFAULTS
needs no new row (all-zero correct, whole-struct copy covers it — one comment line).

## apps/forest — the trail

`build_trail()` called inside `bake_terrain_layers` immediately before `layer_count` is assigned
(forest.c:696 — where splat_origin/size already exist). `TRAIL_POINTS 12`, `TRAIL_WIDTH 3.0`,
`TRAIL_FEATHER 1.5`, `TRAIL_LAYER 3` (gravel), `TRAIL_PROP_MARGIN 1.0`. **Own LCG seeded from
`g_args.seed`** — never `rnd()`, so the scatter stream is untouched (the documented pure-tests
property). Construction: bearing + wiggle from the LCG, points radially from centre to
0.9·extent, greedy 3-way lateral nudge minimizing |Δheight| (no pathfinding), truncate at the
shoal (`height < water_level + 2·PROP_SHORE_MARGIN`). Stored TERRAIN-LOCAL; material gets
`local + center` (the authored frame, same values splat_origin uses); scatter tests in the
storage frame so origin-shift rebuilds stay correct. Default ON; `--no-trail` (forest has no
pixel goldens; all forest arms are numeric/A-B and the trail rides authoredPos on both legs).

**Scatter rejection** in `sample_ground`, WITH the flow/slope tests BEFORE the clump `rnd()`
block: reject at `dist < halfW + feather + TRAIL_PROP_MARGIN` via `roads_polyline_distance_xz`;
count rejections. Rejects rocks too (shared function, one guard site).

**Probe row** appended in `scatter_probe()` (append-only is regex-safe):
`scatter-probe road points=%d trees_on=%d rejected=%llu width=%.2f` — trees_on computed at the
STRICT bound (halfW, no margin), so the reject-vs-assert asymmetry (2.5 world units) bounds twin
drift.

## AS-BUILT

Every phase shipped. The design held exactly as planned -- the weight override, the UBO, the
key, the authoring, the trail -- and two things came out of testing rather than building: one
arm had to be rebuilt after the mutation round proved it was measuring the wrong thing, and the
full suite found a precision property of analytic world-space edges that nothing here predicted.
Seven decisions and corrections, recorded where they live and summarised here:

**1. The road is refused on a UV1 splat, not merely warned about.** The plan had the applier warn
by name; a warning that the code does not honour is worse than none, since the next reader trusts
it. `material_roads_ensure` gates arming on `SPLAT_SPACE_WORLD_XZ`, which is what makes the
warning true: a road is placed in the world, and UV1 has no world frame to place it in.

**2. `layers-road-pages` was rebuilt on a byte read, because the mutation round found it green
with the road deleted.** As planned it compared whole frames -- and two legs of a composite cache
differ by a code or two across every flat surface in view, so a road edge worth a few hundred
pixels sat under tens of thousands that had nothing to do with roads. Three framings were tried
before the instrument changed rather than the camera; the arm now reads a short band of bytes
starting 0.02 inside the road's EDGE at a forced-coarse fallback, where the per-texel truth reads
48, pages read 48, and the fallback alone reads 66.

Note what that band is and is not: it sits inside the HALF-WIDTH, where the mask is still exactly
1.0, so what it measures is the fallback smearing the road's STEP -- not the shape of the
shoulder, which begins further out. Three doc copies said "inside the shoulder" and were wrong
about their own read.

**3. And the forced-coarse fallback is not a convenience.** What pages resolve and the fallback
cannot is a band FOUR TIMES narrower than a fallback texel; at the derived 256 that is under
0.0156 world units, which this fixture's camera does not resolve at all. Measured directly: at
the fixture's own framing all three legs report the same shoulder to five decimal places. A
close-up camera was tried instead and rejected -- it makes pages differ from the fallback
everywhere, which is how the arm passed with no road at all.

**4. The road-pages camera bug is worth recording because it is silent.** `_projector` builds
from `_cscn_camera`, which reads the COMMITTED scene file, so an arm passing `--cam-eye` samples
pixels the render never drew. It reads plausible bytes off the wrong part of the frame. The
helper takes overrides (`_cscn_camera(name, eye=..., target=...)`); an arm that moves the camera
must pass them.

**5. The trail takes its own generator, and that is what keeps forest's scatter where it was.**
One `rnd()` draw inside `build_trail` would have re-placed all five thousand props. The reject
sits with the two pure tests and before the clump draw, so it turns candidates away without
consuming them; measured 165 rejections, 1936 trees to 1935.

**6. `--no-trail` exists because forest's arms are numeric.** The app has no pixel golden to
protect, but the trail changes the scatter, and a bisect lever for "was it the road or the
ground?" costs one flag. It earned its keep immediately -- see 7.

**7. A road makes the DOMINANT-INDEX read ulp-sensitive under an origin shift, and the first
explanation of that shipped wrong.** The full suite caught the symptom: `origin-auto` compares
two runs that shifted the world by different amounts and demands they reach the same frame at
EXACTLY 0 differing pixels, and with the trail on it read 106 px of 1.44M (0.007%). Not the
scatter -- both legs reject an identical 168 candidates -- and not the auto trigger either: two
MANUAL shifts at frames 20 and 25 differ by 61 px, which identifies the shift AMOUNT as the
variable.

**The mechanism recorded here at first was "a road is an analytic world-space edge, and an
analytic edge resolves what a texture lookup quantizes away". That is false in the configuration
measured**, and the review round is what caught it. forest arms the composite cache, so shading
takes `sampleCachedSurface`, which never calls `roadReshapedWeights`; the origin arms pass no
`--no-layers-vt`. The road reaches those pixels THROUGH THE BAKED ATLAS -- a texture lookup,
exactly like the splat beside it.

What actually moves is the DOMINANT-LAYER INDEX. It rides the atlas alpha and is read with an
unfiltered `texelFetch` + `round`, deliberately (bilinear between indices passes through every
index between them). A road puts a hard discontinuity into that field where the splat has only
gentle ones, so an ulp of difference in the reconstructed uv moves the fetched texel by one, the
index flips, and the entire detail tap switches layer. That predicts what was observed: a sparse
~100-pixel scatter along the shoulder, scaling with shift amount, absent without the road.

The distinction is practical, not editorial. The wrong reading points a future reader at "avoid
analytic edges"; the true property is **any splat feature sharp enough to flip the dominant index
is ulp-sensitive under an origin shift** -- which is not road-specific, and which `--no-trail`
does not retire.

Quantizing the position (the `windObjectPhase` remedy) was considered and rejected -- no single
quantum serves both the fixture's 0.02-unit shoulder and a 12,000-unit offset, and it would trade
an invisible difference for a visible staircase. `--no-trail` is instead scoped to `origin-auto`
ALONE, which is the only arm in the suite whose bar a road can break: every other origin arm's
bar is 2% of frame, a 20% floor or a ratio, none of which 0.007% approaches. That scoping matters
because `origin-shift` is the one place in the suite that renders a road under an origin shift at
all -- a road failing to follow `authoredPos` lands in the wrong PLACE, a full-size difference,
and that arm is what would catch it. The first attempt put `--no-trail` on all four and removed
that coverage.

### What remains, honestly

The trail is SUBTLE at forest's scale -- three units wide on a kilometre island, under a canopy.
It is unambiguous in the numbers and hard to photograph, which is the honest statement of where
this sits: the mechanism is finished and the CONTENT is not. What roads want next is width that
varies along the course, a material of their own rather than one of the ground's four layers, and
the paged decals D10 books after them. `MATERIAL_MAX_ROADS` is 4 and `MATERIAL_MAX_ROAD_POINTS`
16 for that reason: a road network wants the paged content era, not a hundred polylines evaluated
per fragment on the per-texel path.
