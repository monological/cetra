# 11.14 -- SSS blur: deliver the authored width at any resolution

**Branch `sss-blur-width`, off `skin-preint` (11.13, complete and gated, unmerged).**

Numbering: 11.13 is pre-integrated skin, so this is 11.14. The roadmap calls
pre-integrated skin "B3"; that label is used below only where it names a roadmap
row.

This spec exists because 11.13 measured its own composition rule failing and named
the cause. Its "Recorded, not fixed" entry reads: *"The blur's width deficit needs
a downsampled chain -- own spec. This is now the blocker on two things, not one:
the authored width the blur never reaches, and the resolution dependence D5 was
meant to cancel and does not. A blur that hits its authored width is also the
reference the two halves would have to be calibrated against."* This is that spec,
and the answer turned out not to need a downsampled chain -- see "Rejected now".

## Context

The screen-space subsurface blur caps its kernel at 48 RENDER PIXELS
(`sss_blur_frag.glsl:30`, `:78`):

    radPx = clamp(sssRadius * projScale / depth, 1.0, MAX_PX)
    projScale = 0.5 * projection[1][1] * fx->height     (postfx.c:1335)

Past the cap the delivered WORLD width is `MAX_PX * depth / projScale`, i.e.
proportional to `1/height`. **The same scene at the same authored radius scatters
differently at different resolutions** -- and, since window size, `ss_scale` and
`render_scale` all multiply into `fx->height`, differently at different render
scales too.

Measured in spec 11.13 phase 0: delivered scatter collapses to **0.282** of its
unclamped value across a 5x resolution sweep. This is a live defect in shipped
code. `specs/4.12-sss.md:115` names it as a risk and nothing was ever built to
test it.

Two things depend on fixing it. Roadmap B3.1/B3.2 would otherwise be built on a
foundation wrong by 3.5x. And spec 11.13's D5 divides work between the angular
falloff and this blur by computing the blur's shortfall -- a division phase R
measured as NOT producing the invariance it claims (13.4% drift across a 4x sweep
against 8.3% with the feature off, in the opposite direction), because the two
halves are not calibrated to each other. **A blur that reaches its authored width
is the only reference they could be calibrated against.**

## P0, as measured -- the defect is worse than 11.13 recorded

On `skin_curvature_fixture` (authored radius 0.28 on a unit sphere), mid sphere,
`--no-skin-preint` throughout so the angular falloff contributes nothing and every
difference is the screen-space pass. Each row renders twice, with and without SSS.

|window|framebuffer|90-10 width adds|5%-of-lit crossing pushes|
|---|---|---|---|
| 250 | 800x500 | +3.19 deg | **+10.96 deg** |
| 500 | 1600x1000 | +2.96 deg | **+10.96 deg** |
| 1000 | 3200x2000 | -0.34 deg | +2.34 deg |
| 2000 | 6400x4000 | -0.45 deg | **+0.27 deg** |

**rho = 0.025** on the crossing push: at 4K the blur delivers 2.5% of the effect
it has at 500 px. 11.13 phase 0 recorded 0.282 on a different rig and fixture;
this instrument is more sensitive and the defect is an order of magnitude worse
than that number suggested.

Two details worth keeping:

**The first two rows are IDENTICAL to 0.01 deg.** That is the unclamped regime
behaving exactly as it should -- delivered width is the authored radius, and the
frame size does not enter. The defect is entirely the cliff after it. Predicted
`radPx` at depth 8.5 is 22.6 / 45.2 / 90.5 / 181 px against the 48 px cap, so the
cliff lands precisely where the cap engages.

**Pick the instrument deliberately.** The 90-10 width is the natural choice and is
nearly useless here: it damps a real effect down to +3 deg on a 35 deg
measurement, and at high resolution it goes slightly NEGATIVE, which reads as
noise rather than as signal. The 5%-of-lit crossing is measuring the same physics
four times more sensitively, because the blur's whole job is pushing light PAST
the terminator and that is where the far tail lands. The 90-10 pair is retained in
the gate anyway, since it is what `_skin_terminator_width` already computes and it
is a fair before/after comparison -- but the crossing push is what P1's exit
criterion is read from.

## The fix: change the cap's UNITS, not the machinery

A pixel is not a fixed amount of world, which is the entire defect. Express the
cap as world scatter per unit of view depth and the resolution cancels
algebraically rather than being compensated for.

`sss_blur_frag.glsl:78`:

    float radPx = max(min(sssRadius / depth, MAX_SCATTER_PER_DEPTH) * projScale, 1.0);

Delivered world width `= radPx * depth / projScale =`

    max( min(sssRadius, M * depth), depth / projScale )

- **Unclamped: exactly `sssRadius`.** The authored value, delivered.
- **Clamped: exactly `M * depth`.** `projScale` cancels identically, so this is
  independent of `fx->height` -- window size, SSAA and render scale at once.
- Residual: the `1.0` px floor stays resolution-dependent, bounded at one render
  texel. It is a genuine "do not blur below a texel" floor. Keep it.

`depth > 0` is already guaranteed by the `centerZ >= 0` early-out at `:65`, so no
new divide hazard. **`postfx_run_sss` is untouched** -- no new pass, target,
uniform or allocation. `projScale` is still uploaded exactly as at `postfx.c:1347`;
the shader now uses it only to convert an already-capped world radius to pixels.

### Choosing M = 0.05

`skin_curvature_fixture.cscn` authors radius 0.28 at front-surface depths of
7.6-9.0, so `want/depth` peaks at 0.0369 -- **cleared with 35% headroom, so the
fixture leaves the clamped regime and gets exactly what it asks for.** At fov 40,
`M = 0.05` equals today's 48 px cap at a ~700 px framebuffer height, at EVERY
height. It is 2.86 degrees of scatter half-angle: dimensionally meaningful and
display-independent, where 48 was neither.

For realistic skin the cap will now essentially never bind (1-3 mm scatter at 1 m
is `want/depth ~ 0.003`, sixteen times under M). What stays capped is
foliage-scale scatter -- `apps/tree` authors 1.5 and 1.0 world units, wanting
~0.10 -- **by a stated ceiling rather than by display accident.**

**Decided: keep M = 0.05 and re-author the foliage radii down** to values the
kernel can actually deliver. Those numbers were picked against a broken cap, so
they were never a real intent. Report before/after on the tree app rather than
changing it silently.

## The second change, which pays for the first

Removing the pixel cap roughly triples `radPx` at Retina 1080p; with UNIFORM tap
spacing the gap goes 8.8 px -> ~27 px and it will band. Re-place the quadrature
nodes quadratically, at zero cost (`sss_blur_frag.glsl:85-86`):

    float u = float(i) / float(HALF_TAPS);
    float t = u * u * radPx * TAIL;                       // quadratic in the index
    vec3 pw = profileWeight(t, sigma) * (2.0 * float(i)); // cell width; uniform's was 1

This changes the quadrature NODES, not the kernel: `profileWeight()` and `TAIL`
are untouched, so `gen_skin_preint_fit.py`'s checked-in table stays valid and the
sum moves CLOSER to the continuous profile it integrated against.

**The `2.0*float(i)` Jacobian is not optional.** With non-uniform nodes, weighting
by `pw(t)` alone is no longer a Riemann sum of the same kernel. In units of
`T/N^2` the centre tap owns exactly 1 unit, so `sumW = vec3(1.0)` at `:83` and the
`profileWeight(0) = 1.0 exactly` invariant at `:82` both stay literally true.
Uniform spacing is the special case where both widths are 1 -- which is why
today's code is accidentally correct.

It also fixes a scale-free defect nobody had noticed: gap/sigma1 is 0.61 for red
but **1.75 for green and 2.44 for blue**, so the green and blue cores collapse
almost entirely onto the centre tap at EVERY radius.

Known imprecision, to be recorded not fixed: the outermost tap's honest half-cell
is `N - 0.5`, not `2N`, over-weighting it by 2.09x = 3.2% of delivered kernel mass
(today's uniform scheme over-weights the same tap by 2x, for 1.4%).

## P3, as measured -- the composition was right; it just had nothing to divide

11.13 could not settle this, because it only ever asked how far short the blur
fell, which is a question about a budget rather than about the answer. With the
blur delivering, there is a reference: Penner's integral, evaluated exactly by
`tools/gen_skin_preint_fit.py` -- the same function whose table pre-integration
ships. One calibration makes it comparable to a tonemapped render: at sigma = 0
the integral IS clamped Lambert, so the gap between analytic and rendered Lambert
is the offset, and the deltas can then be read directly.

Fixture sigma = (0.280, 0.098, 0.070); mid sphere; 1600x1000.

| | 5%-of-lit crossing | moves |
|---|---|---|
| analytic Lambert | 87.31 deg | -- |
| **analytic true scattered response** | **96.27 deg** | **+8.96 deg** |
| rendered Lambert | 84.89 deg | -- |
| rendered blur only | 96.43 deg | **+11.54 deg** |
| rendered blur + pre-integration | 96.43 deg | +11.54 deg |

**The blur alone now delivers 129% of the movement the integral asks for**, and
adding pre-integration changes nothing. So `deficit = sqrt(1 - k^2)` is KEPT, on
evidence rather than inertia: pre-integration standing down where the blur is
unclamped is correct, not a feature going missing. The composition law was never
the problem -- it was dividing a budget that was wrong on one side.

Two things fall out, both recorded rather than fixed here:

**The blur over-delivers by ~29% against the curvature integral.** They are
different models of the same transport -- one integrates the profile over a
screen neighbourhood, the other over the chord of a locally-fitted sphere -- so
exact agreement was never expected, but the sign and size are worth knowing
before anyone calibrates against either. Candidate contributors already
quantified: the outermost tap carries 3.2% more mass than its honest cell (phase
2), and the red tail is truncated at 1 sigma (below).

**Pre-integrated skin is now inert for realistic content.** It fires only where
the authored radius exceeds `SSS_MAX_SCATTER_PER_DEPTH * depth`, under `--no-sss`,
and only on materials that opt in with `curvature_scale > 0` -- which today is one
fixture. That is the correct outcome of fixing the blur, and it is also a real
change in what 11.13 delivers, so the roadmap's B3 row has to say so rather than
leave the feature listed as carrying the terminator.

## Re-deriving the composition (D5), in the same spec

**Fixing the blur makes pre-integrated skin inert under D5 as written.** D5 opens
the angular falloff by `deficit = sqrt(1 - k^2)` where `k` is the fraction of
authored width the blur delivers. Deliver it, and `k = 1`, `deficit = 0`, and
pre-integration contributes exactly nothing.

That is D5's premise surfacing, not a new bug: it treats the two mechanisms as
substitutes competing for one budget. They are not quite substitutes -- the blur
redistributes light already in the diffuse buffer and can never wrap light past a
terminator, while pre-integration changes the falloff shape at the shading point
-- but they are not independent either, which is why the naive sum double-counts.

This spec must settle it, using the reference that now exists for the first time:
the terminator width the UNCLAMPED blur delivers. Two known-bad forms are already
measured and are not candidates: `sqrt(1-k^2)` overshoots (13.4% drift) and
`deficit = 1.0` double-counts (50.25 deg vs 38.37 deg where the blur is
unclamped). The answer is to be measured in phase 3, not assumed here.

One correction lands regardless: `skinSigma` (`preintegrated_skin.glsl:174-189`)
compares against `want`, but the ceiling bounds the WIDEST term, so the comparison
should be against `2.2 * max(scatterColor) * want`.

## The cap has three consumers; they move together

1. `sss_blur_frag.glsl:30` -- the constant, applied at `:78`.
2. `postfx.h:23` -- `SSS_MAX_BLUR_PX`, the C-side mirror (rename to
   `SSS_MAX_SCATTER_PER_DEPTH`).
3. `render.c:133-141` -- `reach = SSS_MAX_BLUR_PX / proj_scale`, uploaded as
   `sssMaxScatterPerDepth`, consumed by D5's deficit. **This collapses to
   `reach = SSS_MAX_SCATTER_PER_DEPTH`** -- five lines and the `proj_scale > 0`
   guard deleted, and spec 11.13's D3 worry that the deficit depends on the
   CAPTURE camera's projection is retired for the projection half.

Consumer 3 is the mechanical reason this is one spec and not two: the moment the
cap stops binding, the quantity D5 divides by is a lie.

## Two gates committed hours ago will fail, correctly

Both were written in spec 11.13 phase R and both encode the defect as an
assumption:

- **`skin-handoff`** (`gates.py:428`) asserts `<= +0.50 deg` at 800x500 and
  `>= +3.00 deg` at 1600x1000. That pair is satisfiable ONLY while the cap is
  resolution-dependent; under a resolution-free cap the legs converge.
- **`skin-live`** (`gates.py:379`) asserts `AE > 0` on the opted-in fixture.
  Unclamped, `deficit = 0` and the frame becomes byte-identical to
  `--no-skin-preint`.

Re-pointing both is part of this change, not a follow-up. It is also the sharpest
confirmation of the D5 finding: a gate written to measure the handoff cannot
survive the handoff becoming unnecessary. Keep a handoff assertion by pushing one
leg into the ANGULAR cap via `--sss-radius`, so the deficit still has something to
detect.

## Rejected now, recorded for later: the pyramid

A scale-space evaluation -- mip the diffuse, evaluate each of the profile's three
Gaussians at a level where its sigma is a few texels -- was designed in full and
is genuinely attractive: **~4x fewer fetches and ~20x less transcendental ALU** at
one profile, an exact per-profile tag separation stronger than today's, a ceiling
of `0.382*tan(fov_y/2)` (4.3x wider at 1080p) and true render-scale invariance.

Not now, for three reasons. It is a much larger change (two mipped MRT targets, a
per-profile build loop, four new shaders, `sss_blur_frag.glsl` deleted) whose
correctness rests on subtleties that fail silently and per-driver -- BASE/MAX
level pinning on BOTH attachments, `LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR` where the bloom idiom it
copies uses `NEAREST`, and an exact second-moment LOD inversion without which the
kernel is up to 12% too wide. It introduces an artifact class that does not exist
today (same-profile self-silhouette bleed) and needs a new fixture to bound it.
And its headline win is width and cost, not the resolution dependence -- which the
two-line change above fixes exactly and algebraically.

**The right sequence is this fix first.** It makes a clean, calibrated baseline
the pyramid can then be measured against, which does not exist today.

### One finding from that design worth recording either way

**The red channel's broad tail is truncated at exactly 1 sigma.** Taps run to
`radPx * TAIL` and, for red with `sssColor.r = 1.0`, `s3 = 2.2 * radPx` IS that
limit. Delivered red masses are `(0.121, 0.447, 0.432)` against an ideal
`(0.0995, 0.379, 0.521)` -- a red-only, +20.7%-into-the-tail shape error. Green
and blue truncate at 3.3 and 5 sigma and are unaffected.

`gen_skin_preint_fit.py` integrates the UNTRUNCATED profile, so **the runtime and
the checked-in pre-integration table already disagree**, and have since 11.13
shipped. The tap re-placement above does not fix this (it moves nodes within the
same truncated range). Record it in the spec, quantified, as the pyramid's
strongest single argument -- and re-check it against the fit tool's verify mode
before deciding whether it needs its own fix sooner.

## Phases

Per `CLAUDE.md`: spec committed alone first, then implementation. Every phase:
`./build.sh`; 16 goldens exact 0; `gates.py` green; determinism x2; raiden
byte-identical.

- **P0 -- baseline.** Reproduce 11.13 phase 0 M1 on this branch: framebuffer
  heights 200/500/1000, terminator shift as a fraction of the image, `rho` expected
  ~0.282. Without this the "after" means nothing.
- **P1 -- the cap.** `sss_blur_frag.glsl:30,78`; `postfx.h:23`; `render.c:133-141`.
  **Exit: rho -> ~1.0 and the delivered fraction goes flat.** This step alone
  satisfies the headline goal.
- **P2 -- the tap warp.** `sss_blur_frag.glsl:85-86`. Exit: no banding at the
  widest configuration; green/blue core softening visible on the fixture; the
  Jacobian invariants at `:82-83` verified unchanged.
- **P3 -- the composition.** Measure the three candidate deficit forms against the
  now-calibrated reference and pick on evidence. Re-point `skin-handoff` and
  `skin-live`; add `sss-invariance`.
- **P4 -- content and docs.** Re-author `apps/tree` foliage radii, before/after
  reported. Bake the first SSS-ON golden (none exists, so nothing catches a
  regression by pixels today). New spec under `specs/`; amend 11.13's D5-as-measured
  and "Recorded, not fixed"; rewrite `preintegrated_skin.glsl:166-173`, whose
  comment asserts the defect.
- **PR -- reviews.** Background agents, wait for ALL, apply as one commit, re-gate.
  Correct the roadmap's B3 row. Merge only on explicit permission.

## Verification

**The headline gate** is a re-pointing of machinery that already runs.
`_skin_terminator_width` (`gates.py:399`) measures delivered width in SURFACE
DEGREES -- comparable across resolutions by construction, which a pixel width is
not. New `sss-invariance`: mid sphere, `--no-skin-preint`, 800x500 vs 3200x2000.
**Bar: <= 2% drift, against the 8.3% recorded in 11.13's D5 table.**

Also: `rho` across the 5x sweep (P0's baseline vs after); the three scale gates
green (`sss_scale_fixture` is a self-comparison, so a different kernel moves both
halves identically); `--no-sss` byte-identity on raiden and c64; `_taa_churn` on
the fixture within 1.3x; look pass on `apps/tree` and `my_models/face`.

**No golden is at risk.** `scene_has_subsurface()` (`engine.c:2544`) skips the
entire SSS path for scenes with no subsurface material, so all 15 goldens are
structurally out of reach; the 16th was baked `--no-sss` (11.13 D10) precisely so
this follow-up could not invalidate it.

## Traps recorded while mapping

- `render -m assets/sss_fixture.gltf` and `-m assets/sss_scale_fixture.cscn` are
  NOT the same SSS configuration. The `.cscn` authors both spheres at radius 0.6;
  the app-side fallback (`render.c:1558-1559`) uses 0.6 and **0.15**. Measuring one
  and reporting it as the other is wrong by 4x.
- Headless refuses `--render-scale` without `--taa --headless-jitter`
  (`render.c:1638`), so a render-scale sweep has TAA live. `-W/-H` moves post res
  too. Pick deliberately.
- `gates.py` never exercises a non-unity render scale (`specs/11.7-taau.md:134`).
- The rejection band at silhouettes widens with the kernel (~48 -> ~150 px at the
  Retina default) -- inherent to a normalised bilateral, present today at smaller
  scale, and the one real regression of raising the cap.
