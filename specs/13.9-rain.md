# 13.9 Rain

## Context

The engine has no rain or weather system. No spec or roadmap row books one; the only hits for
"rain" are the erosion sim's water term. What we have is a set of parts, each of which handles
one layer of rain:
- the shore wet-sand block in `pbr_frag` (11.45), which is Lagarde's wet-surface model already;
- a punctual shadow array that can hold an orthographic depth layer;
- a froxel medium lit by IES-shaped point lights;
- a particle system;
- a touch-ripple sim on water;
- a noise generator inside the vendored miniaudio.

The goal is rain with physically grounded behaviour and artistic knobs on top: falling streaks,
wet surfaces, puddles, ripples, splashes and mist. Silent **rains by default**. Its foggy night
street is where we look at it, and the IES street lamps backlighting the rain are the image we are
after. A dedicated `rain_fixture` carries the engine's gate arms, since silent has none by
decision. Rain also brings silent its first sound.

## Research summary (what the answer rests on)

**Foundational papers**
- **Garg & Nayar.** Physics and appearance of rain.
  - *Photometric model of a rain drop* (2003).
  - *When does a camera see rain?* (ICCV 2005): visibility depends on exposure time and depth of field.
  - *Photorealistic rendering of rain streaks* (SIGGRAPH 2006): drops oscillate as they fall; a streak's brightness depends on light direction × view direction × drop shape; they build a streak database.
  - *Vision and Rain* (IJCV 2007).
  - Core facts: a drop refracts about 165° of the environment. A streak's contrast is set by how long the drop stays over a pixel compared with the exposure.
- **Tatarchuk & Isidoro**, *Artist-directable real-time rain rendering in city environments* (EG NPH 2006; ATI ToyShop demo). The first complete real-time system:
  - image-space rainfall;
  - particle splashes and drips;
  - simulated ripples;
  - drops on glass;
  - warped reflections;
  - halos around lights.
- **Other real-time work**
  - Wang et al. 2006 (MSR).
  - Rousseau et al. 2006: refraction through drops.
  - Puig-Centelles et al. 2009: level of detail and artistic control.
  - Creus & Patow 2013: "R4".
  - Weber et al. 2015: a multiscale model.
- **Wet materials**
  - Lekner & Dorf 1988: why things get darker when wet.
  - Jensen, Legakis & Dorsey 1999: *Rendering of wet materials*.
- **Lagarde, "Water drop" 1–4** (2012–13, Remember Me). This is the production reference.
  - **Falling rain:** four camera-attached cone/cylinder layers with pre-blurred textures.
  - **Occlusion:** a 256² ortho depth map rendered from above; splashes spawn from it with no raycasts.
  - **Wet surfaces:** `diffuse *= lerp(1, lerp(1, 0.2, porosity), wet)`; gloss goes up; puddles are water level against a heightmap, with normals lerped to flat.
  - **Ripples:** a ripple texture of four animated ring layers.
  - **Drying:** two flood levels with different drying rates.
  - The shore block in `pbr_frag` already follows this model.

**Where the state of the art is**
- No newer real-time paper replaces the 2006–2013 work above.
- Academic rain papers since about 2019 are about data synthesis for computer vision:
  - Halder, Lalonde & de Charette, ICCV 2019 / IJCV 2021: physics-based streaks for testing how robust vision systems are to rain.
  - RainyGS, CVPR 2025: a shallow-water simulation on a heightmap, for pooling and ripples on Gaussian splats.
- Production state of the art is a stack of layers:
  - GPU streaks near the camera, and the rain treated as a participating medium further out;
  - a top-down occlusion map;
  - PBR wetness (porosity darkening plus a water film);
  - puddles driven by a height/mask and a level that fills and evaporates over time. AC Shadows (GDC 2025) runs this as a systemic simulation: puddles evaporate after rain, and mist forms later in the day;
  - procedural ripples;
  - splashes spawned from occlusion heights;
  - streaks composited after TAA.
- Other shipped examples:
  - CryEngine 3's Rain entity: amount, puddles, puddle ripples, splashes, occlusion.
  - AC4 (Wronski, GDC 2014): fully GPU-simulated procedural rain.

**Unreal**
- UE5 ships no rain or weather system of its own. Games build rain from parts:
  - **Niagara GPU sprites:** velocity-aligned, attached to the camera. Collision has three modes:
    - Scene Depth: cheap, but only works on screen.
    - Global Distance Field: about the same cost, rounds off thin shapes.
    - GPU ray tracing: experimental.
    - Shelter comes from that collision or from a top-down SceneCapture depth.
  - **Material Parameter Collection:** a global "Wetness" value read by master-material functions. These darken base colour, lower roughness, put puddles in low spots (height or vertex paint, or RVT on landscapes) and add ripple flipbook normals.
  - **Exponential Height Fog / Volumetric Fog** for mist.
  - **Translucency "After DOF / After Motion Blur"** so streaks skip TAA and blur.
- The packaged solution most people use is the marketplace's Ultra Dynamic Sky/Weather:
  - particle collision plus occlusion volumes;
  - wet roughness that appears in height lows first;
  - flattened puddle normals with ripples that respond to rain amount;
  - Niagara systems that drip from meshes.

**Physics that sets the defaults.** R is the rain rate in mm/h.
- **Categories:** light < 2.5, moderate 2.5–7.6, heavy > 7.6, violent > 50.
- **Drop sizes (Marshall–Palmer):** `N(D) = N0·exp(−Λ D)`, with `N0 = 8000 m⁻³mm⁻¹` and `Λ = 4.1 R^−0.21 mm⁻¹`.
- **Terminal velocity (Atlas 1973):** `v(D) = 9.65 − 10.3·exp(−0.6 D)` m/s. That gives 1 mm → 4.0, 2 mm → 6.6, 4 mm → 8.7.
- **Streak length** is `v · t_exposure`: 2 mm at 1/60 s → 11 cm.
- **Extinction:** with Q_ext = 2, `β = π N0 / Λ³` (×1e-6 m⁻¹):

  | Rain | R (mm/h) | β (m⁻¹) | Visibility |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Light | 1 | 3.6e-4 | ~11 km |
  | Moderate | 10 | 1.6e-3 | ~2.5 km |
  | Heavy | 50 | 4.3e-3 | ~0.9 km |

  Silent's night fog is 0.02 m⁻¹ plus 0.09 m⁻¹ in the fog volumes. So physical rain mist is under 10% there: in silent the streaks carry the rain, while in clear-air scenes the medium does.
- **Drop count:** about 900 drops per m³ above 0.5 mm at 10 mm/h, which is millions within 10 m. We cannot draw that many; we draw about 1% as streaks. The count knob and the opacity compensation exist because of this.

## Design decisions (and why)

1. **A dedicated `Rain` scene subsystem, not the particle system.**
   - Raindrops never interact with one another, so rain needs no stored state. Each streak's position is a pure function of (instance, time), wrapped inside nested boxes that follow the camera (three LOD boxes, like cascades).
   - That makes it deterministic from the frame clock (headless runs and goldens stay reproducible), with no CPU per-drop work and no upload.
   - The particle system would need a lot of new work first: velocity-stretched streaks (never shipped, M5), an init-velocity module, wrapping, per-frame drift, and clustered lighting. And its TF backend uploads drift once.
   - Owned like water and wind: `scene->rain`, set by a pointer write.
2. **The rain occlusion map is a tenant of the punctual shadow array**, not a new sampler.
   - `pbr_frag` is at 16/16 samplers in its full variant, and unit 6 is already taken by the cloud shadow in the shade pass.
   - The punctual array (unit 15, plain depth `sampler2DArray`) already carries a matrix per layer (`punctual_shadow.glsl:20-33`). An ortho layer works as-is, since w = 1.
   - Rain takes one reserved layer with its own matrix uniform and layer index. This is the tenancy escape, so the sampler ledger does not change.
   - The froxel inject already binds that array on unit 2, so the fog gets occlusion for free.
3. **Streaks are composited after TAA** (after DoF, before metering: `postfx.c:3685`→`3721`), in HDR.
   - Silent always runs jittered TAA. Thin, fast streaks with no motion vectors ghost or get erased by the neighbourhood clamp. This is the same reason `spores` refused TAA.
   - This is Unreal's "After Motion Blur" translucency.
   - Anti-aliasing is analytic: width is clamped to 1 px and alpha scaled by the true coverage, which is also physically right for a sub-pixel drop.
   - Each streak takes its own transmittance from the froxel volume.
4. **Wetness extends the shore block and does not copy it.**
   - Pull the shore block's porosity-darken / film-roughness / normal-flatten / F0 → 0.02 terms (`pbr_frag.glsl:1519-1595`, `1684-1688`) into one `wetSurface()` that both sources feed.
   - Gated by a new bit `PBR_FEAT_RAIN 64`, with `PBR_FEAT_ALL` going to the literal 127. The bit is set in `_scene_pbr_features` (`render.c:792`).
   - The gate uses `#if`, not a folded bool. That keeps every non-rain variant's source identical, so rain off is 0 px (a branch is not byte-identical on this driver).
   - Uniforms go through a `rain_bind()` at program switch, following `water_bind_sea`, and the off state is published explicitly.
5. **Puddles and ripples are procedural (ALU only, no textures).**
   - Puddle mask: world-xz value noise × how up-facing the surface is × height (layer/POM height where present) compared with the puddle level.
   - Ripples: `include/rain_ripples.glsl`, rings from hashed impact cells, rate driven by R.
   - The same include feeds `water_frag`'s shading normal. The touch sim (4 drops per step over 7 m) stays for big drops.
6. **One master knob that means something physical.** `rate_mmh` derives density, drop sizes, velocity, extinction, splash rate, ripple rate and wetting rate. Every artistic knob is a scale that defaults to 1.

## Knobs (public `Rain` struct; SETTINGS unless noted)

| Group | Knob | Default / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Master | `rate_mmh` | 0 = off; drives everything below |
| | `wind` (m/s, vec3) | slants the fall; direction may come from `scene->wind` |
| Streaks | `streak_count` | instance budget per LOD box (~16k) |
| | `streak_radius` | innermost box half-size (m); outer boxes ×3 |
| | `shutter_s` | 1/60; streak length = v·shutter |
| | `streak_width`, `streak_brightness` | scales, default 1 |
| | `streak_forward_g` | forward lobe for backlit rain (~0.8) |
| Surfaces | `wetness`, `puddle_level` | **ENGINE-OWNED**, integrated state 0..1 |
| | `wet_time`, `dry_time` | exact-exponential time constants (s) |
| | `puddle_fill_time`, `puddle_drain_time` | slower than the pair above (Lagarde's second level) |
| | `puddle_coverage`, `puddle_scale` | ceiling on the level; noise scale (m) |
| | `ripple_strength`, `ripple_size` | puddles and water |
| | `drips` | streaks running down vertical surfaces |
| | per-material `Material.porosity` | −1 = derive from roughness (Lagarde) |
| Splashes | `splash_amount`, `splash_size` | scales on the rate-derived impact count |
| Medium | `mist`, `mist_g` | scale on the Marshall–Palmer β (1 = physical); phase |
| Occlusion | `occlusion_extent`, `occlusion_softness` | ~96 m square, texel-snapped; edge bleed under eaves |
| Later | lightning, lens drops, drops on glass | recorded as out of scope |

## Fixture (engine verification; silent stays screenshots-only)

`assets/generators/gen_rain_fixture.py` writes:
- `assets/models/rain_fixture.gltf`;
- `assets/scenes/rain_fixture.cscn`;
- a variant, `rain_water_fixture.cscn`, which adds the existing `water` block for ripples.

It uses `asset_path` / `asset_ref` and follows the `gen_ies_fixture.py` pattern: the answers are known by construction, and the generator states them.

What is in it, and the one question each part answers:
- **A 20 × 20 m ground slab with a roof slab on posts covering half of it.** A covered texel stays dry; an exposed one wets, and the edge follows `occlusion_softness`.
- **Two ground materials side by side, one porous and one sealed.** Porosity darkens the porous one; the sealed one gets only the film.
- **A wall facing the wind.** Vertical exposure and drips.
- **An IES point lamp behind the rain, facing the camera.** The forward lobe: streaks must be brighter backlit than front-lit.
- **Night ambient, TAA on, the camera pinned.**

`--rain-probe <case>` prints `%.9g` numbers that the gate group reads:
- `physics`: Λ, β, v(D) and the streak length at 1 / 10 / 50 mm/h, against closed forms;
- `wetting`: the integrated state at varied dt;
- `exposure`: the occlusion-layer answer at named covered and exposed points;
- `ledger`: the sampler count of the rain variant against its base.

Two render arms compare frames:
- `rain-off`: `rate 0` against no rain, 0 px;
- `rain-determinism`: two runs, 0 px.

### Phase 1

- `rain.c/h` as planned. The state defaults are game time, not weather time: film 20 s to wet
  at the 5 mm/h reference rate and 240 s to dry, puddles 90 s to fill and 900 s to drain,
  coverage 0.6. The puddle level a rate holds up is `coverage x (1 - exp(-R / 5))`, a shape
  choice rather than a hydrology result, and the header says so.
- `--rain-probe` is ONE flag printing every row (physics ladder, velocities, defaults, a
  schedule at 60 and 30 fps, the scene's state), like every other probe in the render app,
  rather than `--rain-probe <case>`. The gate selects rows by tag.
- `.cscn` `rain` block keys: `enabled`, `rate`, `wind`, `wetTime`, `dryTime`,
  `puddleFillTime`, `puddleDrainTime`, `puddleCoverage`, `settled`. Absent `settled` means
  soaked, so a headless frame does not depend on the frame count.
- Arms landed: `rain-physics`, `rain-wetting`, and `rain-tick` (not in the plan: the fixture
  opened dry, which is what shows the ENGINE advances the state on the frame's own clock --
  the schedule arm only exercises `rain_update` in isolation).
- The fixture has no `rain_water_fixture.cscn` yet; it lands with phase 5, which is the
  first phase that can use it.

### Phase 2

- The layer sits past every LIGHT layer the classification asked for
  (`punctual_light_layers`), not past the layers rendered, so a failed light layer can never
  shift it; with shadows off it is layer 0. The array holds `PUNCTUAL_ARRAY_LAYERS` = the
  eight-layer light pool plus this one tenant, so rain never takes a light's layer.
- The array's EDGE is sized from the light layers alone and the map draws into a fixed
  1024² corner (`RAIN_OCCLUSION_SIZE`), with the corner folded into the lookup matrix
  (`rain_lookup`) and not the render matrix (`rain_matrix`). Counting the rain layer in the
  budget would have halved a lone spot's resolution the moment it started raining. The cost
  is that the array can exceed its 96 MB budget by one layer.
- The shadow pass asks for the same capacity (lights + rain) the rain pass does. Asking for
  fewer there would rebuild the array between the two passes -- wiping the lights' maps --
  on every frame it rains.
- The depth spans 250 m either side of the camera along the rain, snapped to the texel grid
  in the rain's own view so cover does not crawl along an edge as the camera moves.
- The "debug view" is `--rain-map <ppm>` on `--rain-probe`, a stretched greyscale dump of
  the map, plus `--rain-probe-at x,y,z` (repeatable), which reads cover back at named points.
  Nothing on screen changes in this phase, so a pbr_frag tint (the `--csm-debug` idiom) would
  have been phase 4's code written early.
- Arms: `rain-exposure` (ten points on the fixture, two of them half a metre either side of
  the roof's footprint, where a straight-down map answers backwards -- they hold the wind),
  and `rain-tenant` (identical answers and stored depths with the lamp casting, layer 6,
  and with shadows off, layer 0).
- Masked cutouts other than foliage are not in the map, as they cast nothing on the depth
  path; glass is, and should be.
- `--only rain` also selects `terrain` and `terrain-stream`: the selector is a substring.

### Phase 3

- **The streaks are what the plan said, and three things about them are not.**
  - **Each streak stands for the drops it replaces.** At physical per-drop opacity (a drop's
    image area smeared along its path, about 1.5% for 1 mm) and ~1/30 of the physical density
    drawn, nothing showed. A streak's opacity is multiplied by `density x box volume / count`,
    so the rain's total light is the real rain's whatever count is drawn, capped at opaque.
  - **A drop refracts the frame, not the sky.** It first showed the IBL irradiance about the
    view ray, then the froxel column behind it; both left drops in front of a lit wall or
    pavement darker than what they covered, which read as black sticks. The canvas is now
    copied and mipped before the streaks draw, and a drop shows its average around it at a
    level whose texel is 1/32 of the frame (a drop's field is ~165 degrees), plus its own
    lamp light through the fog in front of it. The IBL term went with it.
  - **Glints.** Rayleigh's n = 2 mode (and an n = 3 overtone mixed per drop) flashes the
    lamp-lit share of a streak (`streak_glint`, 0.8) along its length, mean-preserving.
- **Two looks were added after watching it, both knobs:** `streak_sheen` (a drop reads
  `(1 + sheen)` times what it refracts; DEFAULT 2, since physical rain at night is invisible
  outside its lamps) and `fall_scale` (1 = terminal velocity; it also tilts the cover map).
- **Silent** sets brightness 3, radius 2.5 m, 6144 drops a box, fall scale 0.5 and sheen 1:
  a drizzle to walk through.
- **The late draw** is a PostFX hook (`postfx_set_late_draw`), called after motion blur and
  before DoF with this frame's fog volume, rather than a rain call inside postfx, which stays
  ignorant of scenes. The engine installs it once and draws the scene's rain from it.
- **Arms:** `rain-off`, `rain-determinism`, `rain-lobe` (backlit against front-lit, 295x) and
  `rain-glint` (0.11%). The lobe reads at sheen 0 and counts clipped pixels rather than
  failing on them -- clipping only lowers the backlit side. The glint arm lights the rain from
  the SIDE: backlit, clipping and the 8-bit floor bias its two sums in opposite directions.
- The group's selector is `rainfall`, not `rain`, which `--only` also matched in `terrain`.
- Not done here and still planned: nothing. The ripple arm moves to phase 5 with the water
  fixture.

### Phase 4

- `include/wet_surface.glsl` holds the film constants and the four terms (porosity darkening,
  the Lekner-Dorf hue power, film roughness, normal flattening) that the shore block had
  inline; the shore calls it with its 0.38 and rain with the material's porosity. All 31
  goldens stayed 0 px across the refactor, and `beach` and `pbr-variant` pass.
- `PBR_FEAT_RAIN` is 64 and `PBR_FEAT_ALL` 127, set by the scene while `rain_active`. The
  rain variant declares 12 samplers, the same as `pbr-0` (`rain-ledger`).
- Cover on a surface is PCSS-shaped, which the plan did not have. The first version was a 3x3
  of taps `occlusion_softness` apart, 2.7 texels at the 0.25 m default, and a lamp arm
  thinner than a texel printed THREE dry stripes on silent's pavement a tap-spacing apart
  (the classic ghosting of a regular kernel wider than the map's texel). Now: a blocker
  search at most a texel wide, then a 12-tap Vogel disk rotated per pixel by IGN, its radius
  the blocker's height times tan 8 degrees -- rain does not fall in parallel lines. A car
  keeps a sharp dry outline; a lamp head 5 m up leaves nothing. The default softness is 0.1 m
  and is capped at a texel. A softer minimum edge would need a pre-filtered map.
- Porosity is per material (`porosity`, -1 derives it from roughness per Lagarde:
  `saturate((r - 0.5) / 0.4)`), scaled by `RAIN_POROSITY_DARKEN` 0.5, the top of the natural
  band. Metals take the film and none of the darkening. Only surfaces facing up hold a film
  (smoothstep of N.y over 0.35..0.85).
- `wet_darkening` (1 = physical) was added after looking: soaked rough concrete at physical
  darkening falls to about a quarter of its dry brightness, which in silent took the lamp
  pools out. Silent sets 0.5; the engine default stays 1.
- Arm `rain-wet`: under the roof exactly 1.0 on both halves, open porous 0.19 and open sealed
  0.48 of dry.
