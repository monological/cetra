# Spec 11.79 — stars in the night sky

## Context

`apps/tree` can already be given a below-horizon sun (`--sun-elevation -10`), and the
Hillaire atmosphere handles it correctly — `specs/4.7-physically-based-sky.md:83` lists
"below-horizon night (no NaN speckle)" as a shipped acceptance gate. What it renders is a
black sky, because Hillaire models scattered **sunlight** and nothing else. There are no
stars anywhere in cetra: a grep for `star|night|moon|celestial` across `cetra/`, `apps/`,
`specs/` and `docs/` returns only incidental prose (`sky.c:668`, `specs/4.7:83`) and a build
hostname. The roadmap has never booked it and never rejected it — zero hits. This is
greenfield.

The ask: stars visible at night, in `apps/tree`, becoming more visible as the sun goes down.

Booked as roadmap row 45 / Track B entry **B10** (commit `abef9445`), alongside B11 (the
day/night cycle, a separate later spec). This spec implements B10's star half exactly as the
row sketches it. User decisions locked in: procedural star field, elevation-ramp fade,
library placement off by default (on in tree), and **stars alone** — the night floor, the
moon and the Purkinje shift all stay deferred as the roadmap entry records.

## The insertion point, and why it is the whole design

`cetra/shaders/include/sky_radiance.glsl:7-24` already adds an analytic point source to the
sky-view LUT sample — the sun disc, attenuated by `transmittanceToSky`. A star field is that
same pattern a second time:

```glsl
sky += starRadiance(starDir) * transmittanceToSky(transmittanceLut, r, dir.y) * starIntensity;
```

Four properties fall out of that one line, none of which need code:

- **`transmittanceToSky` returns `vec3(0.0)` when the ray hits the ground**
  (`atmosphere.glsl:136-141`), so stars stop at the horizon with no special case — and near
  the horizon they are dimmed and reddened by real airmass, out of the existing LUT.
- **Clouds occlude them.** `sky_background_clouds_frag.glsl:37` is `sky * cloud.a + cloud.rgb`,
  so a term added to `sky` is multiplied by deck transmittance for free. It must go in *before*
  that multiply, in both variants.
- **Geometry occludes them.** The background draws at the far plane under `GL_LEQUAL`
  (`sky.c:740`).
- **Nothing else sees them.** `sky_radiance.glsl` is included by exactly two files — the two
  background variants. The env cube, IBL, probes, GI, aerial and fog are all on separate
  paths and are untouched. That containment is deliberate and matches
  `sky_env_frag.glsl:6-11`, which already refuses the *sun disc* for firefly reasons: a
  0.53° point source aliases the prefilter importance sampler. Stars are smaller.

Sampler headroom is not a constraint here: `sky_background_frag` declares **2** of 16,
the clouds variant **3**.

## Procedural, and what that costs

Ship `starRadiance(vec3 dir)` as a procedural field in a new
`cetra/shaders/include/stars.glsl`. No asset, no sampler, no new program, no new draw call,
no ordering problem. The three things it must get right, because they are what separate a
night sky from TV static:

1. **Magnitude distribution.** Star counts go as `N ∝ 10^(0.6m)` — roughly 4× more stars per
   magnitude step — so flux is Pareto-distributed with exponent −2.5. Sampling flux as
   `u^(-1/1.5)` gives the real heavy tail: a few dominant stars, a wash of faint ones. A
   uniform-brightness field is the single biggest tell.
2. **Subtle colour.** B−V index → temperature → Planckian RGB. Most stars are near-white;
   a handful read orange or blue. Over-saturating is the second biggest tell.
3. **A Milky Way band.** A point catalog cannot produce one (it is unresolved stars), so
   every engine adds it separately. Procedurally it is a density and glow modulation along a
   fixed great circle — about five lines here.

Grid: octahedral map of `dir` to `[-1,1]²`, 128×128 cells, one jittered star per cell at
~55% occupancy ≈ 9,000 stars, which is the naked-eye limit (magnitude 6.5) by coincidence
worth noting. Jitter inside the inner 80% of the cell so one tap suffices instead of nine.
Each star is a small Gaussian of fixed angular width rather than a thresholded point, so it
lands as a stable ~1.5 px blob rather than a flickering single pixel — subpixel point
sources are the reason star fields alias, and `apps/tree` runs TAA.

**The interface is `starRadiance(vec3) -> vec3`.** Swapping in a baked real catalog later
(Yale BSC5 → `tools/gen_star_catalog.py` → a table, in the `gen_ltc_lut.py` idiom) is a body
swap behind that signature, not a redesign. Choosing procedural now forecloses nothing.

## The fade — and the finding that shapes it

The intended design was for the fade to be **emergent**: add real radiance, let HDR and
auto-exposure do the rest, the way UE and Unity both leave it to eye adaptation. That is
half-blocked here, and the reason is worth stating because it is a deliberate engine
invariant, not an oversight.

`cetra/src/exposure.c:121-139` ends `return fminf(fmaxf(gain, exp2f(-20.0f)), 1.0f);` — the
comment at `:126-136` and `exposure.h:163-166` are explicit that **auto-exposure only ever
darkens; brightening is what it must not do.** The histogram range reaches starlight fine
(`postfx.c:38-40,53` — "roughly starlight (1e-4)…"), the metered clamps are inert at the
bottom, and the meter will report the low value in the GUI. The gain is then discarded the
moment it exceeds 1.

The half that survives is the one that matters for *this* feature: in daylight the gain is
well below 1 and darkens the stars along with everything else, so they vanish on their own.
At night it saturates at exactly 1.0 and the stars sit at their raw radiance. So:

- **Stars disappearing in daylight is emergent.** No code.
- **Stars appearing at night is not.** Add a `starIntensity` uniform computed CPU-side from
  `sun_elevation_deg`, alongside the hard-coded `sunIntensity` at `sky.c:727` — a
  smoothstep ramp from about +3° to −8°, i.e. through civil twilight, which is when stars
  actually come out. It is C, so write it ascending (`1.0f - glm_smoothstep(-8.0f, 3.0f, e)`);
  a GLSL-style `smoothstep(3, -8, e)` with edge0 > edge1 is the classic transcription slip.

This is a visibility ramp standing in for an exposure adaptation the engine deliberately
does not perform. Say so in the comment; do not dress it up as physics.

**Known consequence, deliberately not fixed here:** with the key light faded to zero
(`sky.c:677`, 3°→0°) and the env cube near-black, the *world* at −10° is black too. That is
what a moonless night is, and every game cheats it. The cheap opt-in is a night exposure
lift driving `ex->multiplier` (a long exposure is what a real camera does at night) — booked
below as out of scope, not forgotten.

## Changes

**`cetra/shaders/include/stars.glsl`** — new. `starRadiance(vec3 dir)`: octahedral cell
grid, Pareto flux, Planckian colour, Milky Way band. Uses `include/noise.glsl`'s existing
`hash21`/`hash13` with its own `k` constants, per that file's parameterised-hash contract.

**`cetra/shaders/include/sky_radiance.glsl`** — `skyRadiance()` gains `starIntensity` and a
`mat3 starFrame` parameter and the one `sky +=` line. Both callers pass them; samplers are
already parameters here by the `atmosphere.glsl` convention, so the shape is unchanged.

**`cetra/shaders/sky_background_frag.glsl` and `sky_background_clouds_frag.glsl`** — two new
uniforms each, threaded into the existing `skyRadiance` call. In the clouds variant this
lands before the `* cloud.a` multiply by construction, since it is inside `skyRadiance`.

**`cetra/src/sky.h`** — `bool stars_enabled`, `float stars_brightness`, `float latitude_deg`,
`float star_hour_angle` on `SkyAtmosphere`, beside the sun placement group at `:138-143`.

**`cetra/src/sky.c`** — in `sky_render_background` (`:715-736`), next to
`uniform_set_float(u, "sunIntensity", 20.0f)`: compute the elevation ramp, upload
`starIntensity` and the `starFrame` mat3 (latitude → pole altitude, hour angle → rotation
about it — one uniform, and it is what makes a day/night cycle turn the sky rather than
freeze it). No re-bake: this is sampled live, exactly like `sun_disc_deg` (`gui.c:527-529`).

**`cetra/src/gui.c:509-558`** — a Stars checkbox and brightness slider in the Sky group, and
widen the sun elevation slider from `-6.0f` (`:521`) to `-18.0f` so astronomical night is
reachable from the UI at all. `apps/tree/src/tree.c:591` has its own copy at `-5.0f`; widen
both.

**`cetra/src/cscene.c`** — `environment.stars` (bool or nested object), and it **must** be
appended to the closed `known[]` at `:142-144` or the app warns on a key it reads. If
nested, give it its own `warn_unknown_keys` call — the nested `sun` object has none today,
which is how `water_fixture` silently ran at the wrong sun angle for four specs (`:134-141`).
Consumer wiring in `apps/render/src/cscene_apply.c:72-91`.

**`apps/render/src/render.c`** — `--stars` (implies `--sky`, like `--clouds` at `:1011`),
`--stars-brightness`, `--no-stars` as the way off a `.cscn` that asked for them.

**`apps/tree/src/tree.c`** — `--stars` / `--no-stars` via the four-site idiom (`TreeArgs`
field `:747-808`, default after the memset `:859-870`, parser arm `:876-956`, usage
`:810-856`). **Stars ON by default in tree**, since the ask is tree-specific; off by default
in the library.

**`cetra/src/config_snapshot.c`** — plain `CFG_ROW` stores under `sky` at `:601-605`, not
`CFG_ROW_FN`: nothing re-bakes. `config-coverage` (`gates.py:11804`) *requires* the new GUI
widgets map to rows, and `config-perturb` will exercise them automatically.

**`scripts/gates.py`** — a `stars` group. Rendered through `render` on
`assets/aerial_fixture.gltf` (the suite's sky fixture — upward long-throw camera,
`environment.mode: "sky"`), **not** through tree, which is documented at 31k px
run-to-run. Arms, in the house idiom, each with its named falsifying mutation:

Every arm pins exposure (`--no-auto-exposure -E 1.0`) — cross-elevation brightness reads are
meaningless without it, and it is the house style. Every read is the star CONTRIBUTION — the
delta against a `--no-stars` twin at the same configuration — never raw box brightness,
because the twilight sky's own radiance dominates a raw read and moves the OPPOSITE way as
the sun drops (the `beach-shoreline` lesson: four specs red because an argmax read dry sand).

| arm | asserts | falsified by |
|---|---|---|
| `stars-night` | at `--sun-elevation -12`, ≥ N px move vs `--no-stars` | the feature doing nothing |
| `stars-daylight` | at `--sun-elevation 35`, stars on vs off is **0 px**. Both legs upload `starIntensity` 0 by design (ramp at zero vs disabled), so the docstring must say what the arm is FOR: it is the guard that the ramp actually zeroes | deleting or mis-ranging the elevation ramp |
| `stars-emerge` | the star contribution (luma delta vs the `--no-stars` twin) at +5° / 0° / −6° / −12° is strictly increasing | a constant `starIntensity` |
| `stars-horizon` | the star contribution in a LOW-sky box (~1-3° elevation) is dimmer and redder than in a HIGH-sky box (~25°). NOT "zenith": the fixture's committed frame spans about −12°..+28° (eye (0,400,500) → target (0,3211,−19500) looks ~8° up at fov 40), so no zenith is in frame — and airmass ~27 vs ~2.3 across the reachable band still separates cleanly | dropping `transmittanceToSky` from the star term |
| `stars-clouds` | at −12° with `--clouds`, the star contribution over the sky band is smaller than without clouds. Whole-band, not deck-vs-gap boxes: the deck drifts with wind, so a box's content is not stable across configurations | moving the term after the `* cloud.a` |
| `stars-det` | two runs, 0 px (no clouds — keep the deck's temporal blend out of a determinism arm) | a wall-clock term |

Plus `gate-docs` registration — `run_gate_docs_gate` (`:12143`) parses the docstring and
fails if the arm list or **its order** differs from the run list.

**`specs/11.79-stars-in-the-night-sky.md`** — this plan, committed alone on branch
`11.79-stars-in-the-night-sky` off master before any code.

## Predictions, written before implementing

1. **All 27 goldens 0 px, no rebake** — because the library default is OFF. This is the
   load-bearing prediction and the reason for that default. `cornell_rooms` renders at
   **−10°** (`gates.py:12905`, `cornell_rooms.cscn:9`) and would otherwise get a face full
   of stars; `dither-band-inverse` reads flat runs in `aerial_fixture`'s sky gradient
   (`gates.py:3982-3986`) and stars shorten them; `EMISSIVE_SPEC_SKY` pins `--sun-elevation
   -10` assuming a low-variance backdrop (`:12890-12901`). All three are protected by
   off-by-default and by nothing else. **Eight** sky-bearing goldens (`aerial_fixture`,
   `cloud_fixture`, `cloud_fixture_lowsun`, `froxel_fog`, `contact_debug`, `water_fixture`,
   `water_submerged`, `cornell_rooms` — `froxel_fog` counts because `contact_fixture.cscn`
   is sky mode and `--no-aerial` still draws the background) recompile a changed shader, so
   PAE 1/255 codegen movement is the one acceptable deviation — anything more is a wrong
   diagnosis, stop.
2. `stars-daylight` reads 0 px. If it does not, the ramp is wrong, not the physics.
3. Perf below the profiler floor: one hash chain in a pass that already samples two LUTs.

## Deliberately out of scope

All booked in the roadmap (B10's deferred list and row 46/B11), user-confirmed as deferred:

- **A real star catalog.** Behind the same `starRadiance` signature; a body swap.
- **The moon.** The dominant night light source and by far the largest item — a disc with
  phase and earthshine *plus* a real directional light (~0.25 lux) that casts.
- **The night sky floor** (airglow, zodiacal light, integrated starlight, ~0.0002 cd/m²).
  Jensen et al. 2001's actual contribution. Needed to stop the world being black, and it
  would have to reach `sky_env_frag` too, which is a lighting change rather than a
  screen-space one.
- **Purkinje / scotopic shift** (Kirk & O'Brien 2011) — the desaturate-and-blue-shift that
  makes a dark frame read as night rather than as an underexposed day. Would live in
  `tonemap_frag.glsl`, driven by the metered luminance the CPU already reads back.
- **A time-of-day driver** — roadmap B11. `apps/tree`'s sun is static; `render_scene_callback`
  (`tree.c:701-742`) already has `engine->render_delta` in hand and is the natural hook, but
  the blocker is the 0.11 s sun re-bake, which needs B11's time-sliced env bake first.
