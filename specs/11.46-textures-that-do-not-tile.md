# 11.46 — Textures that do not tile

## Context

This came out of 11.45. The beach was reported, repeatedly, as showing "a grid", "columns",
"static well-like patterns", "the same tile boundary". Every one of those reports was read as a
water bug and none of them was. The ground was the problem, in three separate ways, and the
misattribution cost most of a session — which is the first thing worth recording here.

The general problem is older than the beach: **a repeated texture is recognisable as repeated**,
and ground is the one surface seen over tens of repeats at once. Nothing else in this tree has
had to solve it, because nothing else tiles a texture that far.

## The three defects, in the order they had to be fixed

They are ordered by dependency, not by severity. Each is a precondition for the next being
visible at all.

### 1. The tile was never periodic (`b6da1b4`)

`sand_height_field` is built from `veg_fbm2` and `veg_worley2`, and both sample an unbounded
lattice: the field at u = 1 has no relation to the field at u = 0. The sand is tiled 40 times
across the island, so there were 40 hard discontinuities in each axis.

**This is not a filtering artefact and no mip level, anisotropy setting or blur touches it.** It
is a discontinuity in the data. It has to be fixed in the generator.

The three noise primitives gained periodic variants that wrap the integer lattice index, so the
cell after the last is the first and the field closes. Two constraints follow and both are real:

- The caller's frequencies must be **whole numbers of cells**. A fractional frequency has no
  period to wrap on.
- The Perlin period is capped at 256, the permutation table's own size. fbm doubles the period
  per octave, so deep octave counts stop tiling before they stop working — the cap is on useful
  octaves, not on the period.

The ripple train paid for this with its free choice of angle: its phase is now a whole number of
cycles along each axis, so its direction is quantised to a ratio of integers. At the 45 degrees
the sand uses it is exact anyway.

### 2. The tangent frame did not match the UVs (`6298504`)

The island mesh wrote a **circular** tangent, `(-sin θ, 0, cos θ)` — the frame a polar
parameterisation wants. Its UVs are **planar**, `u ∝ x`. So the normal map was applied through a
basis that disagreed with the map it indexes.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that reads as a rendering bug rather than an art
one: the ripple train rotated around the island instead of running one way across it, and
because the frame turns with the ring while the UVs do not, the disagreement changes from vertex
to vertex and **creases along every triangle edge** — a quilt over the whole beach, strongest
where the normal map has the most to say.

The tangent is now Gram-Schmidt from the world X axis against the surface normal, so it stays in
the surface where the beach is sloped, and `cross(N, T)` still lands along +v so the handedness
is unchanged.

The general lesson: **a tangent is `d(position)/du` and nothing else.** A frame that looks
geometrically natural for the mesh is wrong unless the UVs agree with it.

### 3. A seamless tile still repeats (`64ec31f`)

With 1 and 2 fixed the grid is gone and the repeat is not. This is the part with a literature
answer.

## By-example stochastic texturing

Heitz & Neyret 2018, *High-Performance By-Example Noise using a Histogram-Preserving Blending
Operator*. Skew the UV onto a triangle lattice, take the three vertices of the triangle it falls
in, sample the texture at a different random offset per vertex, and blend. Neighbouring points
read different parts of the tile, so the period stops existing.

The blend is where it lives or dies. A weighted average of three samples **divides the variance**
— contrast collapses and the ground is flat mush, which is what a naive three-tap blend looks
like. The fix is to divide by the root of the summed squared weights rather than by their sum,
which preserves the variance of a single sample:

```
g = mean + Σ wᵢ (T(uvᵢ) − mean) / √(Σ wᵢ²)
```

**That step is exact only for a Gaussian input**, which is the entire reason the histogram
machinery exists. So the texture is transformed at bake time into one whose per-channel histogram
IS Gaussian, and the original histogram is kept as a small inverse table applied after blending.
Sample the transform, blend it, map back — and the result carries the original histogram exactly.

### An earlier claim, and why it was wrong

It was argued during this work that the histogram machinery could be skipped, on the grounds
that a procedural texture's histogram is ours to author and a sum of noise octaves is
near-Gaussian by CLT. **That is wrong and the argument is worth keeping so it is not made
again.** The field is a sine train plus fBm plus Worley: a sine's distribution is arcsine and
U-shaped, Worley's is nowhere near normal, and the ripple carries a deliberate skew (a long
windward face and a short steep lee, which is what separates a wind ripple from a corrugation).
Nor is there an analytic escape, because the field's CDF is not a closed form anyone chose. The
transform is not optional.

### It costs no sampler unit, and that is the point

`pbr_frag` has been at 16/16 since 4.10 — the roadmap's Wall 1. This runs there anyway:

- **The transformed texture takes the original's slot.** The shader samples T′ and never T, so
  the transform is written back over the source at bake time. Zero new units.
- **The inverse CDF is 64 entries, three channels — 768 bytes.** Uniform space, which is the
  ledger's second escape, precedented by the clustered-light block (which consumes zero units)
  and 11.45's shore-film block.

A `sampler2DArray` was considered for the tables, and would have worked as the ledger's fourth
escape — one declaration, many layers. It is not needed: nothing here has to be a texture.

### Two traps, each paid for once

**The map is baked NON-sRGB and decoded after the inverse table.** The transform and its inverse
are both defined on the stored codes. A hardware sRGB decode sitting between them means the
inverse is undoing a transform of a curve of the values it was built from — they are no longer
inverses.

**The array uniform setter bypasses the value cache.** `UniformBinding.value` is `float[16]`, and
`uniform_write_wanted` takes an `unsigned char` count. Routing 192 floats through it smashes the
slot. `uniform_set_vec3_array` therefore writes straight to GL and accepts the redundant write,
which is why it is documented as being for tables a material changes rarely and never for
anything set per draw.

**And the lattice offsets need `textureGrad`.** They are piecewise constant, so the offset UVs
step from cell to cell; an implicit lookup takes its mip from those derivatives and prints the
lattice as a mosaic of differently blurred patches. The offsets are translations and a
translation does not change a footprint, so the gradients are the undisplaced ones. This is the
same trap 11.45 hit on the foam pattern's advection, from the same cause.

## Verification

**The measurement is the standard deviation, not the look.** A broken transform shifts the
colour; a broken blend flattens the contrast. Neither moved:

| | mean | std dev |
|---|---|---|
| periodic tile, plain sampling | 0.5329 | 0.02876 |
| periodic tile, stochastic | 0.5317 | 0.02881 |

Contrast held to **0.15%**, where an unweighted average of three barycentric taps gives up
roughly a third of it. Same mean, same contrast, different arrangement — which is the whole
claim.

Taken on `apps/tree`'s beach at `--headless-jitter`, frame 60, over a 1500x200 box of dry sand.
Note tree is **not** pixel-deterministic and has no exposure pin, so this is a statistic over a
region and deliberately not a pixel diff.

## The normal map, in slope space

Albedo and normal both go through the lattice; they share it, because two maps on two lattices
would show the albedo of one part of the tile over the relief of another.

**The normal does NOT get a histogram transform, and cannot.** Its three channels are not
independent — they are a unit vector — so running each through its own transform and blending
breaks the constraint and yields non-unit vectors with a distribution the map never had. That
would be wrong rather than approximate.

Slopes are the linear space for it. A tangent-space normal encodes a height gradient as
`normalize(vec3(-dh/du, -dh/dv, 1))`, so recovering the gradient gives two unconstrained
quantities that blend meaningfully, and reconstructing afterwards restores the constraint
exactly. It is the same space the water's far-field LOD works in, for the same reason: slope
filters linearly and a normal does not. The mean is zero — a tangent-space map describes
deviation from the surface, so a surface with no net tilt has none.

**Blending slopes without a transform is a deliberate approximation.** The blend is exact only
for a Gaussian, and a slope field is closer to Gaussian than the height it came from
(differentiation is linear and pulls a distribution toward normal) but is not exactly Gaussian.
What imperfect preservation costs a normal map is a slight softening of relief contrast — a far
smaller error than transforming a constrained vector would be, and it needs no second table.

Measured: standard deviation 0.02876 plain → 0.02881 albedo-only → **0.02893** with the normal.
Relief contrast preserved rather than softened, which is the failure this was watched for.

## Roughness is not a texture here, and that is why it is not done

`pbr_frag` reads roughness from the shared `maskArray` layer, not from a `sampler2D`. Sampling it
stochastically therefore means transforming a packed, multi-material `sampler2DArray` that six
scalar masks share — a different and much larger change than the two maps above, touching
machinery every material in the engine depends on.

It is also the least valuable of the three: roughness modulates gloss on a surface authored at
0.9, where the visible tiling was carried by albedo and relief. Left undone deliberately rather
than overlooked.

## What is not done

- **Roughness**, for the reason above.
- **Cost is unmeasured.** Six fetches on the ground now, where it was two. `--profiler` has not
  been run against it.
- **Nothing but tree's sand opts in.** No golden contains a stochastic material, so all 24 should
  be 0 px — predicted, not yet measured.
