# 10.5 — Cascade shadows: finish the policy the punctual path already shipped

## Context

`contact_fixture` (46 rocks over a 12x12 m ground plane, sun at elevation 35°
from its `.cscn` sky) renders directional cascade shadows full of defects: lit
streaks inside the umbra, hard blocks stamped over and beyond the true shadow,
and hard per-frame glitching in the interactive window. Nothing recent broke it
— the cascade code is byte-identical across the suspect range. The defect was
always there; the fixture-winding fix made the ground plane render for the
first time, and the floor is the first large lit receiver a sun-lit scene has
ever had in this repo.

The mechanism is the one specs/10.3 measured and fixed for punctual maps,
still live on the cascade path:

1. **Far-side storage stores sliver garbage.** The cascade depth pass
   front-face culls (`shadow.c:810`), storing each caster's far side, with NO
   polygon offset (`GL_POLYGON_OFFSET_FILL` is only enabled inside the
   punctual section). A curved caster's far side near its silhouette
   rasterizes as slivers that miss texel centres (holes → "compare against
   nothing reads lit" → lit streaks inside the umbra) or land partial garbage
   depths (→ false-dark runs beyond it). On spheres — all silhouette — the
   maps are riddled with both. Causal, verified directly: flipping the cascade
   pass to `GL_BACK` removed the blocks from the raw shadow term (dumped
   `FragColor = vec4(vec3(shadow))` pre-post).
2. **All three cascades carry the same class of garbage on their own texel
   grids** — which is why `--shadow-cascades 1` did not remove the stripes
   (the scene-fit single map is far-side too) — and the min-walk
   (`calculateShadow`, `pbr_frag.glsl:575`) unions three independently-fitted
   grids of it.
3. **View-dependence makes it glitch.** The inner cascade fits re-fit and
   texel-snap with the camera (the outermost is scene-fit and static,
   `c597ac7`), so the garbage re-lands every frame of motion; TAA jitters
   across the hard edges and never converges (44k px churning at a static
   camera between frames 120 and 150; 0 px with TAA off).

Ruled out by measurement this session: contact shadows (0-px diff with
`--cs-distance 0`), GTAO, bloom, aerial, the cluster grid, cascade count, map
resolution (4096 shrinks blocks, does not remove them). An earlier theory —
that coarse view-slice cascades stamp meter-scale texels through the min-walk —
is wrong on arithmetic: the outermost cascade is scene-fit and every cascade in
this scene has ≤17 mm texels. The walk unions garbage; it does not create it.

**Why this failed when it was tried before, and why it will work now.** The
comment at `shadow.c:850-855` records that near-side cascades were already
tried and reverted: "at low sun elevation the froxel_fog fixture answered with
banded ground acne and hard terminator crescents on convex casters that a
larger offset made worse, not better." That attempt had only the polygon
offset — the receiver-plane bias did not exist yet (it landed later,
`78435ae`, punctual-only). Grazing receivers are precisely the geometry the
plane bias is exact for, and the punctual commit measured exactly that win
("the near-edge-on box face is clean at 40x brightening where it used to
stipple"). My near-side experiment this session reproduced the same half-fix
failure: blocks gone, acne back. **Consequence: the depth-pass flip and the
plane bias are one unit — separate commits for bisectability, judged and if
necessary reverted as a pair — and low-sun scenes are a mandatory regression
gate.**

## Related finding: the cornell_leak wall-base band (punctual, on master)

`cornell_leak` (spot-lit) renders a dark wedge along the base of the left
wall — the shadow term (gone with `--no-shadows`), on the punctual path. It
is fallout from the already-merged 10.3/10.4 punctual work: the fixture's
generator documents the design assumption that work broke
(`gen_cornell_rooms.py:216-217` — "The shadow pass culls front faces, so from
a light inside the room every wall is culled and the partition is the only
occluder in the map"). With near-side storage every wall is now IN the spot's
map, and at the wall base — the most grazing receiver geometry in the fixture
— the receiver-plane bias's extrapolation clamp does not fully cover
self-comparison. The `78435ae` commit message records `cornell_leak 3201 px`
— measured, reported, accepted without 1:1 inspection (the committed golden
was already stale and could not catch it). 3201 px of 1.92M is exactly a thin
wedge.

Consequences: Phase 0 gains a punctual grazing gate (FAILS today), and Phase
2 root-causes this in `punctual_shadow.glsl` FIRST — fix the reference, then
port the corrected form, so cascades never inherit the defect and one
implementation stays canonical.

**Phase 1 as built — with a correction recorded the moment it was caught.**
The flip landed as planned. The Phase 1 commit message then blames a
still-broken hole gate on `glPolygonOffset`'s slope term "displacing curved
casters' silhouette facets by metres" — THAT CLAIM IS WRONG, and the error is
worth recording because of how it happened: the fixture's original camera at
(0,3,4) looked THROUGH the floating sphere at the far half of its own umbra,
so every probe there silently read the sphere's lit front face instead of
ground. All the downstream arithmetic was built on a phantom. With the
camera moved off-axis and two guards added to the gate (a sight-line
clearance skip and a reference-luma check that raises on non-ground
samples), the honest measurement is: slope factor 2 produces NO visible-umbra
artifact on either sphere, and slope factor 0 REGRESSES the punctual path
(cornell_leak's screen-grazing wall answered 0.39, because the screen-space
plane gradient degrades exactly where the receiver is screen-grazing while
the raster's own slope measure does not). The offset stays at 2/2 for every
map, and a "slope allowance" lookup term invented to justify slope 0 was
built, measured, and deleted.

### Phase 2 — lookup: receiver-plane bias for cascades

**First, fix the reference.** Root-cause and close the cornell_leak wall-base
band in `punctual_shadow.glsl` (instrument the per-tap compares at the wall
base; suspects are the `PUNCTUAL_MAX_PLANE_BIAS` extrapolation clamp at
grazing and its interaction with the depth-pass polygon offset). The punctual
grazing gate arbitrates. Only then port.

**Phase 2a as built.** Instrumented per-tap: along the wall-base band, every
failing tap failed with a depth gap under 1e-4 NDC and NOT ONE held a
genuinely different surface — so neither suspect was quite right. The
mechanism is the plane term's POSITIVE side: at a concave junction the
receiver's plane extrapolates INTO the adjacent surface, which truncates it
and stands epsilon nearer, and a reference depth pushed deeper by up to the
clamp (0.01 NDC — three orders of magnitude above the actual gaps) reads
that surface as an occluder. No symmetric clamp value can be right. The fix
is structural: the clamp is now ONE-SIDED, `clamp(dot(duv_dz, off),
-PUNCTUAL_MAX_PLANE_BIAS, 0.0)` — the plane may only ever make the test more
lenient (the acne direction), never stricter, because a deeper-than-fragment
prediction can only manufacture occlusion a flat compare would not report.
Grazing gate: 0.5220 → 1.0000. Penumbra gate byte-stable at centre 0.7512.

Port `punctualShadow`'s plane bias (`punctual_shadow.glsl:109-156`: 2x2
solve, `det` degeneracy guard, extrapolation clamp, constant floor in
DEPTH_COMPONENT24 ULPs — same texture format both arrays) into
`pbr_frag.glsl`'s cascade path, with the ortho simplifications:
- No perspective divide: the plane gradient per screen axis is
  `0.5 * mat3(lightSpaceMatrix[layer]) * ddxWorld` — one mat3 multiply, no
  `w` guards, constant over a planar receiver; recomputed per layer inside
  the walk. (The 0.5 cancels through the 2x2 solve, so it may be dropped —
  but from the whole vector or not at all; scaling only uv or only z corrupts
  `dz_duv` by 2x.)
- `calculateShadow` and `cascadeShadowTap` gain `ddxWorld/ddyWorld`, reusing
  the values hoisted at `pbr_frag.glsl:863`. **The hoist is mandatory, not a
  convenience**: the cascade call site (`:1177`) sits INSIDE the fused
  `numDir + clusterCount` light loop (`:1037`) whose trip count is
  per-fragment — non-uniform control flow, where a local `dFdx` is undefined.
- Replace the heuristic at `cascadeShadowTap:516` entirely (per-tap plane
  offset + floor; delete the NdotL term). The bias feeds three sites:
  - `shadowPCF3x3` compares: plane offset per fixed ±1-texel tap.
  - PCSS variable-radius compares: `dot(dz_duv, POISSON16[i] * radius)`, with
    the extrapolation clamp sized against `filterRadiusUV`, not ±1 texel.
  - **PCSS blocker search: plane-relative classification.** With near-side
    storage the receiver's own surface is in the map, and a flat separation
    on linearized depths is NOT sufficient: at grazing, the receiver's own
    stored depth at a far tap deviates from `currentDepth` by
    `dot(dz_duv, off)` — up to ~1 m at 10° sun across the max PCSS disk — so
    a flat epsilon either counts the receiver as its own blocker (defeating
    the `blockerCount < 0.5` fully-lit early-out) or ignores real blockers
    within that band. Classify each tap against the plane-extrapolated
    receiver depth at that tap (`currentDepth + clamp(dot(dz_duv, off), …)`)
    minus a small separation epsilon.
  - Expected behaviour change to record, not fix: `zBlocker` becomes the
    caster's near face, so PCSS penumbrae widen by caster thickness.
    `PCSS_PENUMBRA_SCALE` is retuned only if the fixture gate says so.
- **Grazing guard, decided by the low-sun gate**: the punctual clamp is sized
  for ±1-texel taps; a PCSS disk reaches ~10 texels, where a 1%-of-range
  clamp leaves residual acne at low sun and a 10x clamp re-opens silhouette
  leaks by the same factor. Either port a grazing fade (the
  `PUNCTUAL_GRAZING_FADE` NdotL-trust pattern — which the deleted heuristic
  implicitly provided) or slope-cap the PCSS radius; pick by measurement on
  the 10° fixture and record the tradeoff in the spec.
- Cleanup (same commit, 10.4 "replace, don't stack" pattern):
  - `cascadeParams[layer].w` (biasScale) becomes dead — remove every write
    (`shadow.c:317`, `:758`, and the `:764-796` tail) and both shader reads
    (`pbr_frag.glsl:516`; `csm.glsl:65`, where it is always 1.0); fix the
    field comments (`shadow.h:74-76`, `csm.glsl:28`).
  - `punctualTexelScale` is dead (declared, never read since `78435ae`) —
    remove the uniform, its upload (`shadow.c:389`), the `ss->` field and
    `compute_punctual_matrices` out-param, and the uniform.c cache + drift
    check entries (`uniform.c:154,159`).
  - Delete stale `fog_frag` references (`csm.glsl:15-19`,
    `froxel_inject_frag.glsl:103`) — that shader no longer exists.

### Phase 3 — the min-walk, measured

With sound content and bias, re-measure `calculateShadow`'s walk:
- The edge-position gate at cc=3 detects union dilation against ground truth;
  the pillar exercises the walk's motivating case (a caster clipped from a
  tight inner cascade while the receiver stays covered — `c597ac7`). If
  needed, a temporary own-cascade-only shader variant diffed against the
  union localizes exactly what the walk adds.
- Expected outcome: the walk is *sound* once the maps are sound (the
  scene-complete outermost is a real guarantee) and it stays. If the gate
  shows the union still degrading the fine cascade's answer, restrict to the
  fragment's own cascade + the scene-fit outermost, and verify the `c597ac7`
  zoom-sweep artifact does not return. Decision by numbers, recorded in the
  spec either way.

**Phase 3 as built: the walk stays, no code change.** The numbers:
- Degradation test: the edge-position gate reads -0.9984 at cc=3 and -1.0204
  at cc=1 against the analytic -1.0098 — 11 mm each side, a 22 mm spread ≈
  1.3 outermost texels. The union costs at most ~1 coarse texel of edge
  movement, judged against ground truth, and passes the same bar the single
  map does.
- Redundancy test: the pillar band was scanned along its full length with
  the walk enabled and with a first-covering-only variant — 0.0000 both
  ways. The static fixture CANNOT discriminate: the eye pushback keeps the
  pillar inside every cascade at the gate camera, and `c597ac7`'s motivating
  failure was camera-motion-coupled (interactive zoom sweeps re-fitting the
  inner boxes), which no static render exercises. Removing protection that
  was verified interactively, on the strength of one non-discriminating
  static scan, is exactly the under-measured change this branch exists to
  end.

## Verification protocol (non-negotiable, learned this session)

- Full-frame comparisons at the fixture/scene's authored camera; no crops as
  evidence.
- Temporal checks with TAA on (`--taa --headless-jitter` for windowed
  parity); the churn metric is shadow-attributable (measured against the
  `--no-shadows` floor).
- Gates with closed-form expected answers run before and after every phase;
  Phase 1 alone is EXPECTED to fail the acne gate — that is the pair-judgment
  working, not a reason to ship a blur.
- All renders pinned: `--no-auto-exposure -E 1.0`.

## Coda — the artifact that survived every phase was never a shadow

After all gates passed, the interactive repro (contact_fixture at its .cscn
camera, TAA on) still showed hard rectangular streaks stamped over and beyond
the shadows, flickering per frame. The trail of eliminations, each by
measurement at the user's exact framing:

- present with PCSS on and off (identical);
- present at --shadow-cascades 1, so not the min-walk or the camera-fit maps;
- identical on the pre-branch master build, so nothing 10.5 introduced;
- unmoved by 2048 -> 4096 maps (only penumbra micro-shifts in the term diff),
  so not map-texel content;
- the dumped scene-fit depth layer is clean (smooth discs over a smooth
  ground gradient), and a CPU replay of the lookup against the dumped map +
  matrix reproduces the GPU term, so not the lookup either;
- the divided shadow term is bimodal (0.02 / 1.00) once bloom, GTAO and SSR
  are off — the streaks lived in the POST stack, and the earlier "clean term"
  methodology had been switching them off in one bundle with the noise.

The mechanism: the render app's sky path force-enables the shadow catcher,
whose quad hardcodes y=0 (catcher_vert.glsl) — exactly co-planar with any
scene that ships its own ground plane. The catcher draws last, depth-tested
under the JITTERED projection; where interpolation rounding lets it win the
z-fight it blends its own flat-bias shadow term over the already-shaded
ground. The winning set is a jitter-dependent pattern of long thin
rectangles, hence "glitches hard with TAA, static without". Every observed
gating fell out: the GUI shadows toggle and catcher toggle both kill the pass
(render.c gates on both), --no-shadows kills it, and the headless --no-ssr
flag flipped it only because postfx_ssr_active switches the catcher's
surfaceMode discard — which is why an SSR-toggle test in the GUI showed
nothing while a --no-ssr A/B seemed conclusive.

Fix: glPolygonOffset(1, 8) on the catcher draw — the virtual floor is pushed
a few depth ULPs behind everything so real geometry always wins the tie. The
backdrop dome floor writes no depth, so the catcher's actual job (grounding a
floating model, raiden verified) is untouched.

Gate: run_catcher_gate — the TAA churn measurement on contact_fixture, the
suite's only sky+catcher+real-ground scene, at DEFAULT settings (the defect
was invisible to every gate that pins the pipeline down). Fails 366,732 px
against a 101,316 px no-shadow floor on the pre-fix build; passes at
93,386 px (below the floor) with the fix.

Moral for the suite: the fixtures all author their lights precisely to keep
ambient out of the measurements — which also keeps the catcher out of every
frame the gates ever look at. The artifact class lived in the app-layer
default path, not the engine's shadow math.
