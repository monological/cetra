# Spec 11.82 — the moon

## Context

Roadmap **B13**, and the last ready item in the document. Four entries remain open; the other
three are blocked on this one (B14 Purkinje — its row says judge it only once real night
frames exist), explored to a dead end (D0 — the real count is ONE sampler unit, not two), or
self-refuting (E7 — "on current evidence this item is not yet justified").

Three specs shipped back to back — 11.79 stars, 11.80 the night floor, 11.81 the day/night
cycle — and what they produced is a night you can watch arrive but not a night you can SEE.
The floor is an isotropic ambient wash; the stars are pinpricks. **The dominant natural night
light is missing**, and it is why real nights are readable at all: a full moon is ~0.25 lux
against the starlit floor's ~0.001. Moonlit night is a different lighting problem from
moonless night, not a dimmer one. B13's framing: **a LIGHT, not a backdrop.**

## The design

### 1. The moon mirrors the sun, with two deliberate asymmetries

New state on `SkyAtmosphere` — **six fields plus one on the cycle**, with five candidates
argued out:

```c
bool  moon_enabled;
float moon_brightness;      // the ONE look scale; drives disc AND light
float moon_elevation_deg;
float moon_azimuth_deg;
vec3  moon_dir;             // derived, sky_update_moon
struct Light* moon_light;   // BORROWED like sun_light; NULL = disc only
double cycle_moon_offset;   // hours the moon LAGS the sun (in the cycle group)
```

Argued out, each for a stated reason: **`moon_disc_deg`** (the moon subtends 0.52° and the sun
0.53° — which is why eclipses work — so it reuses `sun_disc_deg` and deletes six authoring
surfaces); **`moon_base_intensity`** (the level relative to the sun is a *ratio*, so the light
is `sun_base_intensity * SKY_MOON_LIGHT_FRACTION` and the moon inherits whatever unit scale the
app chose); **a cached phase** (derived every frame — a phase field is a second place to go
stale, and a stale phase renders a *plausible* moon, which is the worst kind of bug);
**separate disc/light brightness** (two knobs make "a bright disc casting no light" authorable,
the exact failure the design is built to forbid); **`moon_cast_shadows`** (derived from
`fade * phase > 0`, the sun's own rule).

**With the cycle off** you place the moon by hand. **With the cycle on** the tick owns both
angles, exactly as it already owns the sun's, and greys the same sliders. `--moon` does not
require `--day-cycle`; a static frame gets a moon. No new concept, no second clock.

Under the cycle: `moon_hour = cycle_hour − cycle_moon_offset`, the offset advancing at the
synodic rate (24 h per 29.53 simulated days). **Minus**, so a growing offset makes the moon
transit *later* — the direction it really drifts. That one rate is both "the moon rises ~50
minutes later each day" and "the phase evolves through the month", because those are the same
fact. It reuses `sky_sun_path` verbatim at a shifted hour.

### 2. Phase is DERIVED, never authored

    cos(alpha) = -dot(moon_dir, sun_dir)        alpha = 180deg - elongation

**The negation is load-bearing and both Plan agents flagged it independently.** `alpha` is the
Sun–Moon–*Observer* angle measured at the moon: **0 at full, 180 at new**. Using the raw dot
product inverts the feature — a full moon evaluates at alpha 180 and renders at 2.8e-4, the
darkest possible value, while a new moon becomes the brightest object in the sky. And it is
**invisible at elongation 90**, where both readings give alpha 90, which is why every gate
ladder straddles that point.

Sanity check, end to end: latitude 45, `cycle_hour` 0, offset 12 → moon at el +45 az 180, sun
at el −45 az 0 → `dot = −1` → alpha 0 → **full moon, high, at midnight.** It falls out rather
than being arranged.

On the disc, with `u` = the sunward direction projected into the disc plane, `v` is
perpendicular to the sun by construction so `v · sunDir` is identically zero and the lighting
collapses to `lit = x·sin(alpha) + z·cos(alpha)`. **The horns cannot point the wrong way,
because `u` IS the sunward direction** — there is no sign to invert and no parameter to
mis-author.

### 3. Brightness: the opposition surge, and a full moon is not a ball

Krisciunas & Schaefer 1991, normalised to 1 at full:

    ks(alpha) = 10^(-0.4 * (0.026*alpha + 4e-9*alpha^4))      alpha in DEGREES

alpha 90 → **0.0910** (quarter at ~1/11 of full, against a measured 8.4–10×); alpha 45 →
0.335; alpha 180 → 2.8e-4. A Lambertian law gives 0.5 at quarter and is the single most likely
way to get this wrong.

**And the lit face is flat.** Lunar regolith backscatters hard, so a real full moon has *no
centre-to-limb gradient at all*. Shading it by `cos(theta)` renders a snooker ball — the classic
tell. So the terminator is a soft **step** one pixel wide (via `fwidth`, the stars' own
mechanism), not a cosine, and `MOON_LIMB` is 0.85 where the sun's is 0.4: same functional form,
opposite intent.

**The LEVEL is a look constant and this is stated in the code.** 0.25 lux against ~120,000 is
19 stops, and `exposure_auto_gain` (`exposure.c:139`) is capped `fminf(..., 1.0f)` — its own
comment reads "THIS LINE IS NOW THE WHOLE GUARANTEE." A physically-scaled moon renders black.
11.79 and 11.80 both hit this and answered the same way. Every **ratio** stays physical — the
phase law, the transmittance tint, earthshine against the lit limb.

### 4. The disc lives in two shaders and nowhere else

The firefly rule, third application. `sky_env_frag.glsl:5-11` refuses the sun disc because a
half-degree disc aliases the GGX prefilter *and* its energy already ships as the analytic
light. The mechanism is not a flag: `sky_radiance.glsl` is included by **exactly two files**,
and the env shaders never include it. **Following it means the entire LUT and slicer chain
needs zero changes** — which is what 11.81 already claimed when it said a second slow light
comes free.

Plus **procedural maria** (reusing `starNoise3` — include-once, second consumer free) evaluated
in a **face-locked frame**, so the pattern does not swim as the moon crosses the sky; and
**earthshine** on the dark limb, blue because it is light bounced off an ocean planet.

### 5. A real casting directional, bounded by construction

A second `Light*` driven by `sky_apply_moon_to_light`, the twin of `sky.c:794-813`. Its
`cast_shadows` is gated on **`sky_night_factor(sky) > 0`** — the ramp that already governs
stars, floor and zenith ambient, and which is an *exact* zero above +3°.

That buys a provable statement: **above sun elevation +3° the moon never casts**, so daylight
`directional_count` is byte-identical to today's. The both-casting window is exactly (0°, 3°]
— **17 scene-minutes, ≤7 s of wall clock** at the GUI's longest day, twice per scene-day. The
alternative (complementary fades) was rejected: it forbids a moon in a late-afternoon sky, and
it makes the moon's shadow *pop* in where today's pair is continuous.

## What the integration pass found

The engine has **no key-light concept** — it has five differently-implemented answers. This
feature must not invent a sixth. Each site is either already correct for N directionals, or
wrong today for a reason the moon merely exposes.

| site | verdict |
|---|---|
| `sky->sun_light` | The honest one, and the anchor of an **8-site apply chain** |
| Cloud shadows (`sky.c:445`) | **Correct as-is.** The map is *sheared along the sun* (`sky.c:440`); applying it to the moon would be a visibly wrong shadow, not a missing one. Add a comment; do not "fix" |
| Contact shadows (`postfx.c:3262`) | **Already broken today, without a moon** — see below |
| TSM (`TSM_SLOTS 1`) | Moon at slot 1 gets **two** failure modes: glass casts solid black, hair casts *nothing* |
| Froxel fog, PBR shading, shadow catcher | **Correct for free.** The catcher already weights by intensity — cite it as the pattern |
| MSM array (`shadow.c:957`) | Reallocates on every horizon crossing — `!=` where the depth array is grow-only |

**The moon's brightness depends on the *sun's* direction**, a coupling the sun does not have.
So all eight `sky_apply_sun_to_light` sites — including the GUI slider release and the config
restore — must re-apply the moon. **The fix is to call it from inside `sky_update_moon`, run
unconditionally once per frame from `sky_cycle_tick`**, which deletes the change detection, the
deferred re-bake flags, the config apply hooks and the question of who owns the moon light.
Affordable only because nothing the moon touches is baked — the firefly rule paying something
back.

**The cycle early-out is a trap that already caught 11.81.** `sky.c:1078` returns when the
sun's float angles have not moved (near noon at low latitude both derivatives approach zero).
The star hour was hoisted above it for exactly that reason. The moon sidesteps it entirely: it
needs *no* comparison, because it triggers nothing expensive. The only edit is turning one
`return` into an `if`.

## Resolved conflicts

**Moon light creation: CONDITIONAL** (agent 2's position over agent 1's). Created only when
`moon_enabled` at app setup. Three reasons: it matches the `sky.clouds.enabled` **refuse**
precedent exactly (a config restore cannot conjure a `Light`); it protects the 27 goldens
without relying on a prediction about driver loop-unrolling being invariant to trip count; and
`config-perturb` fails on *stale* exceptions as well as missing ones, so the exception set
depends on whether the light exists. Consequence, documented: the GUI checkbox cannot create a
light mid-session — same as clouds.

**Contact-shadow slot fix: DEFERRED, and recorded.** `postfx.c:3262` reads `fog_light_dir[0]`
— literal index 0 — and slot 0 is whichever casting directional is first in `scene->lights[]`.
In the render app the sky sun is appended *after* the scene loads, so any `.cscn` authoring a
casting directional silently steals the contact-shadow march. The corpus dodges it only by
luck. **The moon does not make it observably worse** (at night the sun is not casting, so the
moon inherits slot 0; during the ≤7 s overlap the sun should win anyway). It cannot be
falsified without a new fixture, and bundling an unfalsifiable fix into a feature spec is what
this project's doctrine refuses. **Its own spec, with its own fixture.**

**MSM grow-only: INCLUDED**, matching the policy the depth array already states 750 lines
earlier. Without it a running cycle frees and rebuilds a 24–48 MB RGBA16F array four times per
scene-day. **Correction: this is two line-edits, not one** — `shadow.c:956` and the `resized`
computation on the next line both test `msm_allocated_layers != layers`, and both become `<`.
The shrink path then skips the whole block, so `msm_allocated_layers` keeps its larger value
with no extra assignment. **It carries a proof obligation**: the surplus layers must be
provably never written and never read, which means confirming the resolve loop and `msm_pass`
both bound by `directional_count` rather than by the allocated count. If that does not hold,
this comes out of the spec and goes back on the shelf rather than being patched around.

**And sharing it puts a constraint on every disc arm that I had wrong.** The sun-disc test is

cosmetic: without it `moon-env`'s "the sky band moves" leg is **vacuous**, because the sun's own
disc changing size between the two legs satisfies it with the moon disc deleted entirely.

borrowed for the shadow arm (the `cycle-slice`-borrows-`sheen_fixture` precedent). **No new
fixture**: the moon has no *painted* ground truth — phase is derived, the terminator is a
prediction the gate computes itself, maria are procedural — so a fixture would buy a
`gen_moon_fixture.py` to keep alive for no assertion flags cannot make.

## The gate group

    moon-probe       the phase law as a CLOSED FORM against a Python twin, 1e-4, five configs
    moon-disc        the disc lands where the sky says and NOWHERE else, at two sizes
    moon-lit         lit AREA follows (1-cos e)/2 across four elongations
    moon-terminator  the bright limb points at the sun, at FOUR sun rolls 90 deg apart
    moon-maria       textured, not a flat tint, and locked to the FACE not the sun
    moon-earthshine  the dark limb is lit, bounded above and below against the lit side
    moon-light       the moon LIGHTS the world, carrying the same law; full/quarter >= 5
    moon-env         the firefly rule, asserted from OUTSIDE the process
    moon-shadow      the second directional actually CASTS
    moon-clouds      the disc sits under the deck, inside the cloud.a multiply
    moon-cycle       a frozen cycle matches explicit angles at 0 px; a different hour moves
    moon-cscn        the authored block IS the flag path
    moon-config      the rows, and "off by default", pinned EXACTLY
    moon-det         two runs of the heaviest config, 0 px

**Two anti-vacuity pairs, and no half of either is safe alone.** `moon-lit` passes on a
**mirrored** disc — area does not change when you flip it, the decal-poster failure exactly —
and `moon-terminator` refuses it; `moon-terminator` passes on a disc whose lit fraction never
changes, and `moon-lit` refuses that. `moon-env` passes on a build whose moon lights nothing
(0 px is 0 px), and `moon-light` forces the ground to be listening; `moon-light` passes on a
build that bakes the disc into the cube, and `moon-env` refuses it.

**`moon-terminator` is the highest-value arm** and is the moon's answer to "paint it in four
quadrants". Four sun rolls about the moon vector, scored by the centroid of the contribution
against the projected bright-limb direction. A mirrored disc scores **0 of 4**; a transposed
tangent basis **2 of 4**; always-light-screen-`+X` **1 of 4**; a phaseless disc fails the
displacement leg on all four. One roll cannot separate these.

**`moon-env` answers "how do you assert absence from a bake, from outside the process":** render
twice changing **one number**, `--sky-disc 0.53` vs `40`, with `--night-floor` on so the
env→IBL path is genuinely live. A 40° disc is 3% of the sphere; baked, it would dominate night
irradiance by orders of magnitude. The observable is the **terrain band at 0 px** while the sky
band moves and the probe's printed light intensity is unchanged. It catches the likeliest wrong
turn — putting the moon term in `sky_view_frag.glsl`, which is exactly where 11.80 put the
night floor.

**One open detail in `moon-shadow`, recorded rather than papered over.** `--no-key-light` is
what suppresses the shadow catcher (`render.c:3030-3046` — the same guard creates the sun light
*and* sets `scene->shadow_catcher`), and its help text reads "no analytic lights/shadows". The
moon is an analytic light, so it belongs **inside that guard** — which means `moon-shadow`
cannot use `--no-key-light` as its catcher lever. The existing `dir-shadow` arm never needed
one because its fixture authors its own light and no sky. Adding `mode: sky` to a copy of it
turns the catcher on. Whether the catcher disturbs the umbra read at `DIR_ERODE 0.75` is
unknown and must be **measured during implementation**; if it does, the arm needs a different
lever and that is a spec amendment, not a workaround.

**Not covered, said out loud:** the maria's *orientation* (procedural, so no ground truth to
mirror against — only `moon-det` holds it still); the absolute photometric level (a look
constant chosen by eye, like `SKY_NIGHT_FLOOR_RADIANCE`); a scene already at the three-caster
cap plus a moon (11.23's subject, no fixture authors three); the GUI's disabled-slider guards
(no headless arm reaches them).

Every `PLACE BY MEASUREMENT` bar follows the 11.80 procedure: **kill the twin first, record
both readings, put the bar at the midpoint, write both numbers into the comment** — before the
arm ever passes.

## As built

Seven commits. Every prediction held, and the design survived intact — phase derived
rather than authored, the disc in two shaders only, the light bounded by the night ramp.
What did not survive was some of my own gate work: **the falsification round caught one
arm claiming something it did not test**, which is the finding worth carrying out of this
spec.

### Predictions, scored

1. **27 goldens 0 px, no rebake — held**, at every one of the four steps, and better than
   allowed for: I budgeted PAE 1/255 of codegen drift for the two new background uniforms
   and got literal zero.
2. **stars / night-floor / cycle byte-identical — held.**
3. **The two extractions 0 px before anything built on them — held**, which is what made
   the rest safe.
4. **The moon costs nothing in daylight — held by construction**, via the `sky_night_factor`

### The one design change

**The moon light is created UNCONDITIONALLY, not gated on `moon_enabled`.** The plan
resolved this the other way, on the `sky.clouds.enabled` precedent, and that was wrong for
a reason the plan did not see: a config snapshot **cannot create a Light**, so a moon light
that exists only when the flag was passed makes `sky.moon` a row that restores a disc and
no light. Measured **139,768 px** against the session it claims to reproduce.

It is free when disabled, and structurally rather than hopefully: `shadow.c`'s
classification pass skips non-casting lights *before* it counts directionals, so the caster
count and every cascade layer are untouched, and the cluster's extra entry contributes
`radiance × 0 × NdotL`. The 27 goldens confirm it at 0 px.

### The arm that failed its own falsification

`moon-maria`'s docstring claimed the maria are "fixed to the FACE rather than to the sun".
Evaluating them in the terminator's own sun-relative frame — the natural wrong turn, since
that code already builds such a frame — **passed every arm in the group**. I had
implemented two of the arm's three legs and dropped the one that carried the claim.

Fixed by comparing the **albedo** (on ÷ off, which is what the maria multiply and therefore
independent of how brightly each roll is lit) at two sun rolls with the moon fixed.

Two corrections while placing its bar, both the same mistake wearing different clothes.
A `max` over ten thousand pixels read **0.127 on correct code** — quantization on a ratio
of two small codes near the terminator, shaped like a finding; it takes the mean now, over
pixels well clear of the terminator. And the first bar sat at 0.05 **with the mutation at
0.044** — a bar above both readings, which is no bar at all. Killed the twin first the
second time: **0.000 correct against 0.044 mutated, bar at 0.01.**

### Three things the gate work found the hard way

- **`--moon-elevation` implies `--moon`**, so a `--no-moon` twin that leads with the switch
  is re-armed by its own arguments and renders two identical frames. Every off leg was
  silently on. The switch goes last now.
- **A 40-degree diagnostic disc BLOOMS onto the terrain** — 15,978 px of it, which reads
  exactly like the disc having entered the bake. `--no-bloom` on every disc arm is
  load-bearing, not hygiene.
- **A full moon puts the sun at its exact antipode**, so the sun's elevation is not a free
  parameter beside the elongation. The plan's "pin the sun below −(half-angle)" was
  over-constrained; every arm derives the sun by ROLLING at the required elongation
  instead, and roll 180 is the one that keeps it lowest.

### A pre-existing sensitivity the moon exposed

`moon-config` renders with `--no-shadows`, and that is isolating rather than dodging. The
settings round-trip is **exact** — the two dumps compare field for field identical, and the
frames are 0 px. With shadows on the pair differs by **917 px lying entirely along the
terrain silhouette**: a restored session recomputes `camera.near_clip` from the
camera-to-target distance rather than restoring it (the snapshot deliberately does not carry
it, per its own documented omissions), and the cascade fit reads that.

**The moon did not cause this; it made it visible**, by giving `aerial_fixture` a caster it
never had at night — the sun does not cast below the horizon. It belongs to a config spec,
not this one, and is booked rather than fixed here.

### Look calibration

`SKY_MOON_LIGHT_FRACTION 0.04` and `SKY_MOON_DISC_RADIANCE 4.0`, frozen after A/B crops
with the user. Measured ground lift **0.0033** red over 81,638 px — the same order as the
entire night floor's 0.0025, so the moon is a real key light rather than a rounding error.

**The first calibration attempt was confounded and worth recording**: measured on
`apps/tree`, the moon appeared to make the ground *darker*. Tree touches exposure nowhere,
so auto-exposure was compensating for the light the moon added. Every look reading here is
on `aerial_fixture` with exposure pinned.

### Known-remaining

The sea in `apps/tree` still reads day-bright under a night sky — water's own shading does
not follow the night floor, and `water.c` prefers `sky->sun_light` whose radiance is a hard
zero at night, so the moon gets no glitter. Booked in the plan's out-of-scope list; it is
the moon's best image and deserves its own row rather than a footnote here.
