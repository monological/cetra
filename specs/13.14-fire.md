# Spec 13.14 -- fire

## Context

The user wants proper fire support in the engine, not an app-level effect. Spec 13.13 (silent's Gothic house, being built by another agent now) gives its castle fireplace a stand-in fire, made of emissive cards, embers, a flickering light and a crackle, and names 13.14 as where engine fire replaces it. **13.13 merges first, then this spec starts.**

The aim is fire as a Scene subsystem, like rain and water:
- a GPU combustion simulation, rendered as a glowing, absorbing volume;
- the light it casts derived from what it draws, so the room flickers with the flames;
- small procedural flames for candles;
- the fog, the depth buffer and the exposure all respected;
- a test fixture with gate arms, and silent's hearth and candles switched over at the end.

The user decided fire sound: the fire publishes how hard it is burning, and an app swells a recorded CC0 crackle with it. There is no new audio code in the engine.

## Research

### How Unreal and Godot do fire
- **Industry default, both engines: flipbook sprites.**
  - A fire is simulated offline (Houdini, or EmberGen, which is real time) and baked to a sprite sheet with motion vectors, emissive and six-way lighting.
  - The sheet plays on camera-facing particles, with motion-vector blending between frames.
  - The light is a separate point light with a scripted flicker:
    - Unreal: a Niagara light renderer.
    - Godot: an OmniLight3D flickered by a script.
  - **Strengths:** cheap, and fully art-directed.
  - **Weaknesses:**
    - It is flat close up and from above.
    - It cannot react to a firebox, wind or fog.
    - Its light does not follow its shape.
  - **This is what 13.13's stand-in is.**
- **Unreal 5: a live simulation.**
  - **Niagara Fluids** (plugin): Grid3D Gas emitters simulate density, temperature and velocity on a GPU grid. Fire is temperature mapped to a blackbody colour.
  - **Heterogeneous Volumes** ray-march the result, lit by the scene.
  - **Sparse Volume Textures** cache or import OpenVDB sims for playback.
  - **This spec takes this route:** a grid sim plus a volume march, on GL 4.1's fragment-shader passes.
- **Godot 4: no fluid sim.**
  - Fire is GPUParticles3D with flipbooks.
  - A FogVolume's emission adds a glow to the volumetric fog, but by Godot's own docs fog emission lights nothing else.

### Papers this spec uses

| Paper | What it gives this spec |
|---|---|
| Nguyen, Fedkiw & Jensen, *Physically Based Modeling and Animation of Fire* (SIGGRAPH 2002) | Buoyant combustion; **blackbody emission** from temperature; the **blue core** from the reaction zone; T⁴ radiative cooling. The base model. |
| Fedkiw, Stam & Jensen, *Visual Simulation of Smoke* (SIGGRAPH 2001) | Boussinesq buoyancy and **vorticity confinement**, which keeps the curls a coarse grid loses. |
| Stam, *Stable Fluids* (SIGGRAPH 1999); Selle et al., *An Unconditionally Stable MacCormack Method* (2008) | Semi-Lagrangian advection, then MacCormack for sharper detail at the same resolution. |
| Crane, Llamas & Tariq, *Real-Time Simulation and Rendering of 3D Fluids* (GPU Gems 3, ch. 30, 2007) | The whole recipe as rasterised 3D-texture passes with obstacles, from DX10 (before compute). It is the template for GL 4.1. |
| Pegoraro & Parker, *Physically-Based Realistic Fire Rendering* (EG NPH 2006) | Emission as soot absorption times Planck radiance (σₐ·B(T)), in absolute units. |
| Lamorlette & Foster, *Structural Modeling of Flames for a Production Environment* (SIGGRAPH 2002, Shrek) | Flames as **spines** with stochastic flicker and Kolmogorov noise: cheap and directable. This is the **candle** tier. |
| Horvath & Geiger, *Directable, High-Resolution Simulation of Fire on the GPU* (SIGGRAPH 2009) | A coarse 3D sim refined by view-aligned 2D slices. Kept in reserve if the grid's detail falls short. |
| Nielsen, Bojsen-Hansen, Stamatelos & Bridson, *Physics-Based Combustion Simulation* (ACM TOG 2022) | Modern: real fuel thermodynamics and adiabatic flame temperatures. A **source for default temperatures**, not its full solver. |
| Wrede et al., *Fire-X* (ACM TOG 2025, SIGGRAPH Asia) | Newest: stoichiometric heat release across fuels. Cited as the current frontier, but too heavy for real time. |
| Chadwick & James, *Animating Fire with Sound* (SIGGRAPH 2011) | Flame sound from the sim's heat release. Its premise, that sound follows heat release, is what the published `heat_release` is for. Its synthesis is not built. |

## Design decisions

1. **A live GPU grid simulation, not flipbooks.** Fields:
   - velocity;
   - temperature, fuel, soot and reaction, in one RGBA16F texture;
   - pressure and divergence, R32F, read by `texelFetch` only;
   - a static obstacle mask.

   The step, per GPU Gems 3 and Nguyen:
   - sources, then combustion;
   - buoyancy (Boussinesq);
   - vorticity confinement;
   - MacCormack advection;
   - divergence, Jacobi pressure (warm-started), projection;
   - T⁴ cooling.

   Combustion: fuel above ignition burns into heat and soot, and the burn rate is the reaction field. Flicker comes from the turbulence and seeded source noise. Nothing scripts it.
2. **GL 4.1 passes the engine already proves.**
   - **Writing:** 3D textures through `create_texture_3d_float` (`texture.c:134`), one slice per draw by `glFramebufferTextureLayer`, with a `sliceIndex` uniform: froxel's `draw_volume_slices` (`postfx.c:110`) and the aerial bake (`sky.c:513`).
   - **The slice helper:** `draw_fullscreen_quad_slices` is hoisted to `util.c` as `sky.c:506` proposes. Fire is the third caller.
   - **Ping-pong:** an index flip.
   - **GL state:** `_water_pass_begin/_end` (`water.c:2040`) is hoisted beside it to save and restore state.
   - **Programs:** `create_post_program` (`program.c:1280`), registered by `engine_find_program`, with the feature latching off if one is missing.
   - **If draw count dominates:** the water FFT's MRT layer blocks (`water.c:1430`), 8 slices per draw.
   - **Format risk:** R32F as a 3D target is unproven here, so slice 0 is checked for completeness, with a fallback to RGBA32F.
3. **Stepping is fixed-rate from the render clock.**
   - It runs at 60 Hz, capped at 4 steps a frame, after the water touch sim (`water.c:2846`).
   - The step INDEX comes from absolute `render_time` rather than an accumulator, so headless runs repeat exactly.
   - The tick runs in `engine_run` beside `water_update`/`rain_update` (`engine.c:3019`), before the shadow pass (`3160`), so a fire's light is final before shadows are drawn.
4. **Emission in absolute units.**
   - Planck's law is integrated against the CIE 1931 fit that water's caustics already use (`_water_cie_lobe`, `water.c:501`), hoisted to a shared `spectrum.c/h`. The result is XYZ, then linear Rec.709 in nits (683 lm/W), stored in a 1D LUT over temperature.
   - Emission is σₐ(soot)·B(T), plus a blue-core term from the reaction field.
   - **Unit check:** a blackbody at 2041.4 K is 60 cd/cm², the old candela definition.
5. **Drawn in the post chain's late draw, beside rain** (`engine.c:2288`, `postfx.c:3722`). That position:
   - is after TAA, so a volume with no motion vectors does not ghost;
   - is before DoF, metering and bloom, so the fire blooms and is exposed;
   - fogs itself at its own depth through `froxelSampleMedium` (`rain_frag.glsl:80`);
   - clips to the scene depth;
   - blends premultiplied;
   - lights its smoke from the clustered lights, as `rain_vert.glsl:95` does, so smoke is lit by its own fire.

   Known gaps, named in the spec: fire is not in SSR, refraction or probe captures. Its light carries it into GI and reflections instead.
6. **The light comes from the fire.**
   - Each frame a reduction sums, in fire-local space:
     - luminous intensity I = ∫σₐ·L_v(T) dV, in candela;
     - its centroid;
     - the emitted RGB;
     - the heat release.
   - These are read back through a fixed-latency PBO ring, latency 3, following `_rain_ask_pass` (`shadow.c:1900`) and `_water_probe_pass` (`water.c:2370`), so headless runs repeat.
   - `fire_drive_light(fire, light)`:
     - **point or spot:** intensity I, at the centroid;
     - **area:** luminance I/A, with the panel staying where it was authored;
     - colour from the emitted chromaticity.
   - The app chooses the type and whether it casts shadows: shadow layers are scarce, 8, and a point light takes 6.
   - The intensity treats the flame as optically thin, which is stated. The light is borrowed, the pattern the sky uses for `sun_light`.
7. **Candles are structural flames, not grids** (Lamorlette & Foster):
   - a CPU spine of about 8 points, stepped at the same fixed rate;
   - buoyancy, a damped follow, seeded flicker events and the scene's wind;
   - a teardrop radius profile, cooler at the edges, with a blue base;
   - drawn by the same march evaluating the field analytically from a uniform array, so many candles cost almost nothing;
   - the light intensity from the same profile, by CPU quadrature.
8. **Heat shimmer is OFF by default.** When on, the march samples the late draw's mipped behind-copy (`rain_render.c:143`) offset by the projected temperature gradient, as rain's drops refract it.
9. **Ownership follows rain.**
   - **`scene->fire`, a `FireSystem`:**
     - owned and freed by the scene;
     - up to `FIRE_MAX` fires;
     - each a GRID or a FLAME;
     - no GL in `fire.c`.
   - **`engine->fire_renderer`:** engine-owned, created lazily, holding all the GL.
   - **Off is free:** with no fire block, nothing is allocated and nothing runs, and all 31 goldens stay at 0 px.

  - logs;
  - fog and the scene's wind;
  - a driven point light.

  It tests the smoke plume, wind tilt, fog glow, and fog at the fire's own depth.
- **Twin scenes:**
  - no fire block, against zero sources;
  - wind off and on;
  - obstacles removed;
  - a wall placed in front of the fire.

  - `--fire`/`--no-fire`;

## As built

### Phases 0 to 2, and the parts of 3 and 4 that came with them

**Not committed phase by phase, which the plan required.** Phases 1 and 2 and parts of 3 and 4
were written together before anything after the spec was committed: the blackbody table lives in
`fire.c` and its probe in the render app, so phase 1 only became testable once the fire system
existed, and rather than make it stand alone the work ran on to a first frame. The spectrum
refactor was committed alone (6c29580a) once that was noticed; everything below landed as one
commit. From here each phase ends in its own commit.

- **Phase 0.** The worktree came off master before 13.13 merged, at the user's request; it
  rebases onto master when 13.13 lands. The papers commit also changed `docs/papers/`'s policy:
  the repository is public, so converted texts stay local unless openly licensed (Fire-X is
  CC BY 4.0 and is committed), and the four papers committed before were taken out of git.
- **Phase 1.** `spectrum.c/h` holds the CIE fit (moved from `water.c`, whose gates pass
  unchanged), Planck's law and the blackbody integral. `fire.c` builds the 256-texel table
  both halves read, chromaticity plus log10 luminance, and the shader interpolates it by
  `texelFetch` in fire.c's arithmetic. Measured: 2041.4 K gives 5.98e5 cd/m² (the old candela
  definition's 6.0e5); 2856 K gives x 0.4464, y 0.4085 against Illuminant A's 0.4476, 0.4075;
  6500 K gives 0.3135, 0.3237. The `fire-blackbody` arm is not written yet.
- **Phase 2, and one change from the design.** Each grid is a **flat 2D atlas of its z
  slices**, not a 3D texture written a slice per draw (decision 2): at about 32 passes a step
  and 32-64 slices a pass, the per-slice idiom is over a thousand draws and render passes a step
  on this driver, where the atlas makes every pass one draw. Trilinear reads are two bilinear
  taps, held half a texel inside the tile. So `draw_fullscreen_quad_slices` was not hoisted:
  fire is not a third caller. The pass bracket was (`gl_pass_begin/_end`).
  - **The pressure solve uses the wide Laplacian**, the divergence of the central-difference
    gradient. With the compact 7-point stencil the projection cut the divergence from 14 to 9
    (RMS) and could not do better however long it ran; with the consistent one, 14 to 1.3 after
    24 warm-started Jacobi iterations.
  - **Open faces are zero-gradient**, not the wind: as a fixed velocity they were walls of
    still air the plume could not leave through.
  - The readback ring and the driven lights (phase 4) are built and work: the GPU's sum of
    what a grid casts matches the CPU's over the same read-back field to six digits.
- **Phase 3, the first look: wrong.** The first frame was a blue-violet, grainy curtain filling
  the firebox, at about 10 fps windowed. The user's verdict: it looks like a Bunsen burner. The
  code had been written from memory of the papers rather than from them, with every constant
  invented. Read afterwards, Nguyen et al. 2002 contradicts it three ways:
  - the blue core is a thin sheet at the reaction front (§3.1), where the code glowed blue
    wherever fuel burned;
  - temperature follows a reaction coordinate, hot just past the front and cooling as T⁴
    (§4.3), where the code burnt fuel slowly everywhere and peaked at 1270 K;
  - fire only looks yellow-white because the eye adapts to it, and Nguyen renders through a
    von Kries transform to the white of the fire's hottest blackbody (§5, eq. 23); without
    it, a blackbody fire is deep red, and red plus the blue reads violet.

  The grain is the march's per-pixel dither drawn after TAA, where nothing averages it, and the
  vertical streaks are fuel injected with fresh white noise every cell every step.

### Phase 3, the second pass: Nguyen's model, read this time

- **The scalars are now Nguyen's**: temperature rise, the reaction coordinate Y, soot, and the
  blue core's weight. A source alight sets Y to 1 and the gas to the peak temperature (the
  paper's shortcut for large flames, sec. 4.3); Y falls at k = 1/s (eq. 16, and GPU Gems 3's
  fire); the gas cools by eq. 17's fourth power, solved exactly over the step; the blue core is
  the gas within `core` seconds of the front (sec. 3.1); soot forms in the reacting gas after
  it. The expansion across the front (sec. 3.2) is asked of the pressure solve as a divergence in
  the core.
- **Adaptation** is eq. 23's von Kries transform in Hunt-Pointer-Estevez cone space, from the
  white of a blackbody at the peak temperature to D65, mixed with the identity by `adaptation`
  (0.85, TASTE) and normalised to keep the white's luminance; the driven light takes the same
  colour. **It only works unclamped**: below about 1900 K a blackbody's blue is negative in
  Rec.709, and the first version clamped the table, so the peak came out (0.60, 0.25, 1) instead
  of white and the whole fire read violet. The table, the blue core's colour and every sum stay
  unclamped until after the adaptation.
- **The blue core's colour is its spectrum**: CH* at 431 nm and C2*'s Swan band at 516 nm through
  the observer, where the first version had an invented RGB. Against a white as red as 1400 K's
  the adaptation lifts blue about twentyfold, so its strength is now faint (TASTE).
- **No dither**, a 0.4-cell march step instead; the source noise is a lattice four cells and
  0.15 s across, smoothly interpolated, in place of white noise per cell per step.
- **The smoke box.** Smoke spilling from the mouth rose up the breast inside the 20 cm of box in
  front of it and stopped dead at the box's faces, which drew as a hard-edged rectangle. Two
  fixes: a chimney's `draft` (the flue's air driven up at 1.2 m/s, so incompressibility draws the
  room's air in through the mouth), and the march thinning everything to nothing over the last
  three cells before an open face.
- Measured on the fixture at frame 160: the hearth casts 24 cd, the candle 1-2 cd (a real candle
  is about 1), and both lights come out warm. **Still wrong**: the flames break into blocky,
  disconnected patches. The visible flame is only the gas within about a tenth of the peak
  temperature (emission falls about twelvefold for each tenth below it), which on 2.5 cm cells is
  a cell or two thick, so the grid's own blocks show.

## Revision: flipbooks baked from our own simulation

**Decided with the user after phase 3's second pass**, against references of Unreal Niagara
fireplace fire (Goat Horse Studio's *Modern Fireplaces*): a dense, bright fire of crisp
yellow-white tongues orange at their edges over glowing logs, and gas burners of curling tips.
That is the industry's flipbook technique, which decision 1 refused as "flat close up" -- and it is
the look wanted. A live 44x64x32 grid at 2.5 cm cannot resolve flames that crisp, and one fine
enough costs several frame budgets; the live grid also cost 3-4 ms a frame in a window.

**What changes.** A fire is DRAWN from a flipbook -- a sheet of frames baked offline, played on a
few camera-facing cards with motion-vector frame blending -- and the light it drives comes from
the brightness baked with each frame. The bake is OURS, from the same solver and the same
blackbody and adaptation, run offline at high resolution, so every pixel is owned and nothing is
licensed. The live grid is kept, as the bake tool and as a fire kind for fires that must react
(wind, a box's walls); the candle's structural flame is kept as it is.

**Kept unchanged**: spectrum and blackbody, adaptation, the fire system and its scene and engine
plumbing, the `.cscn` block, the flags and the probe, `fire_drive_light`, the candle, the fixture
room. **Repurposed**: the grid simulation and its march, which render the baked frames.

### Revised design

- **The bake** (`--fire-bake <dir>` on the render app, with a bake scene whose first fire is a
  GRID fire). After the warmup, every `stride` steps the fire's state is marched orthographically
  from the front into an RGBA32F frame -- emission in absolute nits, adapted and clamped,
  premultiplied by coverage -- with a second target holding the gas's emission-weighted
  screen-space velocity for frame blending, and the frame's intensity, centroid and colour from a
  synchronous reduction. Raw floats are written to `<dir>`; no fog, no scene lights, no
  pre-exposure in a bake.
- **The encode** (`tools/bake_fire_flipbook.py`, numpy and PIL, deterministic): `frames + loop`
  frames are crossfaded into a seamless loop of `frames`, packed into a sheet, colour normalised
  by the sheet's peak and sRGB-encoded in RGBA8 PNG with alpha linear, motion encoded in a second
  PNG, and a JSON sidecar written with the layout, the fps, the peak in nits, the bake box, and
  every frame's intensity, centroid height and colour. The committed asset is the two PNGs and the
  JSON; the script reruns the bake and reproduces them.
- **The grid's caps rise** to 128 x 256 x 128 for bakes; a live fire keeps its coarse cell.
- **A FLIPBOOK fire kind**: a flipbook asset and up to `FIRE_MAX_CARDS` cards (centre, width,
  height, phase), each a quad turned about the vertical to face the camera, sampling the sheet at
  the fire's clock with two frames blended along their motion vectors, faded softly where it meets
  geometry, fogged at its own depth, in the late draw. The light: the baked intensity at each
  card's frame, scaled by the card's area against the bake's, summed; the baked colour; the cards'
  centroid.
- **Embers**: a fire may drive a material's emissive strength (`embers`), flickering with its
  intensity about the material's authored strength -- logs that glow and pulse under the flames,
  for one uniform. The fixture's logs get a procedural crack texture as their emissive map.

### Revised phases (from phase 3 on)

3. **Done in part**: the live grid's rendering, fixture and look passes above.
3b. **The bake**: caps, `--fire-bake`, the ortho march, motion and the synchronous reduction, the
   raw output, `tools/bake_fire_flipbook.py`, a bake scene (`fire_bake_logs.cscn`) and the
   committed flipbook. **Look**: the sheet itself.
3c. **The flipbook kind**: `.cscn` keys, loading, the card renderer with motion blending, soft
   depth, fog; the light from the baked frames; embers. The fixture's hearth moves to it.
   **Look**: the fixture against the references. Arms: bake determinism (two bakes byte-identical),
   the loop seam, fire-off, runtime determinism, depth occlusion, the light against the table.
4. **The light** for the live grid stays as built (readback ring); its arms land here.
5. **Outdoors**: a campfire flipbook, and a live-grid campfire in wind.
6. **Candles**: arms for the structural flame.
7. **Heat shimmer**: dropped unless the look asks for it, and said so.
8. **Session and docs**, as planned. 9. **Silent**, as planned, with flipbook fires.

## As built: phases 3b and 3c

**Again not committed per phase**: both phases sat uncommitted through three changes of where the
sheet comes from, and landed together as `515f127e` (the player, the solver changes, the fixture,
the two sheet tools) and `e152c2e2` (the Blender bake).

**3c, the flipbook kind, is built as designed** and drew its first sheet in the fixture. Cards turn
about the vertical, sort back to front with the marched fires in one list, fade into geometry over
8 cm, fog at their own depth, and blend two frames, along the motion sheet when there is one.
`fire_card_frame` is the one statement of where a card is in its loop, read by the light and the
draw alike. The `.cscn` takes `flipbook` (resolved against the scene file like an IES profile, since
the sheets are read straight from disk and never through the pool), `cards` and `embers` (a
material by name, whose emissive strength follows the fire's intensity about its authored value).
The fixture writes three scenes: the flipbook hearth, its GRID twin, and the bake scene.

**3b, the bake from our own solver, was built and did not give the look.** The ortho march, the
motion target and the encoder work; the fire they baked read as a gas burner, not wood. Two solver
changes from it are kept: the Jacobi solve back on the compact stencil, since the wide one
checkerboarded at bake resolution, and entrainment, which cools a plume that otherwise stayed hot
to the top of the box. Its sheets (`fire_logs_*`) are not committed.

**Filmed fire was tried next**, keyed off its background by its green channel
(`tools/fire_flipbook_from_video.py`, a CC BY 3.0 clip, credited in the sidecar). The flames came
out real, but the frame carried the burning logs and coals under them: no key parts a flame from
the glowing log in front of it. A flipbook must be flames only, with the scene's own logs hiding
where they were cut. Not committed.

**The sheet is now baked in Blender** (`tools/bake_fire_blender.py`), after the way a log fire is
built in Mantaflow: a fuel source around each log, the logs as collision effectors the flames curl
round, the smoke kept and absorbing, noise upres, the run at three quarters of real time.
Measured along the way, each a trap that renders a plausible frame or none:
- `surface_distance` is in domain CELLS. Set in metres, nothing is emitted and the bake is empty.
- Fuel from separate small emitters burns as a row of blobs with a tongue on each; rejected.
- A fuel texture reaches the emission (zeroed, the fire never lights) but at contrast alone most
  of the bark still burns and the patches merge into one sheet. Biased so only a quarter of the
  pattern gives fuel, the tongues part at 128 cells.
- ffmpeg's EXR reader clamps to 1, so frames leave Blender as AgX PNGs, which is also what a
  camera makes of a fire.
- **Blender 5's default null-scattering volumes scatter white points through an emission-heavy
  volume, and more samples only sharpen them** (Blender issue 146053). The denoiser, scattered
  light, cubic interpolation, the temperature attribute and a clamp were each tested and are
  innocent; `volume_biased`, the ray marching of 4.x, removes them.
- A few dots remain near the base under ray marching, with every baked field in range and a finer
  step not helping. Under test: the fuel source in the crevice between the logs.

**Not done**: the committed sheet, the look signed off, the exposure (−2 is too dark and murky),
and every arm in 3c's list.

## Review cleanup

Seven reviewers (three thermo-nuclear by layer, four `/simplify` angles) read the branch against
master. Taken, in order, a commit a step: the deletions; the consolidation inside fire's own code
with the bugs they found; a fire hung on a scene node (its origin the node's world position, the
light it drives placed in that light's node's frame, ticked after the transform walk); per-kind
state in `Fire`, with a flipbook off the step clock and the GL file split into simulation and
compositing. Deferred, because each reaches into water, shadow or rain: a shared readback ring,
and the late-draw fog binding and cluster-light walk that rain and fire both carry. Not taken
yet: white balance moved from the fire's emission to the eye (it changes the look, which is not
signed off), and a staggered velocity grid (a solver rewrite worth a phase of its own).

**Phase 1, the deletions, 0 px on both fixtures.** The in-engine bake (`--fire-bake*`, the
march's bake mode and second output, its file writer, `tools/bake_fire_flipbook.py`,
`fire_bake_logs.cscn`) and the grid caps it had raised fourfold; the motion sheet, which only
that bake produced; `shimmer`, plumbed through five places and read by none;
`fire_system_find`, `fire_flame_field` and `spectrum_blackbody_rec709`, which nothing called; the
unread `clock_step` and `checked`. The probe's GPU half is called through the internal
`fire_render.h` now, rather than a public wrapper in a header that promises no GL.
