# Whitecaps that break instead of blobbing

## Context

Deep-water crest foam in `water_frag.glsl` reads as fast-moving, hard-edged, near-opaque white
blobs on the swell. Reported from `apps/tree`; visible in any FFT frame with open water.

Three reference renderers were read in full (`research/water/{gpuocean,poseidon,inkwell-webgpu-water}`).
The principle common to all three and missing from ours: **compression never selects alone** —
each multiplies it by a second physical quantity (crest height, slope, or a stricter second
channel). Ours is also the most opaque composite of the four (0.88 vs inkwell's 0.145–0.22).

We already have the `foamTex` accumulator (11.42), the ridged-web erosion breakup (11.45 P1 —
gpuocean's own technique), wrapped-diffuse foam lighting and a bubble-thickness term. The gap
is narrower than it first looked.

**Outcome:** whitecaps that sit on real breaking crests, move at the speed of the swell rather
than of capillary waves, and read as a slick with sea showing through — while the surf line
stays dense whitewater.

## What the evidence changed about this plan

Four verified findings reordered the work. They are here because the obvious plan — "raise the
threshold to cut coverage" — is probably **wrong**.

**(a) Coverage is likely already right.** `gates.py:3538` records that *"the default 11.5 m/s
state folds rarely enough that the signal is a few per cent"*. Monahan & O'Muircheartaigh give
`W = 2.95e-6 * U10^3.52` = **1.60%** at that wind speed. Those agree. So the complaint is about
**contrast, edge and motion**, not area, and raising the onset may simply make the sea too
clean. **Measure before changing any threshold.**

**(b) The "fast-moving" symptom is the short cascade.** Verified: cascade 2 is a 12 m tile over
k = 1.22–24 rad/m (`water.c:51`), i.e. waves 0.26–5.15 m with deep-water phase speeds
**0.64–2.84 m/s** — and it is re-selected every frame at the *displaced* position. Everything
else in the foam path is slow: the breakup pattern drifts at 0.12 m/s and the accumulator at
0.35 m/s. The short band is 2–8x faster than either and it votes on where foam is.

**(c) The accumulator is not mipped.** Verified: `_water_make_foam_target` creates it
`GL_LINEAR` with no chain and no `glGenerateMipmap` anywhere, and `water_frag.glsl:587-589`
reads it with `texture()`. At the horizon one pixel covers dozens of the 128² texels and takes
one bilinear tap. That is aliasing, and its temporal signature is crawling — the other half of
"fast-moving".

**(d) Raising the onset silently shortens foam lifetime.** The leak is *linear*
(`water_foam_frag.glsl:92`), so a stored fold stops clearing the onset at `turb = 1 - ON`.
Verified arithmetic at `foam_decay` 0.4/s: a fold to turb 0.15 lives **1.7 s** at ON 0.16 and
**1.1 s** at ON 0.40. Coverage is birth rate x lifetime, so raising ON cuts coverage *twice*
and drops `water-foam-persist`'s ratio. `foam_decay` must move with ON, derived from a stated
lifetime rather than dialled.

## The work, in the order the evidence justifies

### S0 — the instrument, and the baseline (no look change)

Coverage cannot be measured off the composited frame: the classifier `gates.py:4133` thresholds
on luma and R/B, so a measurement of coverage would move whenever the opacity being calibrated
moves. An instrument whose sensitivity depends on the constant under test is not one.

- **`--water-foam-debug N`** in the idiom of `--water-fft-probe`: `Water.foam_debug`, one
  uniform, an early-out in `water_frag` writing a **binary** mask (every enabled draw-buffer
  location still written — the file states that contract at `:19-22`). `1` = the crest band
  after erosion, `2` = before, so the erosion's own pass rate is a difference of two numbers.
  The shore band is deliberately excluded: it is calibrated against a swash, not against Monahan.
- **Measure at NADIR.** Looking straight down, a pinhole images a plane affinely: the `1/cos^3`
  of plane foreshortening cancels the `cos^3` of perspective solid angle, so **pixel fraction is
  areal fraction exactly**, with no box placement and no weighting. At any oblique framing it is
  not. Eye at 300 m over a ~3.7 m Hs sea keeps the flat-plane error at 0.3%.
  `oceanProjectedPosition` supports nadir explicitly (`ocean.glsl:190-193`).
- **Record the baseline** at 8 / 11.5 / 20 m/s, 90 frames, both modes, against Monahan's
  0.45% / 1.60% / 11.2%. Six numbers. This is the commit that makes every later claim
  measurable, and it decides whether S3 happens at all.

*Note on measurement discipline:* three coverage measurements were botched earlier in this work
by sampling sky rows and the surf zone, and by forgetting the framebuffer is **2x** the
requested size on this display. Crop the band and look at it before trusting a number.

### S1 — the short cascade stops selecting foam

Drop `max(0.0, 1.0 - shortJ) * fade * 0.62` from both `compression` and `persistent`, and the
`short0` fetch that exists only to feed it. The short band keeps its real jobs: perturbing the
normal (`:538`) and handing filtered slope to roughness.

Per (b) this is the largest single win on the reported symptom, and poseidon excludes its finest
cascade for the same reason in nearly the same words. **Land it alone and measure**, so the look
change is attributable.

It also removes the only `fade`-weighted term in the selection, which makes the `:594-603`
comment moot rather than contradicted — rewrite it to say so and keep the artefact it records.

### S2 — the accumulator is filtered, not faded

Reframes the reference renderers' distance culls (gpuocean 80 m, inkwell 95–188 m) as what this
subsystem already calls its own doctrine: *"the far field is a filtering problem"*
(`ocean.glsl:466`).

- `_water_make_foam_target`: `GL_LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR` + `glGenerateMipmap` at creation, per the
  completeness policy at `water.c:930-932`. (Note: the `mipped` parameter was just removed from
  this function as dead; it comes back deliberately, with the reason.)
- One `glGenerateMipmap` after the foam draw, once the FBO is unbound.
- Read with **`textureGrad`**, gradients from `dFdx(SurfParam)/cascadeLength[b]` — never the
  implicit derivative, because `oceanCascadeUv` wraps with `fract` and would read a whole period
  across every tile seam. This file already makes that argument twice.

The far field then converges to the trail's areal mean — a faint uniform brightening at the
horizon rather than a speckle band or a straight line.

*Known second-order error, to record not hide:* mipping averages the Jacobian minimum and the
shaping is applied after, so by Jensen the far field slightly **under**-reports coverage. That
errs toward less foam at the horizon, which is the safe direction against the artefact the
`:594-603` comment names. The exact fix (per-band coverage in a second attachment, ~128 KB) is
recorded as the rejected alternative.

### S3 — the window, only if S0 says so

`WATER_FOAM_ON` / `FULL` / a new `WATER_FOAM_SHAPE` power. **Conditional on the S0 baseline.**
If coverage already sits near 1.6%, keep ON near its current value and let `SHAPE` do the work —
a linear ramp reads the middle of the window as half-covered sea, and a sea is not half-covered
anywhere between calm and breaking.

If ON does move, `foam_decay` moves with it, derived per (d) from a **3.5 s** lifetime — the
number `SHORE_FOAM_LIFE` already gives foam on the sand. The sea and the beach should not
disagree about how long whitewater lasts.

### S4 — a second gate: crest height

The principle all three references share. Two halves, and the split is the point:

- **Birth, in `water_foam_frag.glsl`:** gate each band's fold record by that band's own
  elevation against its own RMS. Costs no fetch — `.b` of the target-0 texel the pass already
  reads *is* that band's height in metres. Per band because the three channels live in three
  tiling spaces, so one texel index is a different world position in each and there is no total
  elevation to ask about there.
- **Instantaneous, in `water_frag.glsl`:** gate by the assembled `WorldPos.y - waterLevel`,
  which the surface stage does have.
- **The trail read back is NOT gated.** Foam outlives the crest that made it and slides into the
  trough; gating the read would delete exactly the memory the accumulator exists for, and would
  pulse at the wave period.

Normalised in units of the sea's own RMS, not metres — a metre threshold is a threshold on one
sea state. New uniform `cascadeHeightRms[3]` from the existing `cascade_height_var[]`
(`water.h:353`), which today has one reader. **Not** `waterSurfHeight`: it is zeroed by the
`surf` switch, and `water-shoal` runs both its frames under `--no-water-surf`.

Fix while there: `water.h:340` claims that field "has ONE reader" and `:345` calls it "world
units squared". It is **metres squared** — the seeding is entirely SI and `ocean.glsl:610`
converts only at the last line. That stale unit claim is the class of bug 11.44 existed to fix.

### S5 — split the composite opacity

A swash is a decimetre of aerated water; a whitecap is a slick of bubbles millimetres thick with
sea showing through. One opacity cannot serve both.

Two mixes rather than one blended weight — crest first, shore second so dense whitewater wins
where both are present. `WATER_CREST_FOAM_MAX` ≈ 0.30 and `WATER_CREST_FOAM_COLOR` ≈
`(0.88,0.92,0.94)`; `WATER_SHORE_FOAM_MAX` keeps today's 0.88 and `WATER_FOAM_COLOR` its 0.95.
`foam` survives as the union so the bubble relief and branch guard are unchanged.

**The containment property:** where `crestFoam` is 0 the first mix is the identity and the
second is character-for-character the old expression. Both water goldens are Gerstner with no
bed and carry no foam at all, so they must stay 0 px — if either moves, something leaked out of
the spectral branch.

*Risk:* `WATER_FOAM_COLOR` 0.95 is load-bearing — `:194-204` records it was raised because a
swash over pale sand was invisible below the beach's own 0.93 albedo. The crest colour is a
separate constant precisely so that fix is not undone.

## Risks and falsifiers

- **Coverage is already right** (likely, per (a)). *Falsified by:* the S0 baseline. *Response:*
  S3 shrinks to the shaping term; S1, S2 and S5 carry the look.
- **The crest gate cuts almost nothing** — choppy compression already peaks at crests, so the
  two gates may be strongly correlated. *Falsified by:* coverage barely moving across S4.
  *Response:* a real finding; the gate then earns its place only for trough folds.
- **The erosion over-fragments crest foam into dots.** At an amount near 0.3 the bar sits at 0.71
  against a pattern whose median is 0.436 — roughly an 8% pass rate, and poseidon warns that
  carving this hard gives dots. *Falsified by:* mode-1 ÷ mode-2 below ~0.2 plus a speckled frame.
  *Response:* give `waterErodeFoam` `hi`/`span` parameters so one law serves two calibration
  points. **Do not** adopt the recorded `HI 0.564 / SPAN 0.464` alternative — it would take
  *shore* foam at rest from 10% to 50%, an uncommissioned change to the surf.
- **Mipping makes the horizon a flat grey wash.** *Falsified by:* per-row coverage going flat
  *and* the horizon reading as haze. *Response:* the per-band coverage attachment.
- **TAA ghosting** if the crest gate flickers at the wave period. The birth/trail split is what
  avoids it; if ghosting appears after S4, the gate has leaked onto the trail.
- **Weak automated cover.** The only instrument that sees this is `water-foam-persist`, and it is
  being restated in the same change. The A/B carries more weight than usual here.
