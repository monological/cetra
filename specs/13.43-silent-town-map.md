# Spec 13.43: silent's town map

## Context

The user wants silent's first navigation aid: a street map of the town, hanging on the kitchen
fridge, that the player takes, carries in the backpack and opens with M. It is drawn like The Last
of Us Part II's Seattle map: a printed city map with blue-ink hand-drawn marks added as the player
finds things, plus a red ink arrow showing where they stand and which way they face.

**The behaviour asked for:**
- **On the fridge.** E on the map:
  - without the backpack, the player thinks "I don't have anywhere to put this. Maybe I should find
    a backpack or something." and the map stays;
  - with the backpack (spec 13.40), the map goes into the bag.
- **Opening it.** M opens it. In the backpack screen the map's line says M opens it, and M opens
  the map over the bag; closing the map returns to the bag.
- **Printed from the start:** the town, the mansion (a landmark), the lake.
- **Drawn as it is found:**
  - reaching a road out of town that cannot be followed puts an X there, and the player thinks "Hmm,
    can't go that way. Let me mark that on the map.";
  - reaching the lake sketches the cabin on, and the player thinks "There's a cabin here. Let me go
    check it out."

**Decided with the user:**
- A find made before the map is had is remembered: its thought leaves the map out, and the mark is
  on the map once it is taken.
- The marker is a facing arrow.
- From the bag, M opens the map over it.
- Names are invented, not "Kingsbridge", and kept in the spec to change.

**Where things stand.** Master has 13.40 (backpack) and 13.41 (lake and cabin). 13.42, faster GI
captures, is in flight in another worktree and touches `silent.c`: it moves the spawn to (4.5, 13.3)
and adds flags. Whichever merges second rebases.

## Phase 1: the printed map, drawn from the game's own plan

### The export, so nothing is copied into Python by hand
- **`--map-export PATH`** in `silent.c`:
  - implies `--headless --frames 1 --no-cat --no-loading-screen`;
  - writes the town's plan in `on_init` right after `fences_build`, where `StreetPlots` (the seeded
    house footprints) is still in scope.
- **Writer: `town_plan.c/h`** (new), `town_plan_write(path, plots, seed)`. Plain text, one record a
  line, metres to 3 decimals, x east, z south, in a fixed order so a re-export makes no diff:
  - boxes: asphalt, sidewalks, lots, houses (the home flagged), the terrace wall, the cross street,
    the grounds, the mansion, the graveyard, the cabin pad, the cabin;
  - the tower polygon;
  - lines: the dock, the drive, the track, the lip and ridge lip, the barricades;
  - the shore polygon;
  - the place anchors;
  - a height grid and a cover grid (ground, water, chasm, woods) at 2 m.
- **Public samplers**, so the writer reads the code's own shapes:
  - `lake_shore_point` (wraps the static `shore_at`);
  - `lake_track_point` (mirrors `hill_drive_point`);
  - `land_over_chasm`;
  - `woods_cover`.
- **Moves into `layout.h`:** the graveyard box (`grounds.c:117`) and the dock (`lake.c:206`). These
  are pure moves; every kit's printed vertex count must stay unchanged.
- **Committed:** `apps/silent/tools/town_plan.txt`. The tool reruns without building silent, and a
  town change shows as a text diff.

### The tool: `apps/silent/tools/make_map.py` (new)
Reuses `noise`, `age_paper`, `place` and `write_header` from `make_cards.py`, `fetch` from
`fetch_textures.py`, and `make_reeds.stroke` generalised into a tapered ballpoint stroke.

- **The print**, 2048×1536, drawn at 2× and reduced with LANCZOS:
  1. cream paper;
  2. woods in green with stamped tree symbols;
  3. faint contours from the height grid;
  4. the Notch hatched, with cliff ticks;
  5. the lake in blue with water lines;
  6. tan lots, property lines, darker house footprints;
  7. the grounds, Harlow House as a landmark glyph, the graveyard with † marks;
  8. roads: a casing, then a cream fill. Harlow Drive is buffered along its spline; Lake Road has a
     dashed casing;
  9. printed labels, the A–H / 1–6 margin grid;
  10. a title block in the empty south-east corner: MERROW, STREET MAP, a legend, a scale bar, a
      north arrow;
  11. ageing: fold creases on a 4×3 panel grid, worn edges, mottle.
- **Fonts:** IM Fell English (titles), a sans for labels, Reenie Beanie for ink. All are OFL,
  fetched and cached, never committed.
- **The marks atlas**, 1024² RGBA, in print pixels, with 8 px transparent gutters:
  - the barricade X with "road closed — police";
  - the road-end X with "road's gone, no way across";
  - the cabin sketch (gable and chimney) with "cabin — fire's lit?";
  - the red facing arrow in 64 frames, the same jittered polyline turned each time.

  The blue is about (30, 50, 140). Each X is gone over twice.
- **The folded-map set**, 512²: the cover (a period street-map cover) and the back (a crop of the
  print) side by side, with a flat normal and a small roughness map. Stored bottom-row-first like
  every world texture.
- **Outputs:**

  | File | Notes | Budget |
  |---|---|---|
  | `assets/textures/silent/ui_town_map.png` | RGB, top row first, display values | ≤ 1.5 MB |
  | `assets/textures/silent/ui_town_map_marks.png` | RGBA, top row first | ≤ 250 KB |
  | `assets/textures/silent/town_map_folded_{albedo,normal,rough}.png` | as the other sets | ≤ 400 KB |
  | `apps/silent/src/map_art.h` | generated; do not edit | — |

  `map_art.h` holds:
  - the world box ↔ print frame;
  - each mark's UVs (V down), size, anchor pixel and world point;
  - the arrow's grid;
  - the folded faces' UVs (V up);
  - `TOWN_MAP_SEED`;
  - the names.
- **Deterministic:** one fixed-seed RNG and pinned font URLs. The tool prints a sha256 per output,
  which the spec records.

**Look:** the print, the marks sheet, the folded cover, and a debug overlay of the raw plan in red
over the print (`out/town_map_debug.png`, not committed). **The style is iterated with the user here
before phase 2.**

## Folded in after phase 1: room to grow

Asked whether this design constrains a bigger world, the answer was that generating the map from
the plan is what makes growth cheap, and that two choices would be expensive to undo once phases 2
and 3 are built round them. Both are taken now:

- **The map is an ID, not an item of its own.** `MapId { MAP_TOWN, MAP_COUNT }` (`town_map.h`).
  `map_art.h` is generated as one `MapArt` row per map: its print and marks pictures, the frame
  (world box ↔ print pixels), its marks and the arrow's grid. The bag's item carries a map ID; each
  place in `PLACES` names the map it is marked on; the map screen opens a `MapId`. A second map (the
  county, a floor plan) is a second row and a second run of a tool, not new code paths.
- **The map screen is a view onto the print, not the print fitted to the window.** It has a pan
  and a zoom. At zoom 1 the whole sheet fits, which at today's size is the screen as first planned.
  Zoomed in, the view opens centred on the arrow and is clamped to the sheet; the mouse drags it,
  WASD and the left stick move it, and the wheel and the pad's triggers zoom between 1 and 3. The
  print is drawn through `ui_draw_textured_quad_uv` as the visible part of the sheet, and the
  marks and the arrow through the same transform, clipped to the paper. So a larger map later is a
  larger print at the same pixels a metre, with nothing in the screen to change.

Left for when a second road or area is added, since nothing is spent waiting: exporting roads,
buildings and areas as generic records the tool draws without per-feature code, and moving the
label anchors and the printed-only features (Gorge Bridge, the road to Route 9) out of the tool into
layout tables beside what they name.

## Folded in after phase 1: the map you notice, and ink you watch go on

Decided with the user while phase 2 began.

**The map on the fridge has to read as something to take**, not one more thing stuck to the door:
- **Where:** on the freezer door at eye level, in the middle. The lake photo moves down to the lower
  door to make room.
- **Its shape:** it hangs half open from a magnet at its top. The cover panel is held flat on the
  door, and the panel under it sags out about 20 degrees, showing the print. It has a thickness
  and a silhouette, where every photo and note on the door is a flat card.
- **Its cover:** a proper printed cover, like an old gas-station road map, not a crop of the
  print:
  - a deep red band with PALE / RIDGE in white;
  - STREET MAP and "and Visitors' Guide" under it;
  - the manor's drawing;
  - a glossy coated finish that catches the kitchen's light, worn white at its edges and
    corners.

  Everything else on the door is faded and matte.
- **Kept in reserve:** a pulse on it while it is the thing in reach. It would be the only such cue
  in silent; added only if walking it shows the map still gets missed.

**A find's mark is written on stroke by stroke** the first time the map shows it, rather than
faded in. No engine change past the planned UV quad:
- **The tool records when the pen reaches each pixel.** Each mark is drawn as strokes, at a pen
  speed and with a pause between strokes; handwriting sweeps along its line, a letter's time
  apart. The marks picture keeps that time in red and the ink in alpha; the ink's colour is the
  quad's tint, so one picture holds the blue marks and the red arrow. `map_art.h` gains each
  mark's `write_seconds` and the scale its red channel is in.
- **The map screen draws a mark through `map_ink`**, an app UI program set by
  `ui_set_draw_program`. The `uFocus` that call passes is the seconds since that mark began. A
  pixel shows once that time has passed its own, with a soft front about the pen's width.
- **Order:** when the map opens with marks not yet shown, they write one after another, the first
  a moment after the map is up. A mark already shown is drawn whole.

**A hint after a find's thought:** "M   Map" for a few seconds once the player has the map, as
"Tab   Backpack" follows taking the bag. Without the map there is nothing to open, so no hint.

## Phase 3: the map screen, M, and the screen stack

### Engine: one primitive, its own commit
- `ui_draw_textured_quad_uv(dl, rect, tex, uv[4], tint)` in `ui.h` and `ui_draw.c`. `uv` is
  {u0, v0, u1, v1}, v0 at the rect's top.
- `ui_draw_textured_quad` becomes it with {0, 0, 1, 1}, which should change no pixels.
- Covered by a `textured-uv` `--ui-probe` case in gametest, a `ui-textured-uv` gate arm,
  `gates.py --only ui`, and the goldens at 0 px.

### `map_screen.c/h` (new)
- **Loading:** the print and marks through `texture_load_file(pool, …, texture_desc(false))`. Non-sRGB
  and top-row-first, because the UI shows display values and reads the first row as the top. Behind
  a `load_seam("map")`.
- **The screen:** modal, over a dim backdrop, opening a `MapId`. The paper fits about 94% of the
  window's width and 84% of its height at the print's aspect, with a hint "M  CLOSE   ESC  BACK".
- **The view** (folded in above): a pan and a zoom from 1 (the whole sheet) to 3. Zoomed in it
  opens centred on the arrow, clamped to the sheet; mouse drag, WASD and the left stick pan, the
  wheel and the triggers zoom.
- **The paper's draw callback**, everything through the view's one transform and clipped to the
  paper:
  1. the visible part of the print;
  2. each found mark at its anchor, through `map_ink`: written on stroke by stroke the first time
     it is shown, one new mark after another, and whole after that;
  3. the arrow frame `round(heading/2π·64) mod 64`, at the player's feet mapped to the print.

  The heading is `atan2(forward.x, forward.z)` from `player_eye`, so north is up the page.
- **The action:** `map` — key M, pad RB, a UI action. Before the map is had, M thinks "I don't have
  a map."

### The screen stack in `on_frame_input`
The state is derived each frame from `ui_top()`. `back` is sent only when the top screen is modal,
so the HUD is never popped, and the bag is never shown twice.

| Top screen | Tab | M | Esc / pad B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Game | bag, or "I'm not carrying anything." | map, or "I don't have a map." | frees the cursor |
| Bag | close | map over the bag, if the map is chosen | close |
| Map (from the game) | swap to the bag | close | close |
| Map over the bag | close, back to the bag | close, back to the bag | close, back to the bag |

The input stays held through the frame a screen shuts.

**Flags:** `--open-map` implies `--map`; with `--open-backpack map` the map opens over the bag.

**Look:**
- the map at the spawn, the crossroads, the drive and the lake;
- zoomed in on the arrow, and panned to an edge;
- over the bag;
- under `--crt`;
- a pad script through every row of the table.

## Risks

- **Image orientation and colour encoding:**
  - UI images are top-row-first and non-sRGB; world images are bottom-row-first and sRGB.
  - The texture pool keys by path, so the UI files get a `ui_` prefix and never appear in `SPECS[]`.
- **The seed:** the neighbour houses are seeded and the print shows seed 7's. Silent warns when
  `--seed` differs or a place's point drifts from its mark's. Re-export and rerun the tool when
  `houses`, `layout`, `lake`, `hill` or `land` change.
- **The UI stack:** `back` must not be sent with only the HUD up, and "on top" is not "on the stack".
- **Repo size:** about 2 MB of PNGs, so restyle in a few batches.
- **Merge order:** 13.42 moves the spawn; the fridge map's placement is checked after whichever
  rebase.

## As built

### Phase 1: the export, the print, the marks, the folded map, the art table

**The export** is as planned, with these differences:
- **The samplers are three, not four:** `lake_shore_point`, `lake_track_point` and `woods_cover`
  (which shares `valley_site` with `valley_woods`, so the map's woods and the planted woods are one
  rule). `land_over_chasm` was not needed: `land_lip_clearance` was already public.
- **Into `layout.h`:** the graveyard box and `DRIVE_HALF`. The dock stayed in `lake.c`, since the
  map does not draw it.
- **Tower:** its polygon was exported, then dropped once the manor was pictured rather than
  drawn from above.
- **Purity:** checked against master's binary, which built the same kits with the same counts
  (world 1439 colliders, 736328 vertices, 84 meshes, 1302 shadow cells; the mansion, ground, lake
  and cabin kits; both woods). A fresh export is byte-identical to the committed
  `town_plan.txt`.

**The print** came out of several rounds with the user, and differs from the plan in these ways:
- **Names:** the user's: Pale Ridge, Blackwood Manor, Widow's Gorge (see Names).
- **Kept plain, after "a bit too busy":**
  - no legend, points of interest, street index, margin grid, house numbers, spot heights or tree
    symbols;
  - contours every 2 m, a heavier one every 10, over open ground only;
  - the title stacked PALE / RIDGE in the empty corner, with a compass and a scale in feet.
- **Blackwood Manor is drawn standing**, a steep gabled house with its spired tower, the way a
  visitors' map pictures its sight. The user picked it from four styles, then had the year
  removed and the drawing shrunk to about 70%.
- **Widow's Gorge:**
  - hachured from its lip;
  - Widow's Creek laid out from where its two faces stand, since an offset of the lip folded back
    on itself at the corner;
  - Gorge Bridge printed across it, though it is not there in the game, which is what the
    "no bridge!" mark is about.
- **Roads:** drawn as smooth ribbons, since a wide polyline left square ends along the drive.
  Lake Road has dashed edges that stop at the paving.
- **RGBA, not RGB.** The torn edge is in alpha, so the map screen shows the game behind it.
- **2.6 MB, not ≤ 1.5 MB.** PNG on a photographic picture is large: JPEG would be 0.8 MB but has
  no alpha. Holding the colour to 6 bits a channel took it from 4.6 MB, and the paper's grain
  dithers the steps away.
- **The paper:**
  - its wear, the two holes at fold crossings fixed clear of any lettering (`WORN_THROUGH`), and
    edges browned rather than olive;
  - the print, the paper and the ink each draw from their own random stream, so a change to
    what is printed leaves the wear where it was.

**The marks**, blue ballpoint in Reenie Beanie:
- the barricade: an X, "road blocked / police?";
- the road's end: an X, "no bridge!" with an arrow;
- the cabin: sketched in with smoke from its chimney and a ring round it, "cabin / someone here?".

**The arrow** is a red felt-tip chevron, jittered once and turned in 64 frames of 80 px, 8 to a row.

**The folded map's set** is two 512 px faces cut from the print along its own folds: the title's
panel (2, 2) in front and the North Woods panel (1, 0) behind. Its normal is flat and its
roughness 64×32. Its front reads as a street map's cover because the title sits wholly inside that
panel, which is why the title is stacked.

**`map_art.h`** is the town map's `MapArt` row: the pictures, the frame, the marks with their UVs
and anchors, the arrow's grid, the folded faces' UVs and the street seed. The types live in
`town_map.h` beside `MapId` and `PlaceId`, and each place names its map. The tool writes the
header in clang-format's own layout, so the commit hook leaves it as written.

|---|---|---|
| `ui_town_map.png` | 2624 KB | `3d3132756bd0e7b8` |
| `ui_town_map_marks.png` | 206 KB | `78b5f773d1dd5eef` |
