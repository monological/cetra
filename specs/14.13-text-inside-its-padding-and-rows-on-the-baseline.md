# cetra 14.13: a label's text inside its padding, and rows on the baseline

## Context

pcb 6.3.1 shows a key as a small box, its text at 11 points, beside what the key does at the
window's 14: "[Enter] set". The box would not line up with the words. With the row's children
centred across, the box sat about 4 points low; at the row's top, 1.5 points high. Two causes,
found reading the layout:

1. **A bug: a label's text is drawn from its rect's corner, not inside its padding.** The layout
   measures a label with its padding (`_intrinsic`) and breaks a wrapping label's lines at its width
   less its padding (`_break_lines`), but `_emit` draws the lines into `el->rect`
   (`cetra/src/ui.c:1643`), whose top-left is outside the padding. A label's default padding is 4
   points above and 4 below, so every label's text is drawn 4 points high in its box, with all 8 of
   the padding's points under it. Centred on a label's box, anything else is centred 4 points under
   its words. A left padding is likewise drawn over; no default has one. `ui.h:53` claims two
   strings of different sizes in one row "share a baseline": they do not, each is its rect's top
   plus its own ascent.
2. **No alignment of a row by its text.** `UIAlign` places a child across its parent by its box:
   start, centre or end. Text of two sizes sits level when the bottoms of its letters do, its
   baselines, and no box alignment puts them there.

The user chose: fix the first, so centring centres; add the second as a choice beside it; and
compare the two on pcb's key hints.

Branch and spec: `14.13-text-inside-its-padding-and-rows-on-the-baseline`, off cetra's master
(`d5d03886`).

## The design

### A label's text inside its padding

`_emit` draws a label's lines into its rect less its padding, `_content_rect(ui, el)`, the rect a
field's text and a pane's view already use. Every label's text moves down by its top padding and
right by its left padding inside a box of unchanged size: by default 4 points down. No layout
number changes. The comment at `ui.h:53` says what is true: a string's baseline is its rect's top
plus its face's ascent at its size.

### Rows on the baseline

`UI_ALIGN_BASELINE`, appended to `UIAlign` (3), for a row's `align_cross`.

- **An element's baseline** is how far below its top its first line's baseline stands:
  - a label's: its top padding and its face's ascent at its size, as it draws its first line;
  - a panel's or a screen root's: its first child's in the flow, where that child stands in it. In
    a row, where the row's cross alignment puts it; in a column, at the column's top padding, while
    the column's `align_main` is START;
  - none for anything else: a control, a field, an editor, a scrolling pane (its content moves with
    its scroll), a column whose first child is centred or at its end, and an element with no text.
- **A row on the baseline** moves each child that has a baseline down until it stands on the
  deepest one's. A child with none stands at the top, as START puts it, and so does one stretched
  across the row (GROW or SHARE), which fills it. Fitting its children, the row is as tall as the
  most any child reaches below its top so placed. Its own baseline is that line, so a row on the
  baseline inside another lines up with it.
- **Elsewhere it is START**: as a column's `align_cross`, as an `align_main`, and as a label's
  `align_cross`, which is its text's horizontal alignment.
- **One placement**: a child's offset across its parent is one function, read by the arrange, by
  the measure and by `_baseline`, so where a child is put and where its baseline is said to be
  cannot disagree.

The layout runs one axis at a time and measures before it arranges; every height is measured
before a row on the baseline is, so the offsets and the baselines are from the heights the arrange
will use, a stretched child excepted, which stands at the top either way.

## Tests

Two arms in the `ui` group, from gametest's `--ui-probe` (`apps/gametest/src/ui_text_probe.c`),
built with no GPU through `ui_build` and its draw list, as `ui-control-lines` is. A glyph's baseline
is its quad's top less the glyph's own offset from its baseline, at its size.

- **`ui-label-padding`**: a label of `x`s with padding 10 above and 12 to the left. Its glyphs'
  baseline stands at its rect's top plus 10 plus the face's ascent at its size, and its first pen
  at its rect's left plus 12, within 0.01 of a point. Fails before the fix, by 10 and 12.
- **`ui-baseline`**: a row on the baseline holding, in turn, a label at 14 points, one at 28, a
  panel with 3 points of padding around a label at 10, a row on the baseline holding labels at 12
  and 20, and a fixed panel with no text; inside a column that fits it. Every glyph stands on one
  baseline, within 0.01; the textless panel is at the row's top; the row is as tall as its lowest
  child's bottom, less its top, plus its padding; and the row's own baseline -- read through a
  column on the baseline-less START, beside a label -- is where its glyphs are. Fails before the
  alignment, which falls to START.

The `ui`, `canvas`, `idle` and gate-docs groups run after each phase, nothing else rendering.

**Look**: every label moves 4 points down in its box, so the screens built of labels are looked at
before and after: gametest's menus (`--ui-screen`), the canvas app's palette and history, silent's
title and pause menus. And pcb's key hints, centred and on the baseline, side by side for the user.
