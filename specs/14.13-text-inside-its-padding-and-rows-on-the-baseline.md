# cetra 14.13: a label's text inside its padding, and rows on the baseline

## Context

pcb 6.3.1 shows a key as a small box, its text at 11 points, beside what the key does at the
window's 14: "[Enter] set". The box would not line up with the words. With the row's children
centred across, the box sat about 4 points low; at the row's top, 1.5 points high. Two causes,
found reading the layout:

1. **A bug: a label's text is drawn from its rect's corner, not inside its padding.** The layout
   measures a label with its padding (`_intrinsic`) and breaks a wrapping label's lines at its width
   less its padding (`_break_lines`), but `_emit` draws the lines into `el->rect`
   (`cetra/src/ui.c:1643`), whose top-left is outside the padding. A label's default padding is 4
   points above and 4 below, so every label's text is drawn 4 points high in its box, with all 8 of
   the padding's points under it. Centred on a label's box, anything else is centred 4 points under
   its words. A left padding is likewise drawn over; no default has one. `ui.h:53` claims two
   strings of different sizes in one row "share a baseline": they do not, each is its rect's top
   plus its own ascent.
2. **No alignment of a row by its text.** `UIAlign` places a child across its parent by its box:
   start, centre or end. Text of two sizes sits level when the bottoms of its letters do, its
   baselines, and no box alignment puts them there.

The user chose: fix the first, so centring centres; add the second as a choice beside it; and
compare the two on pcb's key hints.

Branch and spec: `14.13-text-inside-its-padding-and-rows-on-the-baseline`, off cetra's master
(`d5d03886`).

## The design

### A label's text inside its padding

`_emit` draws a label's lines into its rect less its padding, `_content_rect(ui, el)`, the rect a
field's text and a pane's view already use. Every label's text moves down by its top padding and
right by its left padding inside a box of unchanged size: by default 4 points down. No layout
number changes. The comment at `ui.h:53` says what is true: a string's baseline is its rect's top
plus its face's ascent at its size.

### Rows on the baseline

`UI_ALIGN_BASELINE`, appended to `UIAlign` (3), for a row's `align_cross`.

- **An element's baseline** is how far below its top its first line's baseline stands:
  - a label's: its top padding and its face's ascent at its size, as it draws its first line;
  - a panel's or a screen root's: its first child's in the flow, where that child stands in it. In
    a row, where the row's cross alignment puts it; in a column, at the column's top padding, while
    the column's `align_main` is START;
  - none for anything else: a control, a field, an editor, a scrolling pane (its content moves with
    its scroll), a column whose first child is centred or at its end, and an element with no text.
- **A row on the baseline** moves each child that has a baseline down until it stands on the
  deepest one's. A child with none stands at the top, as START puts it, and so does one stretched
  across the row (GROW or SHARE), which fills it. Fitting its children, the row is as tall as the
  most any child reaches below its top so placed. Its own baseline is that line, so a row on the
  baseline inside another lines up with it.
- **Elsewhere it is START**: as a column's `align_cross`, as an `align_main`, and as a label's
  `align_cross`, which is its text's horizontal alignment.
- **One placement**: a child's offset across its parent is one function, read by the arrange, by
  the measure and by `_baseline`, so where a child is put and where its baseline is said to be
  cannot disagree.

The layout runs one axis at a time and measures before it arranges; every height is measured
before a row on the baseline is, so the offsets and the baselines are from the heights the arrange
will use, a stretched child excepted, which stands at the top either way.

## Tests

Two arms in the `ui` group, from gametest's `--ui-probe` (`apps/gametest/src/ui_text_probe.c`),
built with no GPU through `ui_build` and its draw list, as `ui-control-lines` is. A glyph's baseline
is its quad's top less the glyph's own offset from its baseline, at its size.

- **`ui-label-padding`**: a label of `x`s with padding 10 above and 12 to the left. Its glyphs'
  baseline stands at its rect's top plus 10 plus the face's ascent at its size, and its first pen
  at its rect's left plus 12, within 0.01 of a point. Fails before the fix, by 10 and 12.
- **`ui-baseline`**: a row on the baseline holding, in turn, a label at 14 points, one at 28, a
  panel with 3 points of padding around a label at 10, a row on the baseline holding labels at 12
  and 20, and a fixed panel with no text; inside a column that fits it. Every glyph stands on one
  baseline, within 0.01; the textless panel is at the row's top; the row is as tall as its lowest
  child's bottom, less its top, plus its padding; and the row's own baseline -- read through a
  column on the baseline-less START, beside a label -- is where its glyphs are. Fails before the
  alignment, which falls to START.

The `ui`, `canvas`, `idle` and gate-docs groups run after each phase, nothing else rendering.

**Look**: every label moves 4 points down in its box, so the screens built of labels are looked at
before and after: gametest's menus (`--ui-screen`), the canvas app's palette and history, silent's
title and pause menus. And pcb's key hints, centred and on the baseline, side by side for the user.

## As built

### Phase 1: the instruments

`--ui-probe label-padding` and `--ui-probe baseline` in gametest, and their arms. A glyph's corner
is snapped to the framebuffer's pixels, half a point in the probe's headless window, so a baseline
or a pen read back from it is held within half a pixel, and two baselines within one. The textless
panel's top and the row's height are layout numbers, held to 0.01.

`UI_ALIGN_BASELINE` is in `UIAlign` from this phase, so the probe compiles; until phase 3 the
layout takes it as START.

On master's layout both fail. `ui-label-padding`: the baseline 9.79 points above its place inside
the padding and the pen 12.21 left of it, the 10 and 12 of padding less the snap. `ui-baseline`:
the five baselines at 340.94, 352.38, 340.81, 355.38 and 362.13, 21.3 apart; the inner row, a panel
of the theme's 16 points of padding, puts its two labels lowest. The textless panel stands at the
row's top and the row is as tall as its lowest child reaches, as START does too.

### Phase 2: a label's text inside its padding

`_emit` draws a label's lines into `_content_rect(ui, el)`. `ui-label-padding` passes, the baseline
0.21 and the pen 0.21 points off, inside the snap. The `ui`, `canvas`, `idle` and gate-docs groups
pass but `ui-baseline`, phase 3's.

**Look**, before and after, headless: gametest's settings screen and HUD, the canvas app's palette,
its `--ui` panel and its vim line under `--editor --vim`. Text that sat at the top of its label's
box is centred in it now: the settings screen's heading and the HUD's two lines 4 points lower, the
vim line's `d` level with the field beside it where it sat high. Nothing else moved.

**The canvas app's palette was built round the bug**: its rows are 30 points with 5 of padding, 20
inside, and a label at 18 points is 26 tall with its own 4 above and below, so it overflowed the
row, and only the text drawn from the label's top kept it in place. Moved 4 points down it sat on
the row's floor. Its two labels now take no padding above or below, and the palette is 0 px from
before.

silent's words are labels too: its menus' (`hud_words`, 6 points of padding above and 18 below)
move 6 points down, the backpack's and the map's hint 4; the HUD's line draws itself and does not
move. Not photographed: silent is the user's to walk.

### Phase 3: rows on the baseline

In `ui.c`, before the measure:
- `_cross_len`: a child's length across its parent's flow, GROW to the room and SHARE its share,
  each to its most, any other its own. The arrange's two lines setting it before.
- `_cross_offset`: where a child stands across, from the start of the room: centred, at the end,
  or on the baseline, as the arrange placed it before for the first two. Read by the arrange, by
  the measure and by `_baseline`.
- `_baseline` and `_baseline_line`, as designed; a label's through `ui_text_baseline` at its top
  padding, the one formula its drawing uses.

The measure takes a child's reach across a row on the baseline as its length plus its drop; the
arrange reads the row's line once, before it places any child. No other layout's numbers move.

`ui-baseline` passes: the six baselines 0.31 points apart, inside the snap. **The fixture took a
sixth label** once the arm passed: its tallest child had also the deepest baseline, so its height
was the same with the measure ignoring the drops and the check could not see one. A label at 10
points with 40 of padding under it, moved down onto the line, now reaches past every other child,
and the row grows from 68 to 85.95 for it. The `ui`, `canvas`, `idle` and gate-docs groups pass.

**Falsified**, each from `f02d3723`, `ui-baseline` failing on each alone:
- the measure taking a child's length without its drop: the row 68 tall, its lowest child reaching
  85.95;
- a column's baseline without its top padding: the label in the padded column 3 points low;
- a row's baseline without where its first child stands: the inner row's two labels 6 points low.

And phase 2's, from `535bc0e0`: the label drawn from its rect again, `ui-label-padding` off by
-9.79 and -12.21 as on master.

### The comparison in pcb

pcb's 6.3.1 branch with `extern/cetra` at `f02d3723`, its key hints' rows centred and then on the
baseline, the properties panel headless. In framebuffer pixels, 2 to a point:
- **Centred**: the box's middle at 42.5, level with the middle of the words with their tall
  letters ("close" from 35 to 50); the key's text on a line 2 pixels above the words'.
- **On the baseline**: the key's text on the words' line exactly, the box's middle at 44, half a
  pixel from the middle of their small letters.

The user chose centring, seen as two pictures side by side: it looks better. The baseline stays in
cetra as a choice beside it.

### Phase 5: the reviews

`/simplify`'s four -- reuse, simplification, efficiency, altitude -- and a thermo-nuclear review,
in background agents at effort high, applied once all five were back.

**Applied:**
- **Each element keeps its baseline** (`UIElement.baseline`, -1 for none), set at the end of the
  height measure from its children's, which are measured first. A baseline asked live went three
  times to a row's first child -- directly, through the row's line, and through the child's
  offset -- so rows nested through their first children cost three to the power of their depth,
  and every measure and arrange of a row on the baseline asked each child twice more. Now it is one
  walk a layout. The arrange reads what the measure found, as the live calls did: every baseline
  was only read by an ancestor before its own subtree was arranged.
- **A row on the baseline's own baseline is its line**, as designed. It was its first child's: a
  row starting with an icon with no text had none and stood at the top, and one starting with a
  stretched child said that child's baseline, not the line. The arm's inner row started with a
  label, the one shape where the two agree; the fixture now has a row starting with a 12-point
  textless panel, and the arm checks the row's own stored baseline against its glyphs' line.
- **One test for a row that lines its children up** (`_lines_up`: a row, the height axis,
  BASELINE), where the measure, the arrange and the placement each had half of it; a column on
  BASELINE walked every child's baseline on the width pass and threw the answer away.
- **The placement split in two**: `_cross_offset(align, room, len)`, the box placement, and
  `_drop(c, line)`, the move onto the line. The measure passed a room of 0 it did not need, and the
  arrange worked a child's length out twice.
- `UI_ROOT` left out of a baseline: a virtual row's holder is the only root that is a child, and a
  pane has none. A label draws through `_content_in(el, &s)`, the style it already holds, as the
  field and the editor do; `_content_rect` resolves one for the callers without. Comments held to
  the house rules: none names its callers, `UIAlign`'s says the contract. The probe's two glyph
  helpers are one, `quad_origin`, and its window width is stated once.

The four groups pass. **Falsified**, from `7286e4f2`: a row on the baseline's own baseline its
first child's again, and `ui-baseline` failed -- the inner row, whose first label is the smaller,
put its two labels 6 points under the line, and the outer row's stored baseline stood 14.5 points
above its glyphs.

**Not applied, for the user:**
- **The controls draw their text with the theme's padding**, not the element's own: `_centred_rows`,
  a slider's label and track and a toggle's bed read `s.padding`, where the measure reads
  `_padding_from`, and a button centres its rows on its whole rect. The same mismatch as a label's,
  latent, since no app sets a control's own padding. With one "where an element's text stands" per
  kind, the draw and the baseline would read it and the controls would have baselines too. A spec
  of its own.
- **The canvas app's palette rows** keep their fixed 30 points with their labels' padding off; a
  row that fits, its height from the font, would keep the virtual rows' estimate from drifting.
- **silent's words** were tuned by eye while a label's text was drawn from its top: `hud_words`'
  6 points above now show. Its `hud_item_look`, which insets a rect "as a label would sit inside
  its padding", is now true of labels.
- `probe_field_caret_x` could take `quad_origin` for its pen; left, as old code.
- A column that centres or ends its children has no baseline, though one that fits them puts its
  first child at its top either way: the room a GROW column is given is the arrange's, after the
  measure has kept the baseline.
