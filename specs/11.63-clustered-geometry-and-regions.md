# Spec 11.63 — D4: clustered geometry, and a quadtree that owns residency

## Context

`apps/forest` is a 1 km² walkable world with **everything resident**: 64 terrain tiles built once and
never freed, 5,000 props in 7,064 nodes, one 131,072-triangle Jolt collider over the whole domain, one
splat texture over one world rectangle. 11.62 removed the *other* blocker on a large world — the origin
can move, so coordinates stay small however far you walk. The target is a land-dominant island of
several kilometres with sea at its edges.

**The number that decides this spec's shape:** forest draws **22.3 M triangles at the gate framing, of
which the terrain is 295 k**. Ninety-nine percent of the geometry is props. A terrain quadtree fixes the
tile-*count* explosion; it does almost nothing about the triangle load.

| at 2.6 m vertices | today (1 km) | 4 km | 10 km |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| terrain tiles | 64 | 1,024 | 6,400 |
| terrain triangles (level 0, resident) | 295 k | 4.7 M | 29.5 M |
| **props (resident)** | **5,000** | **~80,000** | **~500,000** |
| **prop triangles** (drawn at today's framing, scaled) | **22.3 M** | **~356 M** | **~2.2 G** |
| heightfield (4 planes) | 4 MB | 38 MB | 237 MB |
| collider triangles | 131 k | 2.1 M | 13.1 M |

The prop rows past 1 km are extrapolations at constant density, not measurements — they are there to
show *what kind* of number each becomes, and they are why this spec is about **residency and LOD**, not
about streaming bytes off disk. The heightfield is 38 MB at 4 km; disk streaming buys nothing until well
past 10 km and is out of scope.

## What of Nanite is reachable on GL 4.1

Nanite is two halves. The CPU half — the cluster DAG — is the actual innovation and is fully available.
The GPU half (compute-driven selection, software raster of pixel-sized triangles, visibility buffer)
needs compute, SSBOs and atomics, none below GL 4.3, and is aimed at micropolygons we do not have.

**The DAG build ships in-tree already.** The vendored meshoptimizer carries
`demo/clusterlod.h` — a complete, MIT-licensed cluster-LOD library over `meshopt_buildMeshlets*`,
`meshopt_partitionClusters` and lock-aware simplification, explicitly *"intended to either be used as
is, or as a reference"* — plus `demo/nanite.cpp` as a worked example. Its error-accumulation formula is
what makes selection monotonic, so we inherit that guarantee instead of deriving it. `clusterizer.cpp`
and `partition.cpp` are in the library's compiled SOURCES, and `lod.c` already links meshoptimizer.
`clusterlod.h` needs a C++ TU with `CLUSTERLOD_IMPLEMENTATION` behind a small C API — the JoltC pattern,
already precedented in this build.

**Clipmap rings are dropped.** 2005-era, capped near six LOD levels by construction (the snap quantum is
pinned to the coarsest cell), and the roadmap's "inherited reference implementation" contains no pyramid
and no streaming — water evaluated a procedural surface per vertex.

One escape hatch, noted not planned: `glDrawTransformFeedback` (GL 4.0) can draw what a TF pass
captured without a readback, and this codebase already runs TF in two places. If CPU cluster selection
ever binds, that is the door.

## Phase 1 — the cluster DAG (`cetra/src/cluster.c/h` + one C++ wrapper TU)

Build per mesh at import, via `clusterlod.h`: clusters of ~128 triangles; groups of ~8 adjacent
clusters (`partition_size`); each group's union simplified to ~50% with **the group boundary locked**;
re-split; re-grouped *differently* at the next level so locked seams move instead of accumulating.
Per cluster: bounds and normal cone (`meshopt_computeClusterBounds`), own error, parent-group error.

Selection: draw a cluster when its own error passes and its parent's does not. Monotonic by the
library's error accumulation, so neighbouring clusters cannot pick inconsistent levels — no cracks.
Switches happen per cluster below a stated error threshold rather than per whole mesh, which is
smaller than today's pops, not zero popping.

**The batching reconciliation — this is the part the first draft of this plan missed, and it is
load-bearing.** Per-instance cluster cuts would destroy instanced batching: two trees at different
distances want different cluster subsets and can no longer share a draw, un-shipping 11.29. The fix is
to **quantize the cut by distance band**: selection depends only on projected error, so all instances
of a prototype within one band share one cut. A cut is a set of cluster index ranges concatenated into
a **cached EBO segment per (prototype, band)**, rebuilt only when that band's cut changes. The draw
list's batch key goes from `(mesh, lod)` to `(mesh, band)` — structurally the same run-building,
sorting and `UBO_INSTANCE_MAX` machinery, and the sort key's existing 4-bit lod field holds bands 0-15.
Band-crossing switches a few clusters at once, which is the popping granularity. Non-instanced meshes
skip the banding and draw their per-mesh cut with `glMultiDrawElements` (GL 3.3+) — one call, many
ranges.

**Scope: opt-in per mesh, not a replacement of `lod.c`.** Forest's props adopt it; imported and
skinned content keeps the meshopt chains (skinned simplification is refused there for reasons the DAG
inherits unchanged), and the `lod` gate group keeps its subject. `--no-lod` maps to "cut = level 0
everywhere" — the identity lever — and `--lod-bias` scales the error threshold, so both flags keep
their meaning on the new path.

**Honest limit:** foliage cards barely simplify — `raiden` builds 0 chains and `abandoned_window` 23 of
71 today, because a leaf card is all boundary. The DAG helps trunks and rocks; leaves want impostors,
which is a different spec. The measured win to expect is on the wood, not the leaves.

### Correction, after implementation: on triangles this is PARITY, not a win

The paragraph above was right about the mechanism and wrong about the size of it, and the arms below
were written against the wrong claim. Measured on `apps/forest` at three framings, DAG against the
chain: **−2.7% near, −0.1% mid, −1.4% far**. Boundary locking costs about what error-driven selection
gains. The chain's coarsest rung is not the floor the plan assumed it was.

The probe says why, and it is the "honest limit" above turning out to be the whole story rather than a
footnote. Across its bands, bark goes **16,344 triangles to 59** — 288×, over 10 DAG levels — while a
leaf card goes **12,740 to 5,495**, 2.3×, with its first band not moving at all. This corpus is mostly
leaf cards, so the aggregate is the leaves' number and not the wood's.

So **the deliverable of Phase 1 is the seal, not the count.** Cracks are structurally impossible here
in a way the chain cannot claim: every cluster at every level indexes the ORIGINAL vertex buffer,
because simplification only ever drops vertices and never moves them. That is what
`simplify_permissive = false` buys and it is a guarantee rather than a tuning choice. It is also the
one property no frame can check, which is why the arm reads the index buffer instead.

Two consequences for the phases after this one. The DAG is **not** the answer to the prop triangle load
in the table above — impostors are, and they stay a different spec. And terrain keeps `lod.c`'s chain,
now for a measured reason: clustering all 84 forest meshes read 22.87 M triangles against the chain's
22.28 M at matched picture cost, because on a regular grid there are no seams worth respecting and
unconstrained whole-mesh simplification reaches a better surface at the same count.

**Arms, as built.**
- `d4-cluster-seal`: every clustered prototype reports `max_index == vertex_count - 1` and
  `permissive == 0` — the index-buffer statement of "no crack is possible", checkable without a render.
  Falsified by setting `simplify_permissive = true`, which lets simplification move positions.
- `d4-cluster-bands`: triangle count is non-increasing band to band, and the best prototype reduces at
  least 8×. Two bars rather than one because the monotonicity alone is satisfied by a builder that
  emits the same cut in every band. Falsified by shuffling the band order.
- `d4-cluster-batch`: DAG draws and instances equal the chain's exactly (1127 / 2979) — a per-instance
  cut would split every run — and `--no-instancing` gives one draw per instance. The arm that proves
  11.29 survived.
- `d4-cluster-parity`: DAG triangles within 6% of the chain's, and both at least 10% under `--no-lod`.
  It pins the parity rather than asserting the superiority the numbers do not support, and the second
  bar is what keeps it from passing on a builder that selects nothing. Falsified by `--no-lod`.

## Phase 2 — the terrain quadtree (`cetra/src/procedural/terrain_quadtree.c/h`)

A quadtree over `[center ± extent]`, descended per frame against the camera; selected nodes emit
patches; CDLOD vertex morphing removes both cracks and pops between levels.

**Patches are CPU-built per node and cached with residency — NOT a shared unit-space grid with
origin/scale uniforms.** The first draft had the shared-grid design and it is wrong for this engine,
for three reasons worth recording: a VBO with no heights forces vertex-shader height *textures*, which
(a) breaks the one-source contract — `terrain_height_at` is what the visual mesh, the collider, the
scatter and the camera clamp all agree through, and a bilinear texture fetch is a *different surface*;
(b) breaks the per-vertex tint (`terrain_tint` bakes vertex colours; a shared grid has nowhere to put
them); and (c) collides with the pyramid's node-centred convention — GL mips halve 2049→1024, not
1025, so `2^k + 1` levels cannot be texture mips at all. CPU-built patches keep `terrain_height_at` as
the only height source and reuse `build_grid`'s existing vertex layout unchanged.

**Morphing via baked attributes.** Each patch vertex carries its own position/normal *and its
parent-level position/normal* (two new attribute slots; `common.h` has 9-10 free). The vertex shader
lerps by a factor computed from camera distance across the node's LOD band. Because the pyramid's
parent level is an **every-other-node subsample** (see Phase 3), `parent(i) == child(2i)` *exactly* —
so at morph = 1 a fine vertex is bit-equal to the coarse neighbour's surface, and boundary agreement
is an identity, not an approximation.

**The morph is displacement, so it obeys 11.62's hardest-won rule:** it must be identical in every
geometry program — `pbr_vert`, `shadow_depth_vert`, `depth_prepass_vert` — or the shadow casts from a
surface the shading pass never drew, and the one-sided prepass depth test *deletes geometry*. The
morph lives in a shared include with its uniforms uploaded from one function, exactly the
`wind_upload_to_program` shape, and defaults to 0 so every non-terrain mesh is bit-identical.

Replaces `build_terrain`'s double loop (`forest.c:691-707`). Addressing goes through
`terrain_to_domain` (`terrain.h:143`) — the documented single placement chokepoint that 11.62's origin
shift moves.

### Amendments, after implementation

**The morph target is one FLOAT, not a position and a normal.** The parent lattice restricted to a
patch is that patch's even-indexed subset, so the target has the SAME world XZ as the vertex and
differs in Y alone. Slot 12 carries `(parent Y, window start, 1/(end − start))` and slot 13 the parent
normal — still two slots, but the second is the shading normal, which the plan did not have and which
the surface needs: without it the ground slides under its own shading as it morphs, and two patches at
a seam agree about where the ground is while disagreeing about which way it faces.

**The morph WINDOW rides the attribute too, and that is what makes the feature cost nothing
elsewhere.** The alternative — a baked level index plus a uniform array of windows plus a dynamic
index into it in five programs — costs eight bytes a vertex less and buys an off-switch nobody can
forget: an unbound attribute reads (0,0,0), the reciprocal span is 0, the factor is 0. All 25 goldens
are 0 px with no flag, no material field and no scene state.

**`cetra_local_position` takes the model MATRIX where it took an origin.** Every one of its four call
sites already passed `mModel[3].xyz`, so this removes an argument rather than adding one, and it gives
the morph what wind does not need: the factor is a camera distance, so it has to be measured where the
vertex actually is. `engine_upload_displacement_uniforms` is the one call that feeds all FIVE programs
— pbr, pbr_skinned, depth prepass, shadow depth, and shadow absorb, which shares the depth vertex
stage and which the plan's list of three missed.

**The split factor is a proof, not a tuning knob.** A level reaches `f` patch widths, and two things
bound `f` from below: adjacent patches differing by more than one level leaves the morph nothing to
close (needs `f ≥ 2√2`), and a seam vertex needing factor 1 from the fine side while the coarse side
is still at 0 needs `f·(1 − morph fraction) ≥ √2`. At 3.5 and 0.5 both hold with about a quarter to
spare. At 2.0 the coarse side of a seam has already morphed 17% of the way to its own parent.

**`--no-lod` does not reach the quadtree**, against the plan. `--no-lod` is a per-mesh statement and a
patch is already at level 0; pinning the DESCENT instead is a different thing wearing the same name
and costs 4^levels patches — 16,384 at four kilometres against 706. `--no-quadtree` is the lever, and
it draws the fixed grid at its own finest cell, which is the comparison that was wanted.

**An origin shift rebuilds the whole selection, and the group node has to be un-translated.**
`scene_apply_origin_delta` subtracts the delta from every root child, which is right for a subtree
whose vertices did not move; a patch's did, so the translation moves it twice. That defect read 29% of
the frame, FLAT with distance — the wind-hash signature 11.62 measured, and the same class of error:
something reading a position as an identity. `terrain_quadtree_rebuild` re-selects immediately rather
than leaving the tree empty, because the shift lands before the shadow pass and the GI captures.

**`build_grid`'s UV0 now comes from the domain coordinate rather than the world one**, for the same
rule. It moved nothing measurable here — forest's terrain is layered and reads its detail through the
splat — but a rebuilt patch on a non-layered terrain would have slid its detail texture on every
shift.

**Measured.** 1 km: 364 patches, 186,368 triangles, against the fixed grid's 64 tiles and 294,912.
4 km: 706 patches and 361,472 triangles against 1,024 tiles and 4,718,592. Sixteen times the ground
area costs the quadtree 1.94x and the grid 16x, which is the whole claim.

**Arms.** `d4-patch-seam`: no adjacency more than one level apart, and at every seam the fine side
reads factor 1 exactly while the coarse side reads 0 — read at both world sizes, falsified by the split
factor. `d4-morph-parent`: a fully morphed patch is the surface its parent DRAWS, measured against the
parent's own triangles; falsified by the coarse quad's other diagonal, which reads 2.92 world units
against 3.8e-6. That second arm exists because the first cannot see it — every boundary vertex is
even-indexed and therefore its own morph target, so the seam reads clean whatever the interior does.
`d4-patch-count`: the growth comparison above. `d4-morph-depth`: `--depth-prepass` moves no more on the
quadtree than on the fixed grid, whose terrain does not morph; starving one program of `uMorphEye`
reads 9x.

## Phase 3 — the in-memory mip pyramid

`TerrainField` gains levels; `sample_plane` (`terrain.c:192`) gains a level selector; a patch at
quadtree depth *d* samples the level matching its vertex spacing at **build time, on the CPU** — no GPU
pyramid exists or is needed.

Constraints that are easy to get wrong: the grid is **node-centred** (texel 0 on `-extent`, `res-1` on
`+extent`), so levels are `2^k + 1` — 513, 1025, 2049; and the 4×4 Catmull-Rom kernel is a contract
(bilinear facets `terrain_normal_at`'s central differences and reaches the scatter's slope gate as
missing trees). Levels are built by **every-other-node subsampling**, not filtering — that is what
makes `parent(i) == child(2i)` exact and Phase 2's morph an identity. A filtered variant is a tunable
later if subsample aliasing shows; the exactness goes with it, so it is not the default.

Whole chain ≈ 1.33× the base field — 50 MB at 4 km. That is the argument for memory, not disk.

**Arm.** `d4-mip`: `sample_plane` at level N on a lattice node equals the level-0 value at the
corresponding node exactly (the subsample identity); falsified by always returning level 0 — which
changes *between*-node samples, so the arm also reads one midpoint per level.

## Phase 4 — regions: props and collision on the quadtree

**What actually unlocks scale**: the DAG makes each prop cheaper; residency makes most of them absent.

A node at region depth owns its **props** — scatter seeded from the node's cell coordinates rather
than the one global `g_rng` stream (`forest.c:411`), so a region reloads identically and clump
patterns still continue across borders because the clump noise is position-based — and its
**collider**, one Jolt static body per resident region (`physics_create_mesh_shape` /
`free_rigid_body` already suffice; JoltC has no `HeightFieldShape`, so triangle soup stays).
Residency follows the camera with hysteresis: load at R, free at R + margin.

Region unload frees nodes, and **prototype meshes survive by refcount** — `mesh_ref` increments
`mesh->refs` and `free_mesh` decrements, so instance nodes borrow and the prototypes persist. Verify
GPU buffers are actually deleted on the last unref; a leak here compounds per region crossing.

**Engine work this needs, and it does not exist:**
- `remove_child_node` — `free_node` (`scene.c:684`) frees a subtree *without unlinking it*, leaving a
  dangling parent pointer. Add the detach API and state `free_node`'s contract.
- `add_child_node` grows by one element per call (`scene.c:715`) — capacity doubling, as the draw list
  already does.
- The draw list's whole-rebuild-on-mutation (one global epoch, `draw_list.c:15`) is **left alone**:
  regions change every few seconds of walking, a rebuild at that cadence is affordable, and an
  incremental draw list is a bigger change than this spec needs.

**Arms.** `d4-region-determinism`: a region's props are identical after leave-and-re-enter; falsified
by seeding from a global counter. `d4-collider-seam`: the character crosses a region boundary without
losing ground contact, in `forest-rest`'s idiom. `d4-region-leak`: resident node/mesh/GPU-buffer
counts return to baseline after a long walk that loads and frees many regions.

## Phase 5 — forest becomes the island

Radial falloff drops the edges below sea level; `--water` returns; the world grows past 1 km.

**Origin shifting stays OFF on the island, and that is what re-admits water.** At 4 km, positions reach
~±2,800 units, where 11.62's measured curve puts precision cost under 1% of the frame — no shift
needed, so 11.62's `--water`-with-shifting refusal never triggers, and water's origin-anchored bed,
shore chain and foam history are all valid. A larger island revisits this; it is the water-rebasing
work 11.62 deferred, and it stays deferred here.

**The splat is the stated ceiling:** 512² over 1 km is 1.95 m/texel; 2048² over 4 km is the same
density; 2048 is `MATERIAL_TEXTURE_ARRAY_CAP`. **4 km is where a single splat stops working** — past
that it is paged, which is virtual texturing, D10.

## Out of scope

- **Disk streaming** (38 MB at 4 km; windowed `.r16` reads, on-disk mip chain, range manifest — none
  needed; `heightmap.c` reads whole files today).
- **The splat pyramid** — virtual texturing, D10.
- **Per-tile erosion** — the domain is a closed basin by construction (`erosion.c` zeroes boundary
  flux, which is what lets the sediment budget be asserted), and 220 iterations of a 1-cell stencil is
  a whole-field dependency cone. Erode offline at top resolution; stream the result.
- **Hardware tessellation** — GL 4.0 has it, `ShaderType` is `{VERTEX, GEOMETRY, FRAGMENT}`, CDLOD
  morphing buys most of what it would, and Apple's deprecated GL driver makes its performance an
  unknown. A later spike, not a commitment.
- **GPU-driven selection, software raster, visibility buffer** — below GL 4.3, and aimed at
  micropolygons we do not have.
- **Impostors for foliage** — the real answer for leaf cards; its own spec.

## Delivery and verification

Phases land in order, each with gates green before the next starts; Phase 1 is independently shippable
on today's 1 km forest and is where the review effort concentrates, since it touches the draw list.

```bash
./build.sh
python3 scripts/gates.py --only d4,forest,lod,terrain,layers,origin
python3 scripts/goldens.py
python3 scripts/gates.py            # once, at the end
```

Every arm names its falsifying mutation. Standing rules: **forest is not pixel-deterministic** with the
sky on (~35,000 px run to run) — pixel arms use `--render-mode 6` or `--no-sky` and measure the floor at
the exact framing first. The `forest-*` arms assert integers that **will** move (`forest-order` demands
byte-exact instance and triangle equality across runs, and `morton_key` normalises against `2·extent`,
so every key changes when the world grows); they are **re-baselined onto the island**, not preserved
behind a flag — they test batching, ordering, LOD and culling *mechanisms*, which the island exercises
just as well, and keeping the old configuration alive would make the shipped world the untested one.
The `lod` group is unaffected by design (Phase 1 is opt-in per mesh), which is itself an assertable
claim: its arms must not move.
