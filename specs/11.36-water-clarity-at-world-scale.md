# A. Projector aimed at the displaceable slab (was task #22)

## Purpose

`oceanProjectedPosition` (`cetra/shaders/include/ocean.glsl`, ~line 99) intersects each
lattice ray with the **still** plane — that is where the wave field is sampled. The surface
then displaces, so a wave can lift geometry **into view from XZ the lattice never sampled**.
The nearest point a ray can reach is `clearance / tan(top-of-frame angle)`; anything nearer is
absent however deep its troughs go.

Bites when the eye is within ~one wave height of the surface — wading, a boat deck, a camera
at sea level. **At tree's normal framing (eye 95+ above the water) it is irrelevant**, which
is why this is a narrow-regime bug rather than a broken feature. Confirmed twice:
- The submerged FFT fixture (camera 1.65 units under a metre-scale sea) lost the
  near-overhead underside. That is **why `water-submerged` had to move** to a shallow Gerstner
  framing — see `WATER_SUBMERGED_FLAGS` at `scripts/gates.py:2971` (comment block from :2959),
  which attributes the reframing to this gap twice, in so many words.
- `--player` wading: past r≈430 the eye passes through zero clearance above the waterline.

## Fix

Aim the projector at the **near boundary of the displaceable slab** instead of the still
plane: offset the plane toward the eye by the max vertical displacement. **Costs zero lattice
rows** — the row→distance mapping just shifts nearer, and the horizon end does not move
because a grazing ray misses both planes.

In `oceanProjectedPosition`, the current code is:

```glsl
float eye = waterCamPos.y - waterLevel;
eye = above ? max(eye, OCEAN_MIN_EYE_HEIGHT) : min(eye, -OCEAN_MIN_EYE_HEIGHT);
float t = -eye / rd.y;
```

Reduce `|eye|` by the bound before the clamp. Cap the offset at ~0.7·|eye| so a high camera
does not pull the whole lattice near and waste far-field density, and keep the existing
`OCEAN_MIN_EYE_HEIGHT` floor.

**Honesty about what the single-plane offset does NOT fix (review finding, 2026-08-13).**
When the eye is *inside* the slab — which is where both named fixtures sit — this reduces the
artifact class rather than removing it, for two reasons:
- **The 0.7 cap defeats the acceptance test at its own framing.** At level 3.0 FFT, |eye| =
  1.65, so the offset saturates at ~1.15 while the slab boundary is ~0.19 from the eye — the
  near-overhead underside stays under-covered by ~2.6×. If the cap binds, relax it as
  `bound/|eye| → 1` rather than treating 0.7 as sacred; expect the level-3.0 verification to
  only PARTIALLY recover, and judge it against that expectation.
- **The horizon-row clamp is untouched**: `y1 = min(horizonY, OCEAN_OVERSCAN)`
  (`ocean.glsl:131`), and overscan is deliberately not applied at the horizon edge. A crest
  rising above eye level projects *above* the flat-plane horizon row, so no lattice vertex can
  reach it — crest tops clip along a horizontal line when clearance < wave height.
The complete fix is Johanson's own construction: intersect the frustum with BOTH bounding
planes of the displaceable volume and project from an eye pushed outside the slab. The
single-plane offset is the right cheap increment for the wading/boat-deck case (eye above the
slab); the spec for this work must say which regime it claims.

Also note: `water-fft-det` is a currently-passing 0-px determinism arm whose near lattice
remaps entirely under this change (effective eye 0.225 at its framing) — a re-bake is
expected, not a regression.

## The blocker: publishing a displacement bound

Needs a new `waterMaxDisplacement` uniform from `cetra/src/water.c`. **Both bounds below were
re-derived in review (2026-08-13) after the first drafts failed verification — use these.**

- **Gerstner: analytic.** Vertical displacement per octave is `keep * amplitude * sinp`
  (`ocean.glsl:472`) with `keep ≤ 1` and `shoal ≤ 1` only reducing it, and the steepness/Q
  normalisation entering only the horizontal terms. So the bound is
  `waterAmplitude * Σ 0.44^i` for i<4 (`OCEAN_AMPLITUDE_FALLOFF` 0.44, `OCEAN_WAVES` 4,
  `ocean.glsl:27-29`) = **amplitude × 1.7188** — or 1.7857 (the infinite series) if it should
  survive `OCEAN_WAVES` growing. (An earlier draft said 1.8686; that number has no basis in
  the file.)
- **Spectral.** The seeded spectrum is **JONSWAP + TMA + a secondary crossing swell**
  (`_water_jonswap`, `water.c:127-137, 196-233`) — NOT Pierson-Moskowitz, and the sea is
  fetch-limited (χ = gF/U² ≈ 8,900 with `WATER_FETCH` 120000, `WATER_WIND_SPEED` 11.5,
  `WATER_SEA_DEPTH` 54), so any PM formula is the wrong spectrum applied outside its validity
  AND blind to two of the three `#define`s the seeding actually uses. Two honest options:
  - (a) Accumulate the seeded realisation's variance in the cascade seeding loop
    (`Σ h0[0]² + h0[1]²` over the two displacing cascades, both at `amplitude_scale` 0.45)
    and use `k·sqrt(var)`. Exact in principle but requires tracing the inverse FFT's
    normalisation through `water_fft_frag.glsl`, and a wrong constant fails silently.
  - (b) **Recommended:** Hasselmann's fetch-limited relation `Hs = 0.0016·√χ·U²/g` → Hs ≈
    2.04 m; as a one-sided crest bound, σ_eff = 0.45·√(σ_jonswap² + 0.22·σ_swell²) ≈ 0.26 →
    4σ ≈ 1.05, plus the bound-harmonic crest term (`ocean.glsl:302-307`) ≈ 0.1 →
    **≈1.2 units**, padded to **1.5 as an explicitly-labelled constant with margin** —
    over-estimating only costs far-field density. Do not present a padded constant as a
    derivation, and do not use Hs itself (a 4σ crest-to-trough measure) as a one-sided bound.

# B. Camera-relative water positions (was task #23)

## Purpose and priority

Latent large-world correctness. **No consumer today** — nothing in this tree exceeds ~1,000
units from the origin, where all of this is below a last bit. Do it only when a large world
appears, and note the correct fix is engine-wide: water alone would be precise standing next
to jittering terrain.

## The measurement (protocol matters — the obvious version is wrong)

The spectral field is **exactly periodic across the cascade LCM of 960 units** (240/64/12 all
divide it), so the same sea at two offsets differing by a multiple of 960 must be identical.

- 960 → 307,200 units out: **227,458 px of 480,000 differ, RMSE 0.0146, PAE 0.302.**
- Attributed with a flat-surface control (`amplitude: 0`): the same offset moves **PAE 4/255**
  with no waves against **77/255** with them → it is the field lookup, not the view transform.
- Cause: `p = waterCamPos.xz + rd.xz * t`; at 3e5 fp32 quantises `p` to ~0.03 units, which is
  6% of the medium band's 0.5 m texel.
- The camera-snapped `oceanCascadeUv` shipped in 11.35 phase 3 fixed only the DIVISION's
  precision — worth 3% (230,054 → 227,458 px).

**Trap:** do NOT compare an origin frame against a shifted one. The fixture's ramp leaves
frame and its mip'd refraction resolve goes with it; that read 65% of the frame and meant
nothing. The scene format has **no model transform**, which is what forces the
two-shifted-frames shape.

## Fix recipe

Carry the lattice position relative to the eye, so error tracks distance from the CAMERA.

1. `oceanProjectedPosition` returns `rd.xz * t` (relative), not `waterCamPos.xz + rd.xz * t`.
2. `oceanCascadeUv(p, band)` becomes `fract((pRel + camMod) / period + 0.5)` with
   `camMod = camPos.xz - floor(camPos.xz / period) * period`. A per-frame constant, so its own
   quantisation is a uniform sub-texel shift rather than per-vertex jitter.
3. `oceanShoal` needs ABSOLUTE p for the bed lookup → `pRel + waterCamPos.xz`. Fine; the bed
   is a coarse 256² field.
4. Gerstner phase needs absolute p mod 2π/k → `k*dot(dir, pRel) + phaseOffset` with
   `phaseOffset = mod(k*dot(dir, camPos.xz), 2π)`. **Per OCTAVE** (4 fanned dirs,
   `ocean.glsl:442-446`), and the mod MUST be computed CPU-side in DOUBLE: at 3×10⁵ units,
   k·dot(dir, camPos) ≈ 3×10⁵ rad where fp32 spacing is ~0.03 rad — an fp32 mod re-introduces
   exactly the per-frame jitter being removed.
5. `viewPos.xyz = mat3(view) * worldRel` where **`worldRel = vec3(pRel.x,
   waterLevel − camPos.y, pRel.y) + disp * shoal`** — the full surface expression from
   `ocean.glsl:326`, NOT `waterLevel + disp.y` alone: dropping the horizontal displacement and
   the shoal factor deletes the chop from the raster. Valid because
   `view * worldAbs = mat3(view) * worldRel + view * camPos` and `view * camPos = 0`; using
   only the rotation is itself part of the fp32 win (view's translation column is −R·camPos,
   quantised at large camPos).
6. `CurrClip` / `PrevClip` need camera-relative matrices: columns 0-2 unchanged, column 3 =
   `M * vec4(camPos, 1)`. **Both** `uCurrViewProjNoJitter` and `uPrevViewProj`
   (`water_vert.glsl:11-12`) get the surgery — the jittered `projection` needs none, it
   multiplies the already-fixed viewPos. And **both columns use the CURRENT frame's camPos**,
   including the previous matrix: both relative positions share the current frame's origin, so
   `prevCol3 = uPrevViewProj * vec4(camPos_CURRENT, 1)`. Using the previous frame's camPos
   there is a bug that prints camera translation into the velocity buffer.
7. `WorldPos` reaching `water_frag` becomes relative; its only two `.xz` consumers (short band
   `:174`, caustics `crossing` `:321` — a world-space delta, translation-invariant) go through
   `oceanCascadeUv`, so rewriting that one shared include function serves every call site.
   `V = normalize(-ViewPos)` (`water_frag.glsl:209`) is pure view-space — untouched. The
   footprint probes are differences of two projected positions — translation-invariant,
   untouched.

# C. The SSS/MSAA resolve defect — the proper fix

## The rule that was broken

An MSAA colour resolve is a **box filter**, valid only for quantities linear in radiance.
`resolve_color_attachment` (`cetra/src/postfx.c:1992`) is a `glBlitFramebuffer` from the
multisample FBO, i.e. an averaging resolve, applied to every channel. Two channels are not
radiance:

- **Attachment 4's ALPHA is a categorical profile tag** (`pbr_frag.glsl` ~line 2020 writes
  `float(max(sssProfileIndex + 1, 0))`). The average of tag 2 and tag 0 is tag 1 — which does
  not mean "half of profile 2", it means **profile 1**. Meaning is destroyed, not degraded.
- **Aux `.z` is a position** (view Z). The average of two surfaces is a surface that exists
  nowhere.

`cetra/shaders/sss_pyr_seed_frag.glsl:28` then does an exact-integer test,
`int(d.a + 0.5) == profileTag`, and its comment claims *"an averaged tag can never select the
wrong profile"* — **false under MSAA**; the buffer it calls full-resolution is already a
resolve. And the comment is doubly wrong: it claims the test lives "here rather than in the
gather", but **the gather repeats the same defective compare** at
`sss_gather_frag.glsl:105` (`int(center.a + 0.5) != profileTag`), and reads aux `.z` directly
too (`:102`). Both shaders carry both defects; fix both, and the comment.

## The arithmetic that produced the reported artifact

`apps/tree` registers two profiles: leaf → tag 1, grass/flowers → tag 2. At 4× MSAA a grass
or petal pixel with 2 of 4 samples covered resolves to `(2+2+0+0)/4 = 1.0` → `int(1.5)` = **1
= the LEAF tag** → seeded into the leaf walk (radius 0.25 vs 0.15, different channel weights),
with its delta computed against the grass's own diffuse. 1-of-4 → 0.5 → also 1. So
**quarter- and half-covered pixels are systematically misfiled.**

Second amplifier: aux `.z` averaged with the sky's 0 (attachment 2 clears to zero,
`engine.c:102-106/:2408`) drives depth toward 0, so
`basePx = radius * projScale / depth` (`sss_gather_frag.glsl:114`) inflates — bounded by the
sample count (2-of-4 coverage → ×2, 1-of-4 → ×4), but enough to jump the gather to a far
coarser LOD and replace the petal with a broad neighbourhood average.

Accounts for every observation: **MSAA-only** (at 1 sample nothing is averaged),
**TAA-irrelevant** (a resolve artifact, not temporal), **worst on flowers** (small and bright,
so nearly every pixel is a silhouette pixel), **popping** (wind changes per-pixel coverage
every frame, so the tag crosses integer boundaries and the depth swings).

## The fix (amended after feasibility review, 2026-08-13 — all pass/FBO facts verified)

1. **Profile tag → the STENCIL buffer.** Storage is already paid for: `engine.c:622/:624`
   allocate `GL_DEPTH24_STENCIL8` (both sample counts), attached as `GL_DEPTH_STENCIL` at
   `:625`; **zero first-party `glStencil*` calls** — all 8 bits free; the frame clear
   **already clears stencil** (`engine.c:2400`); and the depth-resolve destination texture is
   **already `GL_DEPTH24_STENCIL8`** (`engine.c:1924`), so the resolve is one extra bit flag:
   add `GL_STENCIL_BUFFER_BIT` to `engine_resolve_scene_depth`'s blit (`engine.c:1960`).
   Formats match on both sides, NEAREST already used. Spec wording: the MSAA→1× depth/stencil
   resolve is *implementation-dependent in the GL spec, sample-select in practice* (a stencil
   bitmask has no meaningful mean).
   The per-profile pass structure maps one-to-one: the loop at `postfx.c:1536-1567` walks one
   profile per iteration with `profileTag = p+1` as a uniform in both seed and gather —
   replace both compares with `glStencilFunc(GL_EQUAL, p+1, 0xFF)`, ops KEEP,
   `glStencilMask(0)`. Both tag-consuming passes run at **full render res** (seed:
   `sss_pyr_fbo` mip 0, `postfx.c:1330-1345`; gather: `sss_delta_fbo`, `:1400/:1542`), so the
   stencil attaches at matching size; the downsample mips never touch the tag — detach or
   leave the test disabled there.
   **Note stencil texturing does not exist on GL 4.1** (`ARB_stencil_texturing` is 4.3) — the
   fixed-function test is the only consumer path, so the design is test-based by necessity.
2. **SSS depth → the depth buffer, via a linearize pass — NOT by sampling the resolved
   depth directly.** The naive form is a **GL 4.1 feedback loop**: sampling
   `scene_depth_texture` while the same texture object is attached to the draw FBO for the
   stencil test is undefined (§4.4.3; the write-mask carve-out arrived with GL 4.5 /
   `ARB_texture_barrier`, which macOS never reaches). The fix: **one once-per-frame full-res
   R32F view-Z linearize pass** — sample `scene_depth_texture` with nothing attached, write
   view-Z out — then seed and gather sample the R32F while stencil-testing against the
   depth-stencil. Strictly better than a second D24S8 copy: it also deletes the NDC→view
   conversion from both shaders (they want view-Z, which is what aux `.z` gave them).
   Sharing one depth-stencil across FBOs is already house practice (`engine.c:1986-1987`,
   scene + OIT).
3. **The write side needs a stencil policy for EVERY scene-FBO draw**, because nothing touches
   stencil today and so nothing gets it right by accident: opaque/masked draws REPLACE with
   their own ref (non-skin writes 0, so an occluder clears the tag — falls out naturally);
   skybox, shadow catcher, water, transparent/OIT, particles, and the `--depth-prepass`
   program each get `glStencilMask(0)` or a deliberate ref.
4. Mechanical deltas: the seed currently writes zeros for non-covered pixels, so under
   stencil-discard, mip 0 of both pyramid targets needs an explicit per-profile clear (two
   `glClearBufferfv`, cheap); the gather is already clear-then-additive, where discard ≡ +0.
5. With all of it done, attachment 4 carries only radiance, so its resolve is finally valid
   and its alpha is freed. No second consumer exists: `profileTag` appears in exactly the two
   shaders, `sss_diffuse_texture` binds in exactly two places (`postfx.c:1341/:1553`) — but
   `pbr_frag` must keep writing *something* to the channel while the attachment is bound.

## Deliberately not doing, and why

**Per-sample SSS on edge pixels** (Lauritzen, *Deferred Rendering for Current and Future
Rendering Pipelines*; The Order: 1886 did this). It is the complete answer — even after 1 and
2, an edge where two surfaces of the same profile meet still gets one blurred answer for two
surfaces. But it roughly doubles those passes on edges and needs `sampler2DMS`, which spec
11.17 rejected. **The industry's answer to this exact incompatibility was to stop
multisampling the G-buffer**: it is why MSAA left AAA after ~2013, and UE4/5, Frostbite,
Decima and id Tech 7 all run a 1-sample G-buffer with TAA. Recommend 1-sample + TAA for
anything using SSS — structurally immune, zero new code, already supported here.

## Audit the same root cause elsewhere

Write the rule down: **nothing categorical or positional may live in an MSAA-resolved colour
channel.** Confirmed instance: att4 alpha. Confirmed amplifier: aux `.z`. Untested suspects:
- **aux `.w` roughness** — averaging roughness is wrong for the same reason as normals; the
  correct operation is averaging the NDF (Toksvig/LEAN). Note the water's own 11.35 phase-2
  far-field handover already does exactly that, so the engine gets this right in one place and
  not in the resolve.
- **attachment 1's `.a` SSR marker** — a negative alpha whose magnitude is the catcher's edge
  falloff, so part categorical and part continuous, and averaged.

## Verification — corrected: a two-profile fixture EXISTS; the arm does not

**`assets/sss_scale_fixture.cscn` already authors two profiles** (`sss_skin_a`/`sss_skin_b`;
`gates.py:58` renders it, and `postfx.c:1533-1535`'s own comment says "every shipping scene
has one profile and the fixture has two"). What does not exist is two-profile **thin/small**
geometry and an **MSAA-vs-1-sample** comparison arm. Build that: assert the MSAA frame matches
the 1-sample frame within tolerance — the 1-sample render is ground truth because nothing is
averaged there. `--msaa <n>` exists and composes with `--taa` (`render.c:3041-3043`).

Scope facts that shape the verification: the interactive default is **already** 1-sample +
TAA (`render.c:3029-3040`), structurally immune — the defect lives on the **headless default**
(4× MSAA, TAA off) and explicit `--taa --msaa N`. The skin goldens run headless with no
`--msaa`/`--taa`, i.e. at 4× MSAA: they exercise the defective path but are single-profile

1. **`apps/tree` water clarity — re-scale to physical. DESIGNED; it is not a one-liner.**

   `absorption` is extinction per WORLD UNIT (`water.h:120-126`, `water_frag.glsl:48`, consumed
   once at `:344` as `exp(-waterAbsorption * path)` over a world-unit path). Clear seawater is
   ≈(0.45, 0.09, 0.06) per METRE — what `assets/water_fixture.cscn` authors, since its units
   *are* metres. Tree is ≈22 units/m and authors (0.10, 0.035, 0.020).

   **Two corrections to the framing.** The error is not a uniform 5×: per channel it is
   **4.9× / 8.6× / 7.3×** (luminance mean 6.4×). So tree's triple is not clear seawater at the
   wrong scale — it is a *flatter, less red-selective* spectrum (1 : 0.35 : 0.20 against
   seawater's 1 : 0.20 : 0.13), i.e. genuinely murkier water, which is the "lagoon" its comment
   claims. The change therefore fixes the scale error **and** replaces the murky spectrum with
   a clear one; the lagoon survives only in `scatter`. The existing comment ("a shorter sight
   line through the body than the library default") becomes **false** — it will be exactly the
   library default, in metres. Say so.

   **The companion change, without which this is half-done.** `WATER_MAX_PATH = 64`
   (`water_frag.glsl:89`) is a world-unit clamp whose own comment justifies it in *extinction
   lengths* ("past a few extinction lengths the exponential is already zero") — 3.84 optical
   depths at the fixture's blue, and **0.17** at tree's new blue. Tree's column already exceeds
   64 by r=475, so leaving the clamp gives T=(0.27, 0.77, 0.84) **uniformly with zero depth
   grading** past that radius — a flat translucent sheet, arguably worse than the bug. Make the
   budget scale-free: keep `WATER_MAX_PATH` as a FLOOR and add
   `WATER_MAX_OPTICAL_DEPTH = 3.84`, with the **weakest** channel setting the length
   (`maxPath = max(WATER_MAX_PATH, 3.84 / max(min3(waterAbsorption), 1e-4))`), applied at
   `:229` and `:288`. Pure-uniform, so it stays in uniform control flow and does not disturb
   the documented `fwidth`-before-`discard` invariant at `:279-282`.
   **Golden-safe by fp32 arithmetic, verified (2026-08-13):** `0.06f` and `3.84f` have
   identical significands with exponents 6 apart, so the division is **exactly 64.0f** — and
   so is the reciprocal-multiply lowering. The `max` floor is insurance, not load-bearing.
   Absorption has no CLI flag, and the one arm authoring different absorption (`water-cscn`,
   [2.2, 0.5, 0.35]) gives 3.84/0.35 ≈ 11 → the floor wins → 64 again. **All 22 arms pinned;
   the fixture change is an arithmetic identity — 0 px, not merely "safe".**
   Tree gets maxPath = 1,408 units (64 m). Deepest *vertical* column is 810.6, but the
   shader's `path` is the SLANT path, so a grazing ray to the deep bed (~2,600 units in-water)
   **is still truncated — deliberately, at a stated bound**: at the clamp the weakest channel
   carries exactly 3.84 optical depths, so truncation leaves T ≤ 2.15%, a C0-continuous ≤2%
   bed leak masked by waves/Fresnel/foam. Claim the bound, never "nothing is truncated".
   Grading becomes green-to-blue over the flank: T = 0.66/0.92/0.95 at 20 units →
   0.05/0.56/0.68 at the rim → ~0/0.04/0.11 at the seabed edge.
