# 13.58: a short occluder keeps its soft shadow

## Context

pcb's true-size part pictures showed a dark ghost of a resistor's printed marking on the board.
Bisected in cetra (`scratchpad/ghost.py`): it goes with soft shadows (PCSS) or with shadows off,
not with contact shadows, SSR, AO or TAA.

**The cause** is the cascade PCSS blocker search (`cetra/shaders/pbr_frag.glsl:839`):
`if (zTap < zPlane * CSM_BLOCKER_SEPARATION)` with `CSM_BLOCKER_SEPARATION 0.99` (`:743`).
- **The depths are measured from the cascade's light eye**, which sits `radius + scene_pad` back
  (`cetra/src/shadow.c:580`, `scene_pad = 0.5 * far_plane`, and the render app sets `far_plane`
  to 8 times the scene radius).
- **So "1% nearer" is about 4% of the scene's radius at any scale**: about 0.7 mm on pcb's board,
  and by the same arithmetic about 20 cm in a room 5 m across.
- **A shorter occluder fails the gate**, `blockerCount` stays 0, and the pixel returns fully lit.
  The resistor (0.45 mm) loses most of its soft shadow, and its ink (10 µm higher) clears the
  gate in places, which switches the shadow back on in the shape of the text.

**Measured on pcb's resistor, the board just under the body:**

| render | brightness |
|---|---|
| no shadows | 54.3 |
| PCF | 39.2 |
| PCSS, true size | 46.8, plus the ghost |
| PCSS, the old blow-up | 46.8 |

The old way loses the shadow too; it just never forms text.

**History:**
- Spec 10.5 asked for "a small separation epsilon" (`specs/10.5-cascade-shadow-policy.md:285-293`).
- The code shipped a multiplier instead.
- The 0.99 was measured only on 10.4's perspective area-light PCSS, which never shipped
  (`specs/10.4-area-light-shadow-quality.md:174-182`).
- No gate checks PCSS output against a known answer: `dir-shadow`'s arms all pass `--no-pcss`.
- The scale twin cannot see the bug, since the gate scales with the scene.

**The other thresholds measured as a fraction of the cascade's depth range:**
- the flat `shadowBias` (0.005 in depth01, `shadow.c:203`), used by the catcher, particles, water
  and the translucent front-layer gate (`include/csm.glsl:88,131,153`), and by the fog's cascade
  taps;
- `SHADOW_MAX_PLANE_BIAS` (0.01 in depth01, `include/receiver_plane.glsl:17`).

These are the same kind of rule. The plan measures them and fixes the ones that lose a shadow.

**Outcome:**
- Every soft-shadow threshold is stated in shadow-map texels, which is the tiles' rule already
  (`include/punctual_tiles.glsl:62-64`: `read.y - TILE_BIAS_TEXELS * tileTexelMetres(read.y)`).
- A short occluder keeps its shadow at any scale.
- pcb's pictures need no workaround.

## The rule

A blocker must be nearer the light than the receiver's plane by a margin in **texels of this
cascade's map**, not by a fraction of the eye's distance:

```glsl
float texelWorld = cascadeParams[layer].x * shadowTexelSize.x;   // one map texel, world units
if (zTap < zPlane - CSM_BLOCKER_TEXELS * texelWorld)
```

- **Exactness:** both `zTap` and `zPlane` are already linear world distances (`linearizeOrthoDepth`,
  `pbr_frag.glsl:713`), so the margin is a length and the scale twin stays exact.
- **Choosing `CSM_BLOCKER_TEXELS`:** it is set by measurement between two bars.
  - Too large drops short occluders, which `pcss-short` sees.
  - Too small lets a curved or grazing receiver block itself, which collapses the penumbra
    (10.4's failure), and which `pcss-self` and `pcss-penumbra` see.
- **If no constant clears both:** the margin grows with the tap's own distance in world units, as
  a curving receiver's deviation does: `max(k * texelWorld, s * length(off) * cascadeParams.x)`.
  That form is used only if the measurement asks for it.

The comment at `pbr_frag.glsl:735-742` is rewritten. Its "do not fix it into an absolute epsilon"
warning stays true: the margin is texel-relative, not absolute.

### Phase 2: the instrument, on master

- **`gen_short_occluder_fixture.py`**, on `gltf_pieces.py`:
  - the scene's radius is 5.3071 m, so the slabs are 2.65, 5.31, 10.6, 26.5 and 53.1 cm tall;
  - three scenes: the floor under a 35 degree sun, the same under an 8 degree sun, and the pieces
    with no floor over the sky's catcher, its sun in the same place;
  - every sun stands at azimuth 0, so every shadow falls toward -z.
- **The `pcss` group**:
  - every box is read off the floor plane through `_projector`;
  - each view is tilted about 10 degrees off the vertical, since `_projector` builds its basis
    from world up;
  - darkness is measured on linear light: the light lost in a box behind a slab, against open
    floor in the gap beside it.
- **`catcher-short` is held against the tallest slab's catcher shadow**, not against a floor mesh
  under PCF as planned: the catcher composites over the sky's ground, so its darkness and a lit
  floor's are not the same quantity.

**Master:**

| arm | 0.5% | 1% | 2% | 5% | 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pcss-short`, PCSS / PCF darkness | 0.111 / 0.744 | 0.110 / 0.745 | 0.080 / 0.736 | 0.734 / 0.734 | 0.700 / 0.700 |
| `catcher-short`, darkness | 0.010 | 0.012 | 0.403 | 0.388 | 0.378 |

- **`pcss-self`:** open floor 1.0000, the sphere's lit side 1.0000. It passes on master, as
  expected: a receiver blocking itself shrinks a penumbra, it does not darken a lit surface. It
  stays as the floor under any margin.
- **`pcss-penumbra`:** the pillar's penumbra is 3.03 cm under PCSS against 1.20 under PCF. 3.03
  is `PCSS_PENUMBRA_MASTER`.
- **Under the 8 degree sun, PCF and PCSS agree on every slab** (0.377, 0.373, 0.367, 0.363; the
  tallest's shadow runs out of frame). At grazing the path from an occluder to the floor along
  the light is 7.2 times its height and clears the gate. So `SHADOW_MAX_PLANE_BIAS` loses nothing
  here and phase 5 leaves it alone.

**What decides phase 5:** the catcher loses the 0.5% and 1% slabs. Its flat `shadowBias` (0.005
of a depth range near 42 m) is about 21 cm along the light. Phase 5 is the flat bias.

### Phase 3: the blocker margin in texels

- **The gate:** `zTap < zPlane - separation`, with
  `separation = CSM_BLOCKER_TEXELS * frustumWidth * shadowTexelSize.x`, which is one texel of the
  sampled cascade's map. `CSM_BLOCKER_SEPARATION` and its comment are gone; the new comment says
  why the margin is counted in texels.
- **At 1 texel every soft shadow equals its PCF one**: 0.744/0.744, 0.745/0.745, 0.736/0.736,
  0.734/0.734 and 0.700/0.700. The pillar's penumbra is unchanged at 3.03 cm.

**The margin was bracketed:**

| margin | `pcss-short` | `pcss-self` | `pcss-penumbra` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 texels | pass | pass | 3.03 cm |
| 0.25 | pass | pass | 3.03 cm |
| 1 (shipped) | pass | pass | 3.03 cm |
| 2 | pass | pass | 3.03 cm |
| 8 | **fail**: the 0.5% slab back at 0.111 against 0.744 | pass | 3.03 cm |

- **Nothing on this fixture bounds the margin from below:**
  - the plane extrapolation keeps a flat receiver's own texels out of the blocker search;
  - a convex receiver curves away from the light;
  - so 10.4's failure, the receiver counted as its own blocker, does not reproduce on this path.
    10.4 measured it on a perspective area light, which never shipped.
- **The upper bound is resolution, not scale.** An occluder needs about the margin's worth of
  texels along the light. The 0.5% slab, 4.6 cm along the light, keeps its shadow at 2 texels and
  loses it at 8.
- **One texel is the cached tiles' margin (`TILE_BIAS_TEXELS`)**, so the two paths state one rule.
  Taking more than 0 also covers error in the plane gradient, which comes from screen-space
  derivatives.

**Verified:**
- **At 2 texels:** `scale-twin` (every row 0 px), `catcher`, `moon`, `shadow-lag`,
  `translucent-offpath` and `contact` pass.
- **At the shipped 1 texel:** `pcss` was re-run. The twin is exact for any margin counted in
  texels.

### Phase 5: the catcher

- **The planned form could not work.** A flat bias counted in texels cannot serve both cases:
  - the catcher's 5x5 kernel reaches 3 texels out, so a real ground lying in the catcher's plane
    needs about 4.3 texels of bias under a 35 degree sun, and 21 under an 8 degree one;
  - the 0.5% slab's path along the light is about 4 texels of the outermost map.

  The old flat bias was about 20 texels, which is why the catcher never shadowed itself, and why
  it dropped short occluders.
- **What shipped:** the catcher's receiver is a plane by construction, so `catcher_frag.glsl` now
  compares every tap against the plane's own depth there. That is `receiverPlaneBias` over the
  plane's screen derivatives with the `SHADOW_PLANE_BIAS_FLOOR`, exactly as `pbr_frag`'s PCF
  compares against a receiver's plane. A surface lying in the plane cannot shadow it, so the bias
  is the depth floor.
- **The derivatives are taken once in `main()`**: inside the per-light function they would follow
  an early return.
- **It lives in `catcher_frag.glsl`, not `csm.glsl`.** `pbr_frag` includes `csm.glsl` before
  `receiver_plane.glsl`, so `csm.glsl` cannot include it. Keeping the change in the catcher also
  leaves the particles' and the water's code byte-for-byte as it was.
- **They keep the flat bias:** air and a refracted bed have no plane to extrapolate, and neither
  was measured.

**Measured:**
- **`catcher-short`:** 0.308, 0.401, 0.403, 0.388 and 0.378, every slab now against the tallest's.
  The 0.5% slab is the lighter, as about 4 texels of the coarse outermost map.
- **`catcher`:** churn 76,911 px against 76,936 before.
- **`catcher-transparency`:** passes.
- **The goldens:** the catcher moved none of them; the same four moved by the same counts as
  phase 3.
