# 11.92 — The band that is its own parent

Phase 3 of the Tier 5 performance programme (11.89 carries the shared evidence base; 11.90 was
phase 1, 11.91 phase 2).

## The programme's plan for this phase is wrong, and the data says so

Booked as: *"Give `cluster_build.cpp` the shrink guard `lod.c` already has. Five lines in one
loop."* `lod.c:95-96` refuses a level that fails to shrink by 15%:

```c
if ((float)count > (float)previous * LOD_MIN_SHRINK)
    break;
```

Transplanting that is the wrong repair, for three reasons this spec establishes before writing
any code.

## What is actually there

`--cluster-probe` on forest, all 20 clustered prototypes, triangles per band:

| prototype | band 0 | band 1 | band 2 | band 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trunks (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10) | 16344–17116 | 976–1053 | 241–261 | 57–64 |
| canopies (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11) | 12260–12756 | **identical to band 0** | exactly band 0 / 2 | 3067–5495 |
| rocks (12–19) | 1280 | **1280** | **1280** | 320–640 |

**22 of 80 bands emit their parent's exact triangle count** — 6 canopies at band 1, 8 rocks at
bands 1 and 2. 27.5% of the ladder.

## Why the `break` rule is the wrong transplant

**1. It would delete bands that are not degenerate.** A canopy's band 2 is exactly half of band 0
— a real, useful coarse cut. `break` at band 1 discards band 2 and band 3 with it. Worse, it
would take `lod_levels` to 1, which `mesh_build_cluster_lod` treats as failure (`if
(mesh->lod_levels <= 1 || packed.empty()) return false`), so `finalize_mesh` would fall back to
`mesh_build_lod_chain` and every canopy in the scene would silently leave the DAG. A chain and a
DAG are different surfaces; that is not a performance change, it is a content change.

**2. A degenerate band is not a builder defect.** `lod.c`'s levels are independent
simplifications at decreasing target counts, so one that fails to shrink means the simplifier gave
up and the level is waste. The DAG's bands are not that. Band `b` is *the coarsest cut whose error
fits the limit at distance band `b`*, and `CLUSTER_BAND_BEGIN = {FLT_MAX, 0.045, 0.022, 0.011}`
sets those limits. Band 1 equalling band 0 means **no group in the DAG simplifies within band 1's
error budget** — so drawing band 0's triangles there is the correct answer, not a failure to
produce one. Foliage cards and a low-poly rock behaving this way is exactly what a boundary- and
seam-locked simplifier should do. **Refusing the band would draw geometry coarser than the error
limit permits at that distance.**

**3. It does not fix either thing the band actually costs.** Those are index memory and the batch
key, and `break` addresses neither for the bands it keeps.

## What a degenerate band really costs

- **Index memory.** Band 1 is a byte-for-byte second copy of band 0. Canopies: 12,740 triangles ×
  3 × 4 B ≈ 153 KB each, six of them. Rocks carry two copies each. ~1.2 MB total — real, and the
  smaller half.
- **The batch key.** `draw_run_key_equal` keys on `(mesh, lod)`, because one draw submits one
  index range. Two canopies at band 0 and band 1 draw **identical triangles from identical
  offsets** and still cannot share a draw. That is the half worth fixing.

## The fix: alias, don't refuse

Keep every band. Where a band's cut is identical to its predecessor's, point it at the same range
rather than appending a copy, and make selection collapse onto the canonical index.

1. **In `cluster_build.cpp`**, before appending a band's indices, compare them with the previous
   band's range. If equal, set `offset[band] = offset[band-1]`, `count[band] = count[band-1]`, do
   not grow `packed`, and carry on to the next band.

   **Compare CONTENT, not count.** `lod.c` compares counts because each of its levels is a fresh
   simplification and the question is "did this shrink". Here the question is "is this literally
   the same cut", and two different cuts can coincidentally share a triangle count. A count test
   would alias two distinct index ranges onto one and draw the wrong geometry — a plausible frame,
   which is the failure mode this programme keeps meeting.

2. **In `select_lod` (`draw_list.c`)**, after the existing clamp, walk the level down while it
   names the same range as the level below it. That merges the batch key with no new state: the
   range pair already encodes the aliasing, so nothing has to be stored or kept in agreement.

The picture is **unchanged by construction** — every distance still draws the exact triangles it
draws today, because an alias returns the same `(offset, count)` the copy did.

## What this is expected to buy, and the honest doubt

Unknown, and the spec will not guess. The programme has now twice booked a phase at a number that
measurement deleted (11.91's 105 MB/frame was stale before it was read; its arena's upper bound
was zero), so:

**Measure the draw count first.** The win exists only where instances of one prototype at band 0
and band 1 are ADJACENT in submission order — a merged key does nothing for items the order keeps
apart. Morton order and 11.90's caster regroup both make adjacency likely, but likely is not
measured. `forest-order` currently reports **1667 draws** on the camera lane and
`forest-shadow-order` **645** on the shadow lane; those are the numbers to beat, and if they do
not move, this phase closes the way 11.91's arena did.

The index-memory saving is certain but small, and would not on its own justify the change.

## A related defect, found on the way and NOT fixed here

`CLUSTER_BAND_BEGIN` in `cluster_build.cpp` and `LOD_SWITCH` in `draw_list.c` are the same three
numbers written twice, in two languages, and `cluster_build.cpp:59` already says so in a comment
that calls it a defect. It is not this spec's — but a spec touching both files should say it saw
it, and anything that changes one table has to change the other or the builder cuts for distances
the selector never asks about.

## Steps

1. Content-compare the bands in `cluster_build.cpp`; alias rather than append.
2. Collapse the level in `select_lod` onto the canonical range.
3. Extend `--cluster-probe`'s output to name aliased bands, so the arm below can read them.
4. **A gate arm asserting no emitted band duplicates its predecessor's range** — the analogue of
   `cluster-seal`, and the thing that keeps this from regressing silently.
5. Measure draws on both lanes against the two numbers above.

## What it did

**Draws fall, geometry does not move, and the frame does not care.**

At the gate's own wide framing (`FOREST_CAM_WIDE`), master against this branch, both measured
fresh in the same session:

| | camera draws | shadow draws | triangles | instances |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| master | 1667 | 645 | 36,051,401 | 3971 |
| this branch | **1028** | **612** | 36,051,401 | 3971 |

**−38% camera draws, −5% shadow draws, identical triangles, identical instances.** Two runs each,
byte-identical — this arm has no noise floor to speak of.

At forest's default framing, release, 120 headless frames:

| | master | after |
|---|---|---|
| opaque draws | 928 | 758 |
| shadow draws | 730 | 692 |
| total draws | 1659 | 1451 |
| CPU opaque | 6.89 | 6.18 |
| CPU shadow | 4.92 | 4.73 |
| opaque GPU | 146.8 | 147.2 |
| FRAME wall | 199.8 | 199.4 |

**The frame does not move**, and the spec is not going to dress that up. 208 fewer draws and ~0.9
ms less CPU submission, against a frame whose opaque pass is 147 ms of shading. This is a
submission-side win on a GPU-bound scene, which is the same conclusion 11.91 reached and for the
same reason: **everything left on the CPU side of this frame is small.** It will matter on a scene
that is draw-bound, and forest is not one.

What makes it worth shipping anyway is that it costs nothing to keep — no memory, no new state,
no per-frame work — and it removes the duplicate index copies as a side effect.

### A correction the control caught

The first comparison said triangles moved +2,604. They did not. The baseline figure was read off
an earlier full-suite run rather than from a fresh control, and when master was actually re-run at
this arm it also reported 36,051,401. Nothing changed; the number I was comparing against came
from a different run context. **The same class of error this programme keeps finding**, caught
this time only because "an alias returns the same range, so triangles cannot move" made the delta
impossible rather than merely surprising — an expectation precise enough to be violated is what
turned a plausible number into a question.

The bisect that chased it is worth keeping too: with the builder aliasing on but the selection
collapse off, draws stayed at master's 1667. **All of the draw win is the collapse**; the builder
half only saves memory.

### `cluster-batch` changed from an equality to an inequality

It asserted `DAG draws == chain draws`. That is now 1107 against 1243, because aliasing merges
instances the chain keeps in separate levels — the DAG batches strictly better.

Relaxed to `<=` rather than retuned to the new number. The failure it exists to catch is a
per-instance cut, which makes the DAG's count EXPLODE, and `<=` still catches that. Holding the
equality would assert the DAG must batch no better than the chain, which is not a property anyone
wants. The clause carrying the weight is `same_inst`: fewer draws carrying FEWER instances is lost
geometry, and that is still pinned exactly.

### What `cluster-alias` can and cannot see

The arm compares the builder's alias mask against triangle-count equality. That is weaker than the
builder's own test, which is a `memcmp` of the index data — the probe only prints counts. So the
arm answers "did the mechanism fire on the bands whose counts match", and the memcmp is what
guarantees those bands are genuinely the same cut. Stated because the two are easy to confuse, and
because an arm that could only ever compare counts should not be read as validating the content
test.

## Verification

- **0 px on all 29 goldens**, by construction rather than by hope: an alias returns the same range
  the copy did, so every distance draws what it drew before. Any movement means the content
  compare admitted two different cuts, and is a bug rather than a rebake.
- `scripts/gates.py --only overdraw --only cluster --only forest` during; whole suite at the end.
- **`prepass-crossover` is red on unmodified master on this host** (11.91 measured it three times
  a side: master PASS/FAIL/PASS, branch FAIL/FAIL/PASS, same mean penalty). Expect it to be noise
  here too, and check it the same way rather than re-running until it is green.
