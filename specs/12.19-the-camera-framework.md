# Spec 12.19 — The camera framework

## Context

`docs/game-engine-roadmap.md:270` says the camera framework is **Absent**, and gives the reason
that matters more than the missing features: **"A hand-rolled camera also decides what the
CONTROLS mean"** — a decision that flipped twice (12.13 world-aligned, 12.17 camera-relative) and
was invisible to the suite both times, while two documents described the opposite of what shipped
for four specs.

The row's "every app hand-rolls its own" is really two facts, and the second is the diagnosis:

- **Four apps already share one camera** — `MouseDragController` (`cetra/src/app.c:16-197`), which
  drives `camera_orbit` / `camera_pan` / `camera_zoom_toward_target` / `camera_move_forward` /
  `camera_strafe` and is the live camera for render, tree, spores, and gametest under
  `--no-follow-cam`. It is a camera named after a mouse and filed under "app helpers".
- **Three apps wrote a second one anyway**, and `apps/forest/src/forest.c:254-259` says why in as
  many words: *"There is no follow-camera helper in cetra -- app.h offers only the mouse-drag
  orbit controller, which orbits a fixed point rather than a moving one."*

The measured duplication that follows from that:

| duplicated behaviour | copies |
|---|---|
| yaw/pitch orbit about a focus, `eye = focus - dir(yaw,pitch)*d` | 4, three sign conventions (`gametest.c:3813`, `forest.c:2899`, `player.c:123`, `render.c:4000`) |
| yaw/pitch integrated from `look_x`/`look_y` at a rate, pitch clamped | 3 — and `LOOK_YAW_RATE 1.8f` / `LOOK_PITCH_RATE 1.2f` are **byte-identical** between gametest and forest |
| a movement basis derived from the camera yaw | 3, three sign conventions |
| `--cam-eye/--cam-target/--cam-up` parse + both-or-neither + apply | 4, each differing in desc-vs-camera, once-vs-every-frame, origin-shift-aware or not |
| "stand the other writer down so two things do not fight over the pose" | 5, each reasoned out independently |
| framing a scene from its bounds | 1 in-app, and **no engine-side helper exists at all** |

**Outcome: one camera implementation in the tree; what it does is a field with a name, what the
controls mean is a value it publishes, and a drag can be asserted by a machine for the first
time.**

**On effort, honestly.** The roadmap budgets *"~1 week for a camera system"*. This is that plus a
rail, plus a scripted-pointer seam, plus player settings, plus converting seven apps and deleting
a public type — call it closer to two. The alternative is shipping the rig and leaving the
conversion half-done, which is how there came to be two camera implementations in the first
place.

## Decisions taken (do not relitigate)

- **Scope** — rig + published movement basis + blend between cameras + shake + a rail/spline
  anchor. The sequencer and the dialogue system stay absent; the roadmap itself says a sequencer
  is its own project.
- **`MouseDragController` is replaced and deleted.** `app.h` is on the public allowlist
  (`cetra/CMakeLists.txt:33-34`) and `apps/pcb` builds against it from its own repository (spec
  11.105), so this is a **breaking change to a public header**, taken deliberately. Say so in the
  spec and in `AGENTS.md`.
- **2D keeps its own maths and joins only the ownership rule.** `CanvasController`'s API,
  arithmetic and cursor-anchored zoom are untouched; it writes its pose *through* a rig so that
  who-owns-the-camera is one rule tree-wide. A 2D camera is not a 3D camera with the angles
  zeroed: zoom is `ortho_height` (a projection field, where 3D zoom is a pose field), 2D zoom is
  anchored on the cursor and has no 3D analogue, and rotation is not unused but **forbidden**
  (`camera.h:57-58`).
- **A scripted pointer seam ships with this**, mirroring `input_set_pad_script`. Without it the
  whole evidence for rewriting four apps' camera is "it feels the same".
- **Player-facing camera settings ship with this** — invert-Y, look sensitivity, FOV, and a
  motion-reduction switch — in `GameSettings` and on 12.2's settings screen.

## Design

### `cetra/src/camera_rig.c/h` — a new public header

Engine-level, following **the UI precedent** (12.2) rather than the game framework's: it takes its
input as **plain values**, touches no GL, no physics, no clock and no `game/` type, so `render`
and `shapes` — which never call `game_run` — can use it, and so every arm asserts it **with no
window**, exactly as `ui_layout` is asserted.

### The core is one closed form, and there is no mode enum for it

A rig is **an anchor, an arm and an aim**:

```c
look = anchor + look_lift*Y                      // the point it aims at
eye  = look + eye_lift*Y - dir(yaw, pitch)*dist  // where it sits
if (dist < eps) look = eye + dir                 // first person aims along a direction,
                                                 // not at a point
```

That single form reproduces **all four** hand-rolled cameras: gametest's follow (anchor = player,
`eye_lift` raised while `look` is not — the look-down bias that must be preserved or the two menu
goldens move), forest's orbit and its first person (one `dist`, 0 or `CAM_DISTANCE`), tree's
walker camera, and render's viewer orbit about a fixed anchor. A **pinned pose** is not a mode
either — `camera_rig_set_pose` derives anchor/arm/aim from an eye and a target, which is the
derivation `_camera_sync_orbit` (`camera.c:12-24`) already performs.

The first-person degenerate case is written down rather than smoothed over, and the one-unit
target is not arbitrary: both existing first-person cameras already put it one unit out
(`forest.c:2913`, `player.c:123`), and `distance(position, look_at)` is read downstream by DOF
autofocus (`engine.c:2013`), the near-clip recompute and `max_distance`.

**One enum with two values**, for where the eye comes from: `ORBIT` (the form above) and `RAIL`
(a point sampled along an authored polyline at `rail_t`, aiming at a stated point). A rail
*replaces* the arm rather than complicating it, which is what keeps blend and shake working over
both unchanged.

### What the controls mean — the whole point of the roadmap row

```c
// What the controls MEAN under this camera. A rig that steers them publishes the
// yaw its input frame is read in; one that does not answers false, and the game
// reads its input in world axes.
bool camera_rig_move_basis(const CameraRig* rig, float* out_yaw);
```

with `bool steers_controls` as a SETTINGS field **defaulting to false**. That default is 12.17's
decision preserved exactly and made nameable: the follow camera steers, while a pinned pose and
the drag orbit deliberately do not, because *"pinning a camera states a POSE and should not
silently rotate the controls"* (`docs/cli-reference.md:885-893`). gametest's rotation
(`gametest.c:3152-3154`) becomes a read of this, and `pad-camera-relative` stays green unchanged.

### Four behaviours layered on the form

1. **Arm response.** A 0..1 signal the app supplies, smoothed with **asymmetric** rise/fall rates,
   lerping `(dist, eye_lift)` between a near and a far pair. This is gametest's drop response
   (`gametest.c:3796-3809`) with the *meaning* of the signal removed — which also delivers 12.13's
   out-of-scope "a framing that reacts to speed" for free, since the rig does not care what the
   signal is.
2. **An occlusion probe seam.** `camera_rig_set_probe(rig, fn, user)` answers "how far along this
   ray from the aim point is clear"; the rig applies the skin and the minimum arm. gametest's Jolt
   static raycast answers it directly. **Forest's terrain floor-clamp converts to it**, and that is
   an improvement rather than a translation: shortening the arm preserves the aim where clamping
   the eye's Y silently changes it.
3. **Blend between cameras.** `camera_rig_blend_from(rig, &pose, seconds)` — the animator's
   crossfade shape, over **poses** and not parameters, because blending yaw/pitch/distance between
   two rigs with different anchors is not defined.
4. **Shake.** An amplitude decaying exponentially over a seeded value noise on the rig's own
   elapsed time — deterministic, so a headless run reproduces it — scaled by `shake_scale`, which
   the motion-reduction setting drives to 0.

### The engine seam, and one field deleted

`engine_set_camera_rig(engine, rig)` (borrowed). The engine runs the live rig **immediately before
`_engine_derive_camera` (`engine.c:2857`)**, which extends spec 11.107's rule rather than fighting
it: *the app writes the rig's inputs in its pre-render hook and nothing else; the engine runs the
rig and derives the matrices.*

Installing a second rig stands the first down and logs it by name — the five hand-rolled
"stand-down" guards become that one rule, and it is the rule `CanvasController` joins.

`Engine.camera_mode` and `CameraMode` are **deleted**. The engine core never reads them; the only
three consumers are `app.c`'s auto-orbit gate and arrow-key branch and `gui.c`'s radio pair, all
of which are being replaced. Costs a `config_snapshot` row removal (`config_snapshot.c:392`) and
its `_Static_assert` (`:203`).

### The scripted pointer seam

Mirror `GamepadReadFn` / `input_set_pad_script`: a pointer reader on `engine->input` whose default
asks GLFW, plus `engine_set_pointer_script(engine, path)` replaying a text script in the pad
script's grammar (`0-60 drag=1 dx=3`, `61 wheel=-1`). `--pointer-script <file>` on render, tree
and shapes. GLFW's real device path is what the seam sits on and nothing in the suite exercises
it — the same statement `docs/verification.md` already carries for the gamepad and the audio
device, and it goes in the same place.

### Settings (12.2's table, 12.15's pattern)

`GameSettings` gains `look_sensitivity`, `invert_look_y`, `fov_degrees`, `reduce_motion`, as rows
in the existing descriptor table, plus settings-screen controls. FOV is today a *renderer tuning*
value (`config_snapshot.c:597`, `-F/--fov`), so the precedence is stated rather than discovered:
**defaults → settings file → CLI → snapshot**, matching the chain `.cscn` already has.
`reduce_motion` sets `shake_scale` to 0 and closes the camera half of the roadmap's Accessibility
row (`:274`); the post-stack half stays open and is named as such.

## Phases

Branch `12.19-the-camera-framework` off master; the spec commits first, alone. Each phase ends
with its arms green and **the app run** where the phase changed what it draws.

**Phase 0 — look at it, and record what must not change.** Walk render, tree, shapes, gametest
and forest and write down what each camera does. Then build the **pointer seam first** and use it
to record the OLD `MouseDragController`'s poses under three scripted gestures (a drag, a
shift-pan, a wheel zoom) — before any of it is deleted. That recording is what `cam-drag-identity`
asserts against in Phase 8, and it is the only way this spec can show the rewrite did not change
the viewer camera. Write `cam-orbit-closed` against a rig that does not exist yet: it must be
**red**.

**Phase 1 — the rig core.** Anchor, arm, aim, the pose setter, the pitch clamp. Pure: no GL, no
Engine. Arms: `cam-orbit-closed`, `cam-pitch-clamp`, `cam-first-person`.

**Phase 2 — the behaviours.** Arm response, probe seam, blend, shake, rail. Arms:
`cam-arm-response`, `cam-probe-shorten`, `cam-blend`, `cam-shake-decay`, `cam-rail`. Reuse rather
than rewrite: check `geometry.c`'s bezier evaluator before writing a spline sampler.

**Phase 3 — the engine seam.** `engine_set_camera_rig`, the update before `_engine_derive_camera`,
the one-writer claim, `camera_mode` deleted with its snapshot row and its GUI radio. Arm:
`cam-one-writer`. Run render — nothing should have changed yet.

**Phase 4 — render and spores, and the deletion.** The viewer rig replaces `MouseDragController`,
which is deleted from `app.h`/`app.c`. Auto-orbit and its breathing distance become rig fields; a
shared `camera_rig_frame_sphere(rig, centre, radius, fit)` replaces render's duplicated framing.
`--cam-eye` / `--cam-at` pin the rig. **The 33 goldens must be 0 px** — headless has no drag and no
auto-orbit, so the pose is the auto-framing's, but `camera_enforce_max_distance` must still run.
Assert 0 px rather than re-baking. Then **run render and drag it**.

**Phase 5 — gametest.** One rig: anchor the player entity, `steers_controls = true`, the drop
signal into the arm response, the Jolt static raycast as the probe. `--no-follow-cam` and
`--cam-eye` become two more rigs rather than two more branches. Deletes `follow_camera_update`
(104 lines), 6 file statics and 10 macros. `pad-camera-relative` must stay green and the two menu
goldens 0 px. **Then run it** — walk, turn, pitch, fall off the plate, walk into a wall.

**Phase 6 — forest and tree, and the canvas.** Forest's dual-mode camera becomes one rig with a
distance toggle and a terrain probe; `--cam-set`'s origin-shift correction applies to the anchor.
Tree's `Player` keeps the walking and loses the camera half. `CanvasController` writes its
unchanged pose through a rig. Re-check the `forest`, `region` and `island` groups — residency
reads the camera.

**Phase 7 — settings.** The four fields, the table rows, the settings-screen controls, the stated
precedence. Arm: `cam-settings-roundtrip`, two-sided on invert-Y.

**Phase 8 — the arms that read the apps.** `cam-basis` (a steering rig publishes its yaw; a pinned
rig and the viewer rig answer false — **the arm that makes 12.13/12.17 unrepeatable**),
`cam-drag` (a scripted drag orbits by the expected angle; shift-drag pans; a wheel notch zooms —
the first automated check of the viewer camera in this repo's history), `cam-drag-identity`
against Phase 0's recording, `cam-shake-identity` (`shake_scale = 0` is **bit-identical** to no
shake, the way `ui-theme-identity` is). New group `camera` in `GATE_GROUPS`; `gate-docs` asserts
the documented arm list is the one that runs.

**Phase 9 — the docs.** Roadmap row `:270` → Done, spec 12.19, with the sequencer and the dialogue
system still explicitly absent; the Accessibility row `:274`; Part 3's ordering line `:384`;
`docs/verification.md:795-799` (the follow camera's rotation is covered now) and its
determinism-by-source table; `docs/cli-reference.md:869-893` (the camera-relative paragraph
becomes a rig field, and the warning about four specs of drift is kept); `AGENTS.md`'s Key Files
(a `camera_rig.c/h` row, the `app.c/h` row losing a controller), the gametest / forest / tree app
rows, and the public-header break called out by name.

## Verification

Scoped per phase:

```bash
python3 scripts/gates.py --only camera       # phases 1, 2, 3, 7, 8
python3 scripts/gates.py --only gamepad      # phase 5 -- pad-camera-relative must stay green
python3 scripts/gates.py --only forest,region,island   # phase 6 -- residency reads the camera
python3 scripts/gates.py --only config       # phase 3 -- a snapshot row was removed
```

End of job: `./build.sh`, the full `gates.py`, `goldens.py`.

**The numbers that decide it**, not a green suite:

- **33/33 goldens at 0 px.** The render app's framing changes hands; headless exercises no drag,
  so this is assertable rather than re-bakeable.
- **`cam-drag-identity`**: the new viewer rig reproduces the recorded old poses under three
  scripted gestures, to a stated tolerance. The latch (`start_theta`/`start_phi`/`start_distance`/
  `start_look_at`/`start_position`) is deleted in favour of per-frame deltas, so the tolerance is
  float accumulation over a drag and must be quoted rather than assumed zero.
- **`pad-camera-relative` green throughout**, and `cam-basis` red when `steers_controls` is
  flipped — the two together are what a third silent flip would have to get past.
- **`shake_scale = 0` bit-identical** to a rig with no shake.

**The watch**, which no arm replaces:

```bash
./out/bin/render -m assets/models/puppet.gltf     # drag, shift-drag, wheel, arrows, WASD
./out/bin/gametest                                # walk, turn, pitch, fall off the plate, wall
./out/bin/gametest --no-follow-cam                # the viewer rig under a game
./out/bin/forest --player                         # first person, V to orbit, and back
./out/bin/tree --player                           # arrows turn, WASD walks
./out/bin/shapes                                  # pan, wheel-zoom on the cursor, drag a node
```

**What this spec does NOT get to claim.** There is no sequencer and no cutscene track: a rail is
an anchor a caller drives, with nothing in this tree that scripts one over time, so "a scripted
camera moment" is *possible* rather than *authored*. The pointer seam covers everything above the
device, and **GLFW's real mouse path is still exercised by nothing** — the same hole the gamepad
and the audio device already carry, recorded in the same place. And the motion-reduction switch
reaches the camera only; the post stack's motion blur is untouched.

## As built

### Phase 0 — the pointer seam, and what pressing a mouse found

The seam went in first, as planned, and it works: `--pointer-script` replays a frame-range script
through the engine's own pointer path. A 200-pixel drag on `apps/render` turns `phi` by
**0.400000 rad** against a predicted `200 x 0.002`, and a shift-drag of (200, 100) moves the
target by **0.352061** against a predicted `hypot(200,100) x 3.14893 x 0.0005 = 0.352061`. Six
decimal places, on a path no test had ever reached.

**The recorded-baseline plan was dropped for something better.** The plan had `cam-drag-identity`
comparing the rewritten camera against poses recorded from the old one. Both forms turned out to
be exactly predictable, so the three arms assert the **closed form** instead — a recording says
the camera does what it used to, where a closed form says what it *should* do and a reader can
check the arithmetic. Both constants were falsified independently: a 5% change to `sensitivity`
reddens `cam-drag-orbit` alone (0.420 against 0.400), a 10% change to the pan rate reddens
`cam-drag-pan` alone (0.387267 against 0.352061), and neither touches the other.

**Three defects the seam exposed, none of them mine:**

1. **A press did not zero the drag offset.** `drag_fb_x/y` kept the *previous* drag's value until
   the first cursor move arrived, so every drag after the first applied a stale offset for one
   frame — a visible jump. The first drag of a session was fine, which is why it survived:
   `init_input_state` zeroes it once and nothing ever reset it again. Fixed here, because the
   seam's whole purpose is a baseline that means something.
2. **`apps/render` has no scroll handling at all.** The wheel does nothing in the 3D viewer;
   zoom is arrow-keys-only, through `mouse_drag_on_key`. Only the 2D canvas consumes scroll.
   Nobody noticed because nothing could turn a wheel. The viewer rig should take the wheel in
   Phase 4 as a stated addition rather than a silent one.
3. **`apps/shapes` has no headless mode**, so `CanvasController` — the only 2D camera in the tree
   — cannot be driven by any automated run. Phase 6 needs `-x`/`-f` on shapes before the canvas's
   conversion can be checked by anything but a hand.

The grammar is shared rather than copied: `frame_script.c/h` now owns the line/range/token parser
and the pad kept only its device half, so "the pointer script uses the pad script's grammar" is a
fact about one function rather than a claim about two.

### Phase 1 — the rig core, and the mode enum that was not needed

The closed form survived contact: `look = anchor + look_lift*Y`, `eye = look + eye_lift*Y -
dir*dist`, and **no mode enum at all**. A follow is an anchor that moves, a viewer orbit is one
that does not, first person is `dist` 0, and a pinned pose is `camera_rig_set_pose` deriving all
three — so what varies between the four hand-rolled cameras is which fields a caller writes, not
which branch runs.

Two things the writing settled that the plan had only asserted:

- **`camera_rig_set_pose` must zero both lifts**, and that is a statement rather than a tidy-up: a
  lift shapes a *derived* pose relative to something it follows, and a pose somebody stated has
  already been shaped. `cam-pose-adopt` hands the rig lifts of 9 before adopting, so an
  implementation that kept them displaces the very pose it was given. The adopt round-trips
  through `asin` and `atan2`, so it is exact to **2.4e-7**, not to the bit — quoted rather than
  assumed.
- **`eye_lift` raises the eye and not the aim**, which tilts the view down by an amount the pitch
  does not state. Writing it the "tidy" way — lifting both — is a real temptation and it is wrong:
  it is `apps/gametest`'s follow camera, and the two menu goldens frame the player through exactly
  that bias.

Five arms, all with no window, no GL and no engine — `--cam-probe` returns before one is created,
the way `--ui-probe settings` does. Four falsifications, each red only for its own cause:
deleting the pitch clamp reddens `cam-pitch-clamp` alone (12.000 and −0.000 against 0.2 and
−1.25) while `cam-orbit-closed` correctly stays green, and lifting the aim point with the eye
reddens `cam-orbit-closed` alone (look y 3.0 against 1.5).

### Phase 3 — the engine seam, and a deletion moved a phase later

`engine_set_camera_rig` installs one rig; the engine applies its pose immediately before
`_engine_derive_camera`, which extends 11.107's rule rather than replacing it. **The UPDATE stays
with the app**, and that split is the point: the app owns the input — an action table, a mouse or
a script — and `camera_rig.c` is not allowed to know which of those it is.

**`camera_mode`'s deletion moved to Phase 4.** The plan had it here, but its only three consumers
are `app.c`'s auto-orbit gate and arrow-key branch and `gui.c`'s radio — all of which Phase 4
deletes with `MouseDragController` itself. Removing the field first would mean inventing behaviour
for a controller about to be deleted; removing them together is one coherent change.

**A NULL rig became silent rather than an error**, which is a contract and not leniency: "no rig
installed" is the normal state of an app that poses its own camera, and it is what the frame loop
passes every frame in one. Making it an error would have put a guard back at the single call site
this exists to take one away from — and it let the seam land before any app converted, with all
33 goldens holding.

**The two menu goldens fail in the full run and pass individually**, exactly as
`docs/verification.md` warns. Re-checked one at a time: 0 px each. Worth knowing in advance for
Phases 4 and 5, where a real regression would look the same at first glance.

### Phase 4 — the deletion, and five things it found

`MouseDragController` is gone. What replaced it is **not** a camera: `CameraDrag` is an input
adapter that turns a pointer into what a rig is told — a drag becomes an aim, a shift-drag an
anchor, the wheel and the keys a distance — and `camera_rig.c` decides what any of that does to
the eye. render, spores, tree and gametest's viewer path all run it.

**The identity holds exactly.** The rewritten viewer reproduces the deleted controller's own
numbers: `0.400000` rad of orbit for 200 pixels, `0.352061` of pan for (200, 100) — the same six
decimals the closed-form arms measured against the old code in Phase 0. No tolerance was needed.

Five things the conversion turned up:

1. **The two-writer collision is loud, which is the seam working.** gametest installed a rig while
   `follow_camera_update` still wrote the camera directly, and the engine applying the rig after
   the hook clobbered it — **305,366 px** on both menu goldens, reproducible, nothing subtle about
   it. The rig is now installed there only on the viewer path until Phase 5 converts the follow
   camera; the condition carries a comment saying so and Phase 5 deletes it.
2. **A rig's yaw runs OPPOSITE to a Camera's `phi`** (`phi = -yaw + k`: the rig places the eye
   along `-dir` while `phi` measures the eye's own bearing), and so do pitch and theta. Carrying
   the old signs across silently reverses every drag in the viewer. It did, until
   `cam-drag-orbit` read `-0.380000` where it wanted `+0.400000`.
3. **A trace taken in the pre-render hook is now a frame stale**, because the engine applies the
   rig after that hook returns. Every drag read 19/20 of its true size (`0.334458` against
   `0.352061`, exactly 0.95). `--trace-camera` moved to the render callback, which is where the
   frame's actual camera is.
4. **`camera_rig_set_pose` must NOT clamp the pitch**, and nor must an update that was not asked
   to turn. `--cam-eye` exists to reproduce a view exactly, and a rig quietly pulling a stated
   pose back inside a steering band makes an exact framing impossible to ask for. The clamp now
   applies where a pitch is being *changed*; `cam-pitch-clamp` still passes, so it still works
   where it should.
5. **The pose now round-trips through `asin`/`atan2`, which is sensitive to input precision in a
   way a verbatim-stored pose was not.** `config-camera` handed one leg `%.6f` and the other the
   snapshot's `%.9g`, and the 1e-6 difference came back as **17 px** against a measured 0 floor.
   Both legs now get `%.9g` — the arm was measuring a truncation, not a restore.

**The sixth was the one that mattered, and only the full suite saw it.** `cycle-config` and
`moon-config` both failed at **885 px** on a restore — the sky round-tripping perfectly, the
camera not. A pose decomposed into an angle and an arm and put back together **is not the pose it
started as**, and the error grows with the arm: `aerial_fixture` frames from **20,196 units**,
where the trig round trip lost 0.002 world units. `config-pixels` and the 33 goldens all passed
through it, because every one of them frames from a few units.

So a rig now KEEPS a pose it was given, verbatim, until something moves it — one flag and one
vector. `--cam-eye` and a config restore are exact again rather than nearly exact, and
`cam-pose-adopt` asserts **0.00e+00** of drift at both a 5-unit arm and a 20,196-unit one, the
second being the case that would have caught this before the suite did.

Two consequences recorded rather than hidden:

- **`camera.orbit.{distance,theta,phi}` are no longer restorable**, joining `camera.near_clip` in
  `config-perturb`'s exception table. The rig owns the pose and the Camera's orbit block is
  derived from it. This is one way to state a pose where there were two, not a way that was lost:
  `camera.eye` and `camera.target` still do it, and `config-camera` asserts that end to end.
- **`apps/render` has a wheel now**, closing Phase 0's finding, and the arrow keys pitch
  unconditionally where `camera_mode` used to make them mean either pitching or zooming depending
  on state. Each input does the thing it conventionally does; the mode that decided between them
  is deleted, along with its `config_snapshot` row, its static assert and its GUI radio pair.

### Phase 5 — gametest, and the row's actual complaint closed

`follow_camera_update` went from **104 lines to 50**, and what survived is the half that knows what
this game is about: which drop counts as falling, and that swimming counts as footing. The
smoothing, the two ends, the arm, the aim, the clamp and the shortening are the rig's.

**The follow camera maps onto the rig with no sign conversion at all** — `eye = focus -
dir(yaw, pitch) * dist` is the rig's own form, written out in `gametest.c` and `forest.c` already.
Only the *viewer* needed converting, because that one went through the `Camera`'s `phi`/`theta`,
which run the other way. Both menu goldens are **0 px**, look-down bias and all.

**The roadmap row's complaint is now a boolean.** `cam_yaw` was a file static belonging to the
follow camera, and reading it was how the movement scheme got decided; it is now
`camera_rig_move_basis`, and a rig that does not steer leaves the input in world axes.
`pad-camera-relative` passes with the same numbers as before — camera −104.9°, heading −104.8°,
out by 0.01° — and setting `steers_controls = false` reproduces **104.85 deg** of error, which is
to the second decimal the number spec 12.17 recorded for the world-aligned leg it was reverting.

So the thing that flipped twice invisibly is one named field with a stated default, and an arm
that fails the moment it is wrong. That was the row's argument for a camera framework, and it is
the part that is now finished.

One behaviour difference, stated rather than discovered later: when the arm is shortened by the
probe **and** the eye lift is non-zero, the rig places the shortened eye along the aim where the
old code placed it along the arm from the focus point. They agree exactly while the lift is zero,
which is every frame the player is on the ground — including both goldens. The rig's version
preserves the pitch as it tightens; the old one changed it slightly.

## Out of scope

- **A sequencer or cutscene track, and a dialogue system to drive one.** The roadmap calls the
  sequencer its own project and this spec agrees.
- **Camera shake as a gameplay event source.** The rig takes an impulse; nothing in gametest fires
  one except a debug key.
- **A sweep instead of a ray** for the occlusion probe. gametest's own comment already books
  `physics_world_sweep_body` as the honest fix if the ray proves insufficient; the seam makes it a
  one-line change later.
- **Node-attached cameras.** `node->camera` is written by the importer and read by nothing
  (`import.c:1717`, `scene.c:1224`); making a camera follow a `SceneNode` transform is a separate
  question with a frame-ordering hazard gametest's comment at `:3748` already names.
- **`Engine.camera`'s ownership contradiction.** `engine.h:338` documents it borrowed and
  `free_engine` frees it (`engine.c:385`), while `Scene` separately owns `scene->cameras` — a
  latent double-free for an app that does both. Found while surveying; it is a real defect and it
  is not this spec's.
