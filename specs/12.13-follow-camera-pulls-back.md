# Spec 12.13 — the follow camera sits close, and pulls back when the ground goes

## Context

`apps/gametest`'s follow camera trails the player at ONE fixed distance —
`FOLLOW_CAM_DISTANCE` 9.0 × `PLAYER_SCALE` = 18 world units, `FOLLOW_CAM_HEIGHT` 6
(`gametest.c:518-520`). That single framing has to serve two situations it cannot both
serve: walking about on a 50×50 plate, where it is much too far back to read a character,
and falling 180 units down the shaft, where it is the only thing that shows you where you
are going.

The plate wins the trade today and the result is a small figure in a large empty frame. 12.12
raked the key so the character is lit; this is the other half — it should be close enough to
be worth lighting.

**Outcome: the shot sits close while you have footing, and opens out to roughly today's
framing as you fall away from it.**

---

## The signal, and what it is deliberately NOT

The trigger is **how far the player has fallen below the ground it last stood on** — not
whether it is airborne.

- **Airborne is the wrong test.** A jump is airborne too, and hopping on the spot must not
  lurch the camera. A jump also only ever goes *up*, so a drop measured against the last
  footing is ≤ 0 for its whole rise and small on landing. Falling off the plate is the only
  thing that puts real distance between the player and the last solid thing under it.
- **`player_medium` is the wrong source**, even though `MEDIUM_AIR` looks made for this. That
  enum is an ANIMATION state: it is only reached when `clip_fall` exists
  (`gametest.c:2967-2970`), so the generated puppet never enters it. A camera keyed to it
  would frame the built-in rig differently from an imported one, which is exactly the class
  of bug 12.11 and 12.12 were about.
- **Swimming counts as footing.** Reaching the water at the bottom ends the fall, so the shot
  should come back in rather than stay wide because the controller is not "grounded".

`character_controller_is_grounded` plus `player_swimming` is the whole input, and both are
already maintained.

---

## The change

All in `follow_camera_update` (`gametest.c:3422`) and the constants above it.

- Split the single distance/height pair into a NEAR and a FAR pair. FAR keeps today's values,
  so the wide end is the framing that exists now.
- Track `cam_ground_y`, the height of the last footing.
- Derive a wideness in [0,1] from `cam_ground_y - player_y` across a start/full band, and
  damp it toward that target each frame.
- Lerp distance and height by it; everything downstream — the orbit, the arrow-key yaw and
  pitch, the static-geometry raycast that keeps the eye out of rock — is unchanged and reads
  the lerped values.

**Widening is damped faster than tightening.** A fall wants the shot open before the drop
reads as a mistake; coming back in slowly is what keeps a landing from snapping.

The band's lower edge has to clear the world's own step-downs — the demo staircase's risers
are 0.5 and the ramp climbs 0.25 per unit — or walking downstairs would pull the camera.

---

## Verification

**Looking at it is the acceptance test**, and it is the point of the change: the deliverable
is how the demo feels to move around in.

```bash
./out/bin/gametest                       # close while walking the plate
./out/bin/gametest --puppet <char>.glb   # the same, on an imported rig
```

Scripted, so it can be watched twice the same way — walk to the edge and off it, and capture
the transition with `--screenshot-every`:

```bash
./out/bin/gametest --pad-script <walk-off-the-edge> -S <out> --screenshot-every 20 -f 400
```

Three things to watch, each a way this can be wrong:

1. **Jumping on the spot does not move the camera at all.** If it does, the band's lower edge
   is too low.
2. **Walking down the steps and the ramp does not move it.** Same failure, same fix.
3. **Landing does not snap.** If it does, the tighten rate is too high.

The suites: only two goldens and one gate arm run the `gametest` binary, and all three
photograph a camera this changes.

```bash
python3 scripts/gates.py --only anim     # anim-twin, a gametest pixel arm
python3 scripts/goldens.py --only menu
python3 scripts/goldens.py --only menu_focus
```

`menu` and `menu_focus` photograph the world through an 88%-opaque backdrop **and their own
camera**, so they will move and need re-baking. Everything else — `gamepad`, `audio`, `ui`,
`save`, `ik` and the other 15 `anim` arms — reads probe text and cannot see a camera.

Note the two menu goldens open a MENU, which does not move the player, so what they capture
is the resting close framing rather than a transition.

---

## Out of scope

- **A framing that reacts to speed.** Pulling back as the player runs is the usual next thing
  and is a different signal from this one; it would fight the fall response for the same
  knob.
- **Sweeping a shape rather than a ray** for the rock-avoidance arm. Already a stated limit
  in that block's own comment, unchanged here and no more wrong at a shorter arm.
- **The arrow-key yaw and pitch.** Untouched; this changes how far the camera sits, not how
  it is aimed.
