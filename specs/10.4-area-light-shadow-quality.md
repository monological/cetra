# Area-light shadow quality: fit the projection, then raise density

## Context

`cornell_box` renders area-light shadows with a coarse staircase along every
edge -- the shadow map's texel grid showing through. The first attempt at this
widened the PCF filter until the steps stopped being visible. That was the wrong
instrument: a penumbra is sized by the light source, not by "how much blur hides
the aliasing", and the result was mushy contacts plus a still-ragged edge. That
commit (`540e477`) is reverted by this plan.

The measured cause is texel density. The area panel gets a **fixed 120 degree
cone** (`AREA_SHADOW_FOV`, `shadow.c:521`) regardless of scene. In Cornell the
panel sits at y=1.97 in a 2 m room; at the floor that frustum is **6.82 m wide
over a 2 m room**, so roughly **8.6% of the map's texels land on geometry** --
3.3 mm per texel where a fitted projection gives ~1 mm.

Nothing in the punctual path is fitted at all: `compute_punctual_matrices`
(`shadow.c:539-585`) hands every point face, spot cone and area panel the same
scene-global `ss->near_plane` / `ss->far_plane`, and never consults the light's
own reach even though `light_cull_radius` is already public
(`light_cluster.h:131`) and computed every frame for clustering.

Two corrections to claims made earlier in the investigation, recorded so they are
not re-derived:

- **The near plane is not clipping anything.** The arithmetic that said it was
  used the library default of 1.0; the render app overrides it to 0.1 at
  `render.c:2050`. Cornell runs 0.1 -> 13.86 and clips nothing.
- **Fitting the cone is worth ~1.47x linear here, not 3.4x.** The cone must
  contain the *casters* (a receiver outside the cone cannot be shadowed by a
  caster inside it). The tall box's top corners sit 0.77 m below the panel at
  0.91 m off-axis = 49.7 degrees, so the minimum correct fov is ~100 degrees.
  Fitting to the room instead would clip those corners and drop shadow near the
  wall bases. Cornell is close to the worst case; scenes with casters farther
  from the light gain much more.

**Third correction, from implementing Phase 1: the fit is worth nothing at all
here.** Even 1.47x assumed the boxes were the only casters. The room's own walls
are casters too and they reach the panel's own height, so the conservative bound
is a 180-degree fov. See Phase 1 below -- it was built, measured, and rejected,
and the density win moves entirely to Phase 2.

## Deliberately not doing: variance shadow maps

VSM is the right technique for this symptom and does fit GL 4.1 -- moments in
RG32F, a separable blur over the map, Chebyshev bound in the lookup, and then
edge softness scales with blur radius instead of tap count. It is deferred, not
rejected, for reasons in priority order:

1. **Light leaking is its signature failure and Cornell is the textbook case** --
   a footprint spanning box-top and floor depth makes the Chebyshev bound loose
   and washes the shadow out. EVSM fixes it but needs RGBA32F and exponent
   constants tuned against depth range, i.e. another scene-scale magic number.
2. It **restructures** the depth pass: the array is `GL_DEPTH_COMPONENT24` bound
   as a depth attachment; moments need a colour target plus its own depth.
3. **No spare texture unit** -- the array is on unit 15, the last, and `pbr_frag`
   samples all 16. A moments array replaces it, which drags in the froxel fog.
4. Memory doubles (RG32F) or quadruples (EVSM), plus a blur ping-pong target.
5. **We cannot test it yet** (see Phase 0). Landing a technique whose failure is
   subtle wash-out onto the one path with no working test repeats the defect
   this spec exists to fix.

The fitting work below is a prerequisite either way: leaking scales with frustum
slack and depth range.

## Phase 0 -- a test that can see this change

Today nothing can. `gates.py`'s area entry uses `area_scale_fixture.cscn`, which
has no `cast_shadows`, and its scale gate multiplies both halves identically so
a projection change is invisible by construction. `cornell_box` is the **only**
scene in the repo where an area light casts, and its golden is currently a
reference for the open LTC inversion (`specs/10.3`), so it cannot arbitrate.

- New `assets/area_shadow_fixture.cscn` + generator entry: a shadow-casting
  panel over a caster with a **known analytic penumbra** -- a quad at a fixed
  height above a ground plane, so the expected soft-edge width is computable
  from panel size and heights rather than eyeballed.
- Capture a baseline from a build of the branch tip **before** any change, per
  the protocol in `specs/10.3` (compare against a base-commit build, not the
  committed images).

Gate: the fixture renders; its penumbra width is measured and recorded as the
number later phases must not contradict.

## Phase 1 -- fit the area panel's projection: ATTEMPTED, REJECTED

Implemented as planned -- hoisted `compute_scene_bounds` into
`render_shadow_depth_pass`, fitted the cone from the caster AABB's corners and
`near`/`far` from the nearest corner and `light_cull_radius` -- then measured and
threw it away. It is not sound, and no sound version of it helps these scenes.

**Corner sampling is not conservative.** `axial = dot(p - origin, dir)` is affine,
so its extremes over an AABB are at corners -- but the quantity the cone has to
bound is `perp / axial`, which is a ratio, and its maximum is NOT at a corner. It
runs away wherever a caster approaches the panel's own plane. Measured on
`cornell_box`: corner sampling proposed a 71.3-degree cone, and the room's walls
are casters that run from the floor to the ceiling, so

| wall point (x=1.0) | axial | half-angle |
|---|---|---|
| y = 0.00 | 1.97 | 26.9 |
| y = 1.00 | 0.97 | 45.9 |
| y = 1.50 | 0.47 | 64.8 |
| y = 1.90 | 0.07 | 86.0 |

The fitted half-angle is 35.6 degrees, so **every wall point above y = 0.58 falls
outside the cone** and stops casting. The fit silently deletes casters.

**And the conservative version degenerates.** The panel sits at y=1.97 inside a
room spanning y = 0..2, so the caster bounds SURROUND the light. As a caster
approaches the panel's plane `axial -> 0` and the required half-angle -> 90, i.e.
a 180-degree fov, which one perspective map cannot represent. The 120-degree cap
is already the compromise; there is nothing to narrow.

`near`/`far` degenerate the same way and for the same reason: the nearest caster
in front of the panel is at `axial -> 0`, so a fitted `near` collapses toward
zero and the depth range gets *worse* than the app's 0.1/13.86, not better.

**The Phase 0 fixture does not rescue it either.** Its geometry is entirely below
the panel, so a fit is well-defined -- but the ground plane is 8 units across, so
the fit asks for 141 degrees and clamps straight back to the 120-degree cap.

**Why the premise was wrong.** The waste is real (a 6.8 m frustum over a 2 m room,
~8.6% of texels on geometry) but it is not recoverable by fitting to scene bounds,
because in every scene here the bounds contain the light. What would recover it is
a frustum per CASTER rather than per light -- which is what UE4 did with per-object
shadows, and it needs either an atlas or many more layers than the 8 this array
has. Recorded as the reason the density win moves to Phase 2.

## Phase 2 -- resolution from a VRAM budget

One `GL_TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` means **one size for every layer** (`init_depth_array`,
`shadow.c:25`), and a second array is impossible -- unit 15 is the last one. So
the size becomes a budget decision made at allocation time.

- In `init_punctual_shadow_array` (`shadow.c:155`), pick the largest power of two
  up to 4096 that keeps `size^2 * 4 bytes * layers` inside a stated budget. One
  area light (1 layer) gets 4096 at 67 MB; a six-face point light falls back to
  2048 rather than demanding ~400 MB.
- `PUNCTUAL_SHADOW_MAP_SIZE` stops being a compile-time literal. It is currently
  mirrored in **two** places -- `shadow.h:26` and `punctual_shadow.glsl:22` -- so
  it becomes a uniform, and the shader's PCF texel step and the C-side
  `punctual_texel_scale` both read the runtime value.

Gate: `cornell_point` / `cornell_leak` / `froxel_fog` move (finer shadows, an
improvement) and are inspected at 1:1 before regenerating. Phase 0 fixture
penumbra width unchanged.

## Phase 3 -- receiver-plane depth bias

`specs/9.8:223` already names this as "the untried candidate" for the grazing
case, which is exactly the geometry hurting us: a wall near edge-on to the light,
where one texel spans a large depth range. Isidoro's method takes the receiver's
depth gradient in shadow-map space and offsets each tap's reference depth by the
plane equation, which is correct at any slope and needs no format change.

Replace or floor the current slope-scaled world-space bias
(`punctual_shadow.glsl`, `PUNCTUAL_BIAS_TEXELS`). Note the derivative constraint:
the lookup runs inside the clustered light loop, which is non-uniform control
flow, so `ddx/ddy` must be taken on `WorldPos` **outside** the loop and
transformed per-light inside it.

Gate: the grazing acne that `PUNCTUAL_GRAZING_FADE` currently papers over should
recede; if it does, that fade's threshold can drop or go away.

## Phase 4 -- the penumbra, re-derived

Only after Phases 1-2 have made the resolution adequate. A 0.7 m panel does cast
a genuinely soft shadow, so this belongs -- but sized **only** by the panel:
similar triangles `r * (zR - zB) / zB`, with no cap chosen to hide aliasing, and
the tap count justified by the resulting radius rather than the reverse.

If the Phase 0 fixture's measured penumbra matches the analytic prediction, it is
right; if the tap count needed to keep it clean is unreasonable, that is the
signal to revisit VSM.

## Out of scope

- **The LTC inversion** (`specs/10.3` Open 1) -- a laterally offset panel still
  shades a thin crescent. Independent of shadows, but it blocks regenerating the
  three area-lit goldens, so those stay stale until it closes.
- **Extending the fit to point/spot.** Their near/far is slack too, but the
  froxel fog samples the same array with a raw `pc.z - shadowBias` compare and no
  near/far plumbing (`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:186-189`), so changing a spot's
  near/far silently changes what that bias means. Needs the fog compare converted
  in the same change.
