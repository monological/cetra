# 13.57: rendering at any scale, in true units

## Context

pcb (`../apps/pcb`, its own repository, cetra a submodule) will draw circuit boards in cetra: a 100 mm
board, a 1 mm resistor, a camera from 2 mm to 500 mm away, all in real millimetres. cetra measures in
metres, which is right, and its photometric lighting depends on it (`import.c:92`: candela over
distance squared is only right with distance in metres). But a few dozen of its lengths are fixed
for a world of people and rooms, and at a board's scale they break the frame.

**What breaks, worst first** (two read-only audits of master, 2026-10-27):
- **The camera.** The render app's near plane is `fmaxf(0.05 * r, 0.05)` at load and never below
  5 mm per frame (`apps/render/src/render.c:4903-4918`). For one 1 mm part, near (50 mm) is past
  far (40r, 24 mm), so the projection is degenerate. The drag controller's wheel stops at 50 mm
  (`app.c:33`), and its keys move at least 0.1 m a press (`app.c:150-152`).
- **Shadows.**
  - A cascade's near plane is a fixed 0.1 m (`shadow.c:586`), and so is the render app's shadow
    near plane (`render.c:4768`), which every per-frame spot, point and area face uses
    (`shadow.c:1379-1381`).
  - A lamp 30 mm above the board shadows nothing within 100 mm of it.
  - Directional shadows go degenerate below a 12.5 mm scene radius.
- **AO.** `THICKNESS = 0.25` m is hard-coded (`gtao_frag.glsl:61`), so every occluder is a 250 mm slab. The blur's depth tolerance is floored at 20 mm (`ssao_blur_frag.glsl:33`, `include/ao_upsample.glsl:69`), so AO bleeds across every part's silhouette.
- **Contact shadows.** The ray starts at least 20 mm off the surface
  (`contact_shadow_frag.glsl:110`), above every part on a board, so contact shadows never fire.
- **SSR.**
  - The start bias is at least 20 mm, and nothing within near + 10 mm is traced (`ssr_frag.glsl:298-299`).
  - The thickness default is 50 mm (`postfx.c:684`).
- **Light.** Inverse-square stops rising inside a 1 cm sphere (`include/lights_ubo.glsl:329`), so an LED 2 mm from the board is 25 times too dim.
- **GI and probes.**
  - A DDGI capture clips everything within 50 mm (`gi_volume.c:213`). Its far plane adds 1 m (`:70`), which pushes millimetre visibility moments into fp16's subnormals.
  - Reflection-probe captures floor their near plane at 10 mm and their far at 10 m (`render.c:5178-5181`, `cscene_apply.c:1043-1044`).
- **Smaller ones**:
  - motion blur's `SOFT_Z` 1 m (`motion_blur_frag.glsl:20`);
  - particles' soft fade 0.5 m (`particle_renderer.c:14`);
  - fog's tile bias 0.02 m (`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:134`);
  - `TILE_STREAM_MARGIN` 2 m (`shadow.c:1710`), `GI_STREAM_MARGIN` 2 m, `PROBE_STREAM_MARGIN` 1 m;
  - the sky tests at -1e-4 m in several shaders;
  - the camera rig's `probe_skin` 0.6 m and its 1 m first-person aim (`camera_rig.c:10, 30`);
  - GUI sliders whose minimums are centimetres or metres.
- **The render app's scene-relative rules mostly only raise a value** (`render.c:4927-4962`), as in `fmaxf(0.4, 0.01 * r)`, so a small scene keeps the metre floor.

**What is already right.**
- Depth linearisation reads the matrix (`include/depth.glsl`), and the cluster slices are logarithmic between the camera's own planes.
- LOD, occlusion culling and TAA are relative. Shadow biases are in map depth or texels.
- The SSS gather and pyramid are scale-correct; only the default profile radius (0.2 m) is a metre value.
- The sky and the atmosphere are physical kilometres, joined to the scene by `world_units_per_km`.

**Decided with the user (in pcb's planning of its 3D models, recorded in pcb's ARCH section 9).**
- Keep every size real, and take the human-scale constants out.
- Never scale the world up instead. Scaling makes light fall-off, depth of field and scatter wrong, and every number an app gives cetra would need converting.

**Intended outcome.**
- **The same scene shrunk 1024 times renders the same picture, to the pixel.** The proof is a twin gate, with every length and every physical quantity matched (below).
- **pcb's board renders at true millimetres** with AO, contact shadows, SSR, shadows and SSS working, a close-up 2 mm from a part included.
- **Every metre-scale frame is unchanged:** the 32 goldens at 0 px.

## Design

### Three kinds of length

Every length in the engine is one of three:
1. **Physical**: a property of the thing the scene describes. Light positions, ranges, sizes and source radii; candela; an SSS profile's radius; a material's volume thickness and attenuation distance; fog density per metre; the sky's kilometres; rain and water physics.
   - These are never rewritten. A small scene states small ones.
2. **Tuning**: the engine's own choice of how far an effect looks, a bias, a floor or an epsilon.
   - These follow one setting, the **typical size**, as their present metre value times it. At a typical size of 1 every one is exactly today's number, so every metre-scale frame is unchanged.
3. **Fitted**: derived each frame from the view or the scene.
   - The cascades already fit their spheres to the slices. What is added here is the camera's near and far planes, fitted where an app asks (below).

### The typical size

- **`Engine.typical_size`**, in metres: the size of the things the scene holds. It is 1 for people and rooms, and 0.001 for a circuit board.
  - `EngineConfig.typical_size` sets it at creation, 0 meaning 1.
  - After creation it is a by-function field: `engine_set_typical_size(engine, metres)`.
- **`engine_set_typical_size` writes the tuning defaults of what the engine owns**, each as its metre default times the size, in the manner of `engine_set_2d_preset`:
  - PostFX: the AO radius, the contact-shadow distance, SSR's thickness and maximum distance, the fog's height falloff and far, the DoF focus distance and range, and the default SSS profile's radius;
  - the camera's near and far defaults.

  An app sets the size first and its own values after. It rejects a size that is not positive and finite.
- **Shaders read it from `ViewParams`.** The block's spare `_viewPad` slot becomes `typicalSize` (`include/view.glsl`, `ubo.h`), so the block's size is unchanged. A pass that reads it includes the block.
- **C reads `engine->typical_size`**: the shadow pass, the GI and probe captures, the streaming margins and the controllers.
- **A scene subsystem's settable lengths stay its owner's to set**, as every app already sets the shadow frustum. These are the shadow system's near, far, ortho size and tile tolerance, and the scene's ground-projection radius and height. Their library defaults stay metre values, and their header comments say that a small scene sets them.
- **Reached from outside:**
  - the `.cscn` top-level `typicalSize`;
  - the render app's `--typical-size`;
  - a `config_snapshot` row, restored before the PostFX rows so the size cannot overwrite them;
  - a GUI field.

### The camera's planes, fitted on request

- **`Camera.fit_clip`**, a setting, false by default. When true, the engine sets near and far each frame, at frame step 4, after the transform walk and before it derives the matrices:
  - **far**: the farthest point, plus a margin, of every node's world box in front of the camera. The shadow catcher's quad and water's plane count as boxes when they are drawn.
  - **near**: the nearest such box's distance, less a margin. It is never under `far / CLIP_RATIO_MAX`; `CLIP_RATIO_MAX` is 1e4, written down beside its reasoning.
  - A box holding the camera has distance 0, so a camera inside one falls to that floor. That suits a viewer looking at an object, which is what asks for the fit. A first-person game in a room keeps its own fixed planes.
- **Depth precision with it:**
  - At 2 mm from an 0402, with a 150 mm board in view, near is about 1.5 mm and far about 160 mm.
  - D24's step at the far end is then about z² / (near · 2^24), or 1 µm. That is ample for copper (35 µm) and solder mask on it.
- **The depth format stays D24.**
  - Reversed depth would need `glClipControl`, which is GL 4.5. macOS's GL 4.1 lacks it, and without it the 0.5·z + 0.5 depth mapping cancels the gain.
  - D32F with the standard mapping is no finer at the far end.
  - A logarithmic depth written from the fragment shader defeats early depth and the prepass.
- **Reached by:** the render app's `--fit-clip`, the `.cscn` camera's `fitClip`, a config row and the GUI.

### The render app

- **Its framing floors become relative:**
  - `auto_near = fmaxf(0.05r, 0.05 S)`;
  - `clip_near_floor = fmaxf(0.1 auto_near, 0.005 S)`;
  - the shadow near `0.1 S`;
  - the probe captures' near and far floors `0.01 S` and `10 fmaxf(r, S)`.

  `S` is the typical size.
- **Its raise-only rules use `S` where they now use 1 m**, as in `fmaxf(0.4 S, 0.01 r)`.
- **At the default `S = 1` every expression is today's**, so the goldens hold.
- **With `--fit-clip`**, its per-frame near formula gives way to the engine's fit.

### The GUI

Every length slider's range is its present range times the typical size. Under a typical size of 0.1 it prints millimetres: a 30 mm light range shows as 30 mm, not 0.0 m.

### The twin gate

**The instrument, built first.** It is a new group, `scale-twin`, beside the photometric `SCALE_GATES` (`scripts/gates.py:179-201, 423-451`) and on the same `cscn_copy` and `compare` helpers.

- **Each row renders a fixture twice**: as it is, and as a twin shrunk by **K = 2^-10**. The twin:
  - imports with `--import-scale K` and runs with `--typical-size K`;
  - multiplies by K every length the `.cscn` states: light positions, ranges, sizes, source radius and length, `shadow_near`, the camera's eye and target, the ground projection's radius and height, the SSS profiles' radii, materials' thickness and attenuation distance, the fog's falloff, floor and far, the DoF focus, and `world_units_per_km`;
  - multiplies point and spot candela by K², so illuminance is unchanged, and divides fog density by K;
  - leaves directional lux, area radiance, exposure and the environment unchanged.
- **K is a power of two on purpose.** Multiplying by it is exact in floating point, and so are the sums, products, square roots and quotients of numbers it has scaled. So an engine with no hidden length renders the twin to the same pixels: **0 px**, not a tolerance. A difference names a constant that was missed.
  - The one exception is a stored length falling under fp16's normal range (6.1e-5 m).
  - Twins whose nearest surface is 6 cm or more from the camera avoid it.
- **Phase 1 calibrates it** on a fixture whose path holds no metre constant (no AO, contact shadows, SSR or punctual shadows). That should be 0 px. If it is not, the floor is measured and stated before anything else is concluded.
- **The fixtures are the golden corpus's scenes**, less those whose physics does not scale: water (gravity sets its waves), rain, clouds, particles and anything animated. Each exclusion is listed with its reason, and fixtures are added where a subsystem has no golden: punctual shadows, cached tiles, GI and probes.
- **Run on master in phase 1**, its table of which fixtures fail and by how much is the measure of the work. Each later phase turns rows to 0 px.

### A board at true scale

- **A new fixture, `gen_board_fixture.py`**, written in true metres:
  - a 100 x 60 mm FR4 board, 1.6 mm thick, under glossy green solder mask (clearcoat), with SSS in the mask and the FR4;
  - a row of 1.0 x 0.5 mm chip resistors and 0402 capacitors on tin terminations;
  - a 5 x 5 mm QFN, and a BGA on 0.5 mm balls;
  - a point light 30 mm above the board, and the studio HDR;
  - typical size 0.001 and `fitClip`.
- **It renders at a whole-board view, a three-quarter view, and a close-up 2 mm from an 0402.**
- **It gets a golden once the user has looked at it.** It is the reference pcb's 3D view will be held to.

## Left for later

- **A physical depth of field.** The CoC is `clamp((d - focus) / range) * maxCoC`, a look with no focal length or aperture (`include/depth.glsl`, `cocAtNdcZ`). A thin-lens CoC from the camera's focal length and its exposure aperture would put a macro shot's paper-thin focus where a real lens does, at any scale. This is likely the next spec pcb wants for close-ups.
- **A camera inside a large box** falls to the clip floor. A near plane from last frame's depth, read back, would fit tighter. This is for an app that needs it.
- **Lights' default size** (`light.c:46-48`, 50 m) and the shadow system's metre defaults stay. A small scene states them. A desc whose size defaulted from the typical size would need the engine at light creation, which nothing else asks for.
- **Reversed depth**, if cetra ever gets GL 4.5's clip control.

### Phase 1: the instrument

**`scale-twin`** (`scripts/gates.py`, beside `scale`). Each row renders its fixture twice, both
legs through `cscn_copy` so they differ only in the twin, and passes at 0 px.
- **The twin** (`_twin_scene`, `_twin_flags`) is built from the scene file and the row's flags:
  - lengths times K: the camera's eye and target; a light's position, range, size, source radius
    and length, and shadow near; a probe's position and box; a material's `sss.radius`, layer
    `uvScale`, `splatDomain`, `thickness`, `attenuationDistance` and `furLength`; fog near and far;
    and the flags in `TWIN_LENGTH_FLAGS` (`--cam-eye`, `--area-light`'s position and size,
    `--ortho`, `--dof-focus`, ...);
  - point and spot intensity times K², fog density over K;
  - `--import-scale K` for the model, and `--world-scale 1000K` for the sky.
- **`--world-scale` builds a sky by itself**, so the twin passes it only where the original has
  one: a scene file whose environment is `sky`, or a sky flag among the row's. The first run
  passed it everywhere and failed both calibration rows on every pixel.
- **A key the twin does not know is refused by name**, never copied unscaled: water, rain,
  fire, dust, wind, decals, roads, light overrides, post passes, shader hooks and parameters, a
  point light with no authored intensity, an `sss` with no radius, a layer with no `uvScale`.
  An unscaled length would fail its row and read as one of the engine's.

**Calibration.** `dir_shadow_fixture` with no shadow map, AO, SSR or contact shadows:
- **0 px shrunk and 0 px grown by 1/K, on master.** The instrument is exact.
- The shrunk row passes even though the render app gives its twin a 5 mm near plane where
  K times the original's would be 0.3 mm. On that path depth only orders surfaces.
- The grown row stays in the group, since a floor written `max(x, metres)` bites only a shrink.

**The rows** are the corpus's scenes at their recipes' flags (30 frames, 400 x 300), plus
`tile-core` for the cached tiles, which no golden renders. Punctual shadows are covered by
`cornell-point` and `cornell-leak`, GI by `cornell-box --gi-volume` and probes by
`cornell-rooms`, so no new scene was needed for those.
- **Excluded:** `water_fixture` and `water_submerged` (gravity sets the waves), `local_exposure`
  (`abandoned_window` carries dust particles and a wind), `puppet` (animated).
- **Changed from the plan: the clouds are rows, not exclusions.** The deck is in kilometres,
  joined to the scene by `world_units_per_km` like the sky (`sky_clouds.c:365`). The spec
  excluded them without a measured reason, and a row will give one if there is one.

### Phase 2: the typical size

- **`Engine.typical_size`**, 1 at creation; `EngineConfig.typical_size` (0 = 1) sets it once init
  has built PostFX.
- **`engine_set_typical_size`** refuses anything not positive and finite, then writes:
  - PostFX's lengths through **`postfx_reset_lengths(fx, size)`**, which is now the one place they
    are written: `create_postfx` calls it with 1 where it used to set the nine literals inline
    (AO radius, contact reach, SSR reach and thickness floor, DoF focus and range, fog falloff and
    far, the default SSS profile's radius);
  - the installed camera's near and far, as `CAMERA_NEAR_DEFAULT` and `CAMERA_FAR_DEFAULT` (new in
    `camera.h`, which `create_camera` now reads too) times the size.
- **`ViewParams.typicalSize`** in the old `_viewPad` slot, uploaded with the exposure.
- **Reached by:**
  - the `.cscn` top-level `typicalSize`, refused with a warning unless positive and finite;
  - `--typical-size`, CLI over the scene file as every other key;
  - the snapshot row `engine.typical_size`, FIRST in the table. It restores only on a change, as
    the sun's angles do, so a snapshot restored onto its own session writes nothing;
