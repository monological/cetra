# Close the gaps between our froxel fog and the paper it descends from

## Context

`fog.md` is Wronski's AC4 "Volumetric Fog" (SIGGRAPH 2014) -- the paper this whole
architecture is built on. Reading our implementation against it turns up three things:
two places where I recently moved us AWAY from the reference on bad reasoning, and one
substantial piece we never implemented.

**We match it on the fundamentals.** Frustum-aligned volume, NDC in XY with exponential
depth, lighting once per cell, `RGB = lighting x density` with the scattering coefficient
in alpha, front-to-back accumulation of in-scatter and transmittance, application as a
3D lookup plus FMA under blend, temporal reprojection with jitter (their post-AC4
extension), an ambient term. We are ahead on one: they had "no physically-based phase
functions... purely art-driven, 2 colours", we have Henyey-Greenstein. Clustered light
culling, which they list as future work, we already have.

## 1. Revert the grid to 160x90x64

I raised it to 320x180 chasing the blocky-beam artifact. The paper used **exactly
160x90x64**, argues it is sufficient, and gives the reason:

> Due to the fact that when applying effect, we use quadrilinear filtering on volumetric
> data, we are unable to see single texels of volume texture.

We can see single texels. Their claim is that at this resolution you should not -- which
means the artifact is not a resolution problem, and that matches every measurement taken
while chasing it (raising the grid halved the step size but never removed it; five other
single-condition fixes measured as no change at all).

So 320x180 was treating a symptom against the reference's explicit guidance, and at 4x
the volume memory. Revert `POSTFX_FROXEL_X/Y` to 160/90 in `cetra/src/postfx.h`.

**Keep the dims as runtime fields.** That part is genuinely useful and stays -- it is
what makes the resolution question answerable from the panel instead of a rebuild.

## 2. Revert the `fog_near` default to the camera's near plane

The paper is explicit about which way the slices should bunch:

> We tried various depth distributions and ended up with one **concentrated near the
> camera** -- this is where we need the most precision and aliasing artifacts show up
> easily.

My default (`fog_near = 0` deriving `fog_far/50`) pushes slices the other way, on the
argument that spending them near the lens is waste. For a camera sitting inside a
long-range medium -- their case, and the general one -- that argument is backwards.

`postfx_fog_near()` in `postfx.c` should derive the **camera's** near plane by default,
restoring prior behaviour, with `fog_near > 0` as the opt-in override for scenes that
genuinely want the volume pulled forward. `fog_depth_dist` stays as the paper-aligned
knob for biasing the curve.

This means the froxel passes need the camera near available again alongside `fogNear`;
`froxel_composite_frag` already recovers it as `camNearZ` for the aerial volume.

## 3. Exponential Shadow Maps for the fog shadow path

The real gap. `shadow.c:925` publishes `fx->fog_shadow_map_array = ss->shadow_map_array`
-- the fog samples the scene's **full-resolution 4096^2** cascades with a **single binary
depth compare** (`froxel_inject_frag.glsl` `fogVisibility()`, and the spot's `vis`).

That is precisely the case the paper wrote its shadowing section about:

> Our regular shadowing cascades were very high resolution... It was way too much detail
> for us... For smooth and approximate volumetric fog we needed something of much smaller
> resolution to reduce any flickering / aliasing artifacts from moving foliage etc. First
> implementations using wide kernel PCF had very poor performance and still some
> flickering and aliasing artifacts.

Their answer is ESM, because it makes the shadow term **filterable** -- you cannot blur a
binary depth compare, but you can blur `exp(k*d)`.

**Shape of the work:**

- A fog-owned shadow representation, sized down 4x from the source cascades (theirs was
  R32F 1024x256), living on `PostFX` beside `fog_shadow_map_array` rather than on
  `ShadowSystem` -- the fog is the only consumer and the scene's maps must stay exact for
  surface shading.
- A downsample pass writing `exp(k * depth)`, then a **separable box blur** as two passes.
  `texture.h` has no float 2D-array helper; `init_shadow_map_array` in `shadow.c` is the
  pattern to follow, or add `create_texture_2d_array_float` beside
  `create_texture_3d_float`.
- `fogVisibility()` and the spot's `vis` become
  `saturate(exp(-k * d_receiver) * texture(esm, ...))`, replacing both binary taps.
- `k` is the one tuning constant; ESM's known failure is light leaking, which the paper
  notes "wasn't noticeable in our case in participating media".

**This does NOT fix the blocky beam.** Measured: with the spot's shadows off entirely the
blocky region is pixel-identical. ESM fixes a different, real, documented problem --
shadow aliasing and flicker in the fog -- and it should be justified and verified on that
basis alone, not folded into the other artifact.

## Still open, and deliberately not solved here

The blocky beam needs **two** conditions (spec 11.11): a sub-cell silhouette AND the
result clipping at the tonemap. The paper's reason #1 for why low resolution works is
*"We store low frequency information"* -- and a 2-degree cone from a 20000 cd lamp is
high-frequency. It violates the technique's founding assumption, which is why the AC4
authors never hit it.

The band-limiting fix (widening the cone ramp to the cell's angular footprint) is written
and was reverted because it measured no change -- it addresses condition 1 while
condition 2 still clips the softening away. Worth revisiting only once condition 2 is
understood; not part of this plan.

## Out of scope

- **Density noise.** They animate one octave of Perlin by wind; we have vertical
  attenuation only. They explicitly say density "could be controlled by artists in any
  way", so this is a deliberate simplification, not a defect.
- **Hi-Z early-out** for cells behind geometry. Pure performance, listed as optional in
  the paper.

## Verification

**Grid revert** -- spores headless at the reported camera. Expect the blocks to return to
their pre-320 size; that is the point, and it is what spec 11.11 already records.

**`fog_near` revert** -- a fog fixture rendered before and after must be **0 px**, since
the default returns to prior behaviour. That is the check that the default actually
reverted rather than merely moving.

**ESM** -- the honest test is flicker, not a still. Render consecutive frames of a
fog-lit fixture with a shadow caster and compare frame-to-frame churn before and after;
it must drop. `gates.py` already uses this `_taa_churn` idiom for the catcher and cloud
gates. A still frame should show softer, non-stair-stepped shadow boundaries in the
medium.

**Aerial untouched** -- with fog off, a sky+aerial scene must be 0 px across all of this.

**Goldens** -- still outstanding from the previous change and now compounded. Diff before
re-baking: `froxel_fog_golden.png` plus the cloud pair. `gates.py` must stay green; the
`fog` scale gate currently reads 6 px, 1 LSB.
