# Spec 13.19 -- local exposure

## Context
By day in silent, the kitchen window blows out to pure white while the room looks right. The meter
exposes for the room: its 70th to 95th percentile band is mostly walls. An overcast exterior seen
through fog is roughly 6-7 stops brighter, and no global exposure or tone curve holds that. A camera
does the same thing; an eye does not, because it adapts locally.

Local exposure (Unreal 5's "Local Exposure", bilateral method) works in the log domain. It splits the
frame's luminance into a BASE layer (large-scale brightness, edge-aware) and a DETAIL layer. Only the
base layer's contrast is compressed, so the window comes down without the room going dark and without
flattening texture.

It is a **post-chain switch, off by default, layered on top of** every exposure mode (manual,
physical, metered), which still set the overall level. At Unreal's defaults (contrasts 1, detail 1)
it is an identity. silent turns it on by day. Everything is fragment passes, so GL 4.1 suffices: the
grid is built by GATHER, as the meter's histogram already is.

Agreed with the user before planning: it is in addition to the camera exposure, and it is a switch.

## Method (to be confirmed against the sources in phase 1, not from memory)
- **Per-pixel formula** (Unreal, as ported in kansei PR #49):
  `factor = exp2(mg + (base - mg) * (base > mg ? highlightContrast : shadowContrast) + (y - base) * detail - y)`
  - `y` is the pixel's own log2 luminance, in EXPOSED space (the buffer arrives pre-exposed).
  - `base = mix(grid(uv, y), blurred(uv), blend)`.
  - `mg = log2(exposure.key * 2^greyBias)`: the level the meter maps the frame to. Default 0.18; silent's day key is 0.45. One statement of middle grey.
- **Bilateral grid** (Chen, Paris & Durand 2007; Unreal's sizes):
  - cells of 64x64 HALF-res texels, and 32 log2-luminance bins over 30 stops in exposed space;
  - each texel's (log lum, weight) is split between its two nearest bins;
  - the base is the grid sliced trilinearly at (uv, y), sum over weight.
- **Blurred luminance:**
  - log2 of the mean colour of 32x32 blocks;
  - Gaussian `exp(-16.7 (x/r)^2)`, with `r` from a kernel of 50% of the frame width.
- **Defaults** (Unreal's): highlights 1, shadows 1, detail 1, blend 0.6, kernel 50%, grey bias 0.

## Design
- **Module `cetra/src/local_exposure.c/h`**, owning its programs and textures, made on first use and latched off if it fails, as `glare.c` is. PostFX fields mirror glare's:
  - SETTINGS: `local_exposure_enabled`, `_highlights`, `_shadows`, `_detail`, `_blend`, `_kernel`, `_grey_bias`;
  - ENGINE-OWNED: `struct LocalExposure*` and `local_exposure_failed`.
- **Passes**, all reading `scene_tex` after DoF and before the tonemap, at post res:
  1. **Half-res log luminance:** R16F, log2 of the 2x2 mean colour.
  2. **Grid build by gather:**
     - each (cell, bin) fragment walks its cell's texels and sums the tent-weighted log lum and weight;
     - two stages (sub-blocks, then cells) if a single stage is latency-bound, as the histogram's row split was;
     - deterministic, with a fixed loop order.
  3. **Grid blur:** a small separable blur in x, y and the bin axis, only if phase 1 confirms the method blurs the grid (Chen 2007 does).
  4. **Blurred luminance:** a 1/32 block mean, then a separable Gaussian.
- **Storage: one RG16F 2D ATLAS** (the fire grid's layout, `fire_grid.glsl`):
  - 32 grid tiles, plus one tile holding the blurred luminance;
  - a trilinear slice is two bilinear taps plus a mix, kept half a texel inside each tile.
  - **One sampler:** `tonemap_frag` goes from 14 to 15 of its 16 units, on free unit 14. A 3D texture would cost a draw (a render pass on this driver) per slice per pass, and a second sampler would fill the program.
- **Application point:** `tonemap_frag.glsl:555`, `toneSelect(c * le)` at the end of `sceneToToned`.
  - **Composite with bloom:** it scales the composite including bloom, flare and glare, as Unreal does. The glare's energy balance is unaffected because both halves are scaled together.
  - **Purkinje:** reads absolute radiance before the factor, so it is unaffected.
  - **Sharpen:** the factor is computed once at the centre pixel and reused for the sharpen taps, as AO is (411-414), so the unsharp mask never high-passes the exposure.
  - **Not upstream of the meter:** that would break auto-exposure convergence.
- **Off state:** a uniform gate, like every other tonemap stage (Purkinje's precedent kept the goldens at 0 px). If the goldens move by last-bit codegen drift, show the diff and re-bake. No compiled variant, per the standing rule.
- **Also switched off** in the passthrough debug frames (which skip the tonemap) and by `engine_set_2d_preset`.
- **Stale comments fixed while there:**
  - the tonemap header (5-10) still claims exposure is applied there;
  - `postfx.c:3952` lists the stack without LUT and dither;
  - `postfx.h:247` gives the histogram the wrong size.

## Phase 1 -- what the sources say (read 2026-10-03)

Both papers were converted with `pdf2md` into `docs/papers/` (see its README for links).

**Durand & Dorsey 2002** (`durand-dorsey-2002-fast-bilateral-filtering-hdr.md`) is the method:
- Work in the LOG of intensity, "because pixel differences then correspond directly to contrast".
- Base = bilateral filter of log intensity; detail = log intensity - base.
- Only the base's range is compressed, to a "base contrast" of 5:1 in their examples. Colour is recomposed by ratios.
- Kernels: spatial sigma at 2% of the image size, range sigma 0.4 (log units); Gaussian or Tukey influence.
- **Halos:** they still appear at antialiased edges and around flare, where too few pixels sit on either side to separate base from detail. Their fix blends toward a low-pass by the filter's own uncertainty (the normalisation weight).

**Chen, Paris & Durand 2007** (`chen-paris-durand-2007-bilateral-grid.md`) is the data structure, and it already matches our GL 4.1 shape:
- Cells store HOMOGENEOUS `(w*V, w)`: sum and weight, normalised after interpolation.
- Built by closest-integer accumulation, a box in space.
- Processed by a 3D Gaussian, separable, a 5-tap 1D kernel per axis in a fragment shader, with the sampling rates set equal to the sigmas.
- Sliced trilinearly by the image's own value.
- **Storage:** the z levels are TILED across a 2D texture, so a trilinear slice is "2 indirect texture lookups" with hardware bilinear filtering.
- **Local tone mapping** is Durand & Dorsey's: `B' = alpha*B + beta`, D unchanged, exp, colour by ratios.

**Unreal 5** (Epic's Local Exposure documentation; sizes and formula via the kansei port, PR #49) departs from Chen in four places:
- **A texel is split between its two nearest bins**, a tent in the range axis, rather than rounded to one.
- **Cells are 64x64 half-res texels**, with 32 bins over log2 -10..20.
- **The base is blended with a heavily blurred luminance** (`BlurredLuminanceBlend`, default 0.6), which "helps preserve image appearance and specular highlights, and reduces ringing". This plays the role of Durand's uncertainty fix.
- **Contrast is scaled about middle grey**, separately above and below it (highlight and shadow contrast; good values 0.6-1), with a detail strength; everything at 1 changes nothing.

**What we build, by source:**
- **Exposed log2 luminance:** Durand's log domain, in exposed space because the buffer is pre-exposed.
- **Grid geometry:** Unreal's: 64x64 half-res texels a cell, and 32 bins spanning 30 stops. The bins are centred on log2 of the meter's key, so mid grey sits 12 stops from the bottom (Unreal's own range, -10..20, puts it about 12.5 stops up). **The centring is ours.**
- **Accumulation:** Unreal's tent across the two nearest bins, as homogeneous `(sum, weight)` (Chen).
- **Grid processing:** Chen's 5-tap separable blur in x, y and z, one cell / one bin sigma. **Unreal's own grid blur is not stated in any source we have**; we follow Chen and judge it at look 1.
- **Blurred luminance:** Unreal's. Mean colour of 32x32 blocks, log2 luminance, Gaussian `exp(-16.7 (x/r)^2)` with r from a 50% kernel.
- **Per-pixel formula and defaults:** Unreal's, which at contrast 1 / detail 1 is the identity. Durand's 5:1 base contrast is the classic single knob; Unreal's split highlight/shadow scales generalise it.
- **Colour:** by ratio. The factor multiplies RGB, as Durand and Unreal both do.

## Phase 2 -- as built, and what look 1 found

**Built as designed.**
- Five passes in `local_exposure.c`: half-res, block, blur across, blur down into the atlas, grid gather; then Chen's blur in x, y and the bins.
- One RG32F atlas. RG32F rather than RG16F because a cell's sum of `w * log2 luminance` passes fp16's range.
- The factor is applied in `sceneToToned` and computed once at the centre pixel.
- The tonemap samples 15 of 16 units.

**Two probes**, kept for the gates and for anyone tuning: `--le-probe` gives the frame's luminance percentiles in stops from middle grey; silent's `--exposure-probe` gives the meter's decision.

**Look 1** (silent `--day`, the kitchen; `abandoned_window`):
- **No halo at a window frame in either scene,** at any blend. The edge-aware grid holds the edge.
- **On `abandoned_window` the pure bilateral base (blend 0) recovers the most.** The blurred luminance averages the dark wall into the window's base and compresses it less, so Unreal's 0.6 is the weaker setting here.
- **In silent's kitchen, Unreal's 0.6-1 barely moves the window,** and the reason was MEASURED, after a wrong guess.
  - **The guess:** the window was "about 10 stops" over the room and the room was held dark by the night cap.
  - **The probe:** the room's median is 1.5 stops under middle grey (~66 nits), and the window's 95th-99th percentile is 4.2-4.3 stops over it (~3,600 nits). That is a 5.8-stop gap, a real overcast interior's.
  - **The meter** settled at 0.0023, far from the 0.014 cap.
- **What actually limits it is silent's middle grey:** the day key is 0.45, not 0.18, so about one stop sits between grey and white. The window's base has to come down to within that, which is highlights ~0.2-0.25.
- **The window pulls the meter down,** a third of its 70-95% band, leaving the room 1.5 stops under grey. Shadows < 1 lifts it back toward grey.
- **Chosen for silent's day, by the user from a 3x2 sheet:** highlights 0.2, shadows 0.6, blend 0. It is retunable live once the sliders land.
- **Reported in the same session, NOT local exposure:** an "expanding sphere" as the frame settles on stepping outside.
  - **Cause:** the fog volume is stored pre-exposed and blended 90% with its history (`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:433-455`), and no history in the engine is rescaled when the exposure changes.
  - **Why a sphere:** a pixel's error is the fog's share of it, rising with distance, times the history's leftover error, falling with time. So the settled region grows outward.
  - **Booked as spec 13.20:** rescale histories by the exposure ratio.

## Phase 4 -- measured, and gated

**Cost, and the fix it needed.** The first profile of silent's kitchen by day (1600x900, a 3200x1800 post frame) put local exposure at **18.8 ms** of GPU, against a 1.4 ms tonemap. The plan's risk note had named the cause: one fragment per (cell, bin), each walking all 4096 of its cell's half-res texels, is a few thousand long serial loops. A timed breakdown at 4K, taking passes out one at a time, found the same shape a second time in the blurred luminance's 256-tap blocks.
- **The grid** is now gathered a sub-block at a time (`le_bins_frag`): one fragment per (8x8 sub-block, bin), the 32 bins of a sub-block neighbouring fragments that fetch the same texels together; `le_grid_frag` then sums a cell's 64 sub-blocks. The kitchen moves by one code on 394 pixels, from the order of the sums.
- **The blocks** are summed in two 4x4 stages (`le_block_sum_frag`, then `le_block_frag`), the first carrying a count so a block the frame's edge cuts short still weighs every texel once. 0 px.
- **Measured after both**: 0.80 and 0.87 ms in silent's kitchen over two runs, 1.19 ms on `abandoned_window` at 3840x2160 (2.40 after the grid fix alone). silent's frame went from 58 to about 42 ms of wall clock. The per-pass overhead reads 0.000 on the timer.
- **What is left** is mostly the grid's first pass, about 0.75 ms at 4K. Two bins per fragment in an RGBA target would roughly halve it; not done.

**Goldens:** the feature is off in all 31 existing recipes, which stayed 0 px with no codegen drift. One new golden, `local_exposure`: `abandoned_window` at 520x300 with highlights 0.5 and blend 0, rendered twice at 0 px before it was baked. The grid fix moved it 38 px at one code, from the order of the sums, and it was re-baked before it was ever committed; all 32 then pass at 0 px.

**Gate group `local-exposure`**, on a new fixture (`gen_local_exposure_fixture.py`): a grey wall lit to middle grey, with an emissive window about 67 times brighter, which `--le-probe` puts at +6.07 stops.
- `le-scene`: `post.local_exposure` renders the frame the flags do (0 px), and the default renders `--no-local-exposure`'s (0 px).
- `le-identity`: on at the defaults is 0.0 codes from off.
- `le-compress`: the window, wholly clipped off, clips nowhere at highlights 0.5; the far wall reads 93.6 codes either way.
- `le-halo`: the wall 2-32 px from the window's edge stays at 1.000 of the frame without the feature; a pure blur (blend 1) takes it to 0.369 at 2 px, which is the arm showing it can see a halo.
- `le-config`: the seven rows are carried, off, every contrast 1.
- **A `SCALE_GATES` row** on `cornell_point` with the feature on: 5 px at 1 LSB.
- **Falsified by hand:** `le-scene` with the cscene parse of `enabled` dropped; `le-identity` with the factor taken against the base instead of the pixel (75 codes); `le-compress` with the factor forced to 1; `le-halo` with the grid replaced by the blurred luminance, which failed that arm alone; `le-config` with the `blend` row deleted; the scale row with the grid built from absolute rather than exposed luminance.
- **Run:** `--only local-exposure,scale,glare,purkinje,exposure,config,gate-docs`, all seven groups passing; after the cost fix, `--only local-exposure,scale,gate-docs` again, every number unchanged.
