# cetra 14.26: a gutter sign colour

## Context

pcb 7.4's look found that an error in the code was shown only by its underline, and a `(` never
closed underlines one character. A line with an error or a warning now carries a `>` in the gutter,
beside its number, in its underline's colour: a sign with no fill, so it reads unlike the red and
green lines of pcb's diff, which mark a line with `-` or `+` and a fill.

pcb draws the `>` through what spec 14.16 gave the editor: `UIEditorStyle.sign`, a codepoint drawn
in the gap after the number on the first row of each line a style's ranges reach. That sign is
drawn in the text's plain colour (`docs/ui.md`, The editor), and nothing in a style reaches it. An
error's `>` and a warning's would be the text's colour both, and neither the colour of its line's
underline.

Branch and spec: `14.26-a-gutter-sign-colour`, off cetra's master (`4b0e3b4f`).

## The design

One field in `UIEditorStyle`, after `sign`:

```c
vec4 sign_colour; // the sign's colour; alpha 0 = the text's
```

- **Alpha 0 is the default**, as `UITheme.line_numbers` (14.14) and `selection` say "alpha 0 =" the
  engine's. A zeroed style draws exactly what it draws now, so `ui-theme-identity` and every golden
  are untouched.
- **The colour goes with the sign.** When several styles reach a line, the sign drawn is the last
  of theirs that has one, as now, and it is drawn in that style's `sign_colour`, never in another
  style's.
- `_draw` in `ui_editor.c` keeps the colour beside the sign as it picks the sign, and draws the sign
  in it.

## Tests

**`ui-editor-gutter`**, the arm 14.16 added, gets a fourth flag from its probe,
`probe_editor_gutter` in `apps/gametest/src/ui_editor_probe.c`:
- `coloured`: with the `-` style's `sign_colour` green, the `-` is drawn green, and the `+` of the
  other style is still the code's colour -- the field reaches its own sign and nothing else.

The three flags it has keep their meaning: `fill`, `sign` (both signs in the code's colour with
`sign_colour` zeroed), `zero`. `gates.py` reads four flags with `doc_flags`.
