# Spec 12.12 — gametest lights a floor, not a character

## Context

Spec 12.11 made `--puppet <path>` work on an arbitrary rigged humanoid: a third-party
character now loads, retargets, stands, walks and locks its feet. It also renders as an
almost black silhouette, while the app's own generated puppet reads fine.

**The demo's key light points straight down.** `build_lights`
(`apps/gametest/src/gametest.c:1482`) gives the platform a `LIGHT_SPOT` at `{0, 45, 0}` with
`.direction = {0, -1, 0}`, 100,000 cd, a 38°/47° cone and shadows (`:1493-1520`). It dominates
the plate: the sky's sun is 6.0 lux, and the two `LIGHT_AREA` water panels sit at the
waterline with ranges 70 and 90, so they never reach the plate at all. Measured at the plate
that spot is ~49 lux at centre falling to ~33 lux at the corner — a spread of only 0.58 stops.

Straight down is the one direction that lights a floor fully and a standing figure not at all:

| surface | normal | `N·L` under an overhead key |
|---|---|---|
| the plate | `(0,1,0)` | **1.0** |
| a character's torso, legs, front | horizontal | **≈ 0** |

Only shoulders and scalp catch it. `apps/render` does not show this because a bare model with
no IBL gets a three-point studio rig at 3× (`app.c:330-365`, key 9.0 / fill 4.5 / rim 6.0)
against a flat 0.1 clear colour — the character is lit from three sides *and* is the brightest
thing in frame, so the meter maps **him** to key.

**Outcome: the demo's key rakes across a standing figure instead of pressing down on it, and
an arbitrary `--puppet` reads.**

## GATE — do this first; it can invalidate the whole spec

**One command, no code change:**

```bash
./out/bin/render -m my_models/raiden/source/raiden_textured_rigged.glb --render-mode 8
```

`RENDER_MODE_METALLIC_ROUGH` (`common.h:86`) writes metallic to R, roughness to G.

- **Body solid red → STOP.** The metallicRoughness texture did not bind, `metallicLayer < 0`,
  and `pbr_frag.glsl:1408-1411` then leaves `metallicMap = metallic` — the authored factor
  alone. Every such material becomes a **black mirror by accident**. That is an import defect
  affecting every glTF character, not a gametest lighting question, and it gets its own spec.
- **Body shows real variation → proceed** with the rake below.

### Gate result: PASSED, and it corrects this spec's own premise

The body is **not** solid red. It shows rich variation — bright green hair (rough,
non-metallic), red-orange armour plates (metallic), olive limbs — and the import log confirms
the embedded MR textures bind: `Metalness texture loaded async: *5` / `*8`. So this is not an
import defect, and the rake is the right subject.

**The character is mostly DIELECTRIC, not metal.** That retires the "no diffuse to fall back
on" reasoning entirely — and it makes the diagnosis cleaner rather than weaker. This was never
about metal. It is about `N·L`: a *diffuse* surface with a horizontal normal gets nothing from
an overhead key either. The table above is the whole mechanism, and it applies to any asset.

One thing to know when re-running the gate: the MR textures arrive **asynchronously**
(`import.c:686`), and the loader uploads at most five a frame, so a 3-frame run can photograph
the pre-bind state and read as a false positive. Use `-f 60`.

**Why this gate exists: I previously claimed "7 of 15 materials are `metallicFactor = 1.0`,
i.e. pure metal with no diffuse". That claim was wrong and must not be carried forward.** In
glTF `metallicFactor` *multiplies* the MR texture's B channel, and all seven of those
materials do carry an MR texture (verified: embedded PNGs at `bufferView` 21/51/63). So
`metallicFactor = 1.0` is normal, correct authoring. Those materials are pure metal *only if
the texture failed to bind*. The import path does handle glTF's `aiTextureType_UNKNOWN`
packing (`import.c:806-822`), so binding is expected — but expected is not measured.

## The change

**One light, repositioned and re-aimed.** Not merely re-pointed: the lamp is 44.5 above the
plate (plate surface `y = 0.5`, `GAMETEST_FLOOR_HALF_Y`, 50×50 at the origin), so rotating
`.direction` alone drags the pool off-centre by `44.5·tan θ` — 26 units at 30°, on a plate
whose half-extent is 25. The pool must stay where it was authored.

So: move the lamp to an offset position and aim it back at the plate's centre.

```c
// gametest.c:1490-1497, platform_key
const float rake_deg = <measured>;                       // off vertical
const float reach    = (spot_y - GAMETEST_FLOOR_HALF_Y) * tanf(glm_rad(rake_deg));
.position  = { -reach * 0.8f, spot_y, -reach * 0.6f },
.direction = <plate centre minus position>,              // need not be unit; see below
```

`.direction` need not be normalised — `light_set_direction` copies verbatim
(`light.c:133-141`), and normalisation happens downstream at pack/publish (`shadow.c:313,
336, 939`, and `lights_ubo.glsl:228-236` asserts the same of the cluster path). The existing
three-point rig already passes non-unit vectors.

Three consequences of the geometry, each **measured, not assumed**:

- **Inverse-square.** Lamp-to-plate-centre grows from 44.5 to `sqrt(44.5² + reach²)` — 51.4 at
  30°, so the centre dims to 75%. Compensate intensity by the square of the ratio, or the
  plate darkens and the meter re-opens, undoing part of the gain.
- **The cone footprint becomes an ellipse.** The author sized 38°/47° so the falloff "finishes
  OFF the plate rather than across it" (`:1487-1491`); the far edge now subtends less and the
  near edge more. Check the outer cutoff still clears the far corner; widen if not.
- **`range = spot_y + 120`** was set to reach the shaft rim so inverse-square carries the fall
  down the wall (`:1502-1517`). Re-check the wall still gets its gradient from the new origin.

**The rake angle is chosen by measurement**, not by eye: the smallest angle at which the
character's vertical surfaces read, subject to the pool still covering the plate. Sweep 20–40°
and bisect.

## What to measure

The instrument is a **pinned camera** — `--cam-eye` / `--cam-target` / `--fov` exist in this
app precisely because its camera is derived from the player every frame, so without them a
framing cannot be photographed twice and no visual change can be A/B'd at all.

1. Same pinned framing at rake 0 (today) and across the sweep, with **both** assets: the
   third-party character and the generated puppet.
2. Mean luminance of the **character's pixels** against the **plate's**. The ratio is the
   number that matters; the meter moves both.
   My earlier "an HDR barely helps, 0.289 → 0.291" was taken over a crop that was mostly
   background — **weak evidence, do not carry it forward.**
3. Pool coverage: sample the plate's four corners and centre.

Photograph a **settled** frame. The player spawns at `y = 2` and is still in `touch_down` at
frame 60; by step 100 the trace reads `locomotion`. Use frame 200.

## Verification

**Looking at it is the instrument, and it is not the last step.** The entire deliverable is
how something looks; a green suite only says nothing else broke.

```bash
./out/bin/gametest --puppet <third-party>.glb   # reads as a character, not a silhouette
./out/bin/gametest                              # the generated puppet still reads
./out/bin/gametest --no-puppet                  # the red box, for the plate's own look
```

**Blast radius is fully mapped — only two goldens and one gate arm run the `gametest` binary
at all:**

```bash
python3 scripts/gates.py --only anim     # anim-twin is the one pixel arm, bar >= 20 px
python3 scripts/goldens.py --only menu
python3 scripts/goldens.py --only menu_focus
python3 scripts/gates.py                 # whole suite at the end
```

- `menu` / `menu_focus` **will** move: their backdrop is 88% alpha, so ~23% of the lit world
  reaches every pixel. Re-bake. Both are already documented as knowingly stale since 12.6 and
  unstable to 48,886 px — **re-run before believing any non-zero**
  (`docs/verification.md:704-711, 775-778`).
- `anim-twin` is a self-relative difference between two gametest shots, so a uniform change
  cancels to first order — but the bar is only 20 px over 8 frames in a fogged basin. Check it.
- `gamepad`, `audio`, `ui`, `save`, `ik` and the other 15 `anim` arms read probe **text** only
  and cannot see this.

**No new gate arm.** A pixel arm over a character region needs a fixed rect under a follow
camera and would sit inside the menu goldens' own instability — the reasoning that already
refused a pixel arm for IK (`docs/verification.md:618-622`). The two re-baked goldens are the
regression net; the eye is the acceptance test.

## Out of scope

- **Centre-weighted metering — investigated and REJECTED on arithmetic, not taste.** The meter
  is a weighted mean of log2 luminance, so a subject shifts it by at most
  `(share of metered population) × (its contrast)`. The character is **~13 of 4096 texels on
  the 64×64 measure buffer (0.32%)**; centre weighting at r=0.4 raises that to 0.51%, a ceiling
  of **0.022 stops**. Worse, `meter_low = 0.70` (`exposure.c:65`) discards the darkest 70%
  before the mean — and the character *is* the darkest thing in frame, so its true contribution
  is **exactly zero** under every mode. Worse again: the frame is ~19% dark pit at the top and
  ~81% plate, so centre weighting de-weights *the pit*, pushing the metered value **up** and
  the picture **darker** — the opposite of the intent. And `attach_rig` applies a flat
  `PLAYER_SCALE = 2.0` with no height normalisation, so no fixed `meter_radius` is correct for
  `--puppet`'s whole domain. `METERING_SPOT` tight enough to isolate the subject would average
  ~5 texels of an animated figure and breathe ±0.29 stops continuously — which headless
  determinism would hide from the suite and only the eye would catch.
- **A hero light that follows the player.** The right answer if the demo ever needs a character
  to read *anywhere* rather than on the plate. Not needed for one lamp aimed at one stage.
- **`scene->ambient_radiance`.** Not a lever here at all: read *only* in the no-IBL branch
  (`scene.h:323`, `render.c:624-626`, `pbr_frag.glsl:2268/2458`) and gametest always installs a
  sky-derived IBL. The only global dimmer is `scene->ibl->intensity`, which moves plate and sky
  by exactly as much.
- **The `ik-pole` arm gap (#551).** Already filed: the pole derivation added in 12.11 has no
  instrument, because `ik-pole` runs on the straight-binding generated puppet and exercises the
  fallback path only.
