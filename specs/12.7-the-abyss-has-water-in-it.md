# Spec 12.7 — The abyss has water in it

## Context

Spec 12.6 shipped and merged: `apps/gametest` has a floating 50x50 plate, a long drop, a
walled basin with turquoise water, and swimming for both characters. It works, but the basin
is **built from boxes** — `grotto_box` lays down a seabed slab, four skirt walls, four cliff
walls and a tilted overhang ring (`apps/gametest/src/gametest.c:503-600`). It reads as boxes,
because it is boxes.

The ask:

- The basin becomes a **procedurally generated rocky cave** — real rock shape, real textures,
  real materials. **No roof needed**, which is the decision that makes this tractable: a
  roofless bowl is single-valued in Y, so it is a HEIGHTFIELD and the whole
  `cetra/src/procedural/` subsystem applies.
- The platform is **floating in the air**, lit by **its own light from above** (a point or a
  cone), not by a global sun.
- The drop is **long** — it should feel like jumping into an abyss.
- The water at the bottom is **luminous turquoise, and it is what lights the rock walls**.
  Not black down there: turquoise-visible.

The reference is a **cenote** — a deep limestone shaft with a pool at the bottom, walls lit
from below by the water. That is precisely the described image, and it is a shape a
heightfield can express.

**Why the lighting approach changed.** The first reading of "dark" was "make the pit black",
which fights the engine: IBL irradiance and `ambient_radiance` reach every fragment regardless
of enclosure — there is no occlusion on them — so an open pit under a lit sky cannot be made
black without flattening the sky too. Lighting the platform with a LOCAL light instead makes
that fight unnecessary: the global environment can simply be dim, and every lit thing in the
frame is lit by a light that was placed on purpose.

## Design

### 1. The shaft (geometry)

Replace `build_grotto`'s boxes with one procedural heightfield, built through the terrain
subsystem the engine already has.

The shape is a **shaft**, not a shallow bowl: walls rising far above the waterline so the
player falls *through* rock for most of the drop, with the pool at the bottom.

```
        walkable plate (unchanged - the IK fixture lives here)
     ===========================
      \     rocky underside    /
       \______  mass   _______/
              \______/

           . . . open air . . .        <- the abyss: dark, featureless
                 (falling)                 nothing to see but walls

     \                           /
      \                         /      <- rocky shaft walls, lit from
       \      rocky walls      /           BELOW by the water as you near it
        \                     /
         \~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~/          <- luminous turquoise surface
          \_________________/           <- eroded rock basin under the water
```

**Built from a custom `TerrainField`, hand-filled.** `TerrainParams.field` overrides the
analytic fbm entirely, and `terrain_height_at_level` returns from the field branch *before*
the island lerp (`terrain.c:700-710`), so a hand-written bowl is not reshaped behind your back.

Two APIs that look like the answer and are not:

- **`island_start`/`island_depth` is a DOME with no inverse.** `island_falloff`
  (`terrain.c:646`) only ever moves height *down* with radius, and a negative depth lifts the
  rim while crushing interior relief to a quarter — a lumpy plateau with a ring, not a basin.
  Leave `island_start = 0` (off).
- **`terrain_field_seed` is unusable.** It nulls `.field` and writes the analytic fbm over
  every node (`terrain.c:213-242`). Write your own loop in its shape instead.

The order, every step load-bearing:

1. `terrain_field_alloc(&field, 257)` — `calloc`s all four planes (`terrain.c:62`).
2. **Fill `field->height` yourself**, over `terrain_field_node` / `terrain_world_x|z`: a radial
   profile plus fbm, with a low-frequency angular warp so it does not read as a turned bowl.
   **No Y offset exists** — `TerrainParams.center` is a `vec2`, so bake the absolute world Y
   into the height values.
3. `terrain_field_measure` (`terrain.c:193`) — owed by whoever last wrote the field.
4. `terrain_erode(&field, &p, &ep, &st)` (`erosion.c:490`). It runs over whatever is in
   `height`, never seeds, and takes world scale from extent alone.
5. Install `p.field` **after** eroding; `terrain_field_build_pyramid` **last**, since the
   levels are copies.
6. `terrain_build_tile` per tile -> `mesh->material` -> node; `terrain_build_collider`
   (`terrain.c:1295`) -> `SHAPE_MESH` / `MOTION_STATIC` (mandatory — `physics.c:577` refuses a
   non-static mesh), before `physics_world_optimize`.

**Erosion is what makes it rock rather than noise.** Thermal puts scree at the foot of the
walls, hydraulic carves channels down them, and `talus` must rise from the 0.62 default to
keep walls steep — the threshold is `talus * cell`, so a 0.47-unit cell shaves far harder than
forest's 1.96. The closed-boundary rule (`erosion.c:106`), an artifact for forest, is correct
here: a basin is genuinely endorheic, water ponds in the middle, and the deposit mask lights
up the floor. That is the silt, for free.

**Skip the cook at first.** At res 257 the bake is small and the cook machinery is ~70 lines.

#### The Jolt trap, which this design walks straight into

`SHAPE_MESH` over a large run of exactly coplanar triangles fails Jolt's triangle splitter and
hits `DummyTrace` = `JPH_ASSERT(false)` — **debug builds die, release builds silently build a
bad BVH**, which is the worse half (`AABBTreeBuilder.cpp:205`, `IssueReporting.cpp:9`).

Spec 11.63 hit this exactly: the island falloff saturated, an outer collider became "a plane
of exactly coplanar triangles", and it died in `SHAPE_MESH`. The fix was geometric — the sea
floor keeps a quarter of the terrain's relief (`ISLAND_FLOOR_RELIEF`, `terrain.c:627`).

**A flat pool floor is that same geometry.** So: keep fbm on the **floor**, not only the walls;
never `fmaxf`-clamp the floor to a constant (a saturating clamp is how 11.63 produced its
plane); and keep the floor's noise at a wavelength the **collider's** coarser cell can still
resolve. Do not rely on erosion's deposition to supply the variation — an erosion-off run must
not crash either.

### 2. Rock materials

The engine already makes procedural rock: `terrain_layer_maps(TERRAIN_LAYER_ROCK, ...)` plus
SILT and GRAVEL (`terrain_tex.h:37`), packed for the `MaterialTextureArray` and blended
through `include/layers.glsl`, which is **already triplanar**. Not optional here: a shaft wall
is near-vertical, and a UV-mapped rock face stretches into streaks.

**"Rock on the walls, silt in the basin" is free, and it is decided on the CPU.** There is no
slope or height input in `layers.glsl` — the per-fragment blend reads the splat and the layer
heights only. But `terrain_bake_splat` (`terrain.c:503-517`) already computes exactly this:
rock from **slope alone** (so cliffs are bare rock even with erosion off), silt from the
`deposit` mask, gravel from `flow`. `apps/forest`'s `bake_terrain_layers` (`forest.c:1092`) is
the reference for the arming sequence.

Four ways to arm it wrong, each of which renders a plausible frame:

- **`scene_add_material` is mandatory and gametest never calls it.** Layer indices are resolved
  by `material_texture_array_build`, which walks `scene->materials`
  (`material_texture_array.c:127`). gametest's `create_box_node` just `create_material()`s and
  assigns (`gametest.c:320-332`) — fine for scalar materials, and it leaves every layer index
  at `-1` for a layered one.
- **`splat_space = SPLAT_SPACE_WORLD_XZ`.** `build_grid` writes UV1 as a literal `0,0`
  (`terrain.c:1135`), so a UV1 splat samples one texel and the whole terrain resolves to layer
  0. This shipped broken in 11.60 through a green suite.
- **`p.layered = true`** before any tile is built, or `terrain_tint` writes the ground's colour
  into vertex colour, the shader multiplies colour by layer albedo, and the ground comes out
  near black.
- **`layer_count` LAST** — it is what arms the shader.

### 3. Lighting — the part that carries the whole look

Three sources, each placed on purpose, and a deliberately dim global environment.

**a. The platform light (a cone from above).** A `LIGHT_SPOT` above the plate pointing straight
down. This makes the platform a lit stage hanging in dim air, and it keeps the 12.4 IK fixture
— the ramp and the three steps — readable, which a dark platform would lose. `apps/spores`'
flashlight (`spores.c:186-200`) is the call-site shape.

Three numbers that are not guesses:

- **The cone must be far wider than the default.** `inner_cutoff`/`outer_cutoff` are
  half-angles in RADIANS on the desc, and **0 means 12.5 and 15 degrees** (`light.h:152`) — a
  spotlight circle in the middle of the plate. The plate is 50x50, so its half-diagonal is
  35.4; a light at 40 units up needs ~41 degrees to reach a corner, at 60 up ~31. Start around
  `glm_rad(38)` inner / `glm_rad(46)` outer at 45 units up, and soften the band — spores'
  18->20 is a torch edge, which is the opposite of what a stage wash wants.
- **`range` is the lever that keeps it off the shaft**, and unlike an area panel a spot's
  range genuinely windows the falloff. Set it just past the platform's own depth so the light
  dies in the air below rather than washing the cave.
- **Spot intensity is CANDELA, not nits.** spores uses 20000 for a torch-scale hot spot; a
  soft wash over a 50-unit plate is a different number entirely and wants tuning by eye.

`cast_shadows = true` is worth trying here — a spot renders a perspective shadow map, so the
ramp and steps cast real shadows across the plate, which is most of what sells a lit stage.

**b. The water as a light source — TWO objects, and conflating them is the trap.**

The look and the light are separate mechanisms, and only one of them emits:

- **The surface LOOKS luminous** through `water->scatter_glow`. It is a shading term and
  nothing more — `inscatter = scatter_albedo*incident + scatter_glow` feeds `FragColor` and
  stops there (`water_frag.glsl:1097`). Every escape route is deliberately closed: the
  G-buffer albedo writes `vec4(scatter_albedo, 0)` with the comment that the glow "is not
  reflectance and is deliberately left out" (`water_frag.glsl:1285`), and the SSS and
  spec-occ attachments are written zero. **It delivers exactly zero irradiance.** It does
  bloom, which is most of why it reads as luminous.
  `apps/tree` reverted a glow floor as "an authored constant wearing a new name" — right for
  tree, where the sun lit the sea. Here the glow IS the art direction.
- **The rock is lit by a `LIGHT_AREA` LTC panel** at the water plane. This is the only
  mechanism in the engine that puts light from a horizontal surface onto a wall, and it is
  well suited: **panels are one-sided** (`ltc.glsl:135`), so one facing up lights everything
  above and wastes nothing on the submerged rock.

Four authoring traps, every one of which renders a plausible frame:

| trap | consequence |
|---|---|
| `direction` defaults to `(0,-1,0)` — **down** (`light.c:33`) | the panel lights the seabed and nothing above it. Must pass `(0,1,0)`. |
| `size` of `0` means **50x50**, not zero (`light.c:46`) | a silently wrong-sized pool |
| `range` on a panel only shrinks the **cull sphere**; the area branch `continue`s before the punctual falloff (`pbr_frag.glsl:2018`) | setting it truncates the light at a hard cluster boundary instead of softening it. **Leave it 0** and let `light_cull_radius` derive — for a large panel that is thousands of units, which is what keeps it alive while the player is far above. |
| `intensity` of 0 emits nothing and is a value, not a default | |

`apps/render`'s `spawn_area_light` (`render.c:2685`) is the call-site shape. Panels *can*
cast shadows (one 120-degree map, `shadow.c:992`) — off to start; the cost is a shadow layer
and the look does not obviously need it.

**Three things that cannot work, so nobody tries them later:**

- **`--emissive-lights` can never see the water.** The surface is a screen-space projected
  grid drawn by a direct `glDrawElements` (`water.c:2149`) — no `Mesh`, no `Material`, no
  scene-graph node, so the emissive walk has nothing to find.
- **No capture can see it either** — `water_will_draw` returns false under
  `engine->capturing` (`water.c:892`), so DDGI and reflection probes are both blind to it.
- **The panel will not light the water.** `water_key_light` goes through
  `scene_key_directional`, which skips every non-directional light (`scene.c:357`). The
  water's own appearance comes from `scatter_glow`, the sky ambient and a directional — never
  from the panel beneath it.

#### Where the panels go — and why there are two

**Panel 1 — the wall light.** Centred on the basin, a hair above the surface, facing up:

```c
.position  = {0.0f, GROTTO_WATER_Y + 0.25f, 0.0f},
.direction = {0.0f, 1.0f, 0.0f},        // MUST override the (0,-1,0) default
.up        = {0.0f, 0.0f, 1.0f},        // panel then lies in the XZ plane
.size      = {90.0f, 90.0f},            // the waterline, a little oversized; 0 means 50x50
.range     = 0.0f,                      // let light_cull_radius derive
```

Sized a little wider than the pool so the falloff does not visibly end before the rock does.
One-sided and facing up, it lights every wall above it and spends nothing on submerged rock.
`cast_shadows = false` to start: its light will pass through outcrops, which a broadly concave
basin barely shows, and a 120-degree map is a shadow layer spent on something the look may not
need.

**Panel 2 — the seabed light, and it is not optional.** 12.6's own comment is the reason:
water colour is `bed * exp(-absorption*path) + inscatter`, where `bed` is the refraction
resolve of **real lit geometry** — "a pale floor under shallow water IS the turquoise". Kill
the fill lighting (which section 4 does) and the seabed goes black, so the shallows go black
with it and only the deep water keeps colour, from `scatter_glow` alone.

So a second panel just *below* the surface, facing down, dimmer, lighting the floor it is seen
through:

```c
.position  = {0.0f, GROTTO_WATER_Y - 0.25f, 0.0f},
.direction = {0.0f, -1.0f, 0.0f},
```

The two do not interfere: each is one-sided, and the water surface itself reads **neither** of
them — `water_key_light` goes through `scene_key_directional` and skips every non-directional
light, so the panels light the rock and the bed while the surface's own appearance stays
`scatter_glow` plus sky. No double-brightening anywhere.

### 4. Making the pit dark — what actually keeps it lit

Four terms reach the basin floor, and **three of them are not occluded by geometry at all**.
Any amount of cliff ring is irrelevant to them. In order of size:

1. **The three-point fill rig is the dominant light down there, not the sky.**
   `scene_add_three_point_lights(scene, 0.25f)` (`gametest.c:1332`) creates three lights whose
   descs set no `.type` — so `LIGHT_DIRECTIONAL`, enum zero — and no `.cast_shadows`, so
   false. That leaves `shadow_map_index = -1`, which `pbr_frag.glsl:2129` reads as "no shadow
   test", so `shadow` stays **1.0** forever. Key, fill and rim all point downward and hit a
   +Y floor near-broadside with **no occlusion of any kind**. This is lever number one and it
   also lights the platform, which is what the rig is there for — so it gets replaced by the
   spot rather than simply deleted.
2. **IBL diffuse is `texture(irradianceMap, N)`** — the normal alone. A sealed fragment at
   y=-88 gets byte-identical irradiance to one on the platform. Specular is the same with `R`.
   The only handle is `scene->ibl->intensity`, which dims **everything** equally.
3. **The baked sky lights its own virtual ground**, so even downward directions in the cube
   are lit (`GROUND_ALBEDO 0.3`, `SUN_ILLUMINANCE 3.0`, GLSL literals with no app-facing knob).
   The basin's inward-facing walls sample a sunlit ground, not black.
4. **The sun's single cascade loses most of the basin.** `ortho_size = 80` about the world
   origin, which sits on the platform 88 units above the floor: the basin's outer ring and all
   four corners project outside the map, and `calculateShadow` returns **fully lit** for an
   out-of-range projection. The cliff casts into the middle of the floor and nowhere else.

**Two levers make the pit dark *because it is a pit*; everything else is a global dimmer:**

- **A `FogVolume` over the basin** (`scene_add_fog_volume`, `scene.h:477`) — a world-bounded
  box that cannot touch the sky or the platform. A volume alone arms the pass without
  `fog_enabled`. **Two traps turn it into a brightener:** `fog_ambient_from_sky` is true by
  default and the sky stamps `zenith_radiance` over the ambient *every frame*, so untouched
  fog glows — call `postfx_set_fog_ambient(fx, (vec3){0,0,0})`, which clears the flag too; and
  `fog_sun_boost` adds sun in-scatter through the same out-of-cascade "lit" answer as item 4,
  so set it to 0. Also raise `fog_far` from 60, or the pit sits past the last froxel slice.
- **A GI volume** is the one genuinely occlusion-aware indirect term — `giSampleIrradiance`
  gates its probe reads on a backface test and a Chebyshev visibility test, explicitly to stop
  light bleeding through a wall into an unlit room. It replaces the irradiance map, so it fixes
  item 2 properly rather than dimming it. Cost is a one-frame bake hitch and a hand-fitted
  grid; `scene_bounds` would span y=0 to -190 with 4 probes vertically, far too coarse.

**Two premises from the first draft that the research refuted:**

- **Auto-exposure will not fight this.** `exposure_auto_gain` is clamped to `<= 1.0` — it only
  ever darkens an over-bright scene. A dark cave cannot be brightened back up. It helps: sky
  in frame meters high and stops down. (gametest has no `-E` or `--no-auto-exposure` anyway;
  those are `apps/render` flags.)
- **`ambient_radiance = 0` does nothing.** `pbr_frag` reads it only in the `else` arm of
  `iblEnabled > 0 || giEnabled > 0`, and gametest always has an IBL. It is already zero and
  already unreachable.
- **AO cannot help.** `ssao_radius` is 0.4 view-space units against a 120-unit basin, with a
  further 0.08-UV screen clamp. It is a crevice term, not an enclosure term.

### 5. The long drop

Push the water plane well below 12.6's `GROTTO_WATER_Y -80`. At `GROTTO_TERMINAL_V 25`,
eighty units is roughly four seconds of fall; an abyss wants noticeably more, so the target is
somewhere past -160.

**The middle of the fall should be dark and featureless on purpose.** You leave a lit
platform, fall through unlit air, and the turquoise rises to meet you. That transition is the
whole effect and it costs nothing to build — it is simply what happens between the spot's
`range` ending and the panel's reach beginning.

Two constraints the new numbers must keep, both inherited and both load-bearing:

- **`GROTTO_TERMINAL_V` stops the plunge punching through the floor.** Its existing comment
  works the arithmetic: free fall over 80 units arrives at ~56 m/s, which buoyancy trades off
  over ~13 units of depth — straight through an 8-unit basin and into the seabed. At 25 the
  plunge is under 6. **A longer drop does not change this** (terminal velocity is terminal),
  but the basin must stay deep enough to absorb that ~6 units.
- **The basin floor must stay at least 3 units below `GROTTO_WATER_Y + PLAYER_RIG_DROP`.** A
  floating character's feet sit at water minus rig drop; the IK foot ray reaches three units
  down and `stick_to_floor_distance` is 0.5. A procedural floor that wanders up near the
  swimmer plants their feet on the bottom or reports them grounded. The box seabed was placed
  at -11 against exactly this; a noisy floor has to respect it at its **highest** point, not
  its mean.

## What is NOT changing

- The 50x50 plate, the ramp and the three steps. The `ik` gate casts rays at x=18 and x=-16
  and those must keep their numbers.
- Swimming, the float clamp, the swim clip, the IK swimming gate — all of 12.6's character
  work stands.
- The follow camera.
- `build_ik_ground`'s second caller, `ik_probe_world`, which builds its own floor and must
  NOT get the shaft.

## Phases

Each phase leaves something runnable, so the look can be judged as it is built.

1. **The long drop.** Move `GROTTO_WATER_Y` down past -160 and re-check the two inherited
   clearances. Boxes still in place — this isolates "does the fall still land, does buoyancy
   still catch it, does swimming still work" from every other change.
2. **The shaft geometry.** The hand-filled `TerrainField`, erosion over it, tiles and one
   `SHAPE_MESH` collider; delete `grotto_box`, the box `build_grotto`, and `grotto_bed_height`
   in favour of a `terrain_height_at` adapter. **Floor noise from the first commit** — the
   coplanar-triangle assert is a debug-build crash, not a visual defect.
3. **Rock materials.** Layered triplanar rock/silt/gravel, splat from the erosion masks, and
   the four arming steps (`scene_add_material`, `SPLAT_SPACE_WORLD_XZ`, `p.layered = true`,
   `layer_count` last).
4. **The lighting rig.** The platform spot, the two panels, and retiring the three-point fill
   rig into them. This is the phase where the look either arrives or does not.
5. **The dark.** The basin `FogVolume` with its ambient and sun-boost traps closed, and the
   shadow cascade fix so the sun stops fully lighting the basin's outer ring.
6. **Docs.** `AGENTS.md`'s gametest row, `docs/cli-reference.md` if any flag appears,
   `docs/verification.md` for what no arm sees — which is nearly all of this.

**Phase 4 is the one that matters and the one most likely to need iteration.** The three
before it are mechanical; the numbers in it (spot intensity in candela, panel intensity in
nits, the two colours) are all eye-tuned and none has a right answer on paper.

## Verification

Gates and goldens are stopped at the user's instruction and stay stopped while the look is
being dialled in. The instrument for all of this is the eye:

```bash
./build.sh
./out/bin/gametest                  # walk to the edge, jump, watch the turquoise arrive
./out/bin/gametest --no-chaser      # the swim, isolated
```

Both menu goldens are already stale from 12.6's camera flip and this will move them further.
One re-bake at the end, once the look is settled — not per phase.
