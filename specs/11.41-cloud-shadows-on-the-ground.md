# Spec 11.41 — cloud shadows on the ground

## Context

Spec 11.39 shipped cloud shadows into the froxel volume and measured what that bought:
**sky/air RMSE 0.0353, ground 0.0013**. The froxel half shadows in-scattered light, so it puts
weather in the haze and essentially nothing underfoot. The moving dappled light on terrain — the
thing D2 was written to deliver — is unbuilt, and was recorded as hard-blocked on D0.

**It is not blocked.** `pbr_frag` has no program-variant machinery; unit sharing is one declaration,
a `#define` alias, a structural exclusivity argument and a per-pass bind (`pbr_frag.glsl:248`,
`render.c:388-406`). And unit 6 is set unavailable at the top of the opaque pass and stays unbound
and unread for the whole of it (`render.c:1123`, resolve not until `:1360`). A consumer read only in
the opaque pass takes it by alias at no cost. The surface half needed a free *pass*, not a free unit.
That is committed to the roadmap as Wall 1's third escape (`83f5263`).

**Outcome intended:** moving dappled sunlight on terrain, the catcher and the water, at zero cost to
the sampler ledger and with no golden re-bake of the IBL corpus.

**Scope grew once, deliberately.** Phases 1-4 shipped the surface half and its arms, and those arms
immediately found that the cloud shadow MAP is blank below ~10° of sun elevation and truncated at
every elevation above it — a defect in what 11.39/11.40 shipped, which the ground half made visible
because it put the map on a surface where it could be read. Phases 5-6 fix it here rather than filing
it, on the same reasoning 11.40 used when its debug tile found the map saturated to zero: a feature
that is inert in the band a shipped app actually runs in is not finished.

---

## What the three surfaces actually need — they are three different mechanisms

Reading the shaders changed the shape of this from "the same lookup in three places" to one real
consumer and two small ones.

**`pbr_frag` — the feature.** The light loop already has the sun's CSM slot in the UBO:
`dirShadow.w` is `float(CSM slot)` (`lights_ubo.glsl:21`), which is the **same number** `sky.c`
publishes as `cloud_shadow_light` (`sky.c:368`). So the sun is identified by index, exactly as the
froxel side does it, with no direction comparison — the thing `sky.c:365-367` explicitly warns
against. Multiply into `shadow` at `pbr_frag.glsl:1606-1616`, after the CSM/punctual branch and
alongside the POM self-shadow, which is the existing precedent for a second occluder folded in.

**`catcher_frag` — not a lit surface at all.** It writes `vec4(0,0,0,alpha)` where alpha is
`Σ shadowLightWeight[i] * occlusion_from(i)` (`catcher_frag.glsl:48-61`) — a darkening plane, not
shading. So its cloud shadow is a modification of the occlusion, not of a light term: sun reaching
the ground is `(1 - occlusion) * cloudSun`, so `occlusion → 1 - (1 - occlusion) * cloudSun`. It
declares **zero** samplers of its own (one tap via `csm.glsl`), so it takes a real unit, not the
alias.

**`water_frag` — one line, and much smaller than expected.** Its only sun-dependent code is the
caustics block (`:353-369`); there is **no analytic sun specular** anywhere in the file. The
reflection is a split-sum environment lookup, which already carries clouds through the sky bake, and
the bed arrives through `sceneColorTex` already shaded by `pbr_frag` and therefore already dappled.
So water's whole participation is attenuating caustics by the cloud transmittance — correct, cheap,
and FFT-only by construction since caustics already are. 4 own samplers + 9 from `ocean.glsl`, so it
takes a real unit too.

## Approach

### Phase 1 — the shared lookup and the bind

`cetra/shaders/include/cloud_shadow.glsl`, holding the shear lookup that
`froxel_inject_frag.glsl` currently inlines:

```glsl
vec2 hit = P.xz + cloudShadowShear * (cloudShadowShellY - P.y);
cloudSun = texture(cloudShadowTex, hit / cloudShadowTile).r;
```

Four consumers must state the shell geometry identically, which raises this from convenience to
requirement — the same reason `include/mboit.glsl` exists (11.17) and `include/ocean.glsl` owns both
current and previous position (11.33). Port `froxel_inject_frag` onto it in the same commit so the
include has two callers from the start and cannot be written to fit only the new one.

Bind side: `sky.c` already derives the shear at `:360-364` for the postfx publish. Add a
`sky_bind_cloud_shadow(sky, program, unit)` sibling to `bind_ltc_tables` (`ltc.c:47`) and
`bind_reflection_probe`, so the three scene programs each get it the house way. Sky owns the
"is there a map" test — `shadow_tex` being non-zero is already that statement (`sky.c:348-350`).

### Phase 2 — `pbr_frag`

`#define cloudShadowTex sceneColorTex`, mirroring `oitMomentTex` and carrying the same kind of
comment: why the alias is forced rather than clever, and why the tenants cannot collide. Bind the map
to unit 6 in the opaque pass by extending the two-way tenant choice at `render.c:397-406` to three.
Skip the read under `SUBMIT_PASS_DEPTH_ONLY`, which shades nothing.

### Phase 3 — the catcher and the water

Both take real samplers. Catcher folds into `occlusion_from`; water attenuates the caustic `focus`.
Note the two are **mutually exclusive** — the catcher is suppressed whenever water is active — so
they never share a frame and the combination needs no test.

### Phase 4 — an arm that can fail

11.39 shipped a shadow map saturated to zero at every texel with both its arms passing, and 11.40's
whole subject was green results that could not have gone red. So:

- Leave the three existing `cloudshadow-*` arms alone; they measure the froxel half.
- New arm on the same `aerial_fixture.gltf`, reading a **ground band**. `CLOUDSHADOW_BAND`
  (`gates.py:2839`) covers y 0.05-0.33, the sky; the ground band is a new constant at the terrain's
  y range.
- **The arm must be seen to fail** against a stubbed lookup before it is trusted.
- `--cloud-coverage 0.2`, not the 0.45 default: 11.40 measured map mean 0.023 at 0.45 (near-overcast,
  no dapple) against 0.397 at 0.2.
- Check `--sky-debug`'s shadow tile first. It is the only instrument that separates a bad map from a
  bad lookup, and it is what caught 11.39's dead map.

### Phase 5 — the map is wrong at every elevation, and that is what makes the ground half inert

**Found by the new ground arms, and it is a defect in the SHIPPED map, not in this spec's work.**
`cloud_shadow_frag.glsl:99` caps the marched path at `CLOUD_SHADOW_SPAN_CAP_KM = 1.2`. The deck is
1.5 → 4.0 km, so 2.5 km thick, and crossing it takes `2.5 / sin(elevation)` of slant. The fraction
actually traversed is therefore `1.2 * sin(el) / 2.5`:

| elevation | deck traversed | measured map |
|---|---|---|
| 90° | 48% | — |
| 35° | 28% | mean 0.0229, sigma 0.0843 |
| 15° | 12% | mean 0.0402, sigma 0.1217 |
| 5° | **4%** | **mean 1.0000, sigma 0.0000 — no shadow at all** |

So the cap binds at EVERY elevation and the map has never been a full-deck traverse: it reports
transmittance through the bottom N% of the cloud, where N tracks the sun. Below ~10° the march never
leaves the cloud base, accumulates no optical depth, and the map silently saturates to full sun.
11.40 fixed this same constant saturating the map to ZERO; it saturates to ONE at the other end.
`apps/tree` runs a 0.8° sun, so it has never had a cloud shadow from either half.

**Why 11.40's attempts to lengthen the reach failed, which its own note diagnoses wrongly.** It
records the full traverse and a 4 km cap both going uniformly black and reads that as extinction
being too strong over a long path. The shape tile is **8 km** on `GL_REPEAT`; at 5° a full traverse
is 28.7 km of slant, so the ray wraps the tile **3.5 times** and every texel's ray passes through
essentially every cloud in the field. The map went black because the horizontal excursion
**decorrelated each texel from its own column**, not because tau was too large. The failure is on
the horizontal axis, and truncating the total path threw away the vertical coverage as collateral.

**The fix: separate the two axes the one constant is conflating.**

- **Vertical extent must span the deck**, at every sun angle, or it is not a transmittance through
  the cloud. Step in equal ALTITUDE increments -- `dh = 2.5 / 24 ≈ 104 m` -- rather than in slant.
- **Horizontal excursion must stay bounded**, which is the real constraint: both for the
  decorrelation above and for the physical reason `clouds.glsl:175-177` gives for its own 1.2 km
  cone. Clamp the accumulated XZ offset to that reach; past it the march keeps climbing the local
  column, an explicit assumption that the deck is horizontally homogeneous beyond the radius the
  field can be trusted over -- the same assumption the deck's own cone already makes.
- **But integrate over the TRUE slant length**, `dh / sunY`, not the clamped step. The light really
  does travel that far; only the density estimate is clamped. This is what makes low sun come out
  correct rather than merely non-zero.

**The consequence, and it is the point rather than a side effect: low sun goes uniformly DARK.** At
5° the sun ray crosses 28.7 km of a 45%-coverage field, so it is blocked everywhere and there is no
dapple to preserve. That is what a low sun under a cloud deck does. The current map says the
opposite, which is the bug.

**Phase 5 ends in a measurement, not a commit.** The traverse roughly doubles at useful elevations,
so tau roughly doubles and the map darkens everywhere -- physically right for a 2.5 km cumulus deck,
where the dapple should come from gaps rather than from partial transmission, but a visible change to
the default look. Sweep elevation (5 / 15 / 35 / 75) against coverage (0.2 / 0.45) through
`--sky-debug`, report peak/mean/sigma for each, and decide whether the default coverage needs its own
tuning pass BEFORE re-baking anything.

**Files:** `cetra/shaders/cloud_shadow_frag.glsl` only. The lookup side is already correct -- it
returned 1.0 because the map said 1.0.

### Phase 6 — the arm that would have caught this

`cloudshadow-map` reads the map at the fixture's own sun, so it can only ever see one elevation. Add
a **low-sun** arm on the same tile: at 5° the map must carry range. That is the arm whose absence let
a map that is blank in the entire band below 10° ship and stay shipped.

Expect `cloudshadow-map`'s existing floors (`peak >= 0.30`) to need revisiting at coverage 0.45 once
the traverse is complete -- if a full-deck integral at near-overcast genuinely has no gaps, the arm
should move to the 0.2 coverage the ground arms already use rather than have its floor lowered to fit.

## Verification

**Phases 1-4 are done and measured.** Recorded here because the numbers are the spec's evidence:

- **The defining measurement**, on `aerial_fixture` at `--cloud-coverage 0.2`, own baseline rather
  than 11.39's (which was taken at a different coverage and resolution, so it was never mine to
  compare against): ground RMSE **0.0072 → 0.1241**, a 17x move, with the air band **0.0158 → 0.0158**
  unchanged to four decimals. That control is what proves the include refactor was a no-op and that
  this change touches only surfaces.
- Each surface independently: `pbr_frag` 217,669 px on the dry water fixture; the catcher's ground
  bands move with the sky rows at exactly 0.0000 (no fog, so no froxel half in the frame); water
  6,864 px which falls to **exactly 0** under `--no-water-caustics`, isolating the caustic path.
- The new `cloudshadow-dapple` arm was **seen to fail** at 0.0512 against a stub returning a constant
  0.5, while `cloudshadow-ground` passed at 0.7177 — and `cloudshadow-vary`, the AIR arm, also passed
  at 0.9268, which proves it never read the map's content either. `-map` plus `-dapple` are what
  separate a bad map from a bad lookup.
- Suite: **160 arms pass, 0 fail, 0 skip**; **23 of 24 goldens 0 px**, only `cloud_fixture` moving.
  (Note the arm count changed: `cloudshadow-nofog` removed, `-ground` and `-dapple` added.)

**Still to do, for phases 5-6:**

- The elevation x coverage sweep of the map, reported before any re-bake.
- Re-measure the phase-1-4 numbers above after the map fix — the ground arms were tuned against the
  truncated map and their measured values will move.
- `cloud_fixture_lowsun` currently passes at 0 px **because the feature is inert there**; after the
  fix it must move, and that is the golden that proves the low-sun repair.
- Determinism floor at the chosen framing before quoting any pixel figure.
- `--no-auto-exposure -E 1.0` on every comparison.
- **Re-bake once, at the end.** `cloud_fixture`, `cloud_fixture_lowsun` and any fog golden carrying
  clouds will move; baking now and again after phase 5 would waste a bake and invalidate the first.
- No GPU work alongside the gates.
