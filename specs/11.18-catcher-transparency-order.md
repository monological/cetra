# Shadow catcher vs transparency ordering

## Context

Spec 11.17 put OIT on by default. It inherited a defect 4.17 filed and never
fixed, and the flip changed that defect's status: it used to sit behind an
opt-in nobody turned on, and now it is on the default path.

The shadow catcher is not a floor. It is a **shadow decal** — a quad at y=0 whose
fragment shader emits `vec4(0, 0, 0, alpha)` and discards where nothing is
shadowed (`catcher_frag.glsl:57-61`), blended `SRC_ALPHA/ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA` so
it darkens whatever the environment already drew. It grounds a model on a
backdrop that has no geometry.

It draws last (`render.c:950-1035`). The OIT layer composites later still, in
postfx, after the whole scene FBO resolves (`postfx.c:2604`). So a translucent
surface behind the catcher plane is neither occluded by the floor nor darkened by
the shadow on it — it floats over both.

**Nothing tests this.** The catcher-enabled fixtures (`contact_fixture`,
`aerial_fixture`, `postfx_convergence_fixture`) are all fully opaque; the
alpha-blend fixtures all lack an environment, so none of them gets a catcher.
The near-miss, `abandoned_window`, has real curtains AND an HDR environment but
ships its own point light, so the auto-key-light block is skipped and
`shadow_catcher` is never set. Coverage is zero, both sets, no overlap.

## What the investigation changed

Three findings that make this smaller than it looked.

**1. The catcher already writes depth.** `glDepthMask` is `GL_TRUE` when it
rasterizes and depth test is on — deliberately, per `render.c:1002-1005`, so its
depth feeds the postfx SSAO resolve. It is only harmless today because it is the
last geometry in the frame, so nothing is ever depth-tested against it. That
comment states the assumption a fix has to break.

**2. Nothing existing sits between the old and new positions.** The only draws
between the skybox and the catcher are the refraction resolve, the transparent
pass, and particles — and no catcher-enabled scene has a transmissive mesh, a
blend mesh, or a particle system. So the move is a no-op for every committed
golden and every gate, and its entire effect is on content that does not exist
yet. That is what makes it worth doing now rather than after something breaks.

**3. Under SSR — the default — the catcher writes depth across the WHOLE quad**,
not just the shadowed part (`surfaceMode == 1` removes the discard,
`catcher_frag.glsl:35-39`). With SSR off, only the shadowed footprint writes.
That inconsistency exists today and is invisible today; the fix makes it visible.

## Decisions taken

**Leave the depth write exactly as it is.** `catcher_frag.glsl` is not touched.
Under SSR the whole fake floor occludes; with SSR off only the shadowed patch
does. That inherits an existing inconsistency rather than inventing a new rule,
and it is the only option that cannot move a golden — the alternatives either
start writing depth where none was written (SSAO reads that buffer, so
`contact_debug`, `aerial_fixture`, `cloud_fixture` and `froxel_fog` would all
need re-baking) or take away the surface the SSR march needs, which is the whole
reason `surfaceMode` exists.

**Fix all three consumers together.** OIT, the unsorted `--no-oit` late pass and
the particle soft-depth resolve all read the same depth buffer, so the move
repairs them at once and there is no way to scope it to one without adding
special cases to the others.

## The fix

**Move the catcher block from after the particle pass to immediately after the
skybox, before the refraction resolve.** One block moves, ~85 lines, nothing else
changes.

Everything then depth-tests against the floor the way it should:

| case | today | after |
|---|---|---|
| translucent behind the floor | floats over the shadow | occluded by the floor |
| translucent in front | correct | correct, and not darkened by a shadow behind it |
| particles below the floor | drawn | occluded |
| glass over a shadowed floor | refracts an unshadowed floor | refracts the shadow |

This is the only fix that separates "in front" from "behind", because
transparency writes no depth and therefore nothing downstream of it can tell the
two apart. It also repairs the classic `--no-oit` late pass and the particle pass
for free, which a composite-side fix would not.

Files: `cetra/src/render.c` only.

The comment at `render.c:1002-1005` has to be rewritten — its justification for
depth writes ("this is the last geometry of the frame") stops being true, and the
new reason is stronger: the depth is what orders transparency against the floor.

### Rejected alternative

Compositing the OIT layer into the scene FBO before the catcher restores parity
with the classic path, but parity is the wrong target — the classic path darkens
translucency *in front of* the catcher with a shadow that is behind it. It also
needs a new PostFX entry point to draw a fullscreen quad into a foreign MSAA
framebuffer (`draw_fullscreen_quad` and `quad_vao` are PostFX-private), which is
more machinery for a worse answer.

## Fixture — `assets/gen_catcher_transparency_fixture.py`

House pattern: geometry-only `.gltf` from the generator, hand-authored sibling
`.cscn` carrying the camera and the environment (this is how a fixture gets a
catcher at all — there is no `.cscn` key and no CLI flag for it, only
`"environment": {"mode": "sky"}` promoted through `cscene_apply.c:69-75`).

- An opaque box at the origin, to cast a real shadow footprint on the catcher.
- A **vertical** translucent quad (`alphaMode: BLEND`, alpha ~0.5) standing in
  that shadow, spanning y = -1 to +1 so half of it is below the fake floor.
- Camera slightly above the horizon so both halves are on screen.

Winding follows `gen_area_shadow_fixture.py:55-70`'s note; the ground convention
and the base64 single-file `.gltf` shape follow `gen_contact_fixture.py`.

## Gate — `run_catcher_transparency_gate`

Self-verifying, one render, no stored reference. Sample four points: on the panel
above the floor, beside it above the floor, on the panel below the floor, beside
it below the floor.

- **below**: on-panel must equal beside-panel — the floor hides it.
- **above**: on-panel must differ from beside-panel by a wide margin — the
  inverse half, without which the first assertion passes on any frame where the
  panel failed to render at all.

Fails today, passes after the move. Registered in `main()` beside
`run_catcher_gate`.

# As built

Shipped as planned. The catcher block moved from after the particle pass to
immediately after the skybox; nothing else in `render.c` changed, and
`catcher_frag.glsl` was not touched.

**The fix is exact, not approximate.** The gate measures the translucent panel's
disagreement with the backdrop beside it, above and below the plane:

| | above the plane | below it |
|---|---|---|
| before, moment OIT | 0.1273 | **0.1407** |
| before, unsorted late pass | 0.1575 | **0.1708** |
| after, moment OIT | 0.1273 | **0.0000** |
| after, unsorted late pass | 0.1575 | **0.0000** |

Below the plane the panel now reads the identical backdrop texel — zero, not a
small residual — while its visibility above the plane is untouched to the last
digit. Both transparency paths, which is the point: one plane, three consumers.

## The prediction that had to hold, and did

The whole risk argument was that nothing existing sits between the two
positions, so the move could not touch a committed image. **All 18 goldens
measure 0 px**, and so do two assets that the move could plausibly have reached:

- `abandoned_window` (real curtains, real dust particles) — 0 px, confirming the
  investigation's correction that it never enables the catcher at all, because it
  ships its own point light and the auto-key-light block is skipped.
- the raiden recipe — 0 px. This one *does* get a catcher (no authored lights,
  `-e` supplies an IBL) and *does* have alpha-blend hair, so it is the one real
  asset where the two meet. It is unaffected because the app recentres a model's
  bounding-box base onto y=0, which puts every part of it on or above the catcher
  plane. That is worth knowing rather than assuming: the recenter is what keeps
  ordinary content clear of this, and `--no-recenter` content is what is not.

## Deferred

- **The catcher's depth extent still depends on SSR.** With SSR on it writes
  across the whole quad; with SSR off, only under the shadow. So how much
  translucency the floor hides is keyed on an unrelated post effect. Left alone
  deliberately (see Decisions), but it is now a visible behaviour rather than a
  dormant one, and it is the obvious thing to make explicit if this area is
  touched again — a `catcher_occludes` decision of its own, rather than a side
  effect of `surfaceMode`.
- **The catcher still casts no shadow**, since its quad lives outside the scene
  graph and never enters the shadow depth pass (`specs/10.5:99`). Unchanged by
  this, and correct — it is a stand-in for a floor, not a floor.
