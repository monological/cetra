# Spec 11.76 — the ring is a 2023 bitmask collapsed into a 2011 cone

## Context

11.75 fixed a genuine cliff (`open > 1e-4 ? ratio : 1.0`) and left a residual step. The two
papers the user supplied locate the residual, and it is architectural.

**`gtao_frag.glsl` implements Therrien et al. 2023 (visibility bitmask); `spec_occ.glsl`
descends from Klehm et al. 2011 (bent normals + cones); Therrien §3.2 cites Klehm by name and
supersedes it.** The engine computes a 32-sector directional visibility mask, discards it to
one direction plus one scalar, then runs 2011-era cone arithmetic on the remains. Therrien
§4.1: *"When using a wide radius, GTAO has a tendency to produce halos around objects. Our
method doesn't have this problem."* The engine halos anyway, because the halo is manufactured
after the information that would prevent it is gone. Both papers are now in `docs/`
(50c523a, 050c325) with the deviations recorded in their headers.

**Two paper-checkable defects, both storage-neutral:**

1. **The hit criterion.** Therrien §3 specifies the **round** criterion — a sector registers
   when *half covered*. `sectorBits` (gtao_frag.glsl:76-77) uses `floor(lo·N)` /
   `ceil((hi−lo)·N)`: any overlap sets the sector, so a sample grazing 1% of one occludes it
   whole. Systematic over-occlusion, which is what a dark ring is made of.
2. **The cone aperture is derived from the wrong quantity.** Klehm §3.2 derives it from the
   **length of the mean visible direction** (concentration); `specOcclusionCone` derives it
   from AO (`sqrt(1−ao)`, energy). For the ringing case — a small occluder on open floor —
   AO 0.95 gives a **77°** cone where the unoccluded directions genuinely span the full
   hemisphere. A cone narrower than the truth is what lets the reflection lobe fall outside
   it and read zero.

## What the exploration pass changed in this plan

An Explore pass over every `.gba` consumer returned five corrections. **Four adopted, one
rejected after checking it numerically** — recorded because the rejected one is the sort of
plausible suggestion that would have quietly computed the wrong thing:

- **ADOPTED — the sample count cannot be derived from `occlusion`.** `gtao_frag.glsl:179`
  `continue`s when `projNLen < 1e-5`, skipping a slice's contribution to *both* `occlusion`
  and `bent`, while `occlusion /= SLICES` (:275) divides unconditionally. The derived count is
  then up to 2× wrong at grazing silhouettes. Accumulate it explicitly instead:
  `visCount += float(SECTOR_COUNT - popCount(bitfield));` beside the existing `occlusion +=`.
  One scalar, exact, immune to the `continue`.
- **ADOPTED — the unoccluded length is 2/π, not Klehm's 0.5** (independently confirmed by
  simulating the accumulation loop, and by the closed form `1/sin(π/64) = 20.38` per slice).
  Klehm assumes solid-angle-uniform directions; these sector midpoints are angle-uniform on a
  half-circle. **Transcribing the constant literally maps an unoccluded point to 65° — a
  *narrower* cone than the 77° it replaces, i.e. a regression.**
- **ADOPTED — all three early-outs write unit-length vectors** (gtao_frag.glsl:98 and :117
  store the fixed view-facing +Z, :153 stores N — all length 1 after decode). :153 is real
  geometry nothing downstream guards; leaving it at length 1 beside neighbours at 0.637 puts
  an aperture seam across open ground at the sub-texel-radius threshold. All three want the
  same reference length, named once. `postfx.c`'s `{1,0.5,0.5,0.5}` clear decodes to a ZERO
  vector — a pre-existing `normalize(0)` latent on the `--no-ssao`-with-split path; give it
  the reference length too while there.
- **ADOPTED — fully occluded yields `bent = 0` and `count = 0`.** All three direction
  consumers (`spec_occ.glsl`, `tonemap_frag` twice) call bare `normalize()` with no epsilon,
  so the write site must never emit a zero-length vector.
- **REJECTED — storing `bent / |bentFull|`.** Proposed to make the length view- and
  TAA-independent. It does, but it measures the **visible fraction**, not the concentration
  the aperture needs: 32 sectors visible → 1.000, 4 → 0.195, 1 → 0.049, i.e. it runs the
  *opposite* way to Klehm's `|sum|/count` (0.637 → 0.994 → 1.000). It is a second AO wearing
  a bent normal's clothes. Verified numerically before discarding.

**What makes the simple form safe without that machinery:** every source of drift on the
stored mean — the 2-slice view dependence (0.637 face-on, ~0.45 grazing, at equal full
visibility), TAA's per-frame slice rotation, the blur averaging across neighbours — can only
SHORTEN it, and a shorter mean always maps to a WIDER aperture, i.e. less occlusion, no
ring. Klehm's `max(0, ·)` additionally pins everything at or below the reference length to
exactly a full hemisphere, which is where the unoccluded-case drift lands. One honest
residual: for genuinely concentrated pixels (corners), TAA's shortening widens the cone
somewhat, so corners occlude slightly less with `--taa` than without — a real mode
difference, in the safe direction, recorded as a property rather than hidden behind a
mode-dependent constant. This reasoning belongs in the shader comment.

## Phase 1 — the round hit criterion (`gtao_frag.glsl`)

`sectorBits` (:76-77) takes the paper's round criterion. The expected form is the
edges-rounded one — `startBit = round(lo·N)`, `count = round(hi·N) − round(lo·N)` — which the
paper presents as its own O(1) realisation of "half covered" (it is not exactly per-sector
coverage: a sub-half slab straddling a midpoint still sets one sector; that is the paper's
approximation, not ours to improve). `count` can now legitimately be 0 for a grazing slab, and
the existing `if (count == 0u) return 0u;` already handles it; keep the existing clamps.
**Check against the PDF itself** — the paper cites "pseudo-code at line 15 to 17 in
Algorithm 1", and our markdown conversion MANGLES the algorithm listing, so the check must be
against `2301.11376v2.pdf` (repo root) or the cdrinmatane reference post, not against
`docs/therrien-…md`.

**Prediction, written before implementing** (the discipline 11.75 lacked): the ceil form
carries a ~+0.5-sector bias per occluding slab; removing it makes the MEAN AO term rise by a
few per cent, concentrated where samples graze — thin geometry, grazing floors. Per pixel it
is NOT strictly monotone: a slab at sector coordinates [0.9, 1.6] currently sets sector 0
(10% covered) where round sets sector 1 (60% covered) — same count, different sector, so the
bent normal moves too. Expect a broad shift on ~25 of the 27 goldens (contact_debug reads
only csTex, whose chain never touches `sectorBits`; oit_sphere_moments has survived every AO
change this cycle). **If it moves nothing, or only a 1-2 px band, the diagnosis is wrong and
I stop.**

## Phase 2 — the aperture from the mean's length

`gtao_frag.glsl`: accumulate `visCount` explicitly; at :273/:280, write `bent / visCount` —
the mean, whose length is Klehm's concentration measure — instead of `normalize(bent)`. The
summed vectors are exactly unit (V and tangent are orthonormal by construction, verified),
so the mean is a genuine mean of unit directions. Both direction and
length then ride the existing three channels: **no new channel, no format change**, and the
mean of unit vectors is `≤ 1` by the triangle inequality so the affine encode cannot
overflow. The 16F target from 11.75 is what makes the length readable at all (at RGBA8 the
aperture would step ~0.8° per code; at 16F ~0.09°, against a 14° edge softness).

Early-outs and the fully-occluded fallback emit the reference length, not unit length, and
not zero.

`spec_occ.glsl`: `cosAv` from that length via Klehm's shape **recalibrated to this sampler**,
with the reference `2/π` written beside its derivation (it is a property of a half-circle
sector sweep and changes if `SECTOR_COUNT` or the slice geometry does), not a tuned number.

**Prediction:** the ring's dark band lifts toward 1.0 on open floor — 11.75's residual
0.974 → 0.956 dip should flatten. **Contact shadows must NOT lighten materially**: an
enclosed corner has a genuinely concentrated bent normal and still occludes. If contacts
lighten as much as the ring, the aperture is too wide and the change is wrong.

## The follow-up this spec names and does not do

**Specular occlusion evaluated against the bitmask directly**, which is what Therrien §3.2
prescribes: test the reflection lobe's sectors against the occluded mask per slice instead of
reconstructing a cone downstream. The computation is available — the mask is live in
`gtao_frag`, roughness (`aux.w`) and `R` are in scope. What is missing is **somewhere to put
the result**: `AoOut` is full, `GiOut` exists only under SSGI, a third MRT costs memory every
frame. That storage question is the whole of the work and deserves its own spec.

## Verification

Per phase: `./build.sh`; `--no-ssao` **0 px** against the same-build baseline; `--only ao,contact`
green; determinism ×2. **Each phase's predicted effect and its SCALE measured against the
prediction above before moving on** — a change that moves the wrong amount is a wrong
diagnosis, which is precisely what 11.75 shipped. Perf: interleaved A/B/A/B on a light scene
at 150 frames (warm-up window, per the prepass-crossover finding); the round criterion is
arithmetic-neutral and the mean costs one divide, so expect no measurable change and treat a
regression as a signal.

**The base is verified enough to start.** The full suite on the 11.75 rebake came back with
**2 failures of 56 groups, both `water-fft-*`**, and both for the same non-pixel reason: the
`--water-fft-probe` printed no header at all, so `water-fft-var` had nothing to read and
`water-fft-impulse` declined behind it. Every other water arm in the same run passed,
including ones that render the spectral surface (`water-seastate` at 341k px,
`water-whitecap-coverage` at two wind speeds, `water-foam-persist` 0 px across two 90-frame
runs). Nothing in 11.75 touches water, the FFT, or probe printing — the AO chain is
downstream of all of it — and a probe that emits no header is an instrument failure rather
than a shading one.

So it is **not treated as a blocker for 11.76, and not diagnosed here either**. It is filed
as its own question: run `--only water` in isolation on a quiet machine and see whether it
reproduces, because a probe declining to print smells like the process dying or a resource
limit under a full-suite run, which is the same class of measurement contamination that
produced the `prepass-crossover` chase. If it reproduces in isolation, it is a real defect in
the FFT probe and wants its own spec; if it does not, it is contention and wants recording
next to the other timing-arm notes. Either way it is independent of this plan.
