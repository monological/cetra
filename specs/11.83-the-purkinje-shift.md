# Spec 11.83 — B14, the Purkinje shift

## Context

Roadmap **B14**, the last open item in the B (night) series apart from B15, filed this session.
Its row has said the same thing for four specs: *the perceptual half of night — in dim light the
rods take over, colour drains, everything shifts blue and reds go near-black. This is what makes
a dark frame read as NIGHT rather than as an underexposed day photo, and it is the difference no
amount of radiance tuning in B12/B13 can supply.* Kirk & O'Brien 2011 is the named model.

11.79 stars, 11.80 the floor, 11.81 the cycle and 11.82 the moon all add **radiance**. B14 is the
only one that changes how that radiance is *seen*.

**One concern, stated once.** Both design passes independently recommended landing B15 (water at
night) first, on the roadmap's own argument: `apps/tree`'s night sea renders flat daytime
turquoise, measured at **0.57 HSL saturation at night against 0.29 by day**, and calibrating a
desaturating perceptual model against that frame would tune it to compensate for a radiometric
bug. The plan below proceeds with B14 as asked and handles it by **excluding tree as a
calibration target** — calibration runs on `moon_fixture.cscn` and `aerial_fixture`, and tree
defaults the feature off.

---

## What measurement established before designing

Four things were measured rather than assumed. Each changed the design.

**1. This engine's night is 4.2 stops below its day, where reality is ~17.** Metered absolute
scene radiance on `aerial_fixture`, meter live:

    daylight (sun +35)           0.1807      (log2 -2.47)
    sunset   (sun +2)            0.0484      (log2 -4.37)
    moonlit  (sun -12, moon up)  0.009848    (log2 -6.67)
    moonless (sun -12)           0.009805    (log2 -6.67)

Every literature threshold — photopic above 3 cd/m², scotopic below 0.005 — is therefore
meaningless here, and **the ramp endpoints are look constants**, exactly as
`SKY_NIGHT_FLOOR_RADIANCE` and the moon's two levels already are.

**2. The engine's photometric scale is not the real one.** `view.glsl:10-20` records that the
procedural sky's sun is 10 lux against a real ~100,000 — four decades low, booked as spec 10.2
phase 5 and unlanded. So a "3 cd/m²" threshold would read four decades wrong on precisely the
sky-lit frames this feature exists for.

**3. The moon moves the meter by 0.4%.** Moonlit and moonless nights are metrically almost
identical here — and *"moonlight looks blue"* is the single most-quoted demonstration of the
Purkinje shift. **This is a limitation to state, not to engineer around**; closing it is 10.2
phase 5's job, not this spec's.

**4. A daylight shadow overlaps a night frame.** With only 4.2 stops between them, a shaded
corner at noon sits inside the absolute-radiance range of a whole night frame. A per-pixel
threshold alone cannot separate "night" from "in shadow"; a global one alone wrongly drains a
lamp in a night scene. **The weight needs both, and that is the design's central constraint.**

## The design

### 1. Siting — `sceneToToned`, between the sanitize and the tone curve

`tonemap_frag.glsl:443-444` becomes:

```glsl
    vec3 c = min(hdr, vec3(WS_SCENE_MAX)) * aoFactor + bloomAdd;
    c = purkinjeApply(c, uv, aoFactor, bloomAdd);   // the whole splice
    return toneSelect(c);
```

**`sceneToToned` gains a `uv` parameter and its five call sites update mechanically** (`:548` plus
the four sharpen taps at `:552-558`) — the spectral half alone would need no signature change, but
acuity's neighbourhood taps must fetch the scene at offsets of the tap's own uv. Those fetches go
**through `sceneTap`**, so chromatic aberration applies to them consistently, and they **reuse the
centre's `aoFactor`/`bloomAdd`** — the sharpen block's own documented precedent ("so the mask
measures scene edges, not AO/bloom gradients"). Worst case under `--sharpen` with CA on that is
5 × ~5 × 3 ≈ 75 scene fetches; measure it in phase 6 rather than guessing, and note the acuity
branch is skipped entirely where `w` is 0, which is every daylight pixel.

**The file already carries the argument, on an axis the roadmap missed.** Its stated dividing
line (`:57-76`) is about *authored data space* and only rules out post-gamma. The governing
precedent is `:387-393`, chromatic aberration: *"a LENS effect: it acts on the light before the
sensor, which puts it ahead of the tonemap entirely… Applying it here rather than to the
composited result also keeps it in linear HDR."* That is an **optical-chain** ordering, and
Purkinje *is* the sensor's spectral response. The resulting chain:

> lens aberration (CA) → lens scatter (bloom, flare) → **retinal spectral response (Purkinje)** →
> the response curve (`toneSelect`) → grade → vignette → gamma → LUT → sensor noise (grain) →
> quantization (dither)

Grain is already called "sensor noise"; putting the same sensor's *spectral response* before its
transfer curve is the two halves of one sensor sitting correctly either side of it.

**Three wrong sites, and what each breaks** — all three produce a plausible frame:

- **Inside `sceneTap`** — applies to the scene but not to `bloomAdd`, so every night lamp gets a
  fully-coloured halo round a drained core. And it is *upstream of the `min(hdr, WS_SCENE_MAX)`
  sanitize*, so `mix(c, INF*tint, 0.0)` evaluates `INF * 0.0 = NaN`: one half-float overflow
  anywhere upstream turns the bit-exact identity into a black pixel.
- **Inside `toneSelect`** — debug view 5 calls it directly at `:475`, with no `aoFactor`, no
  `bloomAdd` and a half-res buffer, so the GI view would drain for reasons nothing in the frame
  explains.
- **On `color` at `:548`, after `sceneToToned` returns** — the natural accident. The sharpen
  block's `blur` taps would be un-shifted while the centre is shifted, so the unsharp mask
  high-passes the Purkinje term itself: a coloured halo at every luminance edge.

### 2. The weight — two gates, multiplied

```glsl
// PER-PIXEL: is there light HERE for the cones to answer? c is finite by
// construction (the sanitize one line up), which is what the identity rests on.
float logLpix = log2(max(dot(c, LUMA_W) * oneOverPreExposure, 1.0e-8));
float wLocal  = 1.0 - smoothstep(PK_LOCAL_LO + biasEV, PK_LOCAL_HI + biasEV, logLpix);
// WHOLE-FRAME: is the retina dark-ADAPTED? The metering 1x1, already the
// percentile-clipped mean log2 of absolute cd/m^2.
float logLada = texelFetch(purkinjeAdaptTex, ivec2(0), 0).r;
float wGlobal = (purkinjeHasMeter == 0 || isnan(logLada))
                    ? 1.0
                    : 1.0 - smoothstep(PK_GLOBAL_LO + biasEV, PK_GLOBAL_HI + biasEV, logLada);
float w = purkinjeStrength * wLocal * wGlobal;
if (w <= 0.0) return c;
```

**Both halves of that guard are load-bearing, and neither is defensive decoration.**
`purkinjeHasMeter` (a uniform, 0 when `fx->exposure` is NULL) exists because the shader cannot
know the C side never ran the draws — and an unwritten R32F target's content is undefined, so
without it the global gate reads garbage. The `isnan` exists because `lum_reduce_frag` writes a
**deliberate NaN on an empty histogram** (reachable: spot metering with a small radius zeroes the
population), and `smoothstep(NaN)` makes `w` NaN, `if (w <= 0.0)` is *false* on NaN, and the
`mix` paints a NaN frame — the identity's own early-out is what the NaN slips past. Both fall to
`wGlobal = 1.0` (local gate alone), not 0.0: a flag that silently does nothing is worse than a
degraded picture, and the local gate still behaves.

**A product, not a blend, because both failure cases want a veto** — a daylit shadow (local dim,
frame bright) and a night lamp (local bright, frame dim). A blended driver lands both mid-ramp
and drains both. A product gives an exact zero at either end, which is also **two independent
routes to a bit-exact daylight identity**, each assertable with the other pinned out of the way.

**The photopic weight drives, the scotopic weight is driven toward.** The weight asks how healthy
the *cone* response is, which is photopic by definition; the thing blended toward is the *rod*
response, which is scotopic. Structured this way, a red lamp at night keeps its colour where it
is locally bright and goes near-black where it is dim, with no third choice.

### 3. Where the global term comes from — and why not the CPU

**The roadmap's proposal does not work.** `adapted_luminance` is never written under
`--no-auto-exposure` (`postfx.c:3653-3672` skips metering and calls `exposure_reset_adaptation`
every frame), which is the state of all 27 goldens and ~60 gate arms. A term keyed to it would
be structurally invisible to this repo's own instruments and inert on every pinned scene,
including `moon_fixture.cscn`.

The fix is small and strictly better. `fx->lum_reduce_texture` is a 1×1 `R32F` **allocated
unconditionally at init** (`postfx.c:794-798`) holding the percentile-clipped mean log2 of
absolute cd/m²; only the three draws and the blocking readback are gated. So:

- **Split `postfx_run_metering`** (`:1803-1856`): the three draws run when
  `automatic || purkinje_enabled`; the `glGetTexImage` + `exposure_submit_measurement` +
  `exposure_probe_report` stay gated on `automatic` alone.
- **Bind the 1×1 on unit 7** — free, and it is where its own ancestor lived
  (`postfx.c:3714-3715`, *"was adapted luminance; applied upstream now"*). Tonemap goes 12 → 13
  of 16; Wall 1 unaffected.

Consequences: one source and one behaviour pinned or live; **no stall** (the readback is the
5.253 ms, the draws are not); this frame's value rather than one stale; and **no feedback loop**,
because the meter reads `scene_tex` *before* the tonemap pass and Purkinje lives inside it.
`fx->exposure == NULL` leaves `wGlobal = 1.0` (local gate alone) plus a warn-once — not 0.0, since
a flag that silently does nothing is worse than a wrong picture.

**Rejected: plumbing `sky_night_factor`.** Tempting — it is already "the one definition of night"
and an exact zero above +3°. But it is `static` in `sky.c`, PostFX holds no `Scene`, and it says
"noon" in a windowless room and in every scene with no sky. It is the right *cross-check*, not
the right driver.

### 4. The model

**Rod luminance** — derived, not tuned: V′(λ)/V(λ) at each sRGB primary's dominant wavelength
times that primary's photopic weight, renormalised to sum to exactly 1:

    PURKINJE_SCOTOPIC_W = vec3(0.00714, 0.36584, 0.62702)

Two properties are load-bearing. **They sum to 1**, so a neutral pixel's scotopic luminance equals
its photopic one and the stage does not change the brightness of grey — without which the frame's
apparent exposure jumps as the weight fades in, reading as an exposure bug. And **red is 0.7%
against its photopic 21%**: that single number *is* "reds go near-black", and it falls out of
V′(610) ≈ 0.016 rather than being authored. Sanity anchor: the unnormalised sum × 1700/683 is the
S/P ratio of the display white, published at ~2.4 for D65; this gives **2.35**.
`tools/gen_scotopic_weights.py` integrates the published CIE table properly and asserts the
anchor — the `gen_ies_table.py` idiom.

**The blue shift** — `PURKINJE_ROD_TINT = vec3(0.838, 1.010, 1.378)`, normalised so
`dot(tint, LUMA_W) == 1` exactly. **A fixed RGB tint is not an approximation of Kirk & O'Brien's
per-cone injection, it is algebraically identical**: rods are monochromats, so the LMS round trip
of a scalar folds into one constant vector. Only the constant's *name* is lost — say so in the
header, because "this should be in LMS" is the natural review comment and it is wrong.
The natural wrong turn is deriving the tint as `SCOTOPIC_W / LUMA_W`, giving an absurd cyan: that
is the colour of *the stimulus that best excites rods*, not of *the percept*.

**The blend** — `mix(c, dot(c, SCOTOPIC_W) * ROD_TINT, w)`, not K&O'B's additive rod-plus-cone
sum. A blend cannot add energy (an additive term brightens a night frame and fights every `WS_*`
ceiling and the tone shoulder), and `mix(c, x, 0.0)` is *exactly* `c`, which the additive form is
not.

**Acuity and rod noise ship too** (your call, and they are the halves that make a frame read as
*night* rather than as *graded* — desaturate-and-tint alone is honestly a blue filter with a
luminance mask). Both are their own phase and their own toggles, because both carry a real
conflict with an existing stage:

- **Acuity** is a small-radius blend toward a neighbourhood tap, weighted by `w`. It needs the uv,
  which is why `purkinjeApply` takes one. It sits directly upstream of the sharpen block, so
  `--sharpen --purkinje` at night is unsharp-masking a blur the same pass applied — a documented
  interaction with its own gate arm, not a bug.
- **Rod noise** is retinal and scales with `w`; the existing grain is *sensor* noise sited after
  the display encode. Two noise sources need that distinction written down. The noise **rides the
  `grainSeed` uniform the pass already uploads** ("per-frame, deterministic across equal
  `--frames` runs") rather than adding a second frame term — dither deliberately carries none,
  grain already carries this one, and a third seed would be a third thing to keep deterministic.

### 5. Identity, in three layers

1. **Off** — `if (purkinjeEnabled == 0) return c;`, no arithmetic. All 27 goldens ride this.
2. **On, daylight** — `smoothstep` at `x >= edge1` is exactly `1.0`, so `wGlobal` is exactly 0,
   `w` is exactly 0, and the early return fires. **A daylight frame with `--purkinje` on is 0 px
   against off, exactly, not nearly.**
3. **On, strength 0** — same guard.

Two traps for the comment, both producing plausible frames: never write the ramp with edges
swapped (`edge0 > edge1` is undefined in GLSL), and **never fold strength into the tint**
(`mix(vec3(1), TINT, strength)` desaturates fully at `w = 1` regardless of strength, so
`--purkinje-strength 0` would still drain colour while *looking* like a strength control).

### 6. Constants — one calibration knob

`purkinjeBiasEV` (stops, added to **both** ramp pairs, default 0) is the sole calibration surface.
The quantity that is uncertain is *the engine's photometric scale*, and that is **one unknown
scalar**: when 10.2 phase 5 lands, every threshold moves by the same amount and one bias absorbs
it. It also lets a scene lit by imported photometric lamps — which *are* on the real scale — dial
in while a sky-lit scene sits at 0, and it doubles as the diagnostic that drags the effect into a
daylight frame, the `--cycle-rebake-at` idiom.

Provisional, against the measurements above, all four GLSL `const`:

    PK_GLOBAL_LO -6.0  PK_GLOBAL_HI -4.0   day -2.47 -> exactly 0 (1.53 stops margin)
                                           sunset -4.37 -> 0.09; night -6.67 -> 1.0
    PK_LOCAL_LO  -8.0  PK_LOCAL_HI  -3.0   five stops; HI decides what stays coloured at night
    purkinjeStrength 0.7  (w = 1 is full rod vision; the default sits below it)

**The night-end margin is only 0.67 stops**, which must be said out loud: a night frame brighter
than this fixture's lands mid-ramp.

## The fixture

`lut_fixture` is the right *idiom* (emissive over black: no N·L, no shadow, no IBL, so a patch's
value is a property of `emissiveFactor` alone) and the wrong *shape* — its 18 colours sit at one
set of magnitudes, so it can show the shift's luminance dependence on greys and not the drain's on
colour. Nothing else in the corpus works: every night scene is achromatic (`moon_fixture`),
atmosphere-confounded (`aerial_fixture`), or known-wrong (tree).

**Columns are a luminance ladder in exact powers of two spanning ~7 stops and straddling the knee;
rows are chromaticity held constant.**

| row | what only it can show |
|---|---|
| 0 neutral, exactly `r==g==b` | the blue shift — desaturation is an *exact identity* on grey |
| 1 red | the largest drain, the darkest result |
| 2 green | mid |
| 3 blue | the row a **tint-only** build pushes the *wrong way* |
| 4 mid-saturation warm | 11.58's lesson: a chart of saturated corners leaves the interior untested |
| 5 the rod tint's own chromaticity | near-fixed-point control; must move **least** at every rung |

**The fixture's trick**: the `.cscn` authors `post.exposure` at `E0 ≫ 1`, so the patches' absolute
radiances are scotopic while their displayed codes are mid-tone and readable in 8 bits. Exposure
and the rod ramp are independent by the feature's own contract, so the fixture uses one to make
the other measurable — and if `oneOverPreExposure` is ever dropped, the ladder shifts by
`log2(E0)` and `purkinje-absolute` reports it.

**Generator asserts** (11.58's most expensive lesson was that `gen_lut_fixture.py` asserted three
anti-degeneracy properties on one table and none on another, so making its curve the identity
would have left every arm green): the ladder is monotone, geometric, spans ≥ N stops and
**straddles** the shipped knee; row 0 is exactly achromatic at every rung; every chromatic row is
above a saturation floor at *every* rung; **no two chromatic rows are channel permutations of each
other**, or a build that swapped the tint's R and B answers identically on all of them; read boxes
clear of every patch edge; patch pitch exceeds the default bloom radius.

Plus two the meter forces, found in review. **Patch coverage stays well under the meter's kept
tail** (≤ ~15% of frame against `meter_low`'s discard of the darkest 70%): the meter keeps the
brightest 30% of the *population*, so if the patches filled that tail the fixture's own bright
rungs would push the global mean into the window and every colour arm would measure a diluted
weight — kept black-dominated, the mean sits near the measure shader's −26.57 floor and
`wGlobal` saturates at exactly 1 on this fixture, which is what lets the arms read the local
ramp clean. And **the brightest patch times 4·E0 stays under `WS_SCENE_MAX`**, or the sanitize
clamp binds differently on `purkinje-absolute`'s second leg and its exact-0-px claim dies for a
reason that has nothing to do with the feature (`view.glsl`'s own note: the metered value
contains `min(radiance, C / preExposure)`).

## The gate group — `purkinje`

    purkinje-agree      the LOCAL ramp (debug G) against a Python twin of its closed
                        form, <= 1 code per patch, with wGlobal read off debug B as a
                        measured input rather than re-derived through the meter
    purkinje-weight     within-frame swing, per-row monotonicity, and red >= grey >= blue
    purkinje-absolute   the weight view at E0 and at 4*E0: exactly 0 px
    purkinje-drain      saturation falls on EVERY chromatic row and rises on NONE
    purkinje-blue       neutral row moves at the dim rung, exactly 0 at the bright one
    purkinje-ladder     drain vs rung is monotone with a strictly interior crossover
    purkinje-lamp       moon_fixture: the disc holds its chroma while the star band drains
    purkinje-scene      aerial_fixture -12 moves both bands; +35 is 0 px
    purkinje-sharpen    --sharpen --purkinje: no chroma halo across a hard luminance edge
    purkinje-acuity     the blur is present at the dim rungs and absent at the bright ones
    purkinje-noise      variance rises with darkness, and is 0 with --no-purkinje-noise
    purkinje-strength   0.5 reads exactly half the weight; 0 is 0 px
    purkinje-off        authored == flags 0 px; --no-purkinje over the file 0 px; CLI wins
    purkinje-config     the rows exist and read their shipping defaults; enabled FALSE
    purkinje-det        two runs of the heaviest configuration, 0 px

**Separating "drains" from "shifts blue" is geometric, not a matter of thresholds.** A grey patch
is an *exact* fixed point of desaturation and *not* of the tint, so a desaturate-only build moves
the neutral row by exactly 0 codes and `purkinje-blue` fails at the floor rather than near it.
Conversely a tint-only build (`rgb * mix(vec3(1), tint, w)` — a real one-line wrong turn) *lowers*
the red row's saturation and *raises* blue's and cyan's, which is why `purkinje-drain` asserts a
**sign on every row** rather than a mean.

**Four independent constructions refuse a global implementation**, since a whole-frame saturation
arm is satisfied perfectly by one: `purkinje-ladder` (a global weight drains every rung
identically — flat, no interior crossover), `purkinje-weight` (swing measured within one frame),
`purkinje-lamp` (a real frame with sky, bloom and tonemap in the path — the disc mask **predicted**
from the fixture's own moon angles through `_moon_disc_geometry`, never found by brightness, which
is what left `beach-shoreline` red for four specs), and `purkinje-scene`.

**One contingency on `purkinje-lamp`, measured before its bar is placed.** At gate framing the
size-49 disc covers roughly a third of the frame, so it plausibly *is* the meter's kept-30%
population and the fixture's global mean may land inside or above the window — diluting or
zeroing the star-band drain the arm reads. First read debug B on that frame; if the global gate
is the limiter, the arm renders a smaller `--moon-size` variant (a small disc leaves the kept
population dark) and records the choice in its own message.

**Anti-vacuity pairs, in the docstring**: `drain` ↔ `blue`; `weight` ↔ `absolute` (a constant
weight is 0 px across exposures and passes `absolute` — it **cannot stand alone**, the way
`lut-identity` cannot); `ladder` ↔ `lamp` (synthetic vs real).

Every arm runs `--no-dither --no-vignette --no-bloom`. The last is correctness, not tidiness: a
bright rung's halo raises its dim neighbour's absolute radiance and shifts that neighbour's weight,
so with bloom on `purkinje-ladder` reads the bloom radius.

**Bars are placed by killing the twin first**: build the named mutation, record; build correct,
record; bar between them nearer the mutation; **both numbers printed in the arm's own message** and
written into the spec.

## Phases

0. **Measure whether the threshold exists — before any shader is written.** The per-pixel absolute
   radiance *histogram* of `aerial_fixture` at +35 and at −12, both pinned. **If no threshold
   separates them, stop and say so**: the fix is 10.2 phase 5, not a fudged ramp. Also diagnose

## Not covered, said out loud

**Whether it looks like night** — no arm can say a frame reads as night rather than as an
underexposed day, which is the feature's entire stated purpose. The `purkinje_night` golden exists
to be looked at.

**The threshold's physical correctness**, which is the largest untested claim here. Every constant
is calibrated against this corpus, not against the eye, and a sky-lit scene and a scene lit by an
imported photometric lamp will land in different places on the ramp — not a Purkinje defect but
10.2 phase 5 showing through a new surface.

**Kirk & O'Brien's actual model** — the arms check *structure* (monotone, blue, drains, per-pixel,
exposure-invariant) and a different-but-plausible rod response passes all of them. **Do not put the
citation in the header as though the model were implemented**: cite it for the rod luminance and
the mesopic blend, and name what is a look filter with a physical skeleton.

**V′(λ) cannot be recovered from three primaries** — whatever coefficients ship are a fit and no
fixture distinguishes a good one from a bad one.

**Behaviour under a live meter** — no arm runs one, by design; the substitute is a hand check.

**Temporal adaptation** — rods take ~20 minutes to dark-adapt; nothing here is temporal, and
`purkinje-det` would not catch a frame-index term since two equal-length runs carry the same one.

**Composition with ACES and AgX** (both already desaturate, for reasons that are not Purkinje) and
with `--film`, whose lift/gamma/gain will re-saturate at night what Purkinje drained.

**The two apps where a user would actually see this** — `forest` at 34,991 px run-to-run and `tree`
at 8,925 px cannot be pixel-compared at all.
