# cetra 14.32: a table in a scrolling pane

## Context

pcb 7.4.2's board modal shows a board's stackup: a picture of the stack on the left, each layer
drawn level with its row, and a `ui_table` of the layers' words on the right. The user wants the
picture beside the table, not a column of it, and the two to scroll together when a board has more
layers than the box holds, through a clean API.

Today pcb fakes it, and the reviews of its phases 12 and 13 called it a workaround:
- the table scrolls itself, and the picture, a panel beside it, reads where each row now stands
  from `ui_table_cell_rect` every frame, so the wheel over the picture scrolls nothing;
- the picture works out the header's height from row 0's rect, only while the rows are at the top,
  and clips itself under it;
- the rows are centred in the box by writing the table's top padding every frame.

The right shape is a scrolling pane holding the picture and the table side by side, the table as
tall as its rows, the pane scrolling both. cetra lacks what makes that work:
- **The header** stays in view only while the table scrolls itself. Inside a pane it would scroll
  away with the table's top.
- **The cursor** shows its row by scrolling the table. A table as tall as its rows has nothing to
  scroll, so a cursor moved past the pane's foot would go out of sight.
- **The rows drawn** are those in the table's own view, which for a table as tall as its rows is
  every row.
- **The wheel** goes to the deepest thing under the pointer that can scroll. A table is one, and
  with nothing to scroll it takes the wheel and does nothing, so the pane round it never moves. An
  editor whose text all shows does the same.
- **Nothing outside the table can ask where its header stands**, which the picture needs to put its
  own title level with it and keep its layers below it.

Two smaller things pcb's review found, folded in at the user's asking:
- **A status strip along a rounded modal's foot** has square top corners and round bottom ones.
  `UIStyle` has one `corner_radius`, so pcb draws the strip in a hook that clips a taller rounded
  box. The UI should round some corners.
- **pcb sizes its rows to the room the box has**, through the source's `height`, so a resize changes
  every row's height. `ui_table_rows_changed` is all there is, and it measures every cell's text
  again, though no width changed.

Already in cetra, and named so pcb uses it rather than faking it: **a scrolling pane places content
shorter than its view by `align_main`** (`_arrange`, the room left before the scroll is settled), so
the pane centres the picture and the table when they fit.

Branch and spec: `14.32-a-table-in-a-scrolling-pane`, off cetra's master (`cfced161`).

## The design

### A table in a scrolling pane

A table measures down as its header and every row unless it grows (docs/ui.md, The table). One
measured that way inside a `ui_scroll` is scrolled by the pane. What changes is the table's, read
from what the panes round it show:

- **What its panes show.** At `place`, after the layout, the table keeps the part of the window the
  scrolling panes round it show: the meet of each one's content rect. With no pane round it, that is
  the window. A new internal call in ui.c, `ui_shown_rect(ui, el)`, gives it.
- **The header stays in view.** It stands at the top of what the panes show while the table's top is
  above it, and goes up with the table's last row once that rises past it: at `content.y`, else
  `min(shown.y, content.y + content.h - header_h)`. It draws over the rows, which pass under it. A
  table that scrolls itself is unchanged: its rows view is already below its header.
- **The rows drawn** are those in the rows view that the panes show and the header does not cover,
  found by the same binary search of the tops.
- **The cursor's row is shown in the panes round it** when the table's own rows cannot scroll to it:
  the least scroll of each pane that shows the row whole under the header, as a focus is revealed
  (`_reveal` in ui.c, the least scroll from where each pane is going). The table asks it at its
  next `place`, where it has the UI. A table whose rows scroll keeps showing the row itself.
- **Page Up and Page Down** move the cursor by the rows the panes show, not by the table's height.
- **`ui_table_cell_rect`** is false for a row the panes do not show or that the header covers, as
  for one out of the table's own view now.
- **`ui_table_header_rect(table, &out)`**, new: where the header stands, kept down by what the panes
  show; false with no header or before the first layout. For an element beside the table that
  draws a row's worth of something level with each row: its title level with the header, its rows
  kept under it.

### The wheel passes on

The wheel scrolls the deepest element under the pointer that has somewhere to scroll: one that
scrolls but whose content all shows passes it to the next one round it. A table or an editor in a
pane, all of it shown, then scrolls the pane. A press on a bar is unchanged: a bar is drawn only
where there is room to scroll.

### Some corners rounded

`UIStyle` gains:

```c
unsigned corners; // the corners corner_radius rounds, UI_CORNER_* or'd; 0 = all four
```

with `UI_CORNER_TOP_LEFT`, `UI_CORNER_TOP_RIGHT`, `UI_CORNER_BOTTOM_RIGHT`,
`UI_CORNER_BOTTOM_LEFT`, `UI_CORNERS_TOP` and `UI_CORNERS_BOTTOM`.
- **0 is all four**, as every zero in a style inherits. A theme's or a style's 0 rounds every
  corner, so `ui-theme-identity` and every picture are unchanged.
- **The border rounds with the fill**, as it does now: the ring is the same box inset.
- **It rides the vertex**, in the fill's free `params[3]` (a glyph's edge, unused by a fill), as the
  corners rounded; the fragment stage gives each quadrant of the box its radius or none.
  `ui_draw_rounded`, with no style, rounds all four as it does.

### Heights read again alone

```c
// Every row's height asked of the source's `height` again at the next layout, its cells' widths
// kept: for rows sized to the room the app has, as that room changes. Nothing to do for a source
// with no `height`, whose rows are as tall as their lines.
void ui_table_heights_changed(UIElement* table);
```

The tops are summed again from the first row whose height changed. No cell's text is measured.

## Tests

- **`ui-table-pane`**, a new `ui` arm, from a new probe case `table-pane` in
  `apps/gametest/src/ui_text_probe.c`: a pane 200 points tall holding a row of a fixed panel and a
  table of 40 rows as tall as its rows.
  - Scrolled 300 points: `ui_table_header_rect`'s top is the pane's view's top; row 0's
    `ui_table_cell_rect` is false; a row under the header's foot is true and starts at or below it.
  - The rows drawn, counted by the source's calls in a draw: at most the rows the pane shows and one
    more, times the text columns.
  - The wheel over the table scrolls the pane; over the fixed panel, too.
  - The cursor moved by keys from the first row to row 30 eases the pane until row 30 is whole
    under the header.
  - Page Down moves the cursor by the rows the pane shows.
  - A pane with `align_main` CENTER holding a table of 3 rows centres it.
- **`ui-table-heights`**, the arm 14.30 added, gains a fourth flag: the source's heights doubled,
  `ui_table_heights_changed` measures no cell (`measures` unchanged) and the rows' rects are twice as
  tall.
- **`canvas-ui-table-pane`**, a new `canvas` arm, over `apps/canvas --ui-table-pane`: the table of
  `--ui-table` in a pane beside a drawn panel, scrolled so the header is held, and a strip along a
  rounded box's foot with its bottom corners rounded:
  - the header's rule is its colour along the pane's top, the rows under it;
  - the strip's top corners are its fill and its bottom corners show the box's.
- `ui-theme-identity`, `canvas-ui-table` and the rest of `ui` and `canvas` pass unchanged.

**Falsified**: the header not held, `ui-table-pane` fails on the header alone; the reveal left to the
table's own scroll, it fails on the reveal alone; the wheel taken by the deepest scroller whatever
it shows, it fails on the wheel; `corners` ignored in the fragment stage, `canvas-ui-table-pane`
fails on the strip's corners alone.

## Not in this spec

A press on an element beside a table moving its cursor: an app-drawn panel takes no press, and
pcb's picture keeps the cursor to the table's keys and presses. A header held by a pane for any
element but a table.
