# Spec 13.4 — What Clearwater does better

## Context

We read `../research/water/clearwater` end to end and compared it with our water after 13.3. The comparison covered 15 things.

- **Ours is better on six:** sun glitter, band coverage, where the caustics land, the seabed, refraction, and the loop.
- **One is the same:** the capillary correction to the wave speeds.
- **Theirs is better on nine.** The user asked for every one of those nine to be done Clearwater's way, one phase each.

This spec does exactly those nine and nothing else. Two decisions are already made:
- **The glare star is on by default everywhere.**
- **Touch ripples come from a character wading through water**, in tree and gametest, and from a headless `--water-drop` flag.

The biggest of the nine is physically wrong rather than merely missing: `pbr_frag` lights a submerged bed as if it were in air. `water_frag` then only absorbs along the view path (`bed * T`). So a bed 5 m down gets the same sunlight as one 20 cm down.

## Phases

Each phase ends with:
1. a commit;
2. the spec's as-built section updated and committed;
3. `--only water` (plus `beach` where the phase reaches it);
4. **before/after crops shown to the user** whenever the phase changes the picture.

Goldens are re-baked only once, in the last phase, after the user has approved the look.

### 1. Exact Fresnel
- New shared include, `include/water_light.glsl`, with `fresnelDielectric(cosi, n)`: Clearwater's unpolarised rs/rp formula, returning 1 on total internal reflection.
- `water_frag` changes:
  - The interface weight becomes `Fexact(NdotV) * (ab.x + ab.y)`, where `ab` is the existing BRDF LUT read. Their weight uses the exact Fresnel directly.
  - The glitter's facet Fresnel (`Fs`) becomes exact as well.
- **Shown to the user:** a grazing-angle crop of the horizon band.

### 2. Sunlight reaching the bed
Their formula:
```
Esun = SUN · (1 − F(sun)) · exp(−σt · depth / cosθt)
Esky = sky · exp(−(σa + 0.4σs) · depth · 1.25)
```
- **The helper lives in `water_light.glsl`:** `waterDownwellKey(depth)` and `waterDownwellSky(depth)`.
  - The key uses the key's refracted angle through a flat surface.
  - σ is `waterAbsorption` (per world unit). The sky's share uses the `(σa + 0.4σs)·1.25` form, with `waterScatterAlbedo` splitting σ into absorption and scattering.
- **`pbr_frag`** (the whole family: pbr, pbr_skinned, terrain): a fragment is treated as submerged when it is below `waterLevel` and inside `waterExtent`. For a submerged fragment:
  - every directional light is scaled by `waterDownwellKey`;
  - the ambient terms (IBL diffuse and specular, and GI) are scaled by `waterDownwellSky`.
  - Local lights are left alone, as they are in Clearwater, which has only a sun.
- **Uniforms:** `waterIor`, `waterAbsorption` and `waterScatterAlbedo` are published through the existing `_water_publish_shore` / `water_bind_shore` (`water.c:1784-1814`), the path every lit surface already reads. With no water, `waterSurfHeight` stays 0, which is its off state; add a `waterPresent` int that defaults to 0.
- **Caustics must follow:** `waterCaustics`' `keyIrr` and `causticSkyIrradiance` in `water_frag` switch to the same helpers at the bed depth, so the key's share is computed against the light that actually arrives.
- **Gate arm `water-downwell`:** render `water_fixture` with two bed depths, and check that the bed's key-lit radiance ratio matches `exp(−σ·Δd/cosθt)` within tolerance.
- **Shown to the user:** dome bed crops at shallow and deep points.

### 3. Glow inside the water follows the sun
- Replace `inscatter = waterScatterAlbedo * incident` in `water_frag` (around line 1227) with their shape:
  - the sun term uses `(1 − F)`, is attenuated to half the depth the view ray reaches (`0.5 · path · |refrDir.y|`), and is weighted by a Henyey–Greenstein function with g = 0.8 of `dot(sunInWater, −refrDir)`;
  - the sky term is attenuated to 0.6 of that depth, as theirs is.
- The phase function is scaled by 4π so it averages to 1 over the sphere. That keeps the meaning of an authored `scatter_albedo`: the sea is as bright on average, just brighter toward the sun.
- New `Water.scatter_g`, default 0.8. It goes in the `.cscn` and config snapshot rows with the other water scalars.
- **Shown to the user:** looking toward the sun and away from it.

### 4. Caustics split colour
- Change the caustic target from an R16F array to an RGBA16F array.
- `_water_run_caustic_level` runs the land trace and the lattice draw once per channel. Each pass uses `glColorMask` and its own `waterIor` offset: their `1.3315 / 1.3335 / 1.3365` becomes `ior − 0.002`, `ior`, `ior + 0.003`, so an authored IOR still means something.
- The green channel keeps the current registration.
- `waterCausticSample` returns a `vec3`, and `waterCaustics` is already a `vec3`.
- **Probes and gates:**
  - `water_caustic_probe` prints a mean, minimum and maximum per channel.
  - `water-caustic-energy` checks each channel averages 1.
- Cost and memory are measured and reported: roughly three times the caustic cost, and +12 MB.
- **Shown to the user:** close-up crops of the caustic lines.

### 5. Smoother reads of the wave texture
- Add `oceanTexBSpline(sampler2DArray, uv, layer)` to `ocean.glsl`. It is their `texBS`: a cubic B-spline built from four bilinear taps.
- Use it in `oceanShadingNormal` for each band's slope read, but only where that band is magnified (its LOD ≤ 0). Minified reads keep the existing trilinear path, since the spline only helps below one texel per pixel.
- Applies to the ripple band's reads and to the cascade reads.
- **Shown to the user:** close calm-water highlights, before and after.

### 6. Caustic relief and dark gaps
- Their pseudo-height is the bed colour's coarse-mip luminance. Ours is general: `hgt = lum(bed at lod+2) / lum(bed at lod+5)`, both from `sceneColorTex`, which is already mipped. That is the bed against its own neighbourhood, so no texture-specific constants are needed.
- **Relief:** the caustic lookup point `at` slides along the key by `(hgt − 1) · 0.05 m`, as theirs does.
- **Dark gaps:** `ao = mix(0.55, 1, smoothstep(...))` scales the key's share, their `mix(0.75, 1, ao)` on Esun.
- A `Water.caustic_relief` switch, on by default.
- **Shown to the user:** a pebble-like bed. If no fixture has a textured bed, use `beach_fixture`'s sand.

### 7. Specks in the water
- In `water_frag`, three layers of sunlit particles along the refracted ray, at 0.22, 0.60 and 0.98 m, taken in world metres through `waterUnitsPerMetre`.
- Built as in theirs:
  - a cell grid of 48 per metre, a hash, and a density of 1.2%;
  - drifting with time;
  - kept only where the layer is shallower than `path`;
  - faded by `exp(−σ·tt·2)` and by an fwidth-based fade;
  - lit by the key through `waterDownwellKey` rather than their constant sun.
- The hash is `hash21` from `include/noise.glsl`. The fwidth calls stay in uniform control flow.
- A `Water.specks` switch, on by default.
- **Shown to the user:** close-up crops of clear shallow water.

### 8. Touch ripples from wading
- **Engine side, in `water.c`/`water.h`:**
  - A local wave-equation simulation, 256² over 7 m, following the camera and snapped to whole texels. It uses their update, `v += (avg − h)·0.9`, with damping 0.9955 / 0.9985, and fades at the edges.
  - Two RGBA16F ping-pong targets, plus a normals pass that writes `(h, dh/dx, dh/dz, laplacian)`.
  - Stepped at a fixed 60 Hz from water time, using an accumulator so a headless run is deterministic.
  - Skipped once the water has been calm for 900 steps.
  - A new unit `WATER_TOUCH_UNIT` is bound in `_water_bind_ocean`, which takes `water_frag` to 14 of 16. Update the ledger.
- **Public API:** `water_ripple_drop(Water*, vec2 xz, float radius_m, float strength)`, which queues a drop for the next step.
- **Shading:**
  - `oceanShadingNormal` adds the ripple's slope on both wave models.
  - That makes the caustic land pass trace through the ripples for real, which is better than their Laplacian approximation, so the Laplacian term is not ported.
  - `water_vert` adds `h` to the displacement.
- **Triggers:**
  - The tree app's `--player` and gametest's player and chaser drop ripples at their feet while they are in water and moving. Gametest already has the in-water test for its swimming.
  - `apps/render` gets `--water-drop x,z,frame` (repeatable).
- **Gate arm `water-ripple-drop`:** a drop spreads outward, its peak falls over time, and two runs are byte-identical.
- **Shown to the user:** a frame sequence of a drop, and the tree player wading.

### 9. Diffraction glare star (PostFX, on by default)
- **At init, on the CPU:** build the aperture pattern exactly as `buildPSF` does. That is a 512² aperture with six flats, five scratches and seven dust specks, supersampled 3×3. Then:
  1. run a CPU FFT and take the power spectrum;
  2. sum 8 wavelength bands with their RGB weights;
  3. apply their far-field lift;
  4. normalise each channel.
- **Per frame, a pass after the bloom bright-pass:**
  1. Downsample the HDR image into a 512×256 grid (swapped for a portrait frame), keeping 75% of the grid for the frame.
  2. Forward FFT. Two RGBA32F pairs carry R+G and B as complex values.
  3. Multiply by the pattern's spectrum, which is resampled to the grid on resize.
  4. Inverse FFT.
  5. Tonemap adds the result, like bloom, with their threshold of 14 and weight of 0.9 before exposure.
- New `glare_fft_frag.glsl` is a general radix-2 pass, their `fftGlare`. The water FFT is tied to its twiddle and cascade layout, so it isn't reused.
- **Controls:** `postfx->glare_enabled = true` by default, a `--no-glare` flag, a GUI checkbox, and a config snapshot row. `engine_set_2d_preset` turns it off. It is documented in `CLAUDE.md`'s defaults line and in `cli-reference`.
- **Gate arm `glare-energy`:** the glare image's total equals the thresholded input's total within tolerance, because the pattern is normalised. A second leg checks `--no-glare` moves 0 px.
- **Shown to the user:** sun glints on water, and a bright scene with no water.

## As built

### Phase 0: before, and the floor

Six framings, rendered by a scratch script:

- `water_fixture` Gerstner and FFT at the default eye;
- the 13.3 close framing;
- a framing toward the sun. Water keys off the brightest directional, the sky sun at +x, -z, not the authored `WaterKey`;
- `beach_fixture`;
- the tree camera at 1400x900, `--time-of-day 12`.

Rendered twice, every framing differs by 0 px.

### Phase 1: exact Fresnel

- `fresnelDielectric(cosi, n)` sits in `include/fresnel.glsl`, beside the Schlick forms, not in a new include: that file is already the one home for interface reflectance.
- The interface weight is the exact reflectance times the split-sum LUT sum `ab.x + ab.y`, since the LUT is a Schlick integral and cannot take another curve. The glitter facet term is exact too.
- From below, the same index is used on both sides. The exact form would close Snell's window, and outside it the environment lookup would paint the sky where the underwater scene belongs.
- **Measured.** The far band of `wf_fft` brightens from 68.7 to 69.7 of 255. Near the camera nothing changes, because the two agree at normal incidence. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 2: sunlight reaching the bed

- **`include/water_light.glsl`** holds `waterDepthBelow`, `waterDownwellKey` (the surface transmission `1 − F` times Beer-Lambert along the refracted path, `depth / cosθt`) and `waterDownwellSky`. It owns the `waterIor`, `waterAbsorption` and `waterPresent` declarations, which `water_frag` used to make itself.
- **The sky's coefficient is `σt × 1.0`, not their `(σa + 0.4σs) × 1.25`.** We carry extinction only, with no split into absorption and scattering; over Clearwater's three channels their form comes to 0.92–1.2 × σt, and 1 is the mean.
- **`pbr_frag`** scales every directional light's colour by the key term, and the whole ambient sum (inline and split specular alike) by the sky term, for a fragment below `waterLevel` while a sea is drawn. Local lights are untouched, as in Clearwater.
- **Published by `_water_publish_shore`**, the one place both lit surfaces and the water read. `waterPresent` is `water_active && downwell`, so a sea switched off leaves nothing darkened.
- **The caustics' key share** now weighs the key and the sky as they arrive at the bed, through the same two functions.
- **`Water.downwell`** is on by default, with `--no-water-downwell`, a GUI checkbox and a config snapshot row. It is what the arm compares against.
- **`water_fixture`'s bed is EMISSIVE by design**, so the golden and every existing arm there are blind to lighting. New `water_downwell_fixture.cscn` overrides both materials to a lit grey under still water, with one directional light and nothing else.
- **Arm `water-downwell`:**
  - Four renders: the switch on and off, under a key at 58° and at 33°.
  - Dry wedge: 0 delta.
  - Each submerged row's per-channel ratio solved for depth: the channels agree within 0.058 (limit 0.08), and the two angles within 0.020 (limit 0.05).
  - **Falsified twice:** a vertical path reads 0.169 across the angles, and no Fresnel term reads 0.546 across the channels.
  - The key is at intensity 4 because at 1 the red channel sat near the 8-bit floor, and rounding alone pulled its depth 7% off the others.
- **The look.** The tree's shallow bed loses 10% of its red (48.1 → 43.5) and its caustic highlights go from white to cyan. The fixture wedge darkens with depth while its dry top is unchanged. `--only water` and `--only beach` pass.

### Phase 3: in-scatter follows the sun

- **The column integral, not their mid-depth sample.** A point `s` along the refracted sight line is `s|ry|` deep. The sun reaches it through `exp(−σ s |ry| / cosθt)` and the eye sees it through `exp(−σ s)`, so the column returns `(1 − exp(−σL(1+k))) / (1+k)` with `k = |ry| / cosθt`; the sky is the same with its own coefficient. At k = 0 this is exactly the old `1 − T`. Clearwater's mid-depth sample is an estimate of this integral, and one that sends the glow of bottomless water to nothing.
- **Henyey-Greenstein at `g = Water.scatter_g`** (default 0.8, their value), plus their isotropic floor of 0.02, on the sun's share, which also takes `1 − F` for entering the water.
- **Normalised against the nadir, not the sphere.** Both the refracted sight line and the refracted beam point down, so an eye above the water only ever sees light turned through more than about 90°: the back of a forward lobe. Normalised over the sphere, every sea seen from above dimmed to a third, which Clearwater answers with a constant of 3.2. Against the view straight down, an authored albedo keeps its meaning directly under the eye.
- **From below**, the old uniform source stands: the sight line runs between the eye and the surface, not down the column.
- **`scatterG`** is authored in `.cscn` (and by the fixture generator, at the default, so the corpus covers the key), in the config snapshot, and on a GUI slider. It is clamped to ±0.95 at upload.
- **Measured** on `water_fixture` FFT, deep water:
  - toward the sun: G 87 → 79 of 255;
  - away from it: G 80 → 69.
  - The lobe adds about 4% more toward the sun. That is what an eye above the water can see of it: the refracted sight line is at least 41° below the horizon, so it never looks into the forward peak.
  - The rest is the integral: sunlight fades with depth, so deep water glows less than a uniform source said.
  - Shallow water barely moves.
- **No arm.** A pixel count of the switch would pass on any change at all, and what the physics predicts is a ratio under the tone curve that depends on the sky's share. The look is the check. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 4: caustics split colour

Took four attempts, and the as-built is not what the plan said. In order:

1. **Three traces, one index per colour channel (Clearwater's way):** saturated red and green speckle. Zeroing the spread gave identical grey, so the colour was optics, not a sync fault. The cause is that three single wavelengths are three samples of a continuous spectrum, each as sharp as one wavelength, on a point-source trace.
2. **The key's disc** (half a degree, times the path) became a floor on the lookup's filter. It softened the result but did not fix it.
3. **One trace at 550 nm, spread across the spectrum in the lookup:** 8 taps along the first-order flat-water shift, `−depth · sinθt / (n · cos³θt)` per unit index, weighted by the CIE 1931 observer (Wyman–Sloan–Shirley fit, to linear sRGB, clamped). The index is Cauchy through water's 1.3371 at 486 nm and 1.3311 at 656 nm, which puts 405 nm at 1.3430 against a measured 1.3428. One `_water_caustic_spectrum` serves both the lookup and the reference.
   - A first version put each tap at its own point of the disc. That gave every band its own part of the disc, and red came out off-centre, a warm fringe that is not dispersion. Fixed by filtering the disc instead.
4. **Per-point dispersion:** the trace also wrote each corner's exact `∂landing/∂n`, slope term included, and the lookup spread along that. Error against the reference went 1.65 → 1.43 (storm) and 1.52 → 1.54 (calm). **Reverted:** it did not earn its second target.

**The reference.** `--water-caustic-bands N` traces N bands at their own indices, each blended in at its own colour with `GL_CONSTANT_COLOR`. `--water-caustic-debug 3` draws the bare pattern at the reference plane's depth, so the spread and the bands are directly comparable.

**Measured** in that view, 3 m, fixture FFT close framing. Chroma error against 16 bands, as a fraction of the reference's own chroma:

| | storm | calm | colour rms | water pass, 960×540 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| spread, 1 trace | 1.65 | 1.52 | 0.076 (ref 0.070) | 6.7 ms |
| 4 bands | 0.91 | 0.83 | 0.082 | 9.8 ms |
| 8 bands | 0.76 | 0.71 | 0.075 | ~14 ms |
| 16 bands | – | – | 0.070 | 23.5 ms |

**Why no single trace gets placement right.** At a few metres the ripples' light has crossed itself before reaching the floor, and each wavelength's crossings land elsewhere. The colour is fine specks that reshuffle with the sampling, and 8 bands against 16 still disagree by 0.7 of the signal.

**Chosen** (by the user, from the pictures): the one-trace spread. It has the right amount of colour and costs nothing extra, but places its fringes more tidily than the specks the physics gives.

**Arms:**
- `water-caustic-energy` holds each channel.
- The new `water-caustic-spectrum` holds the spread to the 16-band reference's per-channel light (0.45% apart) and amount of colour (1.07×, limit 0.7–1.4).

`--only water` passes.

### Phase 5: smoother reads of the wave texture

- **`oceanArrayBSpline`** in `ocean.glsl` is Clearwater's `texBS`: a cubic B-spline from four bilinear taps of one array layer at mip 0. It is continuous through the second derivative, where bilinear is continuous in value only, and these textures are slopes, so bilinear put a kink in the normal at every texel edge.
- **`oceanShadingNormal`** reads the short band through it always, since that band is read at full resolution and faded by distance rather than mipped. It reads the ripples through it until their footprint reaches a texel, blended into the trilinear read over the first mip level so the two never meet at a seam.
- **The caustic trace** calls the same function, so the lens stays the drawn surface.
- **Measured on a calm close framing toward the sun:** the glints are rounder and smoother-edged, and the dark reflections softer. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 6: caustic relief and dark gaps

- **The bed's height** is estimated from its brightness against its neighbourhood: the refracted bed's luma at screen mip 1 over mip 4. Clearwater reads its pebble texture's coarse mip; ours has no height texture, and a textured bed is one flat triangle to the depth buffer.
- **Relief:** the caustic lookup slides along the key's refracted path by `(relief − 1) × 2 cm`, their nudge.
- **Gaps:** the key's share is scaled by `mix(0.8875, 1, smoothstep(0.25, 1, relief))`. 0.8875 is their `mix(0.75, 1, ao)` at their darkest ao of 0.55. It is full from the neighbourhood's own brightness up, so a featureless bed is untouched.
- **Controls:** `Water.caustic_relief`, on by default, with `--no-water-caustic-relief`, a GUI checkbox and a config snapshot row.
- **In screen mips, not texture mips**, so the neighbourhood compared against grows with distance. A brightness edge between two materials reads as relief as well; the fixture's wedge edges are exactly that.
- **Arm `water-caustic-relief`:** a uniform patch of the flat-coloured fixture moves at most 1 code, and 9,641 px move at the material edges.
- **Look.** On the tree's sand, 9% of the frame moves by more than 1%, all along the caustic lines: the lines shift slightly and dim where the sand is darker. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 7: specks in the water

- **`waterSpeckLight`** puts Clearwater's motes at 0.22, 0.60 and 0.98 m along the refracted sight line:
  - a lattice of 48 cells per metre;
  - 1.2% of cells holding a mote, a third of those dark grains;
  - drift as theirs;
  - 2.2% of the key's radiance.
- **Where ours differs from theirs:** each mote is lit by `waterDownwellKey` at its own depth and seen through `exp(−σ · along)`, where theirs uses one green-channel fade. All lengths are in metres through `waterUnitsPerMetre`.
- **The pixel footprint** is the `SurfParam` derivative taken at the top of main, since this runs after the shoreline discard.
- **Controls:** `Water.specks`, on by default, with `--no-water-specks`, a GUI checkbox and a config snapshot row.
- **Arm `water-specks`:** 2,770 px against the switch off, 0.58% of the frame. The floor is 500, and the ceiling of 3% is what a broken hash or density would blow.
- **Look:** faint, tiny motes, as in Clearwater: about 2 mm across. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 8: touch ripples from wading

- **The simulation.** `water_touch_frag` is Clearwater's wave-equation step: velocity toward the neighbours' mean ×0.9, damped by 0.9955 and 0.9985, edges damped, and cosine dimples for drops. `water_touch_normals_frag` writes the height and its two slopes by central differences.
  - Two RGBA16F ping-pong targets and a slope target at 256², over 7 m.
  - **Fixed steps at 60 Hz** from the frame clock, not one per frame, so a headless run is deterministic. It skips ahead past 4 steps behind.
  - **The window** sits ahead of the eye where the view meets the water, at most a third of its size away, moved in whole texels with the field shifted to match.
  - It stops after 900 calm steps, and `touchAvailable` then says there is nothing to read.
- **Unit 5** (`WATER_TOUCH_UNIT`, asserted against the ledger) is bound in `_water_bind_ocean`, so every program including `ocean.glsl` has it. `water_frag` declares 14 of 16.
- **`oceanTouch`** feeds `oceanShadingNormal` on BOTH wave models, so the caustic trace refracts through the rings rather than Clearwater's first-order Laplacian lensing, which is not ported. With nothing simulated, a Gerstner sea gets its mesh normal back as before.
- **`water_vert`** adds the height to the current and previous positions alike, so the rings are not reported as motion.
- **API:** `water_ripple_drop(water, x, z, radius_m, depth_m)`, queued up to 4 a step, and `water_touch_probe`.
- **Callers:**
  - `apps/render` has `--water-drop x,z,frame` (Clearwater's tap: 0.154 m radius, 7 cm deep) and `--water-touch-probe`.
  - The tree walker drops a 12 cm ripple every 35 cm walked with its feet under water and its eye above.
  - Gametest's player and chaser drop one every pace while the surface crosses their capsule, wading or floating.
  - Only the render-app path is seen by a headless run: the walker stands still headless and looks level.
- **Arm `water-ripple-drop`:** a tap at frame 2 is probed at frames 20 and 50. The ring goes 0.243 → 0.637 m (2.62×) and its peak 12.8 → 7.5 mm, and a repeat run prints identical numbers.
- **Look:** rings spreading from a tap, clear on near-flat water and faint under a calm sea's own ripples, as in Clearwater. `--only water` passes.

### Phase 9: diffraction glare

- **`glare.c`/`.h`**, owned by PostFX, is Clearwater's glare ported:
  - **The aperture** (512²): six flats, five scratches, seven dust specks from their mulberry32 seed 3, supersampled 3×3.
  - **The pattern:** a CPU FFT and its power spectrum at eight wavelengths in their colours, with the far field lifted ×8 as a phone lens has it, normalised per channel.
  - **Per frame shape:** the pattern resampled to the grid (512×256, or 256×512 for portrait, the frame in 75%) at 1.15 of the frame height, normalised and transformed again.
  - **Per frame:** a thresholded source (R+G as two complex signals, B alone); a Stockham FFT forward; a multiply by the pattern's spectrum; the transform back; unpacked into a frame-shaped RGBA16F image.
- **Shaders:** `glare_source_frag`, `glare_fft_frag` (its own, since the water's is tied to its twiddles and layers), `glare_multiply_frag` and `glare_output_frag`.
- **Tonemap** adds it beside bloom and flare on unit 13, and now samples 14 of 16.
- **Defaults:** strength 0.9 and threshold 8.8 in working space, both Clearwater's: 14 before their exposure of 0.63.
- **Controls:** on by default everywhere, off in `engine_set_2d_preset`. There are config snapshot rows, a GUI group, `--no-glare` and `--glare-probe`.
- **New gate group `glare`:**
  - `glare-energy`: 0.991 / 0.992 / 0.995 of the source's light.
  - `glare-live`: 351,300 px on the sunward water.
  - `glare-dark`: 0 px on the AO fixture, so goldens with no highlight cannot move.
- **Cost:** 1.5 ms GPU at 960×540.
- **Look:** a soft veil and faint diagonal spikes over the glitter. How heavy the veil is follows from their ×8 far-field lift, a phone lens's, which is a choice of theirs and not a physical constant.

### Review round

Thermo-nuclear and /simplify, all four fan-out angles, then the fixes. Committed as `8f3b8a66`.

- **Downwell at the lookups.** The sky's weakening now multiplies the global irradiance and
  prefilter reads, clearcoat, sheen and the flat ambient where each is taken. Before, it was
  applied to the summed ambient afterwards, which also dimmed GI and local probes, whose light was
  gathered in place under the water already.
  - `waterPresent` is renamed `waterDownwell`.
  - `water_bind_shore` is renamed `water_bind_sea`, and with no water it publishes the off
    state: programs are cached across scenes.
- **The downwell applies below the level everywhere**, not only inside `waterExtent`. That
  reverses the plan's phase 2 wording. The extent is the shoaling bed's domain, and forest's shelf
  and tree's open water both lie outside it. `water_light.glsl` records the cost: a dry pit below
  the level would be lit as flooded, and no scene has one.
- **Key through a flat surface, once per frame:** `keyInWater`, `keyCosT` and `keyTransmit`,
  where each helper used to refract the key again per fragment. The spectrum tables are computed
  at `create_water` rather than per frame.
- **Gerstner touch ripples:** the shading normal now takes the touch slope on both models.
- **Caustic target:** R16F by default, RGBA16F only under `--water-caustic-bands`, and remade when
  that changes. The probe reports one `mean`, and `water-caustic-energy` holds it.
  - Per-level uniforms are hoisted out of the band loop.
  - The CLI limit is `WATER_CAUSTIC_REFERENCE_SAMPLES`.
- **Touch ripples:**
  - The square moves only on a step, so a frame without a step keeps its shift for the next one.
  - A drop-forced step advances the clock, so pressing does not speed up the rings.
  - `water_touch_constants.glsl` shares the resolution and the drop count with the step shader.
  - One `_water_view_window` places both the touch square and the caustic windows.
- **Wakes:** `WaterWake` and `water_wake` in `water.h`, replacing tree's and gametest's copies.
  Gametest's scene guard now runs before the depth read.
- **Glare:**
  - Red and green are packed as one complex signal, and the multiply separates them by Hermitian
    symmetry, so there is one ping-pong pair and one transform where there were two.
  - The pattern is a power spectrum, symmetric about its centre, so its kernel is real: one RGBA
    texture of three reals.
  - There is one failure latch, in postfx, and one source scale, `GLARE_SOURCE_SCALE`, which is
    a uniform.
  - The pattern is normalised once, after the resample.
  - The core is kept, as Clearwater keeps it, and the comment now says so. `glare-energy` holds
    at 0.991 / 0.992 / 0.995, unchanged.
- **Shared includes:**
  - `phase.glsl` holds Henyey-Greenstein for the atmosphere, the froxels and the water.
  - `complex.glsl` holds the complex multiply for both water FFT shaders and the glare.
  - The gate probes use `_probe_rows`.
- **Skipped, with reasons:**
  - **Skipping the glare on dark frames:** it needs a max-luminance readback and a cross-frame
    dependence, and the pass already moves 0 px there.
  - **Moving the caustics into `pbr_frag`:** a redesign, recorded as debt. The bed's key share
    would be exact there, but `pbr_frag` is at 16 samplers.
  - **Touch height through `oceanEvaluateAt`:** the surface query answers the waves. The ripples'
    height is added in `water_vert` only, and the comments now say so.
  - **Relief from the albedo attachment:** it costs a sampler and is often not live.
  - **Moving the touch simulation into its own file:** it changes for the same reasons as the rest
    of the water.
- **Full gates** failed five arms outside the water: `clearcoat-off`, `emissive-match`,
  `emissive-gi`, `purkinje-absolute` and `ortho-dof`. Every one passes with the glare's default
  turned off, so they come from phase 9 and not from this round. None is a defect in the glare.
  Each arm reads a surface in isolation and already switched bloom off, or relied on bloom's
  threshold, to keep a halo out. The glare is a second halo that spreads across the frame. Each
  now pins `--no-glare` beside `--no-bloom`, with the reason; the purkinje arm's reason is that
  the threshold is on exposed radiance. With that, the whole suite passes.

### Second review round

The same five reviews again, over the fixes only (`8f3b8a66`, `86f6ff78`). Committed as
`0f9a0d02`.

- **Touch-ripple clock bug.** A drop forced an early step whenever no step was due, including
  when the clock was already ahead. With drops arriving faster than 60 Hz, the rings ran at the
  frame rate and the clock's lead grew without bound. A drop now borrows at most one step; beyond
  that it waits in the queue for the clock.
- **The key's beam through the surface has one home.** `water_light.glsl` has
  `waterKeyThroughSurface`, for the direction, cosT and transmission, and `waterDownwellThrough`,
  for Beer-Lambert. `waterDownwellKey` wraps the two for `pbr_frag`. `water_frag` calls the first
  once at the top of main. The C copy of the Fresnel, the three uniforms and their publisher are
  deleted. The two copies' grazing floors used to disagree; they cannot now.
- **The no-water off state is published by `_water_publish_sea`**, the same function as the sea.
- **`pbr_frag` reads the environment through helpers**: `envIrradiance`, `envRadiance` and
  `envAmbient` apply the downwell. The probe's own read stays direct, since its capture already
  saw the water. `waterDownwellSky` returns 1 above the level.
- **`NO_HALOS`:** one list in `gates.py`, bloom and glare together, swept through every arm that
  pinned bloom off. It replaces the per-arm glare pins.
- **Smaller:**
  - `caustic_target_colour` is a bool.
  - The wake constants are private to `water.c`.
  - Only the drop count is in the shared touch include.
  - `glare.c` reports its own failures, and postfx latches without logging again.
  - Comment corrections.
- **Skipped:** uploading the spectrum uniforms once, and skipping the no-water publish on

- **Full gates:** all passed but `gpu-wall`, a timing arm, at 44% against 40%. It passed alone at
  1%.

### The glare's edge

On the flare fixture the star stopped at a hard square. The source was bright enough that the
spikes were still lit where the stored pattern ends, which is 1.15 of the frame height across.
Clearwater has the same cutoff; its scenes are never bright enough to show it. The pattern now
fades from 0.6 of its half-width to zero at the half-width (`GLARE_PSF_FADE`), so a spike trails
off. A/B on the flare fixture, the clearcoat spheres and sunward water: the box is gone, and
ordinary highlights barely change. `glare-energy` holds at 0.997. Committed as `1f747955`.
