# Spec 13.55: the birdcage and the hands

Branch and spec `13.55-silent-birdcage`, in the same worktree as 13.54
(`.claude/worktrees/13.54-silent-key-haunting`), branched from its HEAD, which is master at 7bc22261.

## Context

Since 13.54 the key lies loose on the basement workbench, and taking it begins the haunting. The
user wants the key to be a discovery:
- The key is inside a small metal **birdcage**, its bars worked in a pattern, with a **weird face**
  cast on its front.
- The player **picks it up and inspects it** in two hands, turning it to look.
- **E opens it**: the face is the cage's door and swings open like a locket, revealing the key.
- E again takes the key.

That needs first-person **arms and hands**, which the game has never had. They show only while
something is held, in the sleeves of an **olive field jacket**. After the key is taken, the hands
set the cage back on the bench, open and empty.

Decided with the user:
- the face is the door;
- the arms show only while holding;
- the cage goes back on the bench;
- the coat is an olive field jacket.

**Added after phase 2, at the user's word: the flashlight clips to the jacket.** It is no longer
held in a hand. It is an army chest lamp, a model the user chose ("Army Flashlight" by
rodolfo.vanucchi on Sketchfab, CC BY 4.0): a flat green camouflage body with a round lens on its
front and a clip on its back.
- Clipped to the jacket, it leaves both hands free for the cage, and the game decides where it
  points.
- Through the whole inspection, from the pickup until the arms drop away, the lamp is on and aimed
  at the birdcage.
- The player thinks aloud: "What is this thing...?", a pause, then "The face... I think it opens."

## Behaviour

**The flashlight, everywhere.**
- **Where it is.** The lamp is clipped to the jacket's left chest: 10 cm left of the eye, 25 cm
  below it and 5 cm ahead, its lens facing forward.
  - It rides the body: the body's heading, the eye's height, a crouch and the bob, but never the
    head's pitch.
  - The left side keeps it clear of the right hand, which opens the door, takes the key and
    carries it down past the chest.
- **Where it points.** In play the beam points where the player looks, trailing a turn a little as
  it does now.
- **The switch.** F turns it on and off, as now.
- **It is never drawn in first person.** No body is drawn, and a lamp hanging in the air below the
  eye would show it. In the inspection the chest stays just below the frame even at the pickup's
  deepest look down.
- **The bag.** The lamp is what the bag holds and what its screen shows turning: "An army
  flashlight. It clips to the jacket. F turns it on and off." The kit's barrel flashlight goes.

**Through the inspection** the lamp is on, whatever its switch says, from the moment the view
starts settling for the pickup until the arms have dropped away.
- It aims at the middle of the cage's face, whether the cage is on the bench, in the hands or
  being set down.
- Its aim eases from the player's look onto the cage while the view settles, and back after.
- Afterwards it goes back to its switch. A lamp that was off goes off again.
- The haunting leaves the lamp alone, as it always has, so when the key is taken the house goes
  red around a cage still in the beam.

**On the bench.** (As built in phase 1.)
- The key's old spot holds a small birdcage of rusted iron, cast solid, about 27 cm tall with its
  carrying ring and 13.6 cm across its base.
- It is a closed, hollow body in a birdcage's shape: its bars, rings, gothic arches and circles
  stand out of it in relief, and nothing is see-through.
- A round door on its front is cast as a face: empty eye sockets, an open O of a mouth, nostril
  slits, sunken cheeks under a heavy brow, and a rim ridged like a shell. The sockets, the mouth
  and the nostrils are cut through, so the brass of the key glints through them from the dark
  inside.
- The key hangs inside on a hook behind the door, its bow in the opening.

**Picking it up.** E at the cage ("E   Pick up"):
- The view settles onto the cage from a set distance and angle. The pickup is authored for that
  one framing, so the hands land on the cage wherever the player pressed E.
- The arms come up from below, bare hands out of olive canvas sleeves with buttoned cuffs. They
  reach down to the cage, the left palm sliding under its base and the right fingers closing round
  its side, and lift it up in front of the eye.
- As it comes up the hands turn it a little, a slow quarter turn and a tilt, as anyone looks at
  something strange.
- The player's movement and look stop while it is held.

**What the player thinks**, as thoughts, the way every line the player has is shown. There is no
voice.
1. At the `look` event: "What is this thing...?". It stays 2 s, then fades.
2. A pause of about a second with nothing on screen.
3. Then: "The face... I think it opens."
4. As the door swings open: "There's something inside."
5. On the take, as now: "A key. It might fit the front door."

The first two are a beat the player watches. The hold's prompt waits for the second line.

**Holding.**
- The mouse, or the right stick, turns the cage. Yaw spins it on the left palm; pitch tilts the
  hands, up to about ±25°.
- From the second thought on, the prompt is "E   Open    RMB   Put down" (X and B on a pad).
- RMB or B sets it back on the bench, shut, and gives the player back their look and movement.

**Opening.** E: the right hand lets go of the cage's side, pinches the face door's free edge and
swings it open on its side hinge, lets it go, and waits before the open door. The key shows. The
prompt becomes "E   Take the key".

**Taking the key.** E:
1. The right hand pinches the key's bow just inside the door, draws it out and puts it away, and
   the key goes to the backpack.
2. The thought "A key. It might fit the front door." shows, and `story_key` runs: the haunting
   begins and the cellar door slams above.
3. The hands set the cage down on the bench, open and empty, and the arms drop out of view.

After that the cage is a prop and is not offered again.

**Saves.** No new rows.
- The cage's door stands open, with nothing on its hook, exactly when the backpack holds the key.
- An inspection under way is not saved. A load or Quit to Title puts the cage straight back on the
  bench.
- A `--key` start finds the cage open and empty.

**Pause.** The pause menu works mid-hold. The hold runs on the frame's sim clock, so it freezes
under the menu with the world.

## Design

**One Blender script per object**, each run headless (`Blender -b --factory-startup -P ...`) in
the shape of `make_cat_blender.py`. The arms' script imports the cage's and builds the cage in its
own scene, so the hands are posed round the real thing.

**`apps/silent/tools/make_birdcage_blender.py`** writes **`assets/models/birdcage.glb`**: static,
no rig, as built in phase 1.
- **Nodes:**
  - `cage`: the base, the bars, the rings, the tracery, the crown and its ring, and the hook
    inside, all as tubes and solids of revolution;
  - its child `cage_shell`: the solid body the pattern stands out of, hollow, with the door's
    opening cut through it;
  - its child `cage_door`: the face. It is a relief mesh, a polar grid displaced by a height
    function, real geometry with smooth normals, with its holes cut by an exact boolean. Its
    origin is on the hinge; it turns about its own y, and a negative angle opens it.
  - its child `key_hang`: where the key's middle hangs and how the key is turned.
- **The surface is baked.** The parts are unwrapped together into one 2048 atlas. A shader takes
  Poly Haven's CC0 `rusty_metal_04` scan, mapped at 0.3 of its scanned size, and shapes it by the
  form:
  - worn to bare iron where the form stands out (pointiness);
  - grimed in the crevices (ambient occlusion within 4 mm);
  - dark deep inside (ambient occlusion within 5 cm).

  Cycles bakes the colour, the roughness, the metal and the normal into the atlas, and the
  roughness and metal are packed as glTF packs them. The glb carries the maps as JPEGs: 3.4 MB in
  all.
- **The scans** come through `apps/silent/tools/polyhaven.py`: the cached download
  `fetch_textures.py` had, moved where Blender's Python can import it too.
- **`--surface ID`** bakes another scan, and **`--preview`** renders Cycles stills lit as the
  basement lights it.

**`apps/silent/tools/make_flashlight_blender.py`** (added after phase 2) writes
**`assets/models/flashlight.glb`** from the model as the user downloaded it, which is committed
as `assets/models/army_flashlight.glb`.
- **Size.** It comes in about 620 units tall in no stated unit, and is scaled to a real chest
  lamp's 15 cm.
- **Frame.** It is turned so its lens faces +Z and its clip's back is at its origin.
- **The `lens` node** is the lens's middle, facing out. That is where the beam starts.
- **The credit** goes in the glb's copyright field, as the hands' does, with no credit file. Its
  own maps are kept.
- **`--preview`** renders stills.

**`apps/silent/tools/make_arms_blender.py`** (phase 2) is in the cat script's shape:
- clips written as functions of time at 30 fps;
- `glb.py` for the export self-check;
- a generated header;
- `--preview clip@s` stills, `--movie` to play clips in turn, and `--clip-check` to report any
  frame where a part of the arms is inside the cage.

It imports the cage's script for the cage's dimensions, and `birdcage.glb` for the stills.

It writes:
- **`assets/models/arms.glb`**: skinned arms, 51 bones and 68,596 triangles, 5.6 MB.
  - **Bones:**
    - `Root`, at the eye.
    - Under it, three bones that deform nothing:
      - `View` is where the game puts the camera while the arms are up, so a clip's lean and look
        are authored with its hands.
      - `Cage` is the held cage's frame, at its base's middle.
      - `Lamp` (added after phase 2) is the beam: at the lamp's lens on the chest, riding the
        clip's lean and knee dip, its +Y aimed at the middle of the cage's face. The game puts the
        flashlight on it while the arms are up.
    - Each arm: UpperArm, ForeArm, ForeTwist, Hand, and four bones for each finger and the thumb.
  - **Meshes:**
    - **The hands** are a sculpted hand, `assets/models/rigged_hand.glb` ("Rigged hand" by Elena
      FF on Sketchfab, CC BY-SA 4.0), with its own maps. Its capped wrist is cut off and the
      forearm grown on from the cut as one surface. The forearm's skin follows ForeTwist, so the
      wrist only bends and the sleeve never wrings. The credit is in arms.glb's copyright field;
      there is no credit file.
    - **The sleeves** are Poly Haven's CC0 `book_pattern` weave in its olive colourway, with
      buttoned cuffs.
  - **Clips:**

    | Clip | Length | Events | What it is |
    |------|--------|--------|------------|
    | `raise` | 2.1 s | `grip` 0.64, `look` 1.42 | the reach down, the grip, the lift and the look |
    | `hold` | 4 s loop | | a breath, the right hand at the cage's side |
    | `open` | 1.3 s | | the side let go, the door's edge pinched and swung, the hand to `wait` |
    | `wait` | 4 s loop | | the right hand relaxed before the open door |
    | `take` | 1.6 s | `key` 0.4, `away` 1.05 | the key pinched, drawn out, put away; back to the side |
    | `lower` | 1.5 s | `down` 0.95 | the cage set back where it stood |

  - The pickup's framing is part of the clips: the view settles to a set pose relative to the
    cage before `raise` and `lower` play, so the reach is authored once.
- **`apps/silent/src/arms_clips.h`, GENERATED.** Never edit it; rerun the script. It holds:
  - each clip's length, looping and events;
  - the pickup's framing: the eye 0.5 m from the cage's axis, the cage's base 0.72 m below it;
  - the door's swing in `open`, 0.5 s to 0.98 s, and its open angle, echoed from the cage's
    script;
  - the bone the key rides between `key` and `away` (`Index3.R`), and the key's frame in it;
  - `ARMS_LAMP`, where the lamp's lens is clipped on, from the eye in the body's frame. In play
    the game places the flashlight from it, so the chest is stated once for the clips and for
    play.

  The preview's torch is the `Lamp` bone with the game's cone (9° inside, 21° outside), and the
  preview's arms cast no shadow, as the game's do not.

**New C modules in `apps/silent/src/`:**

`arms.c/h`, the viewmodel.
- It imports `arms.glb` the way `cat_body.c` imports the cat (`cat_body.c:142-231`): a library
  scene given to the engine, the model moved out and its materials moved to the game's scene, and
  `node_set_programs` with the skinned PBR program.
- **Its animator is the app's, not an entity's.** It comes from `create_animator`, bound with
  `node_set_pose`, and `animator_update` runs once a rendered frame from `on_pre_render` with
  `game->frame_clock.delta`, the clock `player_pre_render` eases by. An entity animator gets whole
  steps of the sim clock and would stutter this close to the eye.
- **Placement:** each frame, after `player_pre_render`, the root is put at the eye in camera space
  from the rig's pose (`rig->pose.eye` and `camera_rig_direction`). That is the flashlight's
  same-frame head-riding (`lights.c:400-415`), before the engine's transform walk, so it never lags
  a frame. While the arms are up, the camera then goes on the `View` bone, which carries the
  clips' lean and look.
- **It is kept out of the light:** `capture_hidden`, and no shadow (`MESH_SHADOW_NONE`), so it
  never shadows the flashlight's own beam.
- **Shown only while holding**, by putting its node on and off the graph.
- **API:**
  - `arms_load`, `arms_free`;
  - `arms_frame(arms, eye, basis, dt)`;
  - `arms_play(clip)`, `arms_finished`;
  - `arms_cage(arms, mat4)`: the `Cage` bone's world transform, read the way
    `cat_body.c:235` reads the eye bones.

`birdcage.c/h`, the cage.
- It imports `birdcage.glb` the same way (static meshes) and keeps the `cage` and `cage_door`
  nodes.
- `birdcage_place(mat4 world)` puts the cage on the bench or in the hands.
- `birdcage_door(angle)` sets the door's angle.
- `birdcage_key_hang(mat4)` gives the key's hang on its hook in the world.
- **The key stays under the scene root.** Its world transform is set from the cage each frame
  rather than by parenting it, so `backpack_lay` and `kit_node_shown` keep putting it on and off
  the graph as they do now. Reparenting it fights them.
- The cage is `capture_hidden`, since it moves.

`inspect.c/h`, the hold's state machine.
- **States:** NONE, RAISING, HOLDING, OPENING, OPEN, TAKING, LOWERING. HOLDING loops `hold`
  and OPEN loops `wait`.
- **What it drives:**
  - the arms' clips;
  - the cage's pose: the bench pose eased into `arms_cage` × the player's turn over the rise, and
    back over the lower;
  - the door's angle over the `open` window;
  - the lamp: on, and eased from its play pose onto the `Lamp` bone over the view's settle, and
    back off it after the lower;
  - the thoughts' beat, and the prompts through `hud_prompt`, the hold's waiting for the second
    thought.
- **What it reads:** the mouse delta and the `look_x`/`look_y` actions to turn the cage, `interact`
  to open and take, and a new `put_down` action (RMB, pad B).
- **The take:** at the `take` clip's `key` event it calls back into silent.c's take. That is
  today's `take_key`: `backpack_add`, the thought, then `story_key`.
- `inspect_drop` puts everything back at once, for a load, Quit to Title or `--key`.

**Changed modules:**
- **`player.c/h`:** a hold lock, `player_hold(p, bool)`. While held it skips `camera_rig_aim` and
  the look actions, zeroes the move in `player_update` and eases the pitch level, and it **keeps the
  cursor captured**. `input_set_suppressed` frees the cursor (`player.c:277-295`), so it cannot be
  used here.
- **`lights.c/h`:**
  - The flashlight is placed at `ARMS_LAMP` from the eye, turned by the body's heading alone. It
    used to be 16 cm right of the look, 22 cm below it and 10 cm ahead.
  - A new `lights_flashlight_hold(lights, const mat4 lamp, float weight)` blends the beam from its
    play pose toward `lamp` by `weight`, and holds it on while `weight` is above 0.
  - The switch is not touched, so the lamp goes back to it.
- **`backpack.c`:** the flashlight's picture is `flashlight.glb`, brought in as the cat is. An
  item names a glb or a kit builder, and the kit's barrel flashlight and its lathe profile go.
  The item's words and footprint change for a flat lamp.
- **`hud.c/h`:** `hud_think_for(hud, text, seconds)`, a thought with its own stay, for the beat's
  first line. `hud_think` keeps the 4 s.
- **`house_key.c`:** the key is no longer laid on the bench. Its lying pose is the cage's hook,
  set by the cage, and its reach point goes. The model is kept: `house_key_model` is still the
  bag's picture.
- **`silent.c`:**
  - the cage is offered with `reach_offer(&reach, cage.at, NULL, pick_up_cage, "E   Pick up")`
    while the key is not held, in place of the key's offer;
  - no other reach is offered while holding;
  - `put_down` joins the action table;
  - wiring: load the arms and the cage, `inspect_frame` after `player_pre_render`, `inspect_drop`
    on load and on Quit to Title;
  - flags: `--inspect closed|open` starts with the cage held, wherever the player stands. That is
    for headless pictures, since `--player-at` cannot reach the basement. `--trace-hold` prints the
    hold's states.
- **Docs:** `AGENTS.md`'s silent row, `docs/cli-reference.md`, and the spec.

## Phases

Each phase that changes what the game draws ends with the user looking at it, and the next waits for
their word. Silent is a demo app: no gate arms, no goldens.

0. **Setup.** `git checkout -b 13.55-silent-birdcage` in the worktree, copy this plan to
   `specs/13.55-silent-birdcage.md`, and commit it alone.
1. **The cage.**
   - The script's cage half, and `birdcage.glb`.
   - Blender stills from the front, three-quarters, side and above, with the door shut and open.
   - Shown in Preview. **The user looks at the face and the pattern**, and I iterate on what they say.
2. **The arms.**
   - The rig, the sleeves, the hands and the five clips, authored around the cage.
   - Stills of each clip's key poses holding the cage.
   - **The user looks.**
2b. **The lamp** (added after phase 2).
   - `make_flashlight_blender.py` and `flashlight.glb`, with stills of the lamp.
   - The arms' `Lamp` bone and `ARMS_LAMP`.
   - The preview lit by the lamp as the game will light it: the beam from the chest on the cage
     through all six clips, the game's cone, no shadow from the arms.
   - A movie of the whole run. **The user looks.**
3. **In the game.**
   - `arms.c`, `birdcage.c`, `inspect.c`, the player's hold lock, the reach, the action and the
     flags.
   - The lamp on the chest in play and on the cage through the inspection, the bag's picture, and
     the thoughts' beat.
   - Headless stills with `--inspect` in a lit room: shut, turned, open, mid-take.
   - The take traced through to `story key taken, the haunting begun`.
   - **The user plays it at the bench in the window.**
4. **Light and edges.**
   - The lamp on a cage 0.4 m from it, and the haunting's red.
   - The lamp from the chest in play: a wall up close, a stair, a crouch.
   - Whether the near object shows TAA, GTAO or contact-shadow artefacts.
   - Pause mid-hold, a load and Quit to Title mid-hold, `--key`.
   - Fixed as found. A light knob is added only if the look needs it.
5. **Docs**, and the spec's as-built notes.
6. **Review.** The thermo-nuclear and simplify reviews, as background agents. Apply their fixes,
   then fast-forward master **once the user says so**, keeping the worktree.

## As built

**Phase 1, the cage.** It went through three looks with the user.
1. **See-through.** The first was a wire cage you could see through, in two faces side by side: a
   doll and a hollow face.
2. **Solid, with the hollow face.** The user wanted it solid with the pattern on it, and the hollow
   face. The doll was "stupid".
   - The wirework now stands half out of a closed shell.
   - The key hangs 1.7 cm lower than first placed, so the door opens on its bow, not a bare shank.
3. **Textured.** Asked whether it would be, the user said to make it look as good as possible. It
   was baked in Poly Haven's `rusty_metal`, `rusty_metal_04` and `rusty_metal_05`, rendered lit as
   the basement lights it, and the user chose `rusty_metal_04`.

What else changed on the way:
- **One script per object, at the user's word.** The cage's script was named for the hands, since
  the plan had one script for both.
- **The scans' download moved into `polyhaven.py`**, which `fetch_textures.py` now imports.
  Rebuilding `rusty_metal_02` through it left the committed files byte-identical.

The render app imports `birdcage.glb` whole: three meshes, the colour, normal and roughness-metal
maps, and the four nodes by name.

**Decided on the way into phase 2:**
- The pickup is a full reach, grip, lift and look rather than the cage gliding into hands that are
  already up.
- The view settles to a set framing first, so it is authored once.
- The player thinks "What is this?".

**Phase 2, the arms.** The hands are the user's pick of a sculpted hand rather than a modelled
one. Its credit stays in arms.glb, with no credit file, since the engine is open source. The user
watched each round as a movie, and each thing they turned back became:
- **The motion was not natural.**
  - The keys run through monotone cubics, with the eye leading and the fingers trailing, each a
    little after the one before.
  - The elbow turns away from the thumb, and the hand turns toward its forearm's line. So no pose
    asks a wrist for more than a wrist does, and each run prints every wrist's range.
- **The hand's edge did not meet the arm.** The forearm is now grown from the sculpted hand's wrist
  cut as one surface.
- **The pickup was straight-armed.**
  - The eye stands 0.5 m from the cage.
  - The body leans 20° into the reach, the shoulder comes forward with it, and the knees dip with
    the lean.
- **Fingers went through the cage.** Each finger now closes until it meets the iron, and
  `--clip-check` reports any frame with a part inside the cage. Nothing goes in further than about
  2 mm: fingertips resting on the iron.
- **A finger through the key's ring.** The take is a pinch instead.
  - The forefinger and the thumb close on the key 1.2 cm inside the door's opening. The thumb now
    rolls across at its base to meet the forefinger.
  - The key is as it was, 10 cm on its hook at the middle.
- **Two fingers stuck out as the door opened, and the hand went back through the cage after it.**
  - The right hand now pinches the door's free edge and rides it for the first half of the swing,
    turned half as far as the door. Then it lets go.
  - It waits, relaxed, before the open door in a sixth clip, `wait`, until E.
  - `take` starts from there, and comes back to the cage's side from outside the cage.

The user passed the whole run, and then `open` and `take` as they are now.

Open for phases 3 and 4:
- **The flashlight.** The preview's torch sat where the game held the flashlight: 16 cm right of
  the eye, 22 cm below and 10 cm ahead.
  - In `take`, the hand passed a few centimetres in front of it on the way down and burned white.
  - The preview's arms cast shadows, and the game's will not. So in the preview the right forearm
    covered the torch for most of the clips, and seemed to switch it on when it dropped away.

  The user's answer was the lamp on the jacket (above). On the left chest it is out of the right
  hand's way, and phase 2b's preview lights the clips as the game will.
- **The wrist in `open`.** As it rides the door, the right wrist bends up to 45° toward the little
  finger, the most of any clip.

**Phase 2b, the lamp.**
- **`flashlight.glb`.** The download is 7.2 by 15 cm and 3.7 cm deep at a real lamp's height. It
  is one mesh of 35,445 triangles with its own maps, 3.8 MB, and its lens is emissive, as its

**Phase 3, in the game.** As designed, except where it says otherwise here.
- **`library.c/h`** is the import the arms, the cage and the bag's lamp share. It loads the file as
  a library scene, waits for its textures, takes its nodes and moves its materials. The cat keeps
  its own, which also dresses its coat.
- **The view has a rig of its own.** The inspection installs a camera rig of its own and gives the
  player's back at the end, as the title does.
  - `camera_rig_set_pose` rewrites a rig's distance and lifts. Posing the player's rig would have
    left a first-person camera a metre behind the body.
  - The glide in and out is the inspection's own, from the player's rig's pose to the `View` bone
    and back. On the way out, the player's look is turned to where the arms' view last looked, so

  the bench: the bench's frame turned half a turn, 0.72 m up and 0.5 m back. The capsule stays where

- **The key goes into the bag at `away`**, not `key`. From `key` to `away` it rides the pinch on
  the graph. Taken at `key` it would have left the graph while still in view. So the cellar door
  slams as the key goes out of sight.
- **Turning it.** The mouse, the stick or the arrows spin the cage on the left palm, from the second
  thought on. The cage turns back square over 0.3 s as the hands open it or set it down.
  - The ±25° tilt is not built: the hands could not follow it.
- **The key's place.** The key hangs on the cage's hook wherever the cage is, and `house_key.c`

- **Exposure.** At night the pinned exposure goes down to a sixteenth, four stops, as the beam
  comes onto the cage, and back up as it leaves (`EXPOSURE_HELD`). By day the meter does it.
- **The bag's lamp** is `flashlight.glb`'s node named `flashlight`. That file turns the lamp so its
  lens faces -x, since the bag's pictures look at an item from its side.
- **The flags:**
  - `--inspect pickup|closed|open` starts holding it, wherever the player stands, once the game is
    played.
  - `--trace-hold` prints each state and event with its frame.
  - A pad script pressing X every half second drives the whole run headless.
- **The headless run went through every state:** settling, raising, `look`, holding, opening,

**Phase 4, light and edges.** Each case was looked at in headless stills.
- **The held lamp's aim, at the user's word.** The aim was centred on the cage's face. From the left
  chest that threw the pool up and to the right, onto the wall behind. It now aims 3 cm to the left
  of the face's middle and 5 cm below it (`LAMP_AIM`), and the pool sits behind the cage.
- **The lamp in play meets the look.** Along the look from the chest, its spot fell low and to the

  - Each frame a ray along the look finds the first wall or door within 12 m, and the beam is aimed
    from the chest at that point. The distance eases as the look does, so the beam does not jump
    past an edge.
  - Across a room the aim is within a degree and a half of the look.
  - Crouched, the lamp goes down with the body. In the kitchen it catches the table's front edge.
- **The haunting's red** leaves the cage and the hands readable in the beam. Nothing changed.
- **A `--key` start** finds the cage open and empty on the bench with no prompt. Before the key is
  taken it is shut, with "E   Pick up".
- **Pause.** The hold runs on the frame clock, whose delta is 0 while paused, so the arms, the spin
  and the beat all freeze. The menu holds the game's input, so neither E nor a put-down reaches the
  hold.
- **Load and Quit to Title.** Both go through the save's `apply`, which drops the hold. On the way
  to the title the title's own camera is installed after that, so the drop cannot leave the player's
  rig in its place.
- **The near image.** The hand held still matched with TAA and without. A thin bright edge showed on
  the hand in one frame of the open, while it moved. The user did not want it chased.
- **Found on the way: a fullscreen launch showed no window** (`engine.c`).
  - The engine makes its window hidden, puts it in the chosen mode, and shows it at the end of
    creation (spec 13.39). GLFW's `glfwShowWindow` does nothing to a window already on a monitor,

  - The engine asks the window system whether the window is visible after that, and warns by name

**Phase 6, review.** Five background reviews: reuse, simplification, efficiency, altitude, and a
strict structural one in place of the thermo-nuclear skill, which only the user can run.

Two findings were breakage:
- **Five tools no longer imported.** `make_cards.py` and `fetch_sounds.py` still took
  `CACHE_DIR` from `fetch_textures.py` after the download moved to `polyhaven.py`, and
  `make_gothic.py`, `make_map.py` and `make_reeds.py` import `make_cards`. `make_gothic.py` and
  `make_map.py` took it directly too, which the reviewer missed. All take it from `polyhaven.py`
  now, and `make_cards.py` rebuilds its committed outputs byte for byte.
- **The engine's material texture array went from 73 MB to 1259 MB.** Every layer takes the size
  of the largest, and the new models' roughness and metal maps were 2048². Every model's mask
  maps are now 256² (`MASK_SIZE` in `blender_kit.py`), and the array is 77 MB, at 1024x256. The
  held cage looks the same.

Applied:
- **The hold's view** is the inspection's own camera rig, posed each frame and blended in and out
  by `camera_rig_blend_from_here`, in place of a hand-rolled glide.
- **The second line of thought** waits for the first to be gone, through `hud_thinking`, and then a
  second (`BEAT_PAUSE`). A hard-coded sum of the HUD's fades went with that.
- **The arms:**
  - their bones are read from the animation's own globals, so a store of inverted inverse binds
    went;
  - the framing is placed once a hold, not copied every frame;
  - the clip playing is the animator's, with no copy of its own;
  - a missing bone or clip frees what was made.
- **`inspect_ready`.** When the arms could not be had, the reach offers the key straight from the
  cage, so the story cannot be cut off.
- **The beam's release** is `lights_flashlight_release`, called once. The look's ray is cast only
  while the lamp is lit and not held.
- **The night exposure** is written only when the lamp's hold moves, so the debug panel's exposure
  slider works outside a hold again.
- **`--inspect`** is parsed once, into an enum, implies `--backpack`, and is refused beside `--key`.
  So the take cannot be refused mid-hold.
- **One glb import** (`library.c`) for the cat, the arms, the cage and the bag's lamp, with the
  skinned program set up there and `node_find` under it. The cat keeps only its coat's dressing.
- **The bag's own kit** owns the materials, so an item drawn from a file gets no kit.
- **The player's hold** is the plain field `Player.holding`; its setter only stored it.
- **The engine's fullscreen launch:** after the show the window is put in the mode asked for,
  whatever it is, rather than a second test for fullscreen.
- **The Blender tools:**
  - share `blender_kit.py`: the spaces, the meshes, the materials, Poly Haven's maps as images, the
    stills and their sheet, and the glTF export and import;
  - `glb.py` gained the triangle count and the credit line;
  - the dead code went;
  - the arms script solves each frame once and caches the take's clip and the key's frame on the
    finger.
- **The bag's lamp** is cut from 35,445 triangles to 5,000, with 512² colour and normal maps: it is
  only ever a picture a few hundred pixels across. The file went from 3.9 MB to 738 KiB.
- **The three models** were made again. `arms_clips.h` came out unchanged.
