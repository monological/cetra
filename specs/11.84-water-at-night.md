# Spec 11.84 — B15, water at night

## Context

Roadmap **B15**, and now the only genuinely open item in the document — everything else is
DONE, rejected on investigation (B3.1, E10), or self-refuting (E7's own row says it is booked
"not because a measurement demands it").

Render `apps/tree` at `--sun-elevation -12`: a black tree against a full star field over a sea
of **flat daytime turquoise**. Measured HSL saturation — **0.57 at night against 0.29 by day**,
so the night frame is *more* saturated than the day one.

**Two independent defects.**

**1. The in-scatter is a constant, not a response to illumination.** `water.c:1851` uploads
`water->scatter` raw; `water_frag.glsl:1056` spends it as
`waterScatter * preExposure * (1.0 - T)` — multiplied by nothing that knows how much light
falls on the water. It behaves like an emissive.

**2. Water sees ONE directional and picks the sky's sun by name.** `water.c:1867-1881` prefers
`scene->sky->sun_light`, deliberately: spec 11.41 put that there because scanning for the first
directional found whatever the scene listed first, so caustics focused from one light while the
deck occluded another. At night the sun still *exists* below the horizon, so the fallback scan
never runs while `sky_horizon_fade` has zeroed its intensity. Water holds a direction pointing
at a sun underground with `sunRadiance` exactly zero, and never reaches the moon.

**The framing changed on inspection.** The roadmap calls defect 1 a bug; shown the frame, the
user likes the colour, and they are half right. It is not a mistake anyone made —
`water_frag`'s header lists the authored scatter among its absolute-radiance inputs, so it is a
*stated design*. It is a **missing mechanism**: nobody chose luminous night water, and the same
absence means you could not get a dark realistic sea either. **So the deliverable is CONTROL,
not correctness.**

---

## What exploration established, and what it changes

**`scatter` has THREE consumers, not one.** The surface body (`water_frag.glsl:1056`), the
**submerged froxel medium** (`water.c:538` → `fx->water_inscatter` → `froxel_inject_frag.glsl:238`),
and `water_frag.glsl:1237`'s `AlbedoOut = vec4(waterScatter, 0.0)` — written **un-pre-exposed,
as an albedo, for SSGI**. That third one is the tell: one consumer already treats this value as
an albedo. Renaming makes it correct rather than accidental. **All three must move together or
the surface and the volume disagree.**

**There is no incident-light term in scope at line 1056.** Everything lit — `reflected`,
`sunVis`, the glitter, the foam's `ambient` — is computed *after* it. The fix has to build one.

**The material for it already exists.** `prefilteredMap` (unit 12) plus `maxReflectionLOD`, and
the foam already takes exactly the term wanted at `:1160`:
`textureLod(prefilteredMap, n, maxReflectionLOD).rgb * iblIntensity` — the top mip, which
`:1132` calls "already a hemispherical average". No new sampler.

**A trap to avoid: do NOT declare `irradianceMap`.** `IBL_IRRADIANCE_TEXTURE_UNIT` is 11, which
is `WATER_SHADOW_UNIT` — a `sampler2DArray`. `bind_ibl_textures` already sets that uniform
during the water draw, harmlessly, because water declares no such sampler. Declaring one puts a
cube and an array on one image unit: `INVALID_OPERATION` at draw.

**Only the glitter and the foam die at night.** Caustics read `sunAvailable` and `sunDir` but
**never `sunRadiance`** — so they keep running at full contrast with the sun underground, and
`max(sunDir.y, 0.12)` walks the crossing point a long meaningless distance. That is the reverse
defect, and picking a real light fixes it as a side effect.

**The cloud deck is already slot-guarded — except for caustics, and the fix is a one-word
swap.** `cloud_shadow.glsl:52` is
`cloudSunForSlot(P, slot) { return (slot >= 0 && slot == cloudShadowLight) ? cloudSunAt(P) : 1.0; }`,
which `sunVis` uses at `:1098`, so the glitter and the foam are already safe under a new pick.
The caustic tap at `:1050` calls the **bare `cloudSunAt`** with no slot test, so it would apply
the *sun's* shear to moonlight. It just needs to call `cloudSunForSlot(..., sunShadowSlot)` like
its neighbour — no new mechanism, and it makes the two deck taps in this file one idiom instead
of two.

**Coverage is a total gap, and it is checkable in one grep**: no arm in the `water` or `beach`
groups passes `--sun-elevation` at all, the only two `.cscn` fixtures carrying a `water{}` block
author +35° and +32°, and the one arm that moves the sun does it by authoring
`environment.sun.elevation` in a `cscn_copy` variant — `WATER_GLITTER_SUN`, **+22°**, still
broad daylight. So every water frame in the suite is taken at +22° or above. Nothing renders
water with a below-horizon sun, nothing renders it with a moon, and nothing asserts that
`sunRadiance` reaching zero produces a sane frame. Both defects could have shipped from any spec
since 11.41 without an arm going red.

**Group sizes, since AGENTS.md is stale on one**: `water` has **31** arms and `beach` has
**four** — `beach-shoreline`, `beach-shoal`, `beach-surf-zone` and `shore-twin`, the last
undocumented. 31 + 4 = 35, which is the roadmap's own figure.

## The design

### Half A — the brightest directional (lands first, alone)

Replace the name-based pick with a rank over `scene->lights`:

```c
// Rank by intensity x peak channel -- light_cull_radius's own reduction
// (light.c:203-208), which is this codebase's canonical "how strong is this
// light". Directionals are all in LUX, so they compare without conversion.
// Strict > keeps the first in scene order on a tie, so the pick stays
// deterministic.
```

**This keeps 11.41's fix rather than reverting it.** That fix was about not choosing
*arbitrarily* — "whichever the scene file happened to list first". Brightest is deterministic
and meaningful, which is strictly more than first-in-array ever was. Its comment gets rewritten
to say so rather than deleted.

**A weight of zero selects nothing**, so `sunAvailable` goes to 0 at true darkness where today
it is 1 with zero radiance. That is what switches the caustics off when there is no sun, and it
is a behaviour change worth calling out.

**Verified safe in daylight before writing it.** `KEY_LIGHT_TOTAL_INTENSITY` is **10.0**
(`render.c:59`) and `sky_horizon_fade` is 1 above +3°, so on both fixtures the pick is
`{sky_sun 10, WaterKey/BeachKey 1.2, sky_moon 0}` — an **8x** margin at the lowest sun any arm
renders (+22°). The crossover where a 1.2 scene key would overtake the fading sky sun is ~0.4°
elevation, twenty degrees below anything in the suite. **Every daylight water and beach arm and
both goldens should be 0 px** — a prediction, and anything else means the ranking is wrong, stop.

Plus the caustic deck guard, and `--water-probe` gains a row naming the picked light so the
choice is observable from outside the process.

### Half B — the in-scatter derived, with an authored floor

```glsl
// What falls on the water: the environment's hemispherical average (the top mip
// the foam already uses) plus the picked directional's own irradiance, cosine-
// weighted for a horizontal surface.
vec3 incident = ...prefilteredMap top mip... + sunRadiance * max(sunDir.y, 0.0);
vec3 inscatter = waterScatterAlbedo * incident + waterScatterGlow;
vec3 body = bed * T + inscatter * preExposure * (1.0 - T);
```

`scatter` → **`scatterAlbedo`** (dimensionless, what fraction comes back) plus
**`scatterGlow`** (absolute radiance, the self-illumination floor). The old key is **refused by
name** with a message saying the units changed — spec 11.48's precedent, and the only detectable
option, since the old value still parses and would silently mean something else.

**`iblEnabled == 0` contributes ZERO, not the foam's `vec3(1.0)`.** The env tap at `:1160` is
guarded and falls back to white, which is right there — it is a *multiplier* on foam shading —
and would be exactly wrong here: `albedo x 1.0` reinstates a constant in-scatter under a
different name, which is the defect this half exists to remove. With no environment there is no
environment light, so only the directional and the glow remain. Both fixtures author
`"mode": "sky"` so they take the live branch; the fallback is the one nothing renders, which is
precisely why it has to be written down rather than inherited.

`AlbedoOut` writes `scatterAlbedo` (now correctly an albedo). `water_publish_to_postfx` gets the
same treatment so the submerged volume and the surface stay one statement.

**A consequence of "keep tree's look exactly" that is worth stating plainly.** Today's
in-scatter is the same value day and night. Reproducing *both* ends with
`albedo x incident + glow` forces `albedo ~ 0` and `glow = today's scatter` — so **tree does not
become physical; it opts into the stylised look explicitly.** That is a coherent choice and it
is what was asked for, but two things follow: tree's sea stays the brightest thing in its night
frame (so it still drives auto-exposure and would close the Purkinje adaptation gate), and what
actually improves tree's night frame is **half A's moonlight glitter**, not half B. The two
fixtures move to albedo-driven and get the physical behaviour.

## Not covered, said out loud

**Whether the derived in-scatter is physically right.** It is a single-scatter albedo against a
hemispherical average — the same approximation the foam's bubbles already make, and no fixture
distinguishes it from a better one.

**Cloud shadows on the moon.** The deck is sheared along the sun; the guard suppresses it for a
non-sun pick rather than marching a second shear. Booked out of 11.82 and still booked.

**Tree's night frame remains stylised by choice**, so it stays the scene where the sea drives
auto-exposure and would gate Purkinje. Changing that is now two authored vectors, not a code
change — which is the whole point of the mechanism.

**Two doc corrections found on the way, both verified**: AGENTS.md's `beach` entry names three
arms and there are four, and its tree determinism note cites `tree.c:704`, `:1029` and `:1209`
where `glfwGetTime()` is now at **`:749`** and `create_mouse_drag_controller` at **`:1200`**.

# AS BUILT

Five commits. Both halves shipped as planned; four things came out differently and each is
worth more than the plan that missed it.

## The recalibration was free, where the plan budgeted for it

The plan's risk table listed six arms expected to need new bars, with `beach-shoal` at 1.27x
against a 1.15 bar called "the tightest margin anywhere". **No bar was re-placed.** Dividing
each fixture's authored value by its own scene's daylight incident reproduced daylight to
within **one 8-bit code** — both water goldens moved at PAE exactly 1/255, RMSE 0.001, and the
35 water and beach arms read within 1% (`water-absorb` 1.48/1.52 against 1.49/1.52,
`beach-shoal` still 1.27x, `water-under-fog` 1.45x unchanged).

The reason is structural rather than lucky: the old value was the PRODUCT of an albedo and a
light, so dividing the light back out is exact. A migration by division, not by taste. The
plan should have predicted this and did not.

## `water-night-lit` could not land in phase 3, and the fixture is why

The plan put it with half A. It cannot go there: the in-scatter is still a constant at that
point, so a moonlit sea is unlit no matter which light is picked. Measured 0.23850 with a moon
against 0.23848 without — one part in 10,000. It moved to phase 4.

Worse, the arm cannot be a brightness read at all. **`water_fixture`'s geometry is EMISSIVE
over a black base** (bed 0.55 grey, ramp 0.42/0.34/0.22), which is exactly what makes it a good
absorption instrument — a backdrop that cannot vary with the light — and exactly what makes it
structurally unable to show night: its water band reads 0.2207 at midnight against 0.2478 at
noon whatever the sea does, because most of that is self-lit sand seen through it. Found by
bisecting the shader after the frame refused to darken; `bed` read 0.444 at both hours.

Every night arm is therefore a twin DELTA. `water-night-lit` reads the in-scatter's own
contribution against a zero-scatter twin: **0.0163 day, 0.0003 moonlit, 0.0000 dark, 62.5x**.
Restoring the constant reads 0.0037 / 0.0035 / 0.0035 and 1.1x.

## Two fields, where the plan assumed one would do

The plan's own "consequence worth stating plainly" was right and understated it. Reproducing
tree's look with `albedo x incident + glow` does not merely force `albedo ~ 0` — at a 0.8
degree sun an albedo of ANY size renders a near-black sea, so the split is not a convenience,
it is the only way both scenes are expressible. `scatterGlow` is what lets `apps/tree` opt into
a stylised sea in one line instead of inheriting it from a constant nobody chose.

## Seven arms, not six, and one exists because nothing else could see the rank

`water-night-weight` was not in the plan. Every real directional in the corpus is near-white,
so `intensity x peak` and `intensity` agree on all of them and the peak factor was
unfalsifiable — the plan named that mutation and had no arm for it. It takes an authored decoy
(intensity 2.0 at colour 0.04) to separate the two rules; ranking by raw intensity fails that
arm alone and no other.

`water-fixture-roundtrip` also gained a third key category. Its source reader cannot tell a
refusal from an accessor — both are `(water, "name")` — so a refused key drifted the `known[]`
and fixture assertions at once. `_cscn_refused_keys` reads them off the user-visible message,
which makes that sentence load-bearing, and the arm now asserts a refused key is in NEITHER
list. Listing `scatter` in `known[]` to silence the duplicate warning was tried and reverted:
that list means what parse_water READS.

## Falsification

Every claim was mutated and measured, not argued.

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| by-name pick restored | 3 of 4 phase-3 arms fail; ladder reads `sky_sun@0.000` at all four dark rungs, glitter exactly 0 px, caustics dark == moonlit == 19,660 px (the moon never reached) |
| rank by `intensity` alone | `water-night-weight` alone fails |
| in-scatter back to a constant | `water-night-lit` alone fails, 1.1x |
| volume lags the surface | `water-under-fog` fails at 1.14x against its 1.25 bar |

## Left alone, deliberately

**The env cube floors well above the sky.** An IBL-lit surface at −40 degrees reads the same as
at −12 (ramp 0.264 both), while the background correctly goes black. Found while chasing the
night sea; it affects every surface rather than water, so it is out of scope here and is the
reason a night frame in this engine still reads brighter than its sky. Worth its own item.

**`--water-probe` gained `incident`**, which is the term this spec added and the one nothing
else can report — a sea lit by the wrong amount still looks like a sea.
