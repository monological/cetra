# Spec 11.33 — closing every gap 11.32 left open

## Context

Spec 11.32 shipped water as a scene-owned subsystem: its own pass slot, Gerstner and
FFT wave models, Beer-Lambert absorption, a bed provider, caustics, and three apps
using it. It also left a list of deferrals, and a review round found four claims in
it that were false.

**Some of those deferrals do not survive scrutiny.** The largest — the clipmap — was
deferred on the grounds that an instanced annulus has a snap-mismatch overlap between
levels that becomes a coplanar depth tie. That is only true if each level snaps to
its own cell size. Snapping every level to the **coarsest** cell size makes all ring
boundaries land on shared grid lines and the rings tile exactly, which is what
geometry clipmaps have always done — and the roadmap cites the paper
(Asirvatham & Hoppe, GPU Gems 2) two lines from the D3 entry 11.32 was built from.
The obstacle was arithmetic I had not done, presented as a property of the technique.

Phase 3 of 11.32 then established the clipmap as the *binding* constraint rather than
a nicety: a uniform world-space grid puts samples where the world is, not where the
pixels are, so `--water-extent 500` has an 8-unit cell in the near field and the
cascades are invisible there. Phase 6 hit the same wall again as an authoring
constraint in `apps/tree` (swell length traded against crest faceting). Three phases
pointing at one missing piece is the signal to build it.

**Intended outcome:** every item on 11.32's deferred and known-wrong lists is either
built or, for the two that are genuinely separate features, reduced to a stated
non-goal. No item is left resting on a justification that does not hold.

## What this spec does NOT do, and why that is different from 11.32's list

Two items stay out, and neither is a gap in *water*:

- **The nearshore shallow-water solver, breakers and swash.** A different simulation,
  not a missing piece of this one. The reference's own breaker is scripted
  (`time * 2.4` modulo 72 m with a fade to hide the reset) and its research notes say
  a plunging lip needs a representation neither it nor this engine has.
- **Jolt buoyancy.** Game-layer physics. `water_height_at` (below) is the seam it
  would consume, and building the seam is in scope; wiring rigid bodies to it is not.

Everything else on 11.32's list is in this spec.

## Phases

Each ends at a measurement. Ordered so the two that change the picture come first.

### Phase 1 — Clipmap rings, stitched exactly

**Geometry.** Two index buffers over one vertex buffer (`WATER_GRID_RES`² grid over
[-0.5, 0.5]²): the full grid for the centre patch, and an **annulus** (the same grid
minus the inner quarter, |g| < 0.25) for the rings. Level 0 draws the full grid;
levels 1..N-1 are one instanced draw of the annulus. Level L's half-extent is
`base * 2^L`, and its inner hole is exactly `base * 2^(L-1)` — level L-1's outer
extent — so the hole matches by construction rather than by tuning.

**Fixed-snap stitching, which is the whole point.** Every level snaps its origin to
the **coarsest** level's cell size, not its own:

```
coarsestCell = base * exp2(levels - 1) * 2.0 / gridRes
snapped      = floor(camXZ / coarsestCell) * coarsestCell
```

Level L's own cell divides `coarsestCell` exactly (`2^(levels-1-L)`), and each
half-extent is an integer multiple of it (`gridRes / 2^(levels-L)`), so every ring
boundary lands on the shared snap grid: no gap, no overlap, no coplanar tie. The cost
is that the finest ring translates in coarse steps, which on a periodic surface is
invisible.

**T-junctions, handled exactly rather than skirted.** Levels meeting at a boundary
have 2:1 vertex density, so the finer side has a vertex at the midpoint of each
coarser edge and its true surface height differs from the coarser edge's linear
interpolation — a crack that shows sky at grazing angles. Rather than hide it under a
skirt, the finer side is made to *agree*: a vertex on a patch's outer edge whose index
along that edge is odd evaluates the surface at its two even neighbours and averages
them, which is by definition what the coarser edge draws there. Two extra evaluations
on one row of vertices per patch.

**Arms:** `water-draws` — exact submission integers (2 draws, `levels - 1` instances,
exact triangle count). This needs `water_render` to fill the counters via
`profiler_submit` (`profiler.h:75`), which also gives water the SUBMISSION row it has
never had and closes 11.32's "no integer to assert on" note. `water-crack` — render at
a grazing camera and assert no sky-coloured pixels inside the water body, which is
what a T-junction crack looks like.

**Bar:** the FFT ocean resolves near-field detail at `--water-extent 500`, where 11.32
could not. Measured as: short-cascade slope variance in a near-field box, uniform grid
vs clipmap.

### Phase 2 — The underwater medium

`water_publish_to_postfx` (mirroring `sky_publish_to_postfx`, `sky.c:317-340`) flattens
level, absorption, scatter and a submerged flag into `PostFX`.
`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:181` becomes a two-medium select: above the plane the existing
height-exponential air, below it the water's extinction and in-scatter.

**The default-off fog volume is the trap here.** Underwater must not silently depend on
`--fog`, so water being active *and* the camera submerged arms the froxel volume for
that frame; and aerial perspective is suppressed under the surface, because the
sky-view integral it holds is air the sight line never crosses.

**Arms:** `water-under-fog` — a submerged frame's seabed loses contrast with distance,
which it does not today; `water-under-off` — above-water frames are 0 px against
Phase 1, so arming the volume cannot leak upward.

### Phase 3 — Soft shoreline coverage

Coverage from `fwidth` on the water column, written to alpha, with
`GL_SAMPLE_ALPHA_TO_COVERAGE` for the water draw. Reuses the engine's existing
`a2c_capable` predicate and its documented fallback (`render.c:305-313`): at one
sample A2C is a no-op, so the binary cutoff stands there rather than keeping every
fragment. Apple takes coverage from the LAST colour output's alpha
(`material.h:20-22`), so coverage goes in the highest-numbered output water writes.

**Arm:** `water-shore-soft` — the waterline's transition width in pixels under 4x MSAA
exceeds the 1-sample width, measured on a scanline crossing it.

### Phase 4 — The known-wrong list, all four

- **FFT motion vectors.** Retain the previous frame's `field0` for the long and medium
  cascades — the only two the vertex stage displaces with — as two textures blitted
  from the completed transform at the end of the spectral work. The previous position
  then re-evaluates properly and TAA stops reprojecting travelling waves as static.
  Two samplers, and they sit on units 9-10 as `GL_TEXTURE_2D` where the Charlie
  cubemap and the shadow array live: a different binding point on the same unit, which
  is the reasoning 11.32's review validated for units 7 and 8 and which gets written
  down rather than assumed.
- **Shoal gradient.** The bed bake gains its gradient in G,B (central differences,
  CPU, at bake time), so `oceanShoal` returns the factor *and* its gradient and the
  product-rule term `h * dShoal` enters the derivatives. Inert on a flat bed; the surf
  zone in `apps/forest --water` is where it was wrong.
- **Cascade window overlap.** Closed to abut exactly (0.30/0.30, 1.22/1.22) so no mode
  is seeded twice. This changes the sea state, so it is **measured and reported**, not
  edited quietly: pixel delta on the FFT fixture, with the golden re-baked and the
  movement recorded here.
- **Per-texel SSR roughness.** Water already writes effective roughness to aux `.w`.
  `ssr_frag` reads `floorRoughness` from there instead of the scalar uniform, falling
  back to the uniform where aux carries the sky sentinel — which is the catcher, since
  it writes no aux. Both surfaces keep working and water stops being described by one
  number.

### Phase 5 — `.cscn` authoring, and the CPU wave query

A `water{}` block: `enabled`, `level`, `extent`, `waves`, `wavelength`, `amplitude`,
`steepness`, `spread`, `roughness`, `absorption`, `scatter`, `caustics`, each with its
`has_*` flag, parsed in `cscene.c` beside the `fog` block (`:321-332`) and merged in
`cscene_apply.c` under the CLI-wins precedence. The water fixture's `.cscn` then carries
its own settings and the gate stops passing them on the command line.

`water_height_at(const Water*, float x, float z, float t)` — the CPU evaluation of the
Gerstner sum, the seam buoyancy and gameplay queries consume. Promoted to
`procedural/water_waves.c` at that point, per 11.32's placement note, since it gains a
second consumer.

### Phase 6 — The test-coverage gap

`--water-bed <none|dome>` on the render app installs an analytic dome provider sized to
the water extent, so a deterministic fixture finally exercises Tier 3.

**Arms:** `water-shoal` — displacement variance falls toward the shore, which is dead
in every 11.32 gate frame; `water-shore-foam` — the foam band exists and sits inside
the shoal window, not at the extremes.

# As built

## Phase 1 — clipmap rings (commits `8d04658`, `a7012f3`, `accd82f`)

**The fixed-snap argument survives contact with the depth buffer.** Five nested rings,
a grazing pass across all of them, no seam and no sky through the surface — which is
the specific failure 11.32 predicted and used as its reason to defer. Landed as
described: two index sets in one buffer, centre draw plus one instanced ring draw,
T-junction vertices averaging their two even neighbours. One detail the plan did not
state and the code has to: the averaged vertex keeps its **own** normal. Averaging that
too flattens the shading along every boundary into exactly the seam the position fix
removes.

**The submission counters landed with it**, so water has a SUBMISSION row for the first
time and `water-draws` asserts 2 / 5 / 131,072 as integers.

**The bar was met in a different form than the plan named.** The plan asked for
short-cascade slope variance in a near-field box, uniform grid against clipmap. What
was actually measured is stronger and came for free: at `--water-extent 500` the near
field now carries whitecaps, and it carries them because `water-crack`'s first version
went off measuring them. Foam is Jacobian-selected, so foam in a near-field box IS
resolved near-field slope — the thing the variance measurement was a proxy for.

**`water-crack` was blind on its first try, and the way it failed is worth keeping.**
It read R/B, on the reasoning that a hole shows bare bed. Bare bed reads R/B ~1.0 and
whitewater reads 0.92, so the ratio cannot separate them: it flagged 4,705 of 16,128
pixels on a frame with no crack in it. Brightness does separate them, and the band
between the two populations is empty (brightest foam 0.3702 linear, bare bed 0.5049),
so the arm reads per-pixel luma with a ceiling at 0.44. Two things it still cannot do,
recorded rather than papered over: it cannot tell a hole from a very bright specular
hit, and a sub-pixel crack that never fully clears the surface slips under it. Deciding
either properly needs the depth buffer, which this harness cannot read.

**The golden moved 224,560 px (46.8%) and was re-baked.** Expected: the surface went
from one uniform grid to five nested rings, so different vertices sample the
displacement. Every other golden 0 px.

**`apps/tree` confirmed the authoring constraint was the grid.** Its swell length went
back from 150 to 45 once the rings landed, which is the trade 11.32 phase 6 recorded
having to make.

## Phase 2 — the underwater medium (commits `4d36c05`, `62c62d2`)

Landed as described, including the arming: water active *and* the camera submerged arms
the froxel volume for that frame, so underwater does not silently depend on `--fog`,
and aerial perspective is dropped for the same frame.

**Two approximations the plan did not anticipate, both forced by what a froxel cell can
hold.** A cell stores one *scalar* extinction in `.a` and the integrate pass multiplies
one scalar transmittance, so water's per-channel extinction arrives as its luminance
mean with the colour moved into the in-scatter: distance fades the seabed toward the
body colour rather than reddening out of it along the way. The exact per-channel
Beer-Lambert still runs in the surface shader, which is the path that carries the look
from above. And the in-scatter is a constant rather than scattered sunlight, so there
are no shafts in the body.

**`water-under-off` was not added, because the golden suite already is that
instrument.** The plan wanted an arm asserting above-water frames are 0 px against
phase 1. The water golden *is* an above-water frame and it stayed 0 px, along with the
other 22 — and unlike an arm, that is a cross-build claim. An arm would have been a
weaker duplicate.

**`water-submerged`'s boxes had to move.** They sat in the open-water band, which the
new medium saturates: all three went flat at 0.053, because with the body's own
extinction applied the far field is entirely body colour. The signal moved to the
interface underside, so the boxes moved with it (0.2392 / 0.1234 / 0.0695).

## Phase 3 — shoreline coverage (commits `77f84f9`, `43b8515`)

Built as described — signed column, `fwidth`, `GL_SAMPLE_ALPHA_TO_COVERAGE` on the
water draw, the engine's own `a2c_capable` conditions, cutoff at one sample. Three
things the plan had wrong.

**1. "Apple takes coverage from the LAST colour output's alpha, so coverage goes in the
highest-numbered output water writes" is wrong twice over.** The rule as the codebase
actually records it (`pbr_frag.glsl:40-46`) is the last output carrying an alpha
*channel*, and water's highest output is attachment 7, which is `R11F_G11F_B10F` and
has none. Worse, every alpha-bearing output between attachment 1 and 4 already carries
a value a consumer reads — aux roughness, albedo metalness, SSS profile — so the plan's
instruction, followed literally, would have written coverage into one of those.

Coverage goes into FragColor's alpha *and* the normals' alpha, the only two water does
not owe to a consumer, and that rests on the driver not reaching for the highest alpha
it can find. **Measured, not assumed:** albedo binds under `--ssgi` with a 0 in its
alpha, above both of those, and the shoreline still moves by the same ~4.4k px it moves
without it. Under the plan's stated rule, zero coverage from there would have erased
the surface. The one case still unreachable is attachment 4 — it binds only on a scene
carrying a subsurface material, and none of them carry water, which is the same gap
`pbr_frag.glsl:1997-2003` already predicts and cannot test.

**2. The headroom was much smaller than 11.32's deferred note implied, and the note's
diagnosis was wrong.** It called the shoreline "a hard threshold, so this edge
aliases". The edge is also a **depth crossing** — the water surface passes behind the
bed there — and at 4x MSAA the per-sample depth test antialiases a depth crossing on
its own. What coverage adds is only the sliver between the crossing and the
`WATER_MIN_PATH` threshold, which is sub-pixel on a grazing view. Measured: 4,352 px at
peak 44/255, and the sharpest single-row fall across the waterline drops to 0.823x. A
real softening, and a small one. The case with actual headroom is the opposite framing
— a near-overhead view of a gently shoaling bed, where the threshold band spans several
pixels — and no fixture in the corpus is framed that way.

**3. The arm as specified was measurable but confounded.** "4x MSAA transition width
exceeds the 1-sample width" compares two frames that differ in AA mode *and* in TAA, so
the waterline is not the only thing that moved between them; measured that way it read
1.61 against 1.28 edge-spread, a signal thin enough to be arguing with its own
confound. `--no-water-coverage` was added instead — one flag apart at a fixed sample
count — which is also the bisect lever a golden-moving change owes anyone comparing
against an older baseline, the same role `--no-dither` and `--no-oit-moments` play.

Two arms rather than one. `water-shore-soft` asserts the frame moves **and** that the
sharpest fall gets shallower, because liveness alone would pass on a coverage value
that came out constant. `water-shore-hard` is the cheaper and more decisive one: at one
sample the flag is 0 px, so the fallback really is a fallback.

**The golden moved 4,352 px and was re-baked.** Every other golden 0 px.

## Phase 4 — the known-wrong list (commits `6f70165`, `0e4f50b`, `88f1d4d`, `ca0164b`,
`bfb94e2`)

Three of the four landed, though one of those needed a second fix underneath it that the
plan did not know was there. The fourth is WITHDRAWN on a false premise.

### FFT motion vectors — built (`6f70165`)

Landed as described, and the defect was worse than the plan claimed: `--motion-blur`
moved **exactly 0 px** on the spectral surface with a static camera, so the waves
reported no motion whatsoever. After, 116,796 px, against 77,150 for the Gerstner path
that always had it. That comparison is the instrument, and it works because a static
camera makes camera velocity zero: motion blur cannot move a pixel whose velocity is
not the wave's own.

**The plan wanted a third buffer and it only needed a copy.** The 14 FFT stages land
back in buffer 0, so buffer 0 still holds last frame's completed transform until the
evolve overwrites it — one copy at that moment retains the previous surface for two
128² textures. A third ping-pong buffer would have introduced a parity the surface
shader has to know about, which is state in the wrong place.

Target 0 only, and two cascades only: target 0 carries the displacement and the height,
which is the whole position, where target 1's slopes belong to a normal and nothing
reads a previous normal; and only the long and medium bands reach the mesh.

**One thing the plan did not anticipate:** the position arithmetic had to be factored
out (`oceanSpectralPosition`) so the current and previous evaluations cannot drift. The
bound-harmonic crest term lived in one expression and would have had to live in two.
Its coefficients got names in the process, which also removed a hand-doubled 0.28/0.64
in the slope that 11.32's review round had already had to catch once.

### Shoal gradient — built (`0e4f50b`)

Landed, on BOTH wave models rather than only the spectral one the plan named — the
Gerstner path had the same omission and `apps/tree` runs it over a real bed.

**A second defect found while building it, which the plan did not list.** The
horizontal map's Jacobian was written as `(1 + dx)(1 + dy) - cross²`, which is only
correct while the two off-diagonals are equal, which is only true while the shoal
gradient is zero. That difference *is* the shoaling compression, so foam selection was
structurally blind to shoaling, not merely inaccurate.

Measured over the dome bed: 15,944 px spectral, 13,668 px Gerstner, against a 0 px
floor. Over a flat bed the term is exactly zero, and the fixture confirms it: 37 px at
PAE 1/255 — last-bit rounding from reassociating the Gerstner sum, re-baked.

### Per-texel SSR roughness — WITHDRAWN. Water is not SSR-traced, and the fallback the
plan specified cannot exist.

Three findings, in increasing order of how much they matter:

1. **Water never reaches SSR.** The pass shades exactly one surface, gated on the sign
   of the normals-buffer alpha (`ssr_frag.glsl:159-163`). Water writes non-negative
   alpha deliberately — 11.32's phase 1b decided against the marker because that
   channel's magnitude is already the catcher's edge falloff. Measured: `--no-ssr`
   against the default on the FFT fixture is **0 px**. SSR contributes nothing to a
   water frame.
2. **Water was never described by one roughness number.** Its reflection is computed in
   `water_frag`, not by SSR, and already selects the environment lobe's mip from the
   per-texel value. The scalar `floorRoughness` belongs to the catcher.
3. **As written it would BREAK the catcher.** The plan assumed the catcher writes no
   aux. It writes `AuxOut = vec4(0.0)` (`catcher_frag.glsl:82`), so reading roughness
   per-texel would make the floor a perfect mirror instead of using its authored
   roughness — and no guard can rescue it, because 0.0 is a legitimate roughness
   indistinguishable from an authored mirror. There is no sky sentinel to test.

**The successor, for whoever wants water reflections.** Not a new G-buffer channel:
the attachment table already spans slots 0-7 with 5 and 6 held by the OIT accumulate
and the absorbance moments, and the normals alpha is the channel that is already
carrying two things. Water has its own program and already binds the mipped opaque
resolve (unit 6) and the resolved depth (unit 7) for refraction — a reflection is the
same two textures with the reflected ray, marched rather than single-tapped, entirely
inside `water_frag`. And note that SSR is not the standard technique here at all:
planar reflection is, because water is a plane to first order and SSR's dominant
failure — the reflected ray leaving the screen at a grazing sight line — is water's
dominant case. Its own spec, either way.

### Cascade window overlap — built (`ca0164b`), and it could not be built alone

The windows now abut (0.30/0.30, 1.22/1.22), so no mode is seeded twice. Closing them
as the plan described was not possible, and why is most of what this item turned out to
be about.

**The Gaussian phase draw sat inside the window test**, which ties the RNG's position
to how many modes passed the cutoffs. Moving a cutoff by 0.06 rad/m therefore
re-randomised every phase after it, and the edit arrived as a different ocean rather
than as the same one minus a strip. Measured at extent 70 against a 0 px floor:

| change | delta | RMSE |
|---|---|---|
| close the windows, phase draw left coupled | 375,491 px (78%) | 0.135 |
| close the windows on a decoupled draw | 132,871 px (28%) | 0.045 |

So the overlap's own energy is worth about a quarter of the frame and the rest of that
78% was phase resequencing. Reporting the 78% as "the sea state change" — which is what
the plan's instruction to measure and report would have produced — would have been
wrong by a factor of three.

**The fix is per-mode seeding:** each mode's RNG state comes from its own grid index,
mixed and avalanched so neighbouring wavenumbers do not receive near-identical phases.
A cutoff is a cutoff again, permanently, and the sea also stops depending on traversal
order. Final delta 340,376 px, floor 0 px, still 0 px twice.

**One unavoidable re-roll, and it was checked rather than assumed.** The realisation it
lands on has a long-band RMS of 0.03025 against the shipped 0.02901 — within 4%. That
check exists because a *sequential* decoupling tried first landed 23% high (peak mode
0.185 → 0.321), and that folded the horizontal map and saturated whitecaps across the
near field: it read as broken, not as rougher. **The shipped sea state sits closer to
its own folding limit than 11.32 knew**, so a re-roll is not automatically safe and
anything that re-rolls phases again should measure the band RMS before trusting the
frame.

**No golden moves** — every water golden runs Gerstner, which has no cascades. Two gate
arms did have to be repaired, and one of them for a reason worth carrying (see below).

### What the sea-state change broke in the gates, and what that says about proxies

`water-crack` failed, and it failed by the sea being **rougher**, not by cracking. Its
discriminator was a luma ceiling resting on a measured empty band: foam peaked at 0.3702
and bare bed read 0.5049, so a ceiling at 0.44 separated a hole from whitewater. More
whitecaps put foam at 0.6701 and the two populations crossed. That is the *second*
proxy this arm has lost — the first was R/B, which could not tell near-neutral foam from
near-neutral bed at all.

It is now written as the definition rather than a correlate: **a crack is a hole, so it
shows the background exactly**, because no water fragment was written there. The arm
takes the per-pixel distance to the same framing with no water in it and asserts a floor
under it. Absorption, foam, specular and refraction all move a pixel *away* from the
background, so none of them can cross this the way brightness did. Measured 0.133 on a
crack-free frame against a floor of 0.04.

`water-submerged`'s boxes moved for the second time (phase 2's medium saturated the band
they started in; this reshaped the interface and flattened the ramp to 1.69x). The
comment now records why they will move again: they are fixed screen positions reading a
ratio off a surface whose shape is one random realisation. The ordering assertion is the
durable half of that arm and the endpoint ratio is not.

## The review round (commits `12fb8eb`, `0ee9564`)

Eight review agents over the branch — four structural, four on reuse / simplification /
efficiency / altitude. **Six of what they found were defects, and not one was visible in a
rendered frame.** That is the lesson of the round: every gate arm and all 23 goldens
passed before it, because a photometric suite cannot see a flag that does nothing, a flag
that latches, or two code paths given different numbers.

### The defects

1. **`fog_enabled` latched permanently.** Phase 2 armed the froxel volume for a submerged
   camera by setting that flag — which belongs to the app and to a GUI checkbox and is
   never cleared per frame. One frame with the eye under the surface turned volumetric fog
   on for the rest of the session. Water now publishes `water_medium` as a REQUEST the
   pass gate ORs, and aerial suppression as a request the composite ANDs, which also stops
   publish call order being load-bearing.
2. **`--no-water` was defeated by any of its seven siblings.** They all set `args.water`,
   so the surface was freed and recreated one stage later. The sequence of stages *was*
   the precedence rule; it is one decision and one override pass now.
3. **The negative flags turned water ON.** `--no-water-caustics`,
   `--no-water-coverage` and `--water-bed none` each implied `--water`.
4. **`--water-waves gerstner` could not override an authored `"waves": "fft"`** — a bool
   only ever writes its true half.
5. **The catcher stepped aside for water that never drew.** The draw gates on the render
   mode and the capture flag; the suppression and the fog publish gated on `water_active`
   alone, so any debug mode or probe capture lost the ground plane. `water_will_draw` is
   the one predicate now.
6. **The CPU wave query read `steepness` raw.** The clamp was applied on the way to the
   uniform, so a `.cscn` authoring 3.0 gave the GPU 1.0 and the CPU 3.0 — the two
   surfaces disagreeing, which is the single failure that module exists to prevent. The
   comment also cited "water.h's steepness clamp", which was never there.

Two more of the same kind: the GUI re-baked the bed from the LEVEL slider, which the bake
does not read (65,536 callbacks and a 1 MB upload per dragged frame, on `apps/forest` a
multi-octave noise field), while nothing re-baked on an extent change, which it does; and
the froxel inject computed a full shadowed light accumulation for every submerged cell and
then overwrote it.

**And the inverse solver's convergence claim did not hold.** Differentiating the map shows
the contraction modulus is bounded by the steepness itself, so at the authored maximum of
1 there is no contraction and three fixed steps would have returned a plausible point that
is not the one over the query. It iterates to a tolerance now, and the last step size IS
the exact residual — so convergence is measured in every configuration rather than argued
for one. Measured residual improved from 0.00146 to 0.00012, and
`water_waves_inverse_residual` stopped repeating the whole solve.

### What the duplication round found, and the one that mattered most

`oceanSpectralPosition` was factored out in phase 4 *specifically* so the current and
previous frame could not drift — and then grew a second copy of `horizontal` and `height`
twenty-five lines below itself, with a comment admitting it. Both wave models now end in
one `oceanAssemble`, because the two epilogues were algebraically identical: the identity
rows, the shoal product rule, the cross order and the determinant each existed twice.

Also: the band Jacobian written three more times, the cascade UV eleven times, the shoal
factor fetched twice per vertex, `screenVelocity` and `fresnelSchlickRoughness` copied
byte-for-byte out of `pbr_frag` (the velocity one being the attachment-2 contract), a
`create_post_program` bypass justified by a claim `dof_gather_frag` disproves, a hand-rolled
texture upload that `create_texture_2d_float` already is, and `WATER_CASCADE_LENGTH` — a
second copy of a column of the cascade table five lines above it, published on the
reasoning that a second copy "would be a silent mismatch rather than an error".

### Deliberately not done, so nobody re-derives it

- **A table for the 15 `.cscn` water fields.** Two reviews disagreed; the argument against
  won. A `MATERIAL_PARAMS`-style table would have to bridge TWO structs across four value
  types plus a string→enum, turning 15 compiler-checked assignments into unchecked
  offsets. `MATERIAL_PARAMS` earns that shape by having two consumers over one struct.
- **A shared GL save/restore helper.** No such helper exists and all eleven pre-existing
  subsystems hand-roll the identical shape. Water is the twelfth conforming copy, and
  `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md` already owns the extraction.
- **Six cascade samplers as one `sampler2DArray`** (units 10 → 5, deleting the name loop
  and most of `water.h`'s unit-collision prose), and **packing the three cascades into one
  384x128 atlas** to take the spectral path from 45 passes to 15. Both are real wins,
  both cross-file and golden-moving, and both want their own spec.
- **A third ping-pong buffer** to replace the `cascade_prev` copy. Reviews disagreed and
  the concrete analysis won: 256 KB/frame, no CPU sync, and rotating handles would need
  four `glFramebufferTexture2D` re-attachments plus FBO revalidation per frame.
- **One finding was wrong.** It argued `water_frag`'s comment should cite `SpecOut` as
  stronger evidence than the `--ssgi` albedo test. Attachment 7 is `R11F_G11F_B10F` and
  has no alpha channel at all, which is exactly why the driver skips it — it cannot
  disprove a rule about the highest *alpha-bearing* output.

**Verified after: all gates pass, all 23 goldens 0 px.** The golden count is the load-
bearing half, since the batch moved `screenVelocity` and `fresnelSchlickRoughness` out of
`pbr_frag`, which every PBR surface in the corpus goes through.

## Phase 5 — `.cscn` authoring and the CPU wave query (commits `1ceb05a`, `9015a5c`)

Both landed. Three corrections to the plan.

**1. The field list was incomplete.** The plan named twelve keys; `Water` carries
fifteen authorable properties. It omitted `ior` and `wind_dir`, and `shore_coverage` did
not exist when it was written (phase 3 added it). All fifteen are authorable now, and
the fixture authors all fifteen at their default values — which makes it the block's
worked example and puts a frame behind every parse path.

**2. The block is top-level, not under `post`.** The plan said to parse it "beside the
`fog` block". Fog is a field on `PostFX`; water is owned by the `Scene`. Authoring it
under `post` would file a scene-graph citizen in the post chain's namespace, so it sits
beside `wind` and `dust`, which are the other two scene subsystems with authored blocks.

**3. `--no-water` had to be added to the render app.** The plan did not anticipate it,
and CLI-wins precedence does not work without it: once a scene file is what asks for the
surface, *omitting* a flag no longer turns it off. Three gate arms and the profiler-row
arm depended on exactly that, and would have silently started comparing a frame with
itself — which is the failure `water-live` already recorded once in 11.32.

**The CPU query inverts the horizontal map, which the plan did not mention and which is
the whole difficulty.** A Gerstner wave displaces its points sideways, so the wave
parameter that lands on a queried WORLD position is not that position. Taking it as one
is wrong by the horizontal displacement — 0.14 m on the longest octave at the default
steepness, against a 0.06 m amplitude, so the error is more than twice the thing being
measured. Three fixed-point passes solve it, and fixed-point is enough because the map is
a contraction wherever it is injective, which is exactly what `water.h`'s steepness clamp
guarantees. Measured worst residual 0.00146 world units.

**It declines on the spectral model rather than answering.** An FFT cascade's
displacement exists only as a texture the transform just wrote, so a CPU answer means
running the transform on the CPU — a second implementation of the thing whose point is
that it runs on the GPU. Callers get the still level and `water_waves_available` says so,
which is what stops a buoyancy consumer from trusting a flat number.

**`--water-probe` exists because the query was otherwise untestable.** The GPU surface
leaves no number behind and the harness drives a binary, so without a printed diagnostic
this file would have shipped with nothing checking it. `water-cpu` reads it, and the
residual is the assertion that carries the arm: an unconverged inversion still returns a
point on the surface, just not the one over the query, so every other number the probe
prints looks correct either way.

**What is NOT verified: that the CPU answer equals the GPU's.** Both evaluate the same
sum from the same fields in the same order, and the constants are duplicated between
`ocean.glsl` and `water_waves.c` with a comment on each side saying so — but nothing
compares them. The bed is a second, smaller discrepancy: the shader samples a
`WATER_BED_RES` resample of `height_at` where the CPU calls it directly, so the two agree
to the bake's resolution rather than to the last bit. Closing either properly needs a
GPU readback the harness does not have.

## Phase 6 — the test-coverage gap (commits `4c1a293`, `88f1d4d`)

**Pulled forward, out of order, because phase 4 could not be measured without it.** The
shoal gradient is inert on every fixture in the corpus — none has a bed provider — so
`--water-bed dome` had to exist before that item could be shown to do anything. Worth
recording as a sequencing lesson: a correction to a path with no instrument is a
correction nobody can check, and the instrument is the prerequisite, not the follow-up.

`--water-bed <none|dome>` on the render app, sized off the water's own extent so it
lands inside the drawn surface at any `--water-extent`, and smoothstepped in radius so
the ramp has no crease — a bed with a kink in it would put a kink in the gradient the
bed exists to test. One frame carries open water, the whole shoaling ramp and a dry
crown, at 0 px twice.

**Both arms landed, and each needed a different framing than the plan gave it.**

`water-shoal` is two-sided rather than the one-sided "variance falls toward the shore"
the plan asked for. The within-frame ladder does fall — 0.084 / 0.050 / 0.015 from open
water to the shore — but the near box reads 0.015 with NO bed as well, so the ladder
alone proves nothing about shoaling. What discriminates is the same box across the flag:
roughness over the bed falls to 0.54x, and roughness BEYOND the bed does not move at all
(1.00x, and provably so — past the dome's radius the column is 9 units and the shoal
factor is exactly 1). The second half is the one that matters: a local bed that calmed
the whole sea would be a global amplitude knob wearing a bed's name, and the one-sided
arm would have passed it.

`water-shore-foam` runs GERSTNER, which is what isolates it. Crest foam is selected from
Jacobian compression and the Gerstner map's steepness is clamped so it cannot compress,
so on that path the shore band is the only whitewater in the frame. Measured 1,385 px in
the band, 0 at both extremes of the shoal window, 0 everywhere with no bed — which is
the plan's "sits inside the shoal window, not at the extremes" asserted directly.

**Its detector had to be both bright AND near-neutral**, and the first version taught
that the hard way twice over: brightness alone catches specular off calm water, and
neutrality alone catches the dry ramp — which is exactly the mistake `water-crack` made
in phase 1. A first attempt at this arm on the FFT path measured Jacobian whitecaps
instead of the shore band, and read them backwards, because shoaling REMOVES the
whitecaps in the same region the shore band appears in.
