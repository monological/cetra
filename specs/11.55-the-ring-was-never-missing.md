# 11.55 — The ring was never missing

## Context

`beach-shoreline` had been red for four specs and the roadmap's own entry closed with "nobody has
a working theory yet". It was booked as a known red rather than an ignored one, with the standing
instruction that a full-suite run reporting exactly one failure and it being this one is a PASS.

It read **16 of 18** azimuths when 11.44 wrote it, **9** after 11.45, **7** by the time 11.48
measured it on master, and **6** after that spec's P6. Each drop was noticed, attributed to
whichever water change had just landed, and left alone. 11.45 named the right experiment — "render
the ring, count whether foam still REACHES the waterline at the failing azimuths" — and nobody ran
it.

**The shore ring was intact at every one of those readings.** The arm could not see it, and the
reason had been sitting in the arm since the day it was written.

## Root cause

The arm walked a ray outward from the island at each of 18 azimuths and took the **brightest**
sample. That ray starts at r 1.0, which is on the **dry crown** — the fixture's sand cap, 0.6 units
above the still line.

Sunlit sand and whitewater are the same brightness. Measured at 800x600 on this build:

```
crown peak   580-591 luma
shore band   570-607 luma
```

So the statistic was competing two unrelated surfaces and being decided by a handful of codes out
of ~580. At **deg 0** the crown peaked at 586 and the window at 586; at **deg 320** both peaked at
591. Exact ties, broken by walk order — the crown comes first — because the comparison is a strict
`>`. Two of the eighteen booleans were decided by nothing at all.

That is what the reported 6 of 18 was. It was never a missing ring, and the band was visible in the
same rays the whole time: the window maximum rises **527 → 572** at deg 120 and **526 → 591** at deg
280 when the bed is installed.

**Clumping the foam did not remove the ring.** 11.45 broke a continuous collar into a web, which
removed the last few codes of margin the arm had been winning by. The arm degraded because its
margin was already inside the noise of a comparison it should never have been making.

### The correction had already been made — at the wrong end of the ray

11.44 bounded the search just past the window, and recorded why: searching on to r 16 put the ray
into open water where the sun's glitter lives, "so the arm was really asking *is the shore brighter
than the sun*".

That reasoning is right and it was applied to one end. Measured across the sweep on this build, the
bound is worth **one azimuth**:

```
brightness argmax, search to r  8.66:  6/18
                            r 10.16:  6/18
                            r 12.16:  5/18
                            r 38.16:  5/18   (unbounded)
```

The crown was never reachable by a bound, because it sits inside the window's **own inner half**.
`BEACH_RING_INNER` is −1.0 deliberately: the run-up climbs above the still line, so the window has
to open below the waterline. An arm that started its walk at the window edge instead reports a
**vacuous 18 of 18** — median argmax 4.81, which is sunlit sand just inside the edge.

So there was no bound that fixed this, and that is why four specs of looking at the renderer found
nothing.

## The fix

One statistic, in `_beach_ring_hits`: the argmax of **|with bed − without bed|** along the same ray,
with a magnitude floor at the existing `BEACH_DIFF_THRESH`.

It keeps 11.44's reason for choosing an argmax — a threshold is a second calibration needing its own
justification, an argmax needs none — and changes only the quantity it is taken over. The arm already
rendered both frames; nothing new is captured.

**Dry sand cannot win, structurally rather than by choosing where to look.** The bed does not touch
the crown, so the crown differences to nothing: 853 of 864 crown samples are bit-identical between
the two renders and the worst channel anywhere is 1 code, the output dither's own LSB. This is not a
new assumption — it is the standing assertion of `beach-surf-zone`, whose crown samples must read
exactly 0 moved, and that arm is green.

The magnitude floor carries the other half of the original claim, "without the bed there is no ring".
The old arm spent a second render and a second count on it (`hits_off <= BEACH_RING_MAX_OFF`, now
deleted); the floor says it directly, and a bed that changed nothing scores zero.

## Results

**16 of 18, against the bar of 13 — unmoved.** That is the number 11.44's own constant comment
records as the arm's design intent, recovered without re-deriving the bar from the reading that made
it green.

Falsified four ways rather than assumed:

| check | hits | |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | **16/18** | passes, want >=13 |
| bed changes nothing (frame against itself) | **0/18** | the floor rejects every azimuth |
| bed 2.56 units off the drawn mesh | **3/18** | fails |
| bed 1.0 unit off | 14/18 | passes — see the resolution note below |

And it measures **registration**, which is the claim only this fixture can make — the analytic bed
and the drawn dome are the same dome. Moving the sea level moves the mesh's waterline, and the peak
follows it:

```
level  0.0   mesh waterline 5.662   median peak 5.70   16/18
level -0.3   mesh waterline 7.033   median peak 7.75   16/18
level -0.6   mesh waterline 8.222   median peak 9.15   17/18
```

**Its resolution is about two units, not a fine one.** The window is 3.5 units wide, so a bed
disagreeing with the mesh by 1.0 still passes at 14/18. That matches what `BEACH_RING_INNER`'s
comment already claimed — "a bed disagreeing with the mesh by more than about a quarter of the
island's radius falls out of it" — and is stated here so nobody reads the arm as tighter than it is.

`BEACH_RING_MARGIN` stays and is still load-bearing under the new statistic, which was checked
rather than assumed: 16 of 18 at margins 0.5 and 1.0, 15 at 2.0, 14 at 4.0, and **11 from 8.0 out to
an unbounded search, which fails**. Out in open water the bed goes on changing the image and can
change it by more than the shore band does.

Beach group green: `beach-shoreline` 16/18, `beach-shoal` 1.0123 → 0.2500, `beach-surf-zone` 0.762
and 0 crown, `shore-twin` pass. Nothing outside that group is reachable from this diff — one helper
with a single caller, one constant used only by that caller, and documentation.

## Two things measured against a first answer that was wrong

Recorded because both were written into a comment before being checked, and both were wrong.

- **The two azimuths that still miss are not breaking whitewater.** They are the two the glitter
  crosses, as they were under the old statistic, but their peak is **negative** — −114 and −86 luma
  at r 8.85 and 8.55. The bed is not adding foam out there, it is *suppressing* a specular
  highlight, because shoaling changes the surface being reflected off. An unsigned argmax counts
  that as the bed's largest effect, and it is one.
- **The 9 → 7 drop has no recorded cause.** It was attributed here to 11.47 on the strength of that
  spec being about whitecaps. Neither 11.46 nor 11.47 records a count for this arm; 11.48 measured 7
  on master and that is the whole provenance.

## The transferable finding

**An argmax needs no threshold, and that is only true when nothing else in the search range can
win.** That is a property of the SCENE, not of the statistic — and it has to be re-checked whenever
either moves.

Here a second bright object was in range from the day the arm was written. The arm passed anyway, on
a margin of a few codes, and reported a healthy 16 of 18. Nothing in that green reading said the
margin was thin. Four specs of renderer changes were suspected before the statistic was, and the one
diagnostic that would have settled it in an afternoon — dump the radial profile and look at where
the peak is — was named in 11.45 and never run.

This is roadmap item 6's blind spot in a third location: a green arm reporting coverage it did not
have.

## Filed

- **No arm asserts the shore band's brightness directly**, only where the bed's effect peaks. A
  build that put the band in the right place at the wrong intensity passes everything here. The
  numbers to build it from are in this spec (window maximum 527 → 572 and 526 → 591 with the bed);
  it was not written because the right bar for "how bright is whitewater" is a judgement this spec
  had no basis to make.
- **`beach_fixture.cscn` still authors `"waves": "fft"` and no arm renders it that way.** Unchanged
  by this spec and still a trap for anyone opening the fixture by hand: it paints a blown-out white
  ring over the whole shoal that no gate covers. Booked in AGENTS.md, not here.
