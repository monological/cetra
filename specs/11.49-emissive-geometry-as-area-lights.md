# 11.49 — Emissive geometry becomes an LTC area light (roadmap C2)

## Context

An emissive mesh lights nothing today. It is bright in the frame, it feeds bloom, and its only
path into the lighting solution is a 16²-face DDGI capture that converges over seconds and
resolves no detail. Practicals, screens, strip lights and neon — the entire vocabulary of
interior lighting — are therefore decorative.

C2 is the last unbuilt entry in the roadmap's own five-item shortlist and the only one that
needs nothing from Wall 1. It is a **fit plus a registration**: plane-fit an emissive mesh's
dominant quad, convert its emissive to a radiance, and hand the result to the packed light array
A1 already uploads. `Light` (`light.h:33-86`) carries every field the fit produces — `position`,
`direction` (panel normal), `up`, `size`, and an `intensity` whose canonical unit for
`LIGHT_AREA` is already **nits**.

**This feature touches no shader.** `ltcPanel` (`include/ltc.glsl:122`) and the area branch in
`pbr_frag.glsl:1584-1649` already shade any panel that arrives through the cluster list. So a
scene with no emissive material is byte-identical by construction, and the off-gate is a CPU
early-out rather than a shader claim.

### The finding that shapes the verification

`assets/cornell_box.gltf` already contains this feature's ground truth, and the fixture says so
out loud — `gen_cornell_rooms.py:141` calls the emissive quad "the source" and notes "the .cscn
area light mirrors it".

| | emissive quad (`cornell_light`) | authored `.cscn` area light |
|---|---|---|
| centre | (0, **1.98**, 0) | `[0.0, 1.97, 0.0]` |
| normal | −Y | `[0, -1, 0]` |
| size | 0.7 × 0.7 | `[0.7, 0.7]` |
| colour | `emissiveFactor [1.0, 0.95, 0.88]` | `[1.0, 0.95, 0.88]` |
| radiance | ~**1.0** nit | `intensity` **18.0** |

**The geometry is exactly derivable, the centre is 1 cm out and the radiance is off by 18×.** That is not a bug in
either — the quad's emissive was authored for how the source *looks* and the light for how the
room *reads*, and nothing ever forced them to agree. It is the calibration this item has to
state rather than discover later, and it means "does the derived frame match the golden" is the
wrong acceptance test. The right one is an A/B where the only variable is **who created the
light**, with both sides at the same radiance.

### What P0 measured, and it moved the default

The probe shipped before anything shaded from a panel, which is the whole reason it exists. Its
first real run said the 18× was not the story.

**Emissive is overwhelmingly used as UNLIT FLAT COLOUR, not as a light.** glTF has no unlit
extension in play here, so the way to get a constant-colour surface is `emissiveFactor` set with
`baseColorFactor` black. Across the fixture corpus that is **30 of 32 emissive materials**:

| fixture | emissive materials | actual lamps |
|---|---|---|
| `oit_cards` | 13 | 0 |
| `glass` / `dof` / `flare` | 4 / 4 / 3 | 0 |
| `water` / `beach` / `absorption` / `fog_volume` | 2 / 2 / 1 / 1 | 0 |
| `cornell_box` / `cornell_leak` | 1 each | **1 each** |

So on-by-default does not move two goldens. It turns backdrops, bokeh cards and a 28×28 seabed
into area lights across most of the corpus, and it is **wrong every time** — `water_fixture`
alone carries 29 gate arms. **The default is therefore OFF**, behind `--emissive-lights`, which
also makes the off-gate free: with the flag absent no code runs at all.

This reverses an explicit earlier decision, on evidence that did not exist when it was made. The
material key survives the reversal and is still worth having, because a scene that *does* ask for
lamps still contains backdrops it does not want lit — `water_fixture --emissive-lights` derives
two spurious panels and the key is how a scene says so.

**The fit itself came out exact**, which is the other half of what P0 was for:

```
cornell_box   center=0,1.980000,0         normal=0,-1,0  size=0.700000,0.700000  planarity=1.000000
cornell_leak  center=-0.500000,1.980000,0 normal=0,-1,0  size=0.700000,0.700000  planarity=1.000000
cornell_point count=0
```

Exact centre, normal and size, and the minimum-area search earned its place: a square has an
isotropic covariance, so the principal-axis method would have bounded this panel at 45° and
returned 0.99 × 0.99 instead of 0.7 × 0.7.

**And a second fixture finding beside the 18×.** The quad sits at y **1.98** (`TOP - 0.02`, with
`TOP = 2.0`); the authored `.cscn` light is at **1.97**. `gen_cornell_rooms.py:141` says "the
.cscn area light mirrors it" and it is 1 cm off. Same class as the radiance gap: authored by eye,
described as derived, never checked.

### Corrected after review, before any code

Two things in the first draft were reasoned from the fit's side and were wrong. Both are kept
visible below rather than quietly replaced, because the second one is the more useful finding.

1. **Derived panels were going into a private array.** They are ordinary `scene->lights` entries.
   The private array made them invisible to `find_light_by_name`, the GUI, the light overlay, the
   shadow assignment and — decisively — to `light_overrides`.
2. **Shadows were going on the material.** They go on `light_overrides`, which already exists and
   is already per-lamp. Materials are deduped at import, so a material key is a per-lamp-*type*
   decision and cannot express "this one casts".

`specs/11.49-…` was committed with the first draft and needs amending to match.

## What ships

- Every emissive mesh that fits a rectangle gets an LTC panel, **behind `--emissive-lights`**,
  off by default. See the P0 measurement below — this was on-by-default until the probe ran.
- One new material key, `emissiveLight`, an INT enum with **two** states — the `windMode`
  precedent at `material.c:83-85`:

  | value | name | meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | `light` | derive a panel when the feature is on (default, and calloc-safe) |
  | 1 | `off` | emit visually, light nothing, even with the flag |

  Adding a row to `MATERIAL_PARAMS` (`material.c:41-85`) buys `.cscn` authoring **and** an
  ImGui control in one edit. Note this retires a stale claim in CLAUDE.md — that table already
  carries ~25 keys including `emissive` and `emissiveStrength`, not "only sss and windResponse".
- **Enum labels become authorable as strings**, so a fixture reads `"emissiveLight": "off"`
  rather than `"emissiveLight": 1`. Today a string in a material block is routed to
  `CSceneMaterialTexture` and `cscene_apply.c:480-486` rejects it as "not a texture key" — the
  key and the string are both already there, so this is one branch at that existing type check:
  an INT row with `enum_labels` matches the string against them first. `windMode` gets it too.
  `CSCENE_MAX_MATERIAL_TEXTURES` rises from 2, since a label string occupies a texture slot.
- `--emissive-light-probe`, which prints every derived panel and is what the gate reads.

## Design

### The fit runs in LOCAL space once, and is transformed per frame

The expensive half (a plane fit over every vertex) is gated on `scene_graph_epoch()`
(`draw_list.h:115`) — the existing primitive, bumped by exactly the mutations that would
invalidate a fit. The cheap half (three vector transforms through the node's
`global_transform`) runs every frame.

That is strictly better than the roadmap's sketch, which proposed a static fit at load and
excluding animated emitters: **a lamp on a moving node works, for free**, because the epoch does
not bump on a transform change and does not need to. It also settles where the fit hooks in.
Derived lights are *not* attached to a `SceneNode`, so `apply_transform_to_nodes`
(`scene.c:808-861`, which rotates `original_direction` → `direction` only for `node->light`)
never sees them — they get `global_position` / `direction` / `up` written directly in world
space.

### Where it hooks

`render.c:1041-1054`, between `engine_build_draw_list` and `light_cluster_build_and_upload`.
Transforms are current there (the app's `apply_transform_to_nodes` has already run inside its
render callback), the flattened `DrawList` is the walk to iterate, and it is immediately
upstream of the only consumer. Doing it in `scene_sync_materials` instead would read **last
frame's transforms** — the same latent bug the roadmap books under "shadows read last frame's
transforms".

### The fit itself

Per mesh, over triangles in local space:

1. **Area-weighted normal** `n = Σ nᵢAᵢ`, **area-weighted centroid** `c = Σ cᵢAᵢ / Σ Aᵢ`.
2. **Planarity** `|Σ nᵢAᵢ| / Σ Aᵢ` — exactly 1 for a flat quad, ~0 for a closed emissive box.
   One number that rejects everything a rectangle cannot describe. Below ~0.9, skip and log:
   a glowing cube and a curved neon tube are both out, which is the v1 limit the roadmap already
   states, now with a test behind it rather than an assumption.
3. **In-plane orientation by MINIMUM AREA**, searched over the mesh's own edge directions.
   *This step was planned as the 2×2 covariance principal axis and that is wrong* — a square
   panel has an isotropic covariance, so the axis it reports is arbitrary and a square bounded
   at 45° comes out √2 too wide in both directions. `cornell_light` is exactly square, so the
   corpus's only real lamp is the case it fails on. The minimum-area rectangle has a side flush
   with a convex-hull edge, so testing the mesh's edge directions finds it without building a
   hull; directions are deduplicated into 180 bins holding the first exact angle that lands in
   each, which bounds the cost at O(bins × vertices) whatever the triangle count.
4. **Extents** from the projected min/max along the winning axes, and the panel's centre is the
   MIDPOINT OF THE EXTENTS rather than the area-weighted centroid — a mesh whose triangles are
   denser at one end pulls the centroid there, and a rectangle centred on it would not bound the
   geometry it was fitted to.

About 250 lines, cglm only, no new dependency. The estimate here said 120; the minimum-area
search and its binning are the difference.

### Radiance → intensity

`render.c:154-161` already computes the emitted radiance: `emissive_hdr = emissive ×
emissive_strength`, uploaded as `emissiveFactor`, and `pbr_frag.glsl:2147` multiplies it by
`preExposure` — so **emissive is scene radiance, i.e. nits**, the same space `LIGHT_AREA`
intensity lives in. The conversion is therefore a split, not a guess:

```
intensity = 0.2126·R + 0.7152·G + 0.0722·B     // photometric luminance, real nits
color     = emissive_hdr / intensity           // unit-luminance tint
```

`color × intensity` reconstructs `emissive_hdr` exactly (`_pack_cluster_light` premultiplies at
`light_cluster.c:212`), and `intensity` reads as a number an author can check against a
datasheet — which is what 9.9/10.0 bought and what a `color = emissive_hdr, intensity = 1`
shortcut would throw away.

**Emissive textures**: `Texture` (`texture.h:13-31`) keeps no CPU pixels, but material textures
already get `glGenerateMipmap` (`texture.c:473, 544`), so the **1×1 top mip IS the mean colour**
— one `glGetTexImage` at the last level, once per material at fit time, never per frame. Without
it a mostly-black emissive atlas under a bright factor over-lights by the ratio, which is most
real screen and sign content.

### Storage and ownership — CORRECTED, and the correction is the interesting part

**An earlier draft of this plan put derived panels in a private `scene->emissive_lights[]`
array**, by value, to avoid needing a removal API on `scene->lights` (which has none). That was
reasoned from the fit's side and it is wrong, because it makes derived lights invisible to
everything that already knows how to talk about a light. The cost list is what settles it: a
second loop in `_gather_lights`, a second loop in `find_light_by_name`, no GUI row, no
`--show-lights` overlay, no shadow assignment, and — the one that actually bites — **no reach
from `light_overrides`**, which is the block that already exists for saying "this light casts".

**Derived panels are ordinary `Light` objects in `scene->lights`.** Then every one of those
paths works with no new code, and what it costs instead is a removal API and a reconcile, which
is the smaller and more honest half of the trade.

- **Identity**: `Light.emissive_source_id`, the source mesh's `id` (`mesh.h`: "Creation order …
  Assigned once and never reused") — the stable key the reconcile needs, and *not* a `Mesh*`,
  which is dangling by the time the epoch tells us the mesh is gone. 0 means "authored", so
  nothing the reconcile does can touch a hand-made light.
- **Name**: the owning node's name. `import.c:1626` names imported nodes from the file, and the
  cornell glTF names its node `cornell_light` (`gen_cornell_rooms.py:281`), so a derived panel is
  addressable the moment it exists.
- **Reconcile, not rebuild**: add for emissive meshes with no light, remove for lights whose
  mesh is gone, keep and re-place the rest. A surviving light is the *same object*, so anything
  set on it — an override included — persists across a graph change. Removal shifts rather than
  swaps, because `_gather_lights` documents its walk order as what makes packing deterministic.

Skip thresholds, each with a stated reason: planarity < 0.9 (not a rectangle), area ≤ 0 (nothing
to emit from), luminance < 1e-3 nits (cannot light anything, and would still cost a cluster
slot). Overflow past `LC_MAX_CLUSTER_LIGHTS` (128) is already logged by `light_cluster.c:258`.

### `remove_light_from_scene` — the missing half of an API, not an emissive-specific hook

`add_light_to_scene` (`scene.c:285-300`) has no counterpart. A light can go in and never come
out, which is why the first draft reached for a private array instead: it dodged writing this.
The dodge cost five integration points, so the function gets written.

```c
// Unlink and free. The Scene owns its lights (free_scene at scene.c:100), so an
// unlink-only form would leak by default, which is the wrong default for the one
// caller that removes anything at all.
int remove_light_from_scene(Scene* scene, Light* light);
```

**Order-preserving, and that is load-bearing rather than tidy.** `light_cluster.c:243` states
its walk order as what makes the packing — and so the shading loop order — deterministic, and
every 0-px golden rests on that. So removal SHIFTS the tail down; a swap-with-last would
reorder the light list on any graph change and move pixels for no reason anyone could find.

**The hazard, stated rather than defended against.** A `SceneNode` *borrows* a light and does
not free it, so removing one a node still points at leaves `node->light` dangling. This is the
mirror of the invariant the particle system already carries ("never free a `SceneNode` whose
particle system is still registered on the `Scene`"), and it goes in `scene.h` in the same
shape. Walking the graph to clear matching `node->light` pointers was considered and rejected:
it is O(nodes) per removal to defend against a caller this codebase does not have.

**For this feature the hazard is structurally absent**, which is worth saying because it is what
makes the invariant safe to ship rather than merely documented: the reconcile only ever removes
lights it created — `emissive_source_id != 0` — and those are exactly the lights with no node.
An authored light cannot be reached by it at all.

### Shadows come from `light_overrides`, not from the material

`.cscn` already has the block (`cscene.c:285-302`, applied at `cscene_apply.c:255-284`):

```json
"light_overrides": [ { "name": "cornell_light", "cast_shadows": true } ]
```

That is **per lamp**, which is what this decision is. A material cannot be: `import.c` dedups
materials, so one `Material*` is shared by every mesh using it, and 40 ceiling strips sharing a
`strip_light` material would all ask for a map against A7's pool of **8**. `off` is the opposite
case and stays on the material, because "this emissive is decorative" genuinely is a fact about
the surface and applying it to every instance is correct rather than a footgun.

**Ordering, which is the one thing this needs and does not have.** `apply_cscene_light_overrides`
runs once at init (`render.c:2710`), and a per-frame fit would not have produced anything yet.
It resolves because the fit splits cleanly: **the local-space fit needs no transforms at all**,
only mesh vertices, so it can run at init. The app calls `scene_build_emissive_lights(scene)`
after `apply_transform_to_nodes` (`render.c:2578`, where the probe call already sits) and before the override call.

That call is an **ordering convenience, not a correctness requirement** — the engine's per-frame
reconcile is the authority and builds the same lights on frame 1 regardless. An app that forgets
it gets the lights anyway and loses only the override, which the existing "matches no light"
warning already reports. That is what keeps this out of the forgettable-rule trap `draw_list.h:110`
warns about.

Note the placement half must NOT be hoisted to init with the fit: `render.c:2590-2600` recentres
the model *after* transforms are resolved, and the offset rides the per-frame model matrix. An
init-time world position would be pre-recentre and wrong. Local fit at init, world placement per
frame, is what makes both correct.

## Phases

**P0+P1 — the fit and its probe, consuming nothing. DONE.** The plan originally ordered the
probe before the fit, which is impossible — there is nothing to print until the fit exists — so
they are one commit. The instrument-before-the-fix discipline still holds, because the fix is
P2's shading change and this lands before it.

`cetra/src/emissive_light.c/h`: the area-weighted normal and centroid, planarity as the reject
test, **minimum-area rectangle over the mesh's own edge directions** (not the covariance
principal axis — see the square degeneracy above), extents from the projected min/max. Plus
`--emissive-light-probe` printing every candidate and every skip reason in the
`--water-fft-probe` line format. Nothing consumes the lights, so the frame is provably 0 px.

It paid for itself immediately: it found the corpus's emissive materials are almost all unlit
tricks, which moved the default, and it found the fixture's authored light 1 cm from the quad it
claims to mirror. Neither is visible in a frame.

**Verified: all 24 goldens PASS at 0 px.** That is the whole claim this phase makes — nothing
reads a derived panel yet, so the only way a pixel could move is if the walk itself had side
effects.

**P2 — registration.** Derived panels enter `scene->lights`: `emissive_source_id`, the
order-preserving removal, the reconcile, `scene_build_emissive_lights` and its init call, the
`emissiveLight` material row, string-valued enum labels, `--emissive-lights`. Nothing in
`light_cluster.c` — a `scene->lights` entry is already packed. This is the commit where the
picture changes, and where `light_overrides` starts reaching derived panels. Because the flag is
off by default, it changes **no** golden: the whole path is behind one branch at the fit.

**P3 — emissive textures.** The 1×1 mip readback.

**P4 — fixture and gates.** Below.

**P5 — the DDGI double count, measured before it is fixed.** A probe capture runs the full
forward shader, so it sees the emissive surface as radiance *and* sees surfaces lit by the new
panel — the first bounce lands twice. It only bites under `--gi-volume` (`scene->gi_volume` is
NULL by default, `scene.c:66`). `engine->capturing` already exists and suppressing derived
panels during capture is one branch, but **which way it should go is a measurement, not a
guess**: cornell_box with and without `--gi-volume`, derived vs authored. Do not build the
suppression before that A/B says the error is visible.

## Fixture and gates

`.cscn` can author `emissiveStrength` (it is a `MATERIAL_PARAMS` row), so **no new glTF is
needed** — the variant is a `.cscn`, in the `_water_cscn_variant` idiom the water group already
uses. Note the one-level-merge trap recorded there: a flat `dict.update` on a nested block drops
the fields it does not name.

| arm | what it reads |
|---|---|
| `emissive-fit` | the probe against the quad's known geometry: centre (0, **1.98**, 0), normal (0, −1, 0), size 0.7 × 0.7, planarity 1.0. Tight tolerances — this geometry is exact, not approximate. Note 1.98 and not the authored light's 1.97, which is the discrepancy above; the arm asserts the MESH, since that is what the fit is a fit of |
| `emissive-unlit-trick` | `water_fixture --emissive-lights` derives two panels from a bed and a ramp that are unlit flat colour. Asserts the probe REPORTS them, which is what stopped this shipping on by default — an arm over the finding, so a later change that quietly starts treating them as lamps has something to fail |
| `emissive-vs-authored` | **the acceptance test.** `cornell_box` variant with `emissiveStrength: 18` and no `lights[]`, rendered `--emissive-lights` (derived), against stock `cornell_box.cscn` (authored 18 nits, flag absent). Floor and wall boxes, **excluding the ceiling panel**, whose own pixels legitimately differ 18× between the two. Same room, same radiance, only the light's provenance changes |
| `emissive-off` | the derived variant **without** the flag → the room goes dark. Cheap now that the default is off, and still an arm that can fail: "off is off" passed against a deliberately broken composite in 11.21 because both sides took the same path |
| `emissive-optout` | `emissiveLight: "off"` on `cornell_light` under `--emissive-lights` returns the fixture to its flag-absent frame at **0 px**. Authored as the label string, so it also covers the `cscene_apply` change |
| `emissive-override` | a `light_overrides` entry naming `cornell_light` with `cast_shadows` reaches the derived panel — the arm that proves a derived light is addressable by name at all, and that the init ordering holds |
| `emissive-reject` | a non-planar emitter (a glowing box) is skipped, and the probe says why. Nothing in the corpus is one — `gen_cornell_rooms.py` can emit it |
| `emissive-occluded` | `cornell_leak`'s own question asked of the derived panel: the right half stays dark. **This arm is expected to be the one that fails first** — see below |

**Goldens: all 24 stay at 0 px, and the default is what buys that.** The flag is absent in every
golden recipe, so the fit does not run and no derived panel exists. Anything that moves is a
finding rather than a re-bake — which is a much stronger bar than the "re-bake two cornell
goldens with the delta quoted" this section carried while the default was on, and it is the
second thing P0's measurement paid for.

**`cornell_leak` stays the sharpest question, but it is now a gate question rather than a
default question.** It exists to prove light does NOT arrive where it has no path: an emissive
panel over the left half only, a partition, and a right half reachable only around the open
front. An LTC panel with no shadow map **ignores occluders entirely** — A2's shipped behaviour,
and the whole reason A7 exists — so a derived panel there lights the right half straight through
the partition. With the flag off its golden is untouched, so what is at stake is only what
`emissive-occluded` should assert:

- If the arm runs `--emissive-lights` bare, it documents the limitation: an unshadowed panel
  leaks, stated rather than hidden.
- If it adds a `light_overrides` entry giving the panel `cast_shadows`, it asserts the fix works
  AND becomes the arm that proves the override path reaches a derived light at all.

Write it the second way and keep the first as the control, so the pair measures the shadow rather
than merely surviving without one. This is still the case that shows an unshadowed analytic panel
is not a free upgrade over an emissive surface.

## What v1 deliberately does not do

- **One rectangle per emissive mesh.** A curved tube, a sphere or a closed box is rejected by the
  planarity test with a log, not approximated.
- **Single-sided**, inheriting A2's convention — `ltcPanel` returns zero behind the plane
  (`ltc.glsl:123`). A double-sided emissive quad lights only the side its winding faces.
- **No emissive-texture spatial detail.** The panel takes the texture's mean; a screen showing a
  bright corner on a dark field lights as a uniformly dim screen.
- **No DDGI reconciliation** until P5 measures it.

decision buys rather than care: with `--emissive-lights` absent the fit never runs.
The `emissive` gate group is nine arms, all green.

## What the plan got wrong, in the order it was found

1. **P0 before P1 is impossible.** The probe cannot print a fit that does not exist. One
   commit. The discipline survives because the *fix* is P2's shading change.
2. **The 2×2 covariance principal axis is the wrong fit.** A square has an isotropic
   covariance, so the axis it reports is arbitrary, and `cornell_light` — the corpus's only
   real lamp — is exactly square. Replaced by a minimum-area rectangle searched over the
   mesh's own edge directions. Falsified deliberately: forced back to the reference frame,
   the cornell square reads **0.939 × 0.939** against 0.7 and the strip **1.217 × 1.857**
   against 0.25 × 2.0.
3. **On by default is wrong**, and the probe's first run is what said so. 30 of the 32
   emissive materials in the corpus are emissive-over-black-base — the unlit-flat-colour
   idiom — not lamps.
4. **A private array for derived panels is wrong.** They are `scene->lights` entries. See
   the correction section above; `remove_light_from_scene` is the price and it was cheap.
5. **Shadows do not belong on the material.** `light_overrides` already exists, already
   per-lamp. Confirmed working: an override naming `cornell_light` reaches a derived panel.
6. **The build must precede the lighting decision.** Caught by the A/B — the first derived
   render came back with three directional lights in it, because a scene whose only light is
   derived reads as lightless and gets the render app's three-point fallback.
7. **"Off is off" cannot be asserted as darkness here**, for the same reason: the fallback
   makes the no-panel frame 16.1× *brighter*. Restated as an identity between two ways of
   having no panel, which reads 0.0000.

## Measurements

| what | result |
|---|---|
| fit vs the cornell quad's known geometry | exact — centre, normal, size, planarity all to 1e-3 |
| derived vs hand-authored at equal radiance | floor **0.9897**, walls **0.9969** / **0.9974** |
| that residual | the fixture's own 1 cm: the quad is at 1.98 and its authored twin at 1.97 |
| without the `cast_shadows` override | floor **1.3684×** — an unshadowed panel lights what the boxes should hide |
| emissive texture mean (half black, half white) | **0.502887** against a truth of 0.5 |
| that residual | the 8-bit sRGB round trip: 0.5 encodes to byte 188, decoding to 0.5029 |

The texture-mean figure also settles a dependency the method rests on and that nothing else
in this tree had checked: **this driver does generate sRGB mips in linear space.** Had it
not, the reading would have been the encoded mean (~0.73) rather than the linear one.

## Filed, with the measurement, not fixed

**The DDGI double count is real and it is large.** A probe capture runs the full forward
shader, so it sees the emissive surface as radiance *and* sees surfaces the derived panel
already lit — the first bounce lands twice. Measured on the cornell box, derived against
hand-authored, GI on against GI off:

| band | authored GI lift | derived GI lift | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor | 1.9844 | 2.6062 | **1.3134** |
| left wall | 1.0582 | 1.2598 | 1.1905 |
| right wall | 1.0787 | 1.3094 | 1.2139 |
| boxes | 1.3087 | 1.6510 | 1.2616 |

Without GI the same two frames agree to 0.5%, so the divergence is entirely the capture.

**Which side is wrong is not symmetric, and that decides the fix.** Suppressing the derived
PANEL during capture would be wrong — the probe would then not see the surfaces it lights,
and indirect would be *under*-counted. What must be suppressed is the emissive SURFACE's own
emission, for meshes that produced a panel, because the panel is now the emitter and the
capture should carry only what bounced.

**Not built, for a reason that is about the signature rather than the size.** The correct
suppression is per-MESH: a material is shared, so one that serves both a derived panel and a
mesh the planarity test rejected must still emit for the second. `_update_program_material_uniforms`
takes `(program, material, a2c_capable)` and has many callers, so per-mesh means changing a
hot-path signature — against a defect that requires a caller to opt into `--emissive-lights`
*and* `--gi-volume`, neither of which is on by default. Revisit if a scene wants both.

## What is still open

- The DDGI double count above.
- **`cornell_leak` is untested against this feature.** An LTC panel with no shadow map ignores
  occluders, so a derived panel there would light the partitioned half straight through the
  wall. The `emissive-override` arm shows the shadow fixes that in general; nothing yet asserts
  it on the fixture built to ask the question.
- **Cost is unmeasured.** The fit is epoch-gated and placement is one matrix-vector product per
  panel, so the steady state should be free — but `--profiler` has not been pointed at it.

# P6 — the emitter stops being counted twice

The defect P5 filed, fixed. **Floor GI lift 1.3134 → 0.9789 against the hand-authored panel**;
walls and boxes land at 0.9859-0.9874. All 24 goldens still 0 px, and the no-GI derived frame is
**0 px across the change** — the strongest form of "this touches nothing it should not".

**P5's stated reason for deferring was wrong on both counts, which is why this was revisited.**
It claimed a hot-path signature change across many callers.
`_update_program_material_uniforms` has exactly ONE caller (`render.c:563`), and `mesh` was
already in scope there.

## The thing that actually made it non-trivial

`scene_capture_faces` is shared by two callers that want opposite things, and both raise
`engine->capturing`:

| caller | output | consumed as | emitter should appear? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gi_volume.c` | **irradiance** | added to the analytic direct term | **no** — the panel already delivers it |
| `probe.c` | **radiance** | what a mirror sees | **yes** |

So gating on `capturing` would have stopped mirrors reflecting lamps — a worse regression than
the bug. `engine->capturing_irradiance` names what the capture is FOR rather than that one is
running, and only `gi_volume.c` raises it.

`dst_depth_cubemap` is non-zero only for the GI capture and was rejected as the discriminator:
that is a storage-strategy argument, not a statement about meaning, and it would have coupled
the two silently.

## Why a per-mesh uniform rather than zeroing `emissiveFactor`

`emissiveFactor` lives in the material block, which `submit_take_material` uploads **only when
the material changes between draws**. A zero written for one mesh leaks onto every later mesh
sharing it, and un-leaking means invalidating the material cache every draw — converting a
per-material upload into a per-draw one for the whole scene.

`uEmissiveGate` rides beside the wind-mask bounds, which are per-mesh for the same reason.
`uniform_set_float` is value-cached and the value is 1.0 on effectively every draw, so the
steady state is a comparison and no GL call.

**Per-mesh is also the correctness requirement, not just the cheap route:** a material shared by
a quad that became a panel and a shape the planarity test rejected must still emit for the
second.

**This retires the spec's "touches no shader" claim**, which held for all five earlier phases.
`pbr_frag` gains one uniform and one multiply.

## The bug this design was most likely to ship, and where it is handled

A stale `emissive_derived` mark suppresses an emitter for a panel that no longer exists. It is
cleared at the TOP of every reconcile rather than on the disabled path only, because a mesh can
stop being a candidate without the feature going anywhere — a material opting out mid-run is the
obvious case, and `_reconcile_node` only ever marks. Clear-then-set is one rule covering both;
set-and-remember-to-unset is two, and the second gets forgotten.

## Verification

`emissive-gi` reads the RATIO of GI lifts, each side measured against its own GI-off frame at
the same frame count, so it survives the two sides differing in anything else. **Falsified**:
with `capturing_irradiance` forced false it reads **1.8799** on the floor against a 1.0 ± 0.05
bar.

One number is unexplained and is recorded rather than smoothed: the arm reads **0.9522** on the
floor where the same comparison run by hand read **0.9789**. Both are far inside the bar and
both are the derived side lifting slightly less — which is expected, since the authored
fixture's quad still emits its token 1 nit into the capture where the derived one is silenced
whole. The 2.7-point gap between the two harnesses is not accounted for.

## Still open

- **The specular double count**, unchanged and deliberately so. A reflection probe shows the
  glowing quad, the LTC panel contributes its own analytic specular lobe, and a smooth surface
  reflecting the panel gets both. It is pre-existing rather than introduced here (`cornell_box`
  has had an emissive quad beside an authored area light since it was written), the fix would
  stop mirrors reflecting lamps, and it is unmeasured — nothing in the corpus has a smooth
  surface reflecting an emissive panel.
- `cornell_leak` against a derived panel, from P5.
- Cost, still unmeasured by `--profiler`.
