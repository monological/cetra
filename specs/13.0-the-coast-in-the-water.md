# Spec 13.0 — The coast in the water

A master spec. It builds nothing. It says what the water subsystem is missing, what it already has
that looks missing, and which of the two the sub-specs under it are allowed to spend time on.

## Context

`../research/water/ocean-simulation` is an MIT single-file three.js island-ocean demo — FFT/JONSWAP
cascades, depth-aware coastal behaviour, foam, caustics, refraction and weather around a tropical
island. It was read end to end against this engine's water.

**Most of what it does is already here.** Roadmap D3 shipped as specs 11.32 + 11.33 + 11.35 and the
ledger says so: *"Gerstner and Tessendorf FFT wave models, Beer-Lambert absorption, caustics and
foam from the surface Jacobian, shoaling against a bed provider, an underwater medium, and a `.cscn`
block."* Four of the demo's six notable techniques have a flag on `Water`, a gate arm, or both. They
are enumerated at the bottom of this file so the next reader does not re-derive the comparison.

**Two things it has that this engine does not — and both are named as limitations in our own
headers, which is why they are worth spending on.**

### 1. The sea does not know the land is there

`water.h:291-295`:

> Travel direction of the waves, XZ. Read by BOTH models — the Gerstner octaves fan off it, and the
> spectral seeding centres its directional spread on it — **so the two cannot describe seas running
> different ways.**

One global direction, for the whole ocean, forever. There is no per-location wavefront direction, so
a swell cannot bend around a headland, cannot arrive down both sides of an island, cannot turn to
meet a beach square on, and cannot leave a cove sheltered. We shoal against a bed
(`ocean.glsl:862-867`) and we break against depth (`:786-843`), but the wave that shoals is still
travelling the way it was travelling in open water.

Nothing in `docs/rendering-roadmap.md` or `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md` books this. A grep for
refraction-around-land, headlands, diffraction or travel time returns nothing in either.

### 2. The spectral surface leaves no number behind

`procedural/water_waves.h:23-27`:

> **THE SPECTRAL PATH IS NOT ANSWERED HERE, and cannot be.** An FFT cascade's displacement exists
> only as a texture the transform just wrote, so reproducing it on the CPU means running the
> transform on the CPU — a second implementation of the thing whose whole point is that it runs on
> the GPU.

`shore_chain.h:29-31` says it again, for the swash solver's forcing: *"the spectral cascades exist
only on the GPU so there is nothing on this side to sample them with."*

Both are right about the approach they rule out, and both then treat that as ruling out the
question. There is a third way neither considered and the demo uses it: render a tiny image in which
each texel **is** the answer for one query point, and read it back. That is spec 13.1.

## Outcome

**The sea knows where the land is, and the CPU can ask the sea where it is.**

| Spec | Title | State |
|---|---|---|
| 13.1 | The spectral surface answers on the CPU | written |
| 13.2 | The coastal field — travel time, refraction, shelter | sketched below |

13.1 goes first, and not because it is the more interesting of the two. It is engine-only, it
touches no shading and can move no golden, and the instrument that measures it already exists and
already reports the failure: `apps/render --water-probe` on a spectral sea prints `available=0` and
then sixteen rows of the still plane.

## 13.2 — The coastal field (sketch)

Solve `|grad T| = 1/c(h)` once over the bed by Godunov fast sweeping, and bake three things into one
texture: the **travel time** as a phase lag against a deep-water plane wave, the **wavefront
direction** (normalised `grad T`), and an **exposure** term from ray-marching the bed upwind. The
surface shader then warps its cascade sample coordinate by the phase lag and rotates displacement and
slope by the direction. One fetch buys refraction around headlands, arrival down both sides of an
island, diffraction shadows behind rocks and sheltered coves — with no extra cascade and no second
FFT.

Three things make it a fit rather than a want:

- **The input already exists.** `WaterHeightFn` (`water.h:171`) is a bed provider, and
  `_water_bake_bed` (`water.c:795-874`) already samples it over 256² and central-differences a
  gradient. The eikonal solve consumes exactly that.
- **The bake is deterministic and content-addressed**, which is the derived-data cook's exact shape
  (spec 11.99).
- **It costs no ledger fight.** `water_frag` declares 11 of 16 samplers, with units 3, 4, 5, 9 and
  10 unoccupied by that program.

What a spec has to answer before it is one:

- **What a scene authors.** Presumably a swell direction and a period on `WaterSeaState`, but the
  relationship to the existing global `wind_dir` has to be stated rather than grown.
- **Whether the exposure term double-counts** against the shoal factor `ocean.glsl:862-867` already
  applies.
- **Which instrument shows it.** This is the awkward one: `water_fixture`'s `--water-bed dome` is
  radially symmetric, so **there is nothing in the corpus for a wave to bend around.** 13.2 likely
  needs a new fixture with an asymmetric bed — a headland and a lee — before it can have an arm at
  all. `apps/forest`'s island and `apps/tree`'s dome are the app-side demonstrations, and neither is
  a measurement.

## Already shipped — the four the demo shares with us

None of these is a spec. Each row's third column is the measurement that would reopen it, and the
governing rule is `docs/verification.md:15`: a pixel count quoted without its noise floor is not a
measurement.

| Demo feature | Ours | Reopen only if |
|---|---|---|
| Caustics — a photon mesh refracted per vertex and marched against the bed | **Reopened and shipped as spec 13.2**: the crop showed the band-Jacobian heuristic missing the focus, so it was replaced by the demo's own method -- a lattice refracted per corner onto the baked bed (or a 3 m plane), drawn additively as beam area over landed area, on both wave models; `water-caustic`, `water-caustic-energy` arms. **Spec 13.3** took the squares away: a 512² ripple band and a fine 2.34 cm caustic level for shallow water | light goes missing over a deep baked bed under waves -- the dome reads 0.80 to 0.85 where it should read 1, on either level; open in 13.3 |
| Foam history | `water.h:317-326` `foam_history` + `foam_decay` + `foam_drift`; a ping-pong accumulator (`water.c:1804-1862`) | **this one has a known defect.** Ours is a cascade texture and therefore **tiles the world** — `docs/verification.md:244` records that as the stated reason `beach_fixture`'s arms run Gerstner rather than FFT. The demo's camera-following region field is the shape that fixes it. Crop first; then possibly 13.3 |
| Unresolved slope energy into the BRDF rather than aliased into sparkle | `water.h:312-314` `glitter` — *"its width comes from the slope the surface stopped resolving"*; roadmap's 11.35 footprint→roughness handover | a distant sea at a grazing angle fizzes |
| Depth-limited shoaling and breaking | `ocean.glsl:786-867`; `beach-shoal`, `beach-surf-zone` | a crop shows the surf zone wrong again, as 11.44 found it at 0.38 m wide |

**The measurement that governs all four**: render `beach_fixture` and `water_fixture` at a framing
that shows the shore, put the demo's equivalent beside it, and look.

## Out of scope for the whole programme

- **Anything the demo does that is scene content rather than engine capability** — its island, its
  vegetation, its weather profiles, its lightning.
- **Its FFT.** Three cascades at 128²/256²/128² against our three at 128²; its evolution and
  butterfly passes are ours with different names. There is nothing to take.
- **Its projected-grid equivalent.** We went through a geometry clipmap and out the other side into
  a projected grid in 11.33 → 11.35, and the reasoning is recorded there.
