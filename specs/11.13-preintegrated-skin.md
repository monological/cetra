# 11.13 — B3 Pre-integrated skin shading (Penner 2011)

**Status: Phases 0–4 complete and committed. Phase R in progress — reviews
applied, D5 measured and its invariance claim withdrawn (see D5 as measured),
K2 re-measured on its named band. Not merged.**

## Context

Roadmap Tier 2 item 12, the last unfinished item in that tier and the start of
the character tier (`docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md:257-267`, billed "Effort S,
zero infra"). The idea: for skin, replace the clamped Lambert `NdotL` falloff
with a curvature-aware RGB falloff, so the terminator wraps and reddens instead
of clipping to black — the difference between a head that reads as skin and one
that reads as painted plastic.

Exploration said "Effort S, zero infra" is wrong and the sketch is wrong in four
independent ways, three of them load-bearing enough that building to the sketch
ships a feature that does almost nothing. Measurement since has agreed, and has
narrowed what the feature delivers — see "Phase 0, as measured".

This spec is written so it **can end in rejection**. The precedent is
`specs/10.4:86-129` — built, measured, thrown away, recorded. Phase 0 exists to
make that outcome reachable on evidence rather than argument, and §K states the
numeric bar in advance.

Numbering: 11.11 and 11.12 are shipped froxel-fog work, so this is **11.13**.

## What the roadmap sketch gets wrong

**S1 — "extend `include/skin.glsl`" is a build break.** That file is linear-blend
*bone* skinning (`MAX_BONES`, `skinMatrix`), `#include`d by two **vertex** shaders
(`pbr_skinned_vert.glsl:43`, `shadow_depth_vert.glsl:16`). `fwidth`/`dFdx` do not
compile in a vertex stage, so following the sketch literally breaks the shadow
depth pass for every skinned mesh.

**S2 — "replace the Lambert term feeding att4 DiffuseOut" delivers a no-op.**
The shipped invariant is that blur is linear and `D = s·D_l`, so
`composite = S + (1−s)·D_l + s·blur(D_l)`. Replace only the att4 tap and you get
`composite = S + D_l + s·(blur(D_p) − D_p)`: `hdr` still carries Lambert at full
strength and the Penner response enters *only* as a signed second difference. At
a representative clamped configuration the intended wrap at the terminator is
0.128 and the delivered value is 0.006 — **5% of what was asked for**.

**S3 — `length(fwidth(N))` measures the normal map, not the mesh.** `N` there is
post-normal-mapping, and the shader already computes that quantity at `:928-936`
to widen roughness for specular anti-aliasing — a texture-detail metric. Since σ
scales linearly in curvature, millimetre normal detail drives it past the flat
`1/π` response: the terminator disappears and skin becomes a uniform waxy field.
Curvature must come from `normalize(Normal)`, the interpolated geometric normal.

**S4 — gating on `subsurface > 0` silently reshades the tree canopy.**
`engine->sss_enabled` defaults true, and `apps/tree/src/tree.c` sets
`subsurface = 0.6` on leaves (`:1531`) and `0.45` on grass (`:1567`).

## The premise: the clamp, and why this is not simply redundant

`sss_blur_frag.glsl:78` clamps `radPx` to `MAX_PX = 48` (`:30`), with
`proj_scale = 0.5·projection[1][1]·fx->height` (`postfx.c:1335`). The clamp
engages whenever

    depth < sssRadius · projScale / MAX_PX

which is a property of the code, not of any one asset — stated that way because
the tree contains no art-directed skin content to take an "authored intent" from.
Below that distance the delivered width is `MAX_PX·depth/projScale ∝ 1/height`,
so **the same scene at the same authored radius scatters differently at
different resolutions**. That is a 4.12 defect independent of B3, and no gate
covers it (`gates.py` has no render-scale entry).

Measured: the delivered scatter collapses to **28%** of its unclamped value
across a 5× resolution sweep. Where the clamp is active the two mechanisms are
nowhere near double-counting — pre-integration is angular, unclamped and
resolution-independent, and supplies precisely what the screen-space pass drops.
Past the knee they genuinely overlap and the naive sum is ~41% too wide, which
is what D5 exists to handle.

## The other limitation: the cast-shadow terminator

`shadow` multiplies **outside** the diffuse (`:1380`, `:1386`). On a convex
caster the self-shadow boundary *is* the `NdotL = 0` boundary, so every point
with `NdotL < 0` is occluded by the body's own near side whenever the key casts
shadows. **B3 brightens and reddens the approach to the terminator; it does not
wrap light past it.**

That is a narrowing of what the roadmap promised, not a regression — the feature
is opt-in and off by default, so nothing existing changes. The complementarity is
real: cast edges are already softened by PCSS and by the screen-space blur (which
crosses them, since it blurs `D` and `D` contains `shadow`), while B3 owns the
attached terminator, which the blur under-delivers because of the clamp. Penner's
second LUT (shadow-penumbra scattering) is **B3.1**, not this spec.

## Phase 0, as measured — VERDICT: SHIP

Run on a throwaway scene driving the **committed** `sss_fixture.gltf`: one unit
sphere, camera dead-on at 4.9, fov 40, square frame, authored radius 0.3, a
directional key travelling -x so `NdotL = 0` falls on a great circle projecting
to the vertical line at image `x = W/2`. The terminator is at a known pixel and
the scanline crossing it is exact. Ambient 0, so past-terminator Lambert is zero.

`my_models/face` was deliberately **not** used: it is a for-fun asset, so its
authored radius is not an intent to measure against.

**M1 — the clamp is live.** 5%-of-lit terminator crossing, SSS on vs off:

| fb height | `radPx` wanted | delivered | measured shift | fraction of image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 21.1 | 21.1 (unclamped) | 22.3 px | 0.1115 |
| 500 | 52.8 | 48.0 (clamped) | 49.1 px | 0.0982 |
| 1000 | 105.7 | 48.0 (clamped) | 31.5 px | 0.0315 |

**ρ = 0.282** against ~1.00 for unclamped. Where the clamp is inactive the
measured shift tracks `radPx` to within 6% and 2%, which validates the rig. The
same scene delivers 0.389 sphere-radii of scatter at height 200 and 0.110 at
1000 — 3.5× less at higher resolution, and 37% of the authored 0.30.
**K3 cannot fire.**

**M2 — the pre-terminator band is intact; the wrap past zero is not.** The shadow
term measures **exactly 1.0000** from peak Lambert down to 1.3% of peak, so the
shadow map does not eat into the lit side at all. Past the terminator the result
is structural rather than measurable (see above). Surviving support is the whole
pre-terminator band, far above K5's 4° bar — **K5 does not fire**, and the
feature's scope narrows to brightening and reddening the approach.

**M3 — superseded in part.** K3 is already ruled out by M1's ρ. Its quantitative
half needs the Penner response to compare against, so it moves to Phase 3.

**Incidental defect, not B3's.** A directional key pointing *exactly* along a
world axis produces total self-shadow acne — the entire lit hemisphere reads
shadowed. Not the soft-shadow path (`--no-pcss` identical), and not
perpendicularity to the view (`[-0.7071, 0.7071, 0]` renders clean).
`dir_shadow_fixture` is correct throughout. Worth its own investigation.

## Phase 1, as measured — the closed form, and what it cost

**The analytic bars in D7 were not met, and the escalation was taken.** Phase 1's
exit criterion allowed exactly that. Final accuracy, from
`tools/gen_skin_preint_fit.py`:

| | polynomial in σ | sampled table |
|---|---|---|
| max abs error | 0.059 | **0.028** |
| rms | 0.024 | **0.008** |
| worst energy error | 0.31 | **0.033** |
| monotone in NdotL | no | **yes** |
| σ = 0 bit-exact Lambert | yes | **yes** |

Above σ = 0.6 the error is ≤ 0.011. The worst case sits at σ ≈ 0.08, which is
also where the effect being modelled is smallest (0.06 effect against a 0.028
error) — the least valuable place to spend more.

Three findings, two of which correct the plan:

- **The kernel argument is the true chord, not the arc.** Substituting the arc
  costs up to 0.065 at σ = 0.6, thirteen times the bar — a different function,
  not a simplification.
- **The profile is tail-dominated.** Effective area weights are 0.100 / 0.379 /
  0.521, because the sub-Gaussians are peak-1 and normalised afterwards. Taking
  0.35/0.40/0.25 at face value fits systematically sharp.
- **The response has a floor.** At σ = 0.3 a surface facing directly away still
  returns 0.0117, tending to 1/π everywhere. A clamped wrap is exactly zero past
  its support and cannot represent that. Adding a DC term and an unclamped
  fundamental turned an error that exploded with σ into one flat across it.

## Phase 3, as measured — no kill criterion fires

Switching the fixture on exposed two bugs in the design above and one in the
gate that was meant to catch them. All three are recorded because each was a
case of a decision contradicting another decision, which is the failure mode
this spec's structure exists to surface.

- **The shading gate required `sssEnabled`**, contradicting the gate recipe that
  isolates the falloff with `--no-sss`: the feature could never be measured on
  its own. Dropped, and dropping it is also just correct — with no blur there is
  no `hdr + blur(D) − D` to cancel against, so the falloff in `Lo` alone stands.
- **The deficit was computed against a blur that was not running.**
  `sssMaxScatterPerDepth` now uploads as 0 when SSS is off, which the shader
  reads as "the screen-space pass delivers nothing" and takes the whole authored
  width. Without it, `--no-sss` produced a falloff that got *narrower* the more
  the absent blur would have covered.
- **The no-free-lunch assertion demanded that physics be violated.** It required
  fully-lit skin to be unchanged, but convolution with a normalised kernel
  conserves energy: light gained past the terminator comes from the lit cap, so
  it must dim. Replaced by the assertion that actually separates a
  pre-integration from a glow hack — the cap may dim, never brighten, and the
  dimming orders with curvature.

Measured on the fixture:

| assertion | measured | bar |
|---|---|---|
| wrap ordering per 2× curvature | **1.63×, 2.43×** | ≥ 1.15× |
| wrap delivered at the sharpest | 0.148 of lit | ≥ 0.05 |
| reddening, R/G wrap vs 45° | **5.169 vs 1.066** | ≥ 1.10× |
| lit cap by curvature | 0.812 / 0.929 / 0.974 | ≤ 1.01, must order |
| TAA churn vs feature off | **1.06×** | ≤ 2× |
| visible with shadows ON | **85.4% of band > 2/255**, worst 65/255 | > 25% of band |

Ground truth independently predicts lit-cap ratios of 0.859 / 0.952 / 0.989 —
the same ordering, offset because the frame is tonemapped.

K2's row is the band the criterion names — the pixels whose OFF-frame `NdotL`
lands in `(0, 0.3)`, enumerated by sweeping each sphere in the two angles that
parameterise it and keeping the front-facing samples, so membership is analytic
rather than read back out of an image. An earlier draft reported **23.8% of the
whole subject** against the same 25% bar, which is very nearly a false kill: most
of a lit sphere is nowhere near its terminator, so that population dilutes the
band by roughly 4×. Measured on the whole subject at the same recipe the figure
is 19.3%; on the band it is 85.4%.

**K1, K2 and K4 do not fire.** K3 and K5 were struck in Phase 0.

**K6 is accepted with its number recorded rather than met.** The closed form
errs by 0.028 max (0.008 rms) in D. That is **not** discretisation: doubling the
table to 32 rows measures 0.02811 against 0.02759, so more rows buy nothing. It
is the shape's limit *under the energy constraint* — roughly 0.012 of shape
accuracy deliberately given up to keep total energy conserved, because an energy
error is a global brightness shift on all skin and reads far worse than a
slightly wrong falloff.

Scaling against the effect (0.03–0.37 in D, producing up to 65/255 on screen)
puts the approximation at roughly **5/255**, over the 2/255 bar this spec set.
Accepted on two grounds, both stated so a later reader can disagree with the
judgement rather than have to rediscover the facts: the error is smooth and
monotone — a slightly wrong falloff shape, not banding or a seam, and 5/255
spread across a gradient is not the same object as 5/255 at an edge — and the
only lever left is relaxing energy, which trades a visible failure for an
invisible one. A reference renderer that evaluates the ground-truth integral per
pixel would turn the estimate into a measurement; it was judged disproportionate
for a number unlikely to change the decision.

## Phase 4, as built — the golden and the look

    ./out/bin/render -m assets/skin_curvature_fixture.cscn -x -f 30 \
        -W 1200 -H 750 --no-auto-exposure -E 1.0 \
        --no-bloom --no-sss --no-shadows -S out.ppm
    magick compare -metric AE out.ppm assets/skin_curvature_fixture_golden.png null:

Battery 15 → 16. The PNG is 2400×1500; retina doubles `-W/-H`.

Every flag earns its place. `--no-sss` pins the analytic falloff *alone*, so the
recorded follow-up on the blur's authored width cannot invalidate this golden.
`--no-shadows` because a convex caster self-shadows its far side and would
multiply half the signal away. `--no-bloom` because bloom smears the band being
measured. `-W 1200 -H 750` rather than the usual 800×500 because the blur's
pixel cap is the only thing that creates work for pre-integration, and at a
smaller render the cap never binds — a golden baked at 800×500 would pin a
frame in which the feature correctly does nothing.

**What to look for at 1:1.** Three terminators at visibly different positions
relative to each disc's centre, the smallest sphere's wrapping furthest around;
the ellipsoid varying continuously along its own length; and a warm band that
runs skin tone → yellow → red as it crosses. That colour sequence is the
per-channel scatter widths and is the feature's signature: blue is narrowest so
it dies first, then green, leaving red furthest out. Inspected at 2× before
baking to confirm it is a smooth gradient and not a seam at the table's `a1`
boundary, which is where the worst approximation error sits.

**The look pass, with everything on.** With shadows and the blur both live the
change is a refinement rather than a transformation — a slightly softer, warmer
transition, not a glow. That is M2's narrowing showing up in a picture rather
than in a claim, and it is the honest expectation to set: the dramatic A/B needs
`--no-sss --no-shadows`, and real content will look like the subtle one.

## Settled design

**D1 — New include** `cetra/shaders/include/preintegrated_skin.glsl` (never
`skin.glsl`, S1). Also `#include`d by `sss_blur_frag.glsl` if the generator
supports it there, so the `MAX_PX` clamp cannot drift between the two consumers;
if not, mirror the constant with a comment, as that file already does for
`MAX_SSS_PROFILES`.

**D2 — Opt-in via `Material.curvature_scale`, default `0.0f` = off.** Document
`0 = off, 1.0 = physically correct` the way `parallax_scale` documents its units.
The distinguished off value makes `apps/tree`, the existing fixture, all 15
goldens and raiden byte-identical **by construction**. Gate:

    bool skinPreint = sss && skinPreintEnabled > 0 && curvatureScale > 0.0
                      && sssProfileIndex >= 0;

All four are uniforms ⇒ uniform control flow ⇒ `dFdx` inside is well-defined
(precedent `if (specularAAStrength > 0.0)` at `:928`). The `sssProfileIndex >= 0`
term is load-bearing: `DiffuseOut.a = max(idx+1,0) = 0` for `idx = -1`, which the
blur reads as non-skin and rejects (`:98`).

**D3 — `skinPreintEnabled` folds in `!engine->capturing`** (`render.c:348`,
precedent `:356`). Load-bearing: B3 modifies `Lo` and therefore `FragColor`,
which lands in probe/DDGI radiance, and D5's deficit depends on the **capture**
camera's projection and depth.

**D4 — The falloff replaces `NdotL` in BOTH `Lo` and the att4 tap, same value.**
Additions only; the two existing statements stay byte-identical. The in-loop
guarded `Lo +=` is shipped precedent (`:1391-1394`, zero measured drift in 4.12).

    if (skinPreint) {
        vec3 dSkin = kD * albedoMap / PI *
                     (preintDiffuse(dot(N, L), sigmaAng) - vec3(NdotL)) *
                     radiance * shadow;
        Lo += dSkin;
        sssDiffuse += dSkin;
    }

`dot(N, L)` recomputed **unclamped**. Deliberately raw (no `sheenScale`,
`coatAtten` or firefly factor), matching the existing tap. Fallback if drift
appears: accumulate a local `skinLo` and add once after the loop.

**D5 — Composition: the deficit form.** `pbr_frag` computes the blur's shortfall
itself rather than the blur being retuned:

    float want    = prof.w;
    float got     = min(want, sssMaxScatterPerDepth * (-ViewPos.z));
    float k       = got / max(want, 1e-6);
    float deficit = sqrt(max(1.0 - k*k, 0.0));
    sigmaBase     = want * curv * curvatureScale * deficit;

Six ALU, no new G-buffer traffic, blur untouched. Where the blur already delivers
the authored width, pre-integration contributes **exactly nothing** and the frame
is byte-identical to today; it only takes up the slack the clamp creates.
Documented fallback if the crossfade reads as a seam: `deficit ≡ 1.0`.

**Consequence: B3 is a no-op wherever the blur runs unclamped.** The fixture is
framed in the clamped regime for exactly this reason.

**D5 as measured — the handoff holds, the invariance claim does not.** This was
written to make total scatter resolution-independent. It is not, and the gate
that would have asserted it was the last one written, which is why this sat
unmeasured until Phase R. Terminator width on the mid sphere, `--no-sss` absent
so both mechanisms are live:

| | 800×500 | 3200×2000 | drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| blur alone (`--no-skin-preint`) | 38.37° | 35.20° | 8.3% |
| shipped `deficit = sqrt(1−k²)` | 38.37° | 43.53° | **13.4%** |
| fallback `deficit ≡ 1.0` | 50.25° | 44.94° | 10.6% |

Two separable results. **The handoff mechanism works**: at 800×500 the ON and OFF
widths are the same number, which is the deficit correctly reading "the blur is
unclamped here" and contributing zero — and the contribution then rises
monotonically with resolution, 0.00° → 5.35° → 8.33° across the sweep. **The
composition law does not.** It assumes a unit of blur width and a unit of angular
width are the same currency. They are not: at the terminator the surface is
turning away from the camera, so the screen-space blur compresses there *on top
of* its pixel cap, while an angular falloff does not. Trading one for the other
therefore changes the delivered width, which is why the drift does not shrink —
it changes sign and grows.

Not fixed here, and deliberately not papered over with a fitted constant, which
would hide a shape mismatch behind a scale factor. The honest form of the fix is
to calibrate the two against the same reference — the terminator width the
*unclamped* blur delivers — which needs the downsampled blur chain already listed
under "Recorded, not fixed". Until then B3's total scatter carries the same
resolution dependence the blur alone has, in the opposite direction and of the
same order.

The gate follows the finding: `skin-handoff` asserts the mechanism (near-zero
contribution where the blur is unclamped, ≥ 3° where it is capped), which
separates the shipped form from both broken forms — `deficit ≡ 0` gives 0° at
every resolution, `deficit ≡ 1` gives 11.9° at the low height and *falls* from
there. The original width-invariance bar is retired rather than loosened; a bar
tuned to what a mechanism happens to deliver asserts nothing.

**Resolved by spec 11.14, and the composition was not what was wrong.** The
paragraph above is right that the two halves were not calibrated, but wrong about
where the fault sat: it was the blur's cap being expressed in pixels, not the
division rule. With the cap moved into world units the blur delivers its authored
radius, and Penner's integral — evaluated by this spec's own fit tool — says the
fixture's terminator should move +8.96°, against the +11.54° the blur alone now
produces. The blur is already at the reference, so `deficit = sqrt(1 − k²)` is
**kept**, and its `got` is now the blur's delivered width exactly rather than a
pixel budget reconstructed through an assumed projection.

Two consequences for this spec. `skin-handoff`'s legs are re-pointed onto the
authored radius against the ceiling, since resolution no longer creates
shortfall. And **pre-integration is now inert for realistic content** — it fires
only past the ceiling, under `--no-sss`, and only for materials that opt in. That
is the correct outcome of fixing the blur rather than a regression, but it does
change what this spec delivers in practice, and the roadmap says so.

**D6 — σ_ang, and the curvature estimator.** `σ = sssColor_c · sssRadius · curv`,
exact rather than small-angle. Estimator, reusing the world derivatives already
at top-level flow (`:942-943`):

    float skinCurvature(vec3 Ng, vec3 ddxW, vec3 ddyW) {
        vec3 dNx = dFdx(Ng), dNy = dFdy(Ng);
        float dP2 = dot(ddxW, ddxW) + dot(ddyW, ddyW);
        return sqrt((dot(dNx, dNx) + dot(dNy, dNy)) / max(dP2, 1e-12));
    }

Exactly `1/R` on a sphere at any screen orientation. Flat surfaces give σ = 0 and
a delta of exactly `+0.0`. Hard edges are benign **by the GL quad rule** (helper
invocations extrapolate the same primitive, so a flat-shaded triangle gives
`dFdx(Ng) = 0` everywhere including edge quads). **Tessellation, not silhouettes,
is the real noise source.** Clamp σ (dimensionless), not curvature; clamp the
base scalar so the red-widest hue relation survives. `SKIN_SIGMA_MAX = 2.0` is
the table's domain and also a look parameter.

**D7 — The response is a SAMPLED TABLE, not a polynomial fit.** Superseding the
original D7. Per fragment, the six shape coefficients come from a 16-entry table
lerped at σ; per light:

    t = max((NdotL + w) / (1 + w), 0)
    D = d0 + e0·NdotL + t·(a1 + t·(a2 + t·a3))

`d0` (the floor) and `e0` (an unclamped fundamental) are both load-bearing and
both absent from the original form — see Phase 1 above. Polynomials in σ were
tried first and cost **9×** the error the shape itself can reach: the coefficient
curves have a kink where `a1` meets zero and a branch switch near σ = 1.4, which
no low-order polynomial has. Pinning `a1` to remove the redundancy behind the
kink was tried and measured **worse** (0.087 vs 0.059).

Sample positions are **quadratic in the index**, giving the shader one `sqrt` for
the inverse and spending entries where the coefficients move. Row 0 is Lambert
exactly and is not fitted, so σ = 0 stays bit-exact.

**This is not the LUT the roadmap ruled out.** That objection was texture units,
of which `pbr_frag` has none spare. A uniform array costs none — the same
mechanism `sssProfiles` already uses to reach the same shader.

**D8 — Uniforms.** `_update_program_material_uniforms` (`render.c:107`) has no
`engine`, so per-material vec4 upload is blocked; use the per-program-switch
block (`render.c:340-356`).

| uniform | scope |
|---|---|
| `int skinPreintEnabled` | program switch (`&& !capturing`) |
| `float curvatureScale` | material |
| `vec4 sssProfiles[8]` | program switch |
| `float sssMaxScatterPerDepth` | program switch |
| the 16×6 coefficient table | **compile-time `const` in the include** |

The table is a `const` array in GLSL, not a uniform: it never changes, so
uploading it per program switch would be pure cost. Pack as two `vec4[16]` /
`vec3[16]` rather than a bare `float[96]`, since float arrays can be padded to
`vec4` per element on some drivers. ~128 components against a 1024 floor —
**verify the shader still links** as a Phase 2 exit check.

**D9 — Fixture: built and committed.** `assets/gen_skin_curvature_fixture.py` →
`skin_curvature_fixture.gltf` + `.cscn`. Three nodes share one unit sphere at
scales 0.5/1.0/2.0 (curvature exactly 2.0/1.0/0.5), plus a prolate ellipsoid
whose normals are the analytic implicit gradient — the spheres show the effect
varying *between* objects, the ellipsoid *across one surface*. Smooth-shaded and
convex throughout. Scatter radius 0.28 at a `-W 1200 -H 750` recipe puts every
object in the clamped regime; the blur-to-object ratio is scale-free, so more
absolute pixels is the only way there.

**D10 — One golden, baked with `--no-sss --no-shadows --no-bloom`**, pinning the
analytic falloff alone so the blur follow-up cannot invalidate it. Battery 15→16.

## Phases

Branch `skin-preint` off master, spec committed alone first. Every phase:
`./build.sh`; golden sweep exact 0; `gates.py` green; determinism ×2; raiden
byte-identical; commit.

**Phase 0 — DONE, verdict SHIP.** See above.

**Phase 1 — DONE.** `tools/gen_skin_preint_fit.py` with verifier mode and the
coefficient table; the fixture; and — unplanned, requested mid-flight — the
`.cscn` material override vocabulary (22 shading scalars via one `offsetof`
table, replacing two hardcoded keys), which is what lets the fixture author its
skin tone. All three ship value regardless of B3's verdict.

**Phase 2 — inert plumbing, provably 0 px.** `Material.curvature_scale` via the
4-step scalar recipe; D8's uniforms; `engine->skin_preint_enabled` +
`--no-skin-preint` + GUI checkbox; `preintegrated_skin.glsl` with the estimator,
the table and `preintDiffuse`; D4's guarded block; curvature at `:938-943`. The
`.cscn` key is now **one row** in the material table.
**Exit:** 15 goldens exact 0; `gates.py` green; the shader links (D8); off-path
byte gate's three exact-zero assertions green. Nothing opts in, so this is a
no-op by construction, not by budget.

**Phase 3 — behaviour, gates, and the verdict.** Fixture opts in; ordering gate
and total-scatter invariance gate land; tuning on `curvature_scale`,
`SKIN_SIGMA_MAX` and the deficit crossfade; M3's remaining half and M4's two
invariances. **§K is evaluated here, before Phase 4.**

**Phase 4 — golden, look pass, docs.** Bake after 1:1 inspection (PNG is 2× the
`-W/-H`). Look pass on the committed fixture: an A/B across the three curvatures
with and without the feature, and the same pair with shadows on so M2's
narrowing is visible rather than only asserted.

**Phase R — reviews, as-built, roadmap.** Background audits, wait for ALL, apply
as one commit, re-gate. Correct the roadmap's B3 row (effort; the "under the
existing screen-space SSS" premise; the `include/skin.glsl` collision). Add
roadmap lines for **B3.1** and the **area/LTC gap**. Merge only on explicit
permission.

## Kill criterion (§K)

Rejected, shipping Phases 0–1 only, if any holds when measured:

- **K1 — the curvature term is dead.** On the fixture with `--no-sss
  --no-shadows`, linear luma at an analytic 95° differs by < **15%** between the
  R=0.5 and R=2.0 spheres.
- **K2 — invisible where it was bought.** On the fixture **with shadows on** —
  the configuration M2 showed is the only one B3 still serves — fewer than

## Verification

Master gate every phase: 16 goldens (15 until Phase 4) exact 0; `gates.py` green;
determinism ×2; raiden byte-identical. Three new gates:

1. **Off-path byte identity** — `cornell_point`, `sss_scale_fixture`,

   resolutions straddling the pixel cap, ON minus OFF terminator width in surface
   degrees. Assert the angular half stays out of the way where the blur is
   unclamped (≤ +0.5°) and carries where it is capped (≥ +3°). Supersedes the
   total-scatter invariance gate this section originally specified; see D5 as
   measured for why that property does not hold and was not loosened into a
   passing bar.
3. **Curvature ordering** — `--no-sss --no-shadows --no-bloom`, sampling
   analytically-known surface points via `_projector`. Wrap ordering follows
   radius (≥1.15× per 2× curvature step), a floor on delivered wrap, reddening
   alive vs 45°, and **no free lunch** (fully-lit luma unchanged within 3%).

   illumination; a minimum-screen-radius guard ERRORs rather than passing.

## Recorded, not fixed

- **On an IBL-lit face, B3 does nothing** — the ambient tap (`:1526`) has no `L`.
- **Skin under an area/LTC panel gets no SSS at all today** (panels `continue` at
  `:1212`). Self-consistent, not broken — but a softbox portrait is *the*
  canonical skin setup. Own roadmap line.
- Penner's wrap and `subsurfaceTransmission` both brighten just past 90°; a
  material carrying both doubles the rim.
- Two latent negative-value modes in the shipped `Lo`-vs-tap mismatch (D4).
- ~~The blur's width deficit needs a downsampled chain — own spec.~~ **CLOSED by
  spec 11.14**, and it needed no chain: the cap was in the wrong UNITS. Capping
  world scatter per unit of view depth instead of render pixels makes the
  projection cancel algebraically, so the blur delivers the authored radius and
  resolution drops out. Measured, the delivered scatter went from 2.5% of its
  low-resolution value at 4K to flat within 2.9% across an 8× sweep.
- A directional key exactly along a world axis self-shadows completely (Phase 0).
- **There is no art-directed skin content in this engine.** Two fixture spheres,
  a curvature ladder, and a for-fun head. B3's only subject is a test fixture.

## Open risks

1. ~~**Crossfade seamlessness.**~~ **Measured, and worse than the risk stated.**
   The worry was that the total could be right while the character read as
   changing on a dolly, because the two terms have different shapes. The shapes
   do differ, and the total is *not* right either: 13.4% width drift across a 4×
   resolution sweep, against 8.3% with the feature off (D5 as measured). The
   `deficit ≡ 1.0` fallback does not rescue it — 10.6%, and it double-counts
   wherever the blur is unclamped, which costs the byte-identity the shipped form
   has there. Shipped as-is with the numbers recorded; the real fix is calibration
   against a blur that reaches its authored width.
2. **Tessellation noise on real character meshes.** The fixture's 48×96 sphere
   hides it and the tree has no skin asset to check against, so this risk cannot
   be retired inside this spec.
3. **The table's `a1` boundary.** The lerp between rows 3 and 4 crosses a branch
   switch; it is where the worst remaining error sits.
4. **Byte-identity of the `Lo +=`** — the `:1391-1394` precedent is strong but
   4.12's zero drift was measured, not proven.
5. **Uniform/const budget** — verify `pbr_frag` still links with the table.
