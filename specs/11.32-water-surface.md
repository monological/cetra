# Water surface for Cetra — comprehensive plan (roadmap D3, realized differently)

## Context

`siliconjungle/inkwell-webgpu-water` (MIT, © 2026 James Addison, cloned to
`<scratchpad>/inkwell-water`) is a ~2,200-line standalone WebGPU study of an FFT ocean:
three JONSWAP/TMA spectral cascades, Stockham inverse FFTs, Jacobian-selected whitecaps,
a Cox-Munk glitter BRDF, a conservative shallow-water nearshore solver, and Beer-Lambert
water optics. Measured on Apple/Metal at 1600×1000: **0.63–0.96 ms simulation,
5.4–7.0 ms render** (its render half includes its own sky/terrain, which cetra replaces).

The question was (a) what integration takes, (b) whether water should be a main engine
feature. **Verdict: yes — build it, as cetra's own subsystem, using this repo as a source
of derivations rather than an architecture to adopt.** The engine has sky, terrain, trees,
rocks, and no water; the roadmap already books it ("D3. Tessellated water — the one
flagship surface the engine does not have," `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md:848`,
**Depends on: nothing hard**). The deeper reason: water is the first surface that forces
three things cetra currently lacks and that glass already wants — volume absorption
(Beer-Lambert), a translucent-reading surface that lives in the G-buffer, and an SSR miss
path that isn't black. Water is the forcing function for all three.

**What we take from the repo** (ports nearly 1:1): the JONSWAP/TMA spectrum seeding with
directional spreading + swell (`buildSpectralOceanData`, ~110 lines CPU math), the
Stockham iFFT stage + twiddle table, the 4-packed-complex-field derivative layout,
Jacobian whitecap selection, Cox-Munk sun glitter, dielectric Fresnel, Beer-Lambert +
single-scatter water body, the camera-snapped clipmap ring vertex reconstruction, and the
refracted-sun-ray Jacobian caustic derivation. **What we don't**: its sky, terrain,
tonemap, scripted breaker (a `time*2.4 mod 72m` front with a fade to hide the reset), the
nearshore SWE solver (deferred — weakest value per line), and every hard-coded art
constant.

**Key enabling fact**: every one of its five compute kernels is a pure
gather-write-one-texel pass — no shared memory, no atomics, no scatter. That is exactly a
fullscreen fragment pass with MRT, so **GL 4.1's missing compute is not a blocker**:
~45 small ping-pong draws at 128², an idiom cetra's post chain already uses everywhere
(`run_temporal_accum` postfx.c:2029, `run_atrous` :2100, froxel volume :2283).

## Architecture decisions (settled)

1. **Single-layer water, not transparent geometry** (UE's "Single Layer Water" model).
   Water draws in its **own pass slot at `render.c:1337`** (verified: between the resolve
   block ending `:1336` and the transparent block starting `:1338`) — after the refraction
   resolve so scene colour incl. sky is samplable, after the shadow catcher, before the
   transparent/OIT lane (so translucents and particles sort over it, same argument as
   `render.c:1234-1241`). Depth writes **ON**. Writes the full G-buffer: att1 (view
   normal + SSR marker), att2 (motion vectors + linear view-Z + roughness). No OIT
   involvement. Writing att2 is what makes froxel fog and aerial perspective composite at
   the *water's* depth for free — verified: `froxel_composite_frag.glsl:52` reads
   `linDepthTex` (the att2 resolve) — no fog-system change needed above water.
   Review-verified constraints on the pass itself:
   - **Skipped under `engine->capturing`** — same hijack that excludes particles and OIT
     (`engine_resolve_scene_depth` blits at MAIN render size and re-binds
     `engine->framebuffer`, `render.c:1413-1416`), and `engine_resolve_opaque_color`'s own
     contract says captures must disable refraction (`engine.c:1809-1811`). Water is
     therefore absent from probe captures; accepted and documented.
   - **att1/att2 participation rides the existing per-frame consumer gates**:
     `engine_set_scene_draw_buffers` builds the draw list from a descriptor table of
     `this_frame` flags (`engine.c:1730-1758`) — no SSR active → marker output lands on
     GL_NONE. Water needs no descriptor edits.
   - **State bracket order matters** (from the opaque + catcher brackets,
     `render.c:1160/1196-1205/1308-1319`): `glDisable(GL_BLEND)` →
     `engine_set_scene_draw_buffers(engine, true)` → draw →
     `engine_set_scene_draw_buffers(engine, false)` → `glEnable(GL_BLEND)`; a blanket
     blend enable while the multi-target list is bound would undo the indexed
     per-attachment disables.
   - **Two matrix conventions, both required** (`pbr_vert.glsl:52,74-76`): rasterize with
     the jittered `draw_projection`, but motion vectors from the **un-jittered**
     `uCurrViewProjNoJitter`/`uPrevViewProj` pair; plus `invariant gl_Position`.
2. **Own shader program** — `pbr_frag` is at 16/16 declared samplers (`engine.h:130-134`);
   a dedicated `water_frag` gets a fresh budget. Estimated need: scene colour, scene
   depth, prefiltered IBL, BRDF LUT, 6 cascade field textures, CSM shadow array, foam
   noise ≈ 12 of 16.
3. **Geometry: camera-snapped clipmap rings** (4 nested levels, grid VBO drawn instanced,
   snap + degenerate-centre logic in the vertex shader — port of reference `:1044-1092`).
   Not tessellation: Crest/WaveWorks also use rings for water LOD. **This forfeits D3's
   stated "spends the tessellation stage" rationale — the roadmap entry and D4's soft
   dependency get re-priced honestly in the as-built notes.** Tessellation remains
   available as its own future foundation item.
4. **Simulation: Gerstner first, FFT second, behind one include.** The surface evaluation
   lives in `cetra/shaders/include/ocean.glsl` with a uniform-selected source. Gerstner
   (closed-form derivatives) proves the entire integration — pass slot, G-buffer writes,
   refraction, absorption, fog, gates — with trivial simulation risk; the FFT swaps in
   underneath without touching consumers. Wind template rules apply: `t - uDeltaTime` for
   prev-frame positions (`pbr_vert.glsl:68`), shared include across shading + shadow
   stages (`wind.glsl:2-4`), `invariant gl_Position`.
5. **Depth-as-provider, three tiers**, so the feature degrades instead of breaking:
   - **Tier 1** (no geometry): constant depth — infinite ocean in an empty scene.
   - **Tier 2** (scene depth): `waterColumn = surfaceY - reconstructedY` from
     `engine_resolve_scene_depth` (`engine.c:1870`, public, mid-frame-safe) — soft
     shoreline fade (formula exists at `particle_frag.glsl:74-79`), thickness-true
     Beer-Lambert against **arbitrary** submerged geometry. Strictly more general than
     the reference, which needs its terrain field for this.
   - **Tier 3** (optional callback): `float (*height_at)(void* ctx, float x, float z)`
     for vertex-stage shoaling (depth-attenuated displacement) — the one thing screen
     depth cannot give (circular dependency). `apps/forest` satisfies it directly:
     `terrain_height_at` is a pure function, no stored heightmap
     (`procedural/terrain.h:42-46`). `NULL` → Tier 2 behaviour.
6. **Scene-citizen lifecycle**: `scene->water`, NULL by default, owned/freed by the Scene
   (mirrors sky/probe, `scene.h:135-147`). `gi_volume.c` is the code template: lazy
   `ensure_targets` + `failed` one-shot latch, programs resolved by name, save/restore
   all touched GL state, `check_gl_error` at pass end.
   **Placement: `cetra/src/water.c/h` at the src root** (settled with user), peer of
   `sky.c`/`probe.c`/`gi_volume.c` — it is a GL-owning per-frame subsystem, not a
   `procedural/` content generator (those are pure CPU mesh/texture builders with no GL
   and no per-frame state). The CPU wave query `water_height_at(x, z, t)` stays a
   function inside `water.c` for v1; it promotes to `procedural/water_waves.c` only when
   buoyancy gives it a second consumer, the same pattern the tree generator followed.
7. **Determinism by construction**: the FFT ocean is a pure function of time
   (`phase = omega * t`), no history — frame N is state N on the headless frame clock.
   Spectrum seeded once on the CPU with the reference's deterministic RNG. This is what
   makes a water golden bakeable. The fixture `.cscn` pins exposure
   (`auto_exposure: false`) so the golden is cross-build comparable.
8. **Working-space output convention**: the reference's `waterFragment` ends in
   `linearToSrgb(aces(color))` — that tail is **dropped**. `water_frag` emits linear
   working-space HDR like `pbr_frag`, and every borrowed environment sample follows the
   house `preExposure`/`iblIntensity` conventions (`probeSample`'s conversion note,
   `ssr_frag.glsl:103-108`, is the model). Cetra's own tonemap/exposure do the rest.
   Known consequence to watch in review, not change up front: water writes real normals
   with a negative marker, the same shape as the catcher floor, so GTAO/spec-occ will
   process water pixels (the catcher comment at `render.c:1291-1293` treats that as a
   feature); if AO shimmers on animated waves, that is a finding for the review round.

## Phases (each ends at a measurement; spec committed alone first)

### Phase 1 — Foundations (each independently valuable, water not yet visible)
- **Beer-Lambert / KHR_materials_volume**: `attenuation_color` + `attenuation_distance`
  on `Material` — two **consecutive** rows in the existing "Transmission" group of
  `MATERIAL_PARAMS` (`material.c:56-59`; rows of a group must stay consecutive per the
  table's own comment). glTF parse in `import.c` inside the existing transmissive-only
  guard (`import.c:346-368`); assimp already exposes both keys
  (`AI_MATKEY_VOLUME_ATTENUATION_DISTANCE`/`_COLOR`, `ext/assimp/.../material.h:1083-1085`).
  Wire into `pbr_frag`'s transmission term at the verified insert point — multiply
  `sceneSample` (`pbr_frag.glsl:1895-1896`) by `exp(-sigma_t * thickness)` with
  `sigma_t = -log(attenuationColor) / attenuationDistance` per the KHR spec.
  **Defaults are inert** (extension absent → attenuationDistance = +inf → factor 1.0),
  so this is provably 0 px on the existing corpus; **glass gets correct absorption too**.
- **SSR: sky fallback + second marker writer — default OFF, coupled to water.**
  Today a missed ray with no probe composites exact black (`ssr_frag.glsl:92-94`) — a
  live defect. Add the third fallback tier on a postfx-published env-cubemap handle
  (publish pattern = `reflection_probe_publish_to_postfx`, `probe.c:165-181`), and the
  fallback must mirror `probeSample`'s contract exactly (`ssr_frag.glsl:91-114`):
  weighted premultiplied `(color*w, w)` with the fresnel × roughnessFade × strength
  weighting, and `* preExposure` (IBL bakes absolute radiance; an unconverted fallback
  would step in brightness at the screen edge, the same hazard `:103-106` documents for
  the probe).
  **Review finding — it cannot default ON in this spec**: `render.c:2318` sets
  `scene->shadow_catcher = scene->ibl != NULL` and the probe is opt-in (`--probe`), so
  the raiden recipe itself has an SSR floor that misses to black today. A default-ON
  fallback moves the raiden cross-build baseline — the one instrument the whole branch
  is verified against. So: `fx->ssr_sky_fallback` defaults **false**; enabling water
  turns it on (water reflections need a miss answer), and `--ssr-sky-fallback` exposes
  it standalone. Flipping the global default is a separate report-and-ask with its own
  measured diff maps.
  Water becomes the second legitimate writer of the negative-alpha marker
  (`material.h:14-22` contract) — magnitude must clear the `0.004` trace cutoff that the
  catcher's `0.002` dead-ring floor deliberately sits under (`ssr_frag.glsl:159-166`);
  water writes −1.0. Water SSR roughness ships through the existing `floorRoughness`
  scalar (`postfx.c` publish); one shared scalar for catcher + water coexistence is
  accepted for v1 (per-texel from att2.w is deferred — the catcher doesn't write att2).
- **Pass slot + lifecycle skeleton**: `water.c/h`, `scene->water` (joins the optional
  block at `scene.h:134-142`, `// ocean/lake surface (optional; owned)`), empty pass at
  `render.c:1337` under `profiler_scope_begin_if(profiler, active, "water")`, gated
  `!engine->capturing`, resolve gating widened (`:1331` predicate gains `|| water_active`;
  water calls `engine_resolve_scene_depth` itself at pass start), `--water` flag (5-6
  sites per the `--depth-prepass` pattern), ImGui group, `.cscn` `water{}` block with
  `has_*` flags.
- **Bar**: **all 21 goldens 0 px, raiden recipe 0 px** — no exception set needed, since
  both foundation changes are default-inert by construction; the gate proving the
  fallback is alive runs on the water fixture where it is on.

### Phase 2 — Gerstner lake (the whole pipeline, simple waves)
- Clipmap ring mesh + vertex snap/degenerate logic; Gerstner sum (4-6 waves) in
  `include/ocean.glsl` with analytic normals and `t - dt` prev positions; full G-buffer
  writes; Fresnel from `prefilteredMap` (unit 12, sky-following, zero new plumbing —
  IOR 1.33 → F0 0.020 already correct via `pbr_frag.glsl:1143-1145` math); refraction
  via the resolve + refracted-exit-point reprojection; Beer-Lambert from Tier 2 water
  column; CSM shadowing on the surface; DRAW_UNBOUNDED-equivalent culling exemption
  (padded bound), TAA-jittered `draw_projection` like all geometry.
- Fixture: `assets/gen_water_fixture.py` → `water_fixture.gltf` + `.cscn` (model:
  `gen_overdraw_fixture.py` — derived camera, stdlib only). A tilted submerged ramp +
  an above-water block, so one frame contains: shoreline fade, depth-graded absorption,
  reflection, refraction offset.
- Gates: `run_water_gate` — `water-live` (AE ≥ min vs no flag; "three zeros are also
  what a dead flag produces"), `water-det` (two runs same build, 0 px — proves the frame
  clock purity), `water-row` (profiler row present with flag, absent without —
  `gpu-off`/`gpu-dof` templates), `water-draws` (submission integers: exact ring draw
  count), `water-column` (analytic: mean linear luma monotone along the ramp's depth
  gradient, relative read per `mask-opaque`, `gates.py:2792`).
- Golden: one recipe in `goldens.py` (corpus 21 → 22) + committed PNG.
- **Bar**: gates green; profiler "water" GPU ms recorded at the fixture and at
  1600×900; noise floor measured first, twice, same config.

### Phase 3 — FFT spectral ocean (the flagship look)
- CPU: port `buildSpectralOceanData` (JONSWAP/TMA + directional spreading + secondary
  swell + twiddle table) to C in `water.c`; upload 3 cascades' initial spectra +
  dispersion (RGBA32F 128²).
- GPU: `water_spectrum_frag.glsl` (evolve, MRT2 out) + `water_fft_frag.glsl` (Stockham
  stage: 2 texel reads + twiddle row, MRT2 out), driven as fragment ping-pong —
  2 axes × 7 stages × 3 cascades + 3 evolves ≈ **45 draws at 128², expected ~1.5-2 ms**
  (reference compute half: 0.63-0.96 ms).
- Surface: `include/ocean.glsl` FFT source — long+medium cascades displace the mesh
  (with the bound-harmonic crest sharpening, reference `:996-998`), short cascade shades
  only, slope→roughness transition with distance (`:1191-1197` — the anti-shimmer
  design); Jacobian whitecaps (`:1265-1295` selection, breakup noise from cetra's CPU
  `noise.h` baked to a small tiling texture); Cox-Munk glitter (`:888-906`) with wind
  direction/speed as scene params, not constants.
- **Bar**: `water-det` still 0 px (time-pure evolution); simulation + render ms recorded
  per profiler; the Gerstner path stays selectable (`--water-waves gerstner|fft`) as the
  bisect lever.

### Phase 4 — Shoreline / Tier 3 + forest integration
- `height_at` provider on the water struct; depth-attenuated displacement + foam band
  from `waterColumn` + Jacobian (shoaling approximation — no SWE solver); wet-sand
  darkening deferred.
- `apps/forest --water`: provider = `terrain_height_at`, water level a flag. Forest is
  **not pixel-deterministic** (35k px run-to-run, Hillaire sky) — forest arms assert
  **integers only** (submission counts), any pixel claim uses `--no-sky`.
- **Bar**: forest integer arms green; screenshots recorded; frame cost at forest's
  profile recorded against the Phase 2/3 numbers.

### Phase 5 — Underwater + caustics
- **Camera-inside-medium**: per-frame side test against the water height; froxel inject
  gains a second medium (`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:181`'s
  `P.y < floorY ? 0 : air` becomes a water/air select with water sigma/scatter from the
  water system, published like `sky_publish_to_postfx`); water surface renders backfaces
  with flipped N when submerged (reference `:1198-1199`).
- **Screen-space caustics** as a small postfx pass before the atmosphere composite:
  reconstruct world pos from att2 linear-Z (`include/depth.glsl` helpers), for pixels
  below the water plane project along the refracted sun ray and evaluate the medium+short
  cascade Jacobians (the reference's derivation at `:763-784`, delivered at visible
  strength this time, on **arbitrary** submerged geometry — no `pbr_frag` sampler cost,
  which is what keeps Wall 1 untouched).
- **Bar**: underwater golden; caustics arm (luma variance on a submerged plane with the
  pass on vs off); goldens outside water scenes 0 px.

## Deliberately deferred (each with the reason)

- **Nearshore shallow-water solver / breakers / swash** — the reference's weakest output
  per line (its breaker is scripted, not simulated; its own research notes say a real
  plunging lip needs a different representation). Tier 3 shoaling covers the visible
  shoreline need. Revisit only with a game-driven requirement.
- **Per-texel SSR roughness** — needs an att2 contract change (catcher writes no att2);
  scalar water roughness is correct for calm-to-moderate water. Own mini-spec if chop
  demands it.
- **Tessellation pipeline** — D3's original foundation promise; unneeded for water via
  clipmap. Stays a bookable item for D4/POM silhouettes; roadmap note records the
  re-pricing.
- **Jolt buoyancy** — vendored (`ext/JoltC/.../Water/`) but unwired; game-layer work
  (`physics.h` exposure + fixed-step call for tagged bodies), not rendering. Natural
  follow-up spec once water ships.
- **Wet-sand / waterline material response** — needs terrain material participation;
  separate item.
- **OIT interaction of water with translucents *below* the surface** — translucents
  behind water are refracted via scene colour only if they were in the opaque resolve
  (they are not); accepted limitation, same class as the existing SSR-vs-catcher
  visibility note in AGENTS.md.

## Review round (plan verified against both trees before implementation)

Every load-bearing claim was re-read firsthand; four corrections landed:

1. **The SSR sky fallback flipped from default-ON to default-OFF** — `render.c:2318`
   gives every `-e` scene (raiden included) an SSR catcher floor, and the probe is
   opt-in, so default-ON would have moved the raiden cross-build baseline the branch is
   verified against. The original Phase 1 bar was self-contradicting; now it is clean.
2. **The water pass is capture-excluded** (`!engine->capturing`) — forced by two
   existing contracts, not a choice: the depth resolve's framebuffer re-bind
   (`render.c:1413-1416`) and `engine_resolve_opaque_color`'s "captures must disable
   refraction" note (`engine.c:1809-1811`).
3. **Motion vectors use the un-jittered matrix pair** while rasterization uses the
   jittered projection (`pbr_vert.glsl:74-76`) — the plan previously stated only the
   jittered half.
4. **The reference's output tail (`aces` + `linearToSrgb`) is dropped**, and all
   environment samples carry `preExposure` — the reference tonemaps in-shader; cetra
   must not double-tonemap or mix radiance scales.

Confirmed intact: the `render.c:1337` slot and its surrounding gates; the
`engine_set_scene_draw_buffers` descriptor gating (water needs no descriptor edits); the

# As built

## Phase 1a — volume absorption (commit `41179de`)

Landed as planned. `attenuation_color`/`attenuation_distance` on `Material`, two
consecutive rows in the Transmission group, parsed inside the existing
transmissive-only guard, applied to `sceneSample` in `pbr_frag`.

**Verified:** 22 goldens 0 px; raiden cross-build **0 (0)** against a same-build
floor measured at **0 (0)** first; all 122 pre-existing gate arms still pass.

**Three things the plan had wrong or did not know:**

1. **The golden corpus was already 22, not 21.** `mask_fixture` landed on master
   in `1785e43` after the plan was written, so the corpus goes 22 → 23, not
   21 → 22.
2. **`--tonemap passthrough` is unreachable from the CLI**, contrary to
   `CLAUDE.md`'s flag list. `render_args.h:148-150` states the sentinel
   deliberately coincides with `POSTFX_TONEMAP_PASSTHROUGH = 0` because
   passthrough "is a blit path and never user-set". Left alone; the gate's arms
   were made tone-curve-independent instead, which is the house rule anyway
   (`gates.py:2806`). Measured cost of the curve: PBR Neutral moves the middle
   panel's green:red from an analytic 0.35 to 0.3016.
3. **`glass_fixture` could not have measured this** — its two panels sit over
   *different* backdrop colours, so a channel ratio there confounds absorption
   with which backdrop quad each panel happens to cover. Hence a new fixture
   rather than an extension of that one.

## Phase 1b — WITHDRAWN. The SSR sky fallback was already built and rejected.

The plan called the black-on-miss (`ssr_frag.glsl:92-94`) "a live defect" and
scheduled a sky tier for it. It is not a defect. `probe.c:157-164`:

> Deliberately NO environment tier on probe-less scenes: publishing the IBL
> prefilter as the miss fallback **was built and rejected** -- SSR only shades
> the shadow catcher, and an invisible catcher that mirrors the sky over a
> darker background prints as a glowing pool the size of its quad. The
> environment answer is only right when a probe's parallax box re-grounds it;
> a probe-less miss stays empty and the march's own fades hide the reach limits.

So the exact change this phase proposed has already been tried and backed out for
a reason the plan did not know, and re-landing it would reintroduce the glowing
pool on every `-e` scene, which is every scene with a catcher.

**What replaces it: nothing needs to.** Water reflects the sky through
`prefilteredMap` in its own fragment shader — which the plan already established
as the one piece needing zero new plumbing, sky-following and correct at IOR
1.33. SSR was only ever the upgrade path.

**Consequence for Phase 1c/2:** water does **not** write the negative-alpha SSR
marker, so the `material.h:14-22` contract keeps exactly two producers and needs
no edit. `--ssr-sky-fallback` is not added.

**SSR-on-water is deferred with a named obstacle**, which is more than the plan
had: the marker cannot distinguish water from the catcher (both would write
negative alpha, and the catcher's magnitude legitimately reaches 1.0), so a
per-surface opt-in needs a channel the G-buffer does not currently have spare —
not merely the roughness and fallback work the plan listed.

## Phase 1c — the subsystem and its pass slot (commit `eb12da9`)

Landed at `render.c:1337` as planned, opt-in and inert when off: 22 goldens and
all 122 gate arms unchanged. Two hazards hit while building it, both already
documented elsewhere in the tree and both found by GL errors rather than by
reading: a `samplerCube` has to be pointed at its unit even with no IBL to
sample (`render.c:441-450` records the same thing costing a fixture 62,009 px),
and the pass has to sit out cube captures.

The phase shipped a still plane rather than an empty pass. A skeleton whose only
measurement is "0 px because it does nothing" proves the plumbing is inert and
nothing else; a flat surface with real optics retires the whole integration risk
— pass order, depth write, G-buffer write, the resolve tap, absorption, the
environment lobe — in the same commit.

## Phase 2 — Gerstner waves, fixture, gates, golden

Gerstner sum with analytic derivatives in `include/ocean.glsl`, the fixture, five
gate arms and a golden. **Corpus 22 → 23.**

**Measured:** `water-det` 0 px across two runs (the surface is a pure function of
the frame clock, which is what makes the golden bakeable); `water-live` 299,283 px
of 480,000; absorption R/B 0.2133 → 0.1452 → 0.0938 with distance (×1.47, ×1.55);
dry ramp R/B 2.0743.

### The shadow catcher is coplanar with water, and it cost an hour

The first fixture renders had a large wandering hole in the water with the ground
showing through. It was not the discard, not the refraction bend, and not the
grid: `render.c:2342` sets `scene->shadow_catcher = scene->ibl != NULL`, so any
scene with an environment gets a catcher quad **at y = 0** — which is where a
water plane usually is. GL_LESS then resolves the tie per pixel off whichever
primitive interpolated its depth differently, and because one is a 6-vertex quad
and the other a 128² grid, the seam wanders in an organic band that reads as a
hole rather than as z-fighting.

**Fixed by suppressing the catcher whenever water is active** (`render.c:1242`).
Two invisible stand-in ground planes in one scene is not a configuration worth
arbitrating per pixel, and water is the more specific one. The catcher's own gate
arms and every golden are unaffected because water is off in all of them.

**Method note worth keeping.** Three hypotheses were wrong before the right one
(a discard threshold, then the refraction validity guard, then the grid). What
ended it was instrumenting instead of reasoning: classify `path` into four flat
colours, then flood every water fragment magenta. The magenta frame showed the
grey region was *not water at all*, which eliminated every shading explanation in
one render. The refraction validity guard added along the way is kept — it is
correct on its own terms, it just was not this bug.

### Deferred out of this phase, with reasons

- **The clipmap rings.** The plan committed to four camera-snapped nested rings.
  Not built: on an indexed grid the reference's per-cell degeneration does not
  translate (vertices are shared, so collapsing one moves every adjacent cell),
  and the honest alternative — a real annulus mesh instanced per level — has a
  snap-mismatch overlap between levels that is a coplanar tie on the *same*
  surface, which is the class of bug that just cost this phase an hour. A single
  grid sized by `extent` is correct and finite; the rings are what an ocean to
  the horizon needs, so they belong with the FFT rather than ahead of it.
- **Soft shoreline coverage.** The waterline is a hard `discard` and therefore
  aliases. Derivative-width coverage needs an alpha, and the surface is in the
  opaque lane precisely so it can write depth. Alpha-to-coverage is the plausible
  route and it is its own change.
- **`.cscn` water authoring.** The fixture and golden drive water from CLI flags.
  A `water{}` block is additive and buys nothing this phase measures.

### An unrelated gate arm is refusing to decide on this machine

`prepass-crossover` fails in both of two consecutive runs — **not** as a
regression but as the arm declining: its own noise floor measured 6% then 5%
against a 3% ceiling, and the signal it is trying to read swung +3% then +10%
between the runs. It is a timing arm on `overdraw_tiles`, where water never runs.
It passed twice earlier in the same session, before this machine had spent an hour
on continuous builds and renders. Its thresholds were left alone: widening a bar
to accommodate a noisy host is exactly the budget drift `gates.py:64-74` argues
against.

## Phase 3 — the spectral ocean

Three JONSWAP/TMA cascades, evolved and inverse-transformed every frame as
fragment ping-pong: 3 x (1 evolve + 14 Stockham stages) = **45 draws at 128²**.
`--water-waves gerstner|fft`, Gerstner still the default.

**The central claim held: it is deterministic.** 45 ping-pong passes over three
cascades, and two runs diff **0 px**. The spectrum is seeded once by the
reference's PRNG, ported exactly rather than swapped for `rand()` — the sequence
IS the ocean's identity, so a different one is a different sea — and evolution is
a pure function of `omega * t` with no history. `water-fft-det` asserts it and
`water-fft-live` asserts the surface actually differs from Gerstner (344,638 px),
because a cascade chain that transformed to zero would pass the first arm alone.

### The cost is CPU, not GPU, and that is the interesting number

Measured at 800x500, single samples, water row only:

| model | GPU | CPU |
|---|---|---|
| Gerstner | 0.514 ms | 0.545 ms |
| FFT | 0.749 ms | 1.730 ms |

**The 45 passes add 0.24 ms of GPU and 1.19 ms of CPU.** The GPU half is
unremarkable — 45 x 128² is 737k fragments, nothing — and cheaper than the
reference's 0.63-0.96 ms compute simulation, which also carried a nearshore solver
this port does not. The CPU half is the price of expressing a compute dispatch as a
draw: 45 framebuffer binds, 45 uniform-set groups and 45 `glDrawArrays`, where
compute would have been 45 dispatches on one pipeline. So the honest statement of
"GL 4.1 has no compute" is not that the FFT is slow — it is that it moves to the
submission budget, which is the resource `apps/forest` already showed is the
scarce one.

### Two things the plan had wrong

1. **The spectral height must NOT take the amplitude knob.** The first
   implementation multiplied it by `waterAmplitude`, whose lake default is 0.06,
   and crushed a physical spectrum to 6% of itself — the ocean rendered nearly
   flat. The spectrum already encodes its sea state through wind speed and fetch,
   so the knob is Gerstner-only now and a calmer spectral ocean is a lower wind
   speed. This is the mirror of the steepness note in Phase 2: a parameter that
   means something at one scale is not free to mean it at another.
2. **`water_frag` had to include `ocean.glsl`.** The plan treated the include as
   the vertex stage's alone. The fragment stage never re-evaluates the surface,
   but it reads the SHORT cascade — the band that by design never reaches the
   mesh — so it needs the same samplers and the same wave-model switch.

### Confirmed working, and worth naming

- **The geometry-to-BRDF transition.** The short band's resolved slope fades out
  between 42 m and 118 m and its energy moves into roughness instead, which is
  what keeps the horizon from shimmering while the simulated detail is still
  there up close.
- **Jacobian-selected foam.** Whitewater appears where the horizontal map
  compresses, not where the surface is tall, and the first render with the
  amplitude bug fixed put a whitecap exactly on a steepening crest.

### Still deferred, and Phase 3 sharpened the reason

**The clipmap is now the binding limitation, not a nicety.** A uniform grid has
uniform WORLD-space density, so at `--water-extent 400` the near field is a few
6 m cells spanning most of the screen and the cascades are invisible there while
the far field resolves them beautifully. The fixture works at extent 70 because
that is where the grid pitch happens to match the medium band. Camera-snapped
rings put the samples where the pixels are, which is exactly the problem visible
here — so they are the next thing this feature needs, and the Phase 2 reasoning
for deferring them (no ocean to justify the risk) has now expired.

## Phase 4 — the bed provider, and forest floods

Tier 3 landed, and `apps/forest --water --water-level <f>` floods the terrain: a
clean waterline against the hills, rocks half-submerged with their submerged parts
transmitting, no z-fighting anywhere (the catcher suppression from Phase 2 is what
buys that). All 126 gate arms and all 23 goldens unchanged.

**Cost in forest**, 800x450, single sample: water **0.535 ms GPU / 0.226 ms CPU**
against an opaque pass of 78.9 ms — 0.7% of the frame.

### A C callback cannot be called from a vertex shader

The plan specified `float (*height_at)(void*, float, float)` and had the vertex
stage shoal against it. That does not work as written: GLSL cannot call into C. The
provider is still the right interface — `terrain_height_at` satisfies it directly,
which is the whole payoff — but it reaches the shader as a **baked 256² R32F
heightfield** over the drawn extent, sampled in the vertex stage.

Baking is legitimate rather than a workaround, for a reason specific to this
surface: the drawn extent is a fixed square about the origin, so it is the same
region every frame and therefore the same samples every frame. The resolution is
chosen for the shoaling RAMP and not for the terrain, because per-fragment water
depth still comes from the resolved scene depth — exact, and against geometry no
heightfield describes. The bake answers only the question screen depth cannot: how
far to shorten a wave in the stage before its position exists.

`water_invalidate_bed` re-arms it if the bed itself moves.

### Shoaling multiplies displacement, not height

Both wave models scale their whole displacement by the shoal factor, horizontal
term included, and the derivatives carry the same factor so the normal stays the
normal *of* the displaced surface. Scaling height alone would slide the surface
sideways across a beach it is no longer above.

### Deferred

- **A dedicated `forest-water` arm.** Water submits no meshes, so
  `profiler.c:504-505` gives it no SUBMISSION row and there is no integer for a
  forest arm to assert — and forest is not pixel-deterministic, so a pixel arm is
  not available either. The GPU-row presence is already covered by `water-row` on
  the deterministic fixture, so a forest arm would restate it against a worse
  instrument. Recorded rather than skipped silently.
- **Wet-sand / waterline response on the terrain material.** Needs terrain
  material participation; the water side has the shoal factor ready for it.

## Phase 5 — caustics, the submerged interface, and one thing NOT done

**Caustics land, and they are the capability the reference study does not have.**
Light crossing the surface is focused by the surface's own curvature, so the
brightness on the bed is read from the cascade compression where the refracted sun
ray CROSSED the surface. Measured 12,509 px on the fixture, `--no-water-caustics`
the bisect lever, `water-caustic` the arm.

Two deliberate departures from the plan, both improvements:

1. **In `water_frag`, not a postfx pass.** The plan specified
   `water_caustics_frag.glsl` reconstructing world position from aux linear-Z. Doing
   it where the refracted sample is taken is physically the same place — the bed's
   radiance is exactly what `bed` holds — and it needs no publish, no new pass and
   no postfx sequencing. It covers arbitrary submerged geometry seen *through* the
   surface, which is the case that matters. What it does not cover is submerged
   geometry with no water surface between it and the camera; that is the postfx
   version's one advantage and it is not worth the plumbing yet.
2. **FFT only, and that is principled rather than a gap.** Caustics come from
   compression, and the Gerstner path's steepness is clamped precisely so its
   mapping cannot compress. The same reasoning already gates its foam. A Gerstner
   caustic would have to be invented rather than derived.

**The submerged interface works**: backfaces with a flipped normal, and the optical
path becomes the sight line rather than the depth behind the surface, because from
below the body is between the eye and the interface. 438,587 px against the
above-water frame; `water-submerged` asserts it. The side test compares the eye
against the STILL level rather than the displaced surface — a camera within a wave
height of the waterline would otherwise switch models several times a second as
crests pass, resetting every temporal history in the frame each time.

### NOT DONE: the underwater medium on everything that is not water

**Underwater is incomplete and the docs say so.** The surface reads correctly from
below, but the seabed seen while submerged is not absorbed at all — it renders as
though in air. Applying the medium to non-water geometry is the two-medium froxel
change the plan scheduled, and it was not attempted here.

The reason it is deferred rather than rushed: it is a genuine multi-system change
(a second medium in `froxel_inject_frag`, a submerged flag and the water's
sigma/scatter published to PostFX, and the fog volume is OFF by default so
underwater would silently depend on `--fog`), and landing it badly at the end of a
long session is how the coplanar-catcher class of bug gets written. It is booked
with its shape known instead.

So: `--water` from above is finished work; `--water` with the camera under the
surface is a correct interface and an unfinished volume.

## Review round (commit `70f16ec`)

Three background reviews: GL-state/correctness, comment-standard conformance, and a
gate-blindness audit. They found **19 distinct problems**, and the split between them
is the useful part — the code reviews found bugs, and the gate audit found that the
arms guarding that code were pointed at the wrong things.

### Corrections to claims made earlier in THIS spec

- **"Confirmed working: the geometry-to-BRDF transition"** (Phase 3) was **false**.
  Roughness was driven by the already-faded short slope, so distant water went
  smooth in both channels rather than handing the removed slope energy over.
  Written now as the unfaded slope times `(1 - fade)`, starting from the authored
  roughness instead of discarding it.
- **Phase 3's "derivatives are free"** was true of the transform and false of the
  surface: the bound-harmonic crest term changed the height and its derivative was
  never added to the slope, so the normal described a different surface than the
  raster — the failure `ocean.glsl`'s own header exists to prevent. The reference
  study has this right (`longSlope * (1 + 0.28 * longHeight)`) and the port dropped
  it.
- **Phase 1c's `t - dt` prev-position claim** does not hold on the spectral path.
  The cascades hold one instant, so `oceanEvaluate(p, t - dt)` returns the current
  surface and a spectral ocean reports **camera motion only** to TAA and motion
  blur. Stated in the shader now, the redundant re-evaluation dropped, and fixing
  it properly needs a retained previous-frame cascade set.
- **The `water-live` figure of 299,283 px was not water.** `--water` suppresses the
  shadow catcher; the fixture has one; its quad covers ~62% of the frame and
  299,283/480,000 is 62.4%. The arm would have passed with every water fragment
  discarded. Re-measured with shadows held out of both sides: **329,312 px**.
- **`--water-extent` implies `--water`**, so the first attempt at a corrected
  off-frame still had water on and the arm compared a frame with itself (0 px). The
  flag lists are composed from an explicit base now.

### Live bugs fixed

`--no-refraction` was silently defeated whenever water was in the scene (water set
`engine->scene_color_this_frame`, the flag's only enforcement point). Water wrote
metalness 1.0 into the albedo attachment, costing it all SSGI indirect diffuse and
most of its AO. Cascade samplers sat at units 16-21, outside the range `render.c`
static-asserts. Gerstner shoaling never reached the horizontal term because `q` is
amplitude's reciprocal and the factor cancelled. Backfacing crest fragments
refracted with a normal facing away from the eye. A spectral-init failure path left
the window framebuffer bound for every subsequent pass; a failed allocation
corrupted the ocean with no log line; blend was set rather than restored; steepness
was documented as bounded but never clamped.

### What the gate audit changed about how these arms are trusted

Three of nine could not fail as written. The pattern in all three was the same:
comparing two renders that differed in more than the one thing the arm named. Every
water flag list is now composed from a single base, and `water-submerged` reads
absorption falling along the submerged sight line instead of counting pixels on a
change that moves 91% of the frame — with its boxes placed from a row scan, because
the underside directly overhead turned out to be wave-dominated and never was the
clean ramp the plan assumed. The absorption gate gained the panel-presence floor it
lacked, which is the hole `run_mask_gate` documents at `MASK_LIT_FLOOR`.

**Known blind spot, recorded rather than implied:** the fixture sets no height
provider, so `bedAvailable` is 0 and the shoaling factor and shore-foam band are
dead in every gate frame. They are reachable only through `apps/forest --water`,
which has no deterministic arm to hang them on.

**Verified after:** all 132 gate arms pass, all 23 goldens 0 px, and the raiden
cross-build recipe **0 (0)** against the pre-branch baseline.
