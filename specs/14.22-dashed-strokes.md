# cetra 14.22: dashed strokes

## Context

pcb 6.5 draws what a version had and now lacks, or had elsewhere, as a dashed ghost of it. The
canvas draws no dashes, so pcb cuts its ghost's lines into dashes itself, and a review of that
phase found the dash belongs in the canvas: a pattern every stroked shape honours. Each gap,
checked against the code at cetra `d94032f3`:

- **No shape has a dash.** `CanvasPaint` is a layer, a style, a net and an object
  (`canvas.h:158-163`); the shapes have widths and strokes and nothing along them (`:165-227`). Each
  kind's distance in the fragment stage measures across the shape only (`canvas_frag.glsl:43-152`),
  and nothing there knows how far along a shape a fragment is.
- **Nothing carries a place along a path from piece to piece.** A polyline is its segments added
  one by one with nothing passed between them (`canvas_edit.c:237-244`), as is each ring of a
  stroked polygon (`:750-760`). A segment or an arc cut at the tile grid gives each piece its own
  ends and nothing of where on the shape it began (`:185-217`, `:333-357`).
- **The record has no room for one.** A record is 48 bytes (`canvas_record.h:46-52`). An arc's
  measures are full, its half width, its centre's offset and its radius (`:18-20`), and a box's are
  full too (`:23`). A segment has three floats spare and a circle four, but a cut arc, the one shape
  that most needs its place along, has none. Growing every record to 64 bytes would add a third to
  every shape of a board, and to every upload (the `canvas-uploads` arm counts 48 bytes a record,
  `scripts/gates.py:31483`), to dash a few ghosts.
- **A store is generic over the record it holds.** `CANVAS_STORES` and its size and `u`-offset
  tables (`canvas.c:182-185`) are what a tile's runs, the upload (`:817-874`), the removal by object
  (`:541-568`) and the clear (`:573-600`) loop over. A third kind of record costs a table entry, a
  vertex array (`_store_gl`, `:747-775`) and a draw in each pass (`_draw_layer`, `:1023-1060`).
- **A record's flags have room**: ten bits, five used (`canvas_constants.glsl:20-24`,
  `canvas_record.h:54-56`).
- **Detail by zoom has its precedents**: a line is never drawn under a pixel
  (`canvas_vert.glsl:77`), and `text_min_px` and `shape_min_px` are settings (`canvas.h:130-132`,
  read at `canvas_vert.glsl:157` and `:180`).
- **pcb dashes by hand** (`apps/pcb/src/window/schematic_shapes.c`):
  - `Pen.dash` (`:39-44`), from the drawing's dash for lines and text, 0 for fills (`:57`);
  - `segment` cuts a line into flat-ended segments, a dash and a gap each `GHOST_DASH` (`:85-101`,
    `:389`), each line starting the pattern afresh;
  - `polyline` gives up the polyline for those segments (`:103-113`);
  - `unit_draw` turns a dashed rectangle into four of them (`:158-163`) and a polygon into its
    outline (`:171-175`), so a polygon with no width, which is filled, loses its fill and draws as
    dashes a pixel wide, a segment of no width being drawn a pixel wide (`canvas_vert.glsl:77`);
  - circles and arcs stay solid (`:179-189`);
  - a ghost costs a shape a dash, not a shape a line.
- **A style is the wrong home in pcb too.** `SSTYLE_MOVED` is "a moved thing's ghost, and the ring
  where it is now" (`schematic_shapes.h:32`): the ghost is dashed and the ring round the unit where
  it now stands is not (`schematic_shapes.c:424-427`). The ghost's text takes the ghost's style
  (`:52`), and is drawn translucent, not dashed.
- **Dashes are on the roadmap's list left for later**, "for schematic buses and courtyards"
  (`docs/eda-roadmap.md:126`, `specs/14.0-the-2d-canvas.md:351`).
- **The canvas has one way to end a stroke and one way to turn a corner.** A segment and an arc end
  round or, `flat_ends`, flat at the end point (`canvas.h:165-185`); a polyline's corners are its
  segments' round ends overlapping (`canvas.h:207`), and a stroked box's outer corners are round
  however square its outline (`boxDistance`, `canvas_frag.glsl:146-152`). PDF, SVG, Cairo and Skia
  give a stroke a cap -- butt, round or square -- and a join -- mitre, round or bevel -- and dash it
  by cutting the path into pieces along its length, each piece stroked with the cap at its ends and
  the join at any corner inside it. A schematic's outlines and a board's courtyards are drawn with
  square corners there; wires join round.

Branch and spec: `14.22-dashed-strokes`, off cetra's master (`d94032f3`), rebased onto `a3bb41ea`
once 14.21 merged.

## The design

Revised at phase 1's look (As built): a first design dashed with flat dash ends and round joins of
its own, and its corners notched where a joint fell near a dash's end. A stroke's ends and corners
are now the paint's cap and join, as PDF's are, and a dash is a piece of the stroke drawn with them.

### How a stroke is drawn is the paint's (`canvas.h`)

```c
// How a stroke's ends are drawn: round, flat at the end point, or flat half its width past it.
typedef enum CanvasCap {
    CANVAS_CAP_ROUND,
    CANVAS_CAP_BUTT,
    CANVAS_CAP_SQUARE,
} CanvasCap;

// How a stroke turns a corner: round, its outer edges met in a point, or that point cut off. A
// mitre longer than four widths is drawn as a bevel, as SVG has it.
typedef enum CanvasJoin {
    CANVAS_JOIN_ROUND,
    CANVAS_JOIN_MITRE,
    CANVAS_JOIN_BEVEL,
} CanvasJoin;

// A stroke drawn in dashes, in units: `length` drawn, then `gap` left, over and over, the shape
// starting `phase` into that pattern. Solid when length or gap is 0.
typedef struct CanvasDash {
    int64_t length;
    int64_t gap;
    int64_t phase;
} CanvasDash;

typedef struct CanvasPaint {
    CanvasLayer* layer;
    uint16_t style;
    uint32_t net;    // 0 = none
    uint32_t object; // 0 = none; what canvas_edit_remove_object removes by
    CanvasCap cap;   // a stroke's ends and its dashes' ends
    CanvasJoin join; // a stroke's corners
    CanvasDash dash; // {0} = solid
} CanvasPaint;
```

- **On the paint, beside one another**, as PDF's graphics state holds them, and the shape its
  geometry and its width. A paint of zeros is round, round and solid: every shape drawn today draws
  the same, and its records are byte for byte what they are now.
- **`flat_ends` goes** from `CanvasSegment` and `CanvasArc`: a flat end is `CANVAS_CAP_BUTT`. pcb
  sets it in a few places, which move to the paint.
- **Not on the style.** A style-level dash or join could change after shapes were added in it, so
  every record would carry its place along and its neighbours in case: 64 bytes a shape, or a
  style that never changes. And pcb draws a dashed ghost and a solid ring in one style. The edit
  bakes the paint's stroke into the records in double precision, so only a dashed shape pays for
  its pattern; changing one is an edit, not a table write. pcb clears and refills its ghost layers
  with every comparison anyway (`schematic_shapes.c:477-480`).
- **Refused by name**, beside the widths and strokes: a dash whose length or gap is negative, or
  `2^30` units or more; a cap or join that is none of the three. A refused paint refuses its shape,
  whole.
- **The units are the app's**, the lengths it states, never scaled by anything.

**Caps**: a segment's, an arc's and an open polyline's two ends, and every dash's two ends; a
closed shape has no ends but its dashes'. A butt end stops at the end point and a square one half
the stroke's width past it, square across the stroke, along the radius on a curve and along the
tangent past an arc's end.

**Joins**: a polyline's points between two segments, every point of a stroked polygon's rings, and a
stroked box's four corners when its corner radius is 0. A round join is the round end the two
segments share today. A mitre extends each segment's outer edge to where they meet; past four widths
from the corner, it is cut square across the corner's bisector at half the width, a bevel. A
rounded box's corners are curves and take no join. Each segment draws its own side of the corner,
up to the corner's bisector, so the two sides meet there with no overlap and no crack.

**Dashes**: the stroke cut into pieces along its length, as PDF cuts the path:

| Shape | Dashed | The pattern starts |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | always | at `a`, running to `b` |
| Polyline | always | at its first point, carried through every point; a repeated point adds nothing |
| Arc | always | at `a`, running to `b`, whichever way round the arc goes |
| Circle | when stroked | at its rightmost point, counter-clockwise |
| Box | when stroked | at the middle of its bottom side in its own frame, counter-clockwise |
| Polygon | when stroked | each ring at its first point, in the order given |
| Polygon, circle, box filled | never: a fill is solid | |
| Text | never: a glyph has no length along | |

- **Each dash is a piece of the stroke**: its ends take the paint's cap, and a corner inside it the
  paint's join. A corner in a gap draws nothing; a shape's own end inside a dash takes the cap, and
  one in a gap draws nothing. A round or square cap reaches past the dash's length, as PDF's does.
- **A closed shape's pattern is stretched to a whole number of periods round it**, the nearest, and
  at least one: a circle, a stroked box, a closed polyline and each ring of a stroked polygon. Its
  dashes then meet themselves where it closes, with no seam of a part dash. The phase is stretched
  with the pattern. An open shape's dashes are their stated lengths.
- **A segment from a point to itself is drawn solid**, its dot being no length to dash.

**`Canvas`, under SETTINGS:**

```c
float dash_min_px; // a dashed shape whose dash or gap spans fewer pixels is drawn solid
```

2 when made, so a pattern on screen is never finer than two pixels on and two off. Below it the
dashes beat against the pixel grid and crawl as the view pans. Drawn solid, a dashed line zoomed out
keeps its weight, as a line thinner than a pixel is kept a pixel wide.

### How it reaches the GPU

Still drawn as it is now: a quad a record and each pixel's distance from the shape worked out in the
fragment stage. The CPU only writes, once per edit, what the record needs to know of its stroke.

- **Caps in the head's flags**: a butt end is today's `FLAG_FLAT_*`, and `FLAG_SQUARE_A` and `_B`
  are new; neither is round. A cut end keeps `FLAG_CUT_*`, and a dashed record's dash ends read the
  paint's cap from its pattern, so a piece between two cuts still has it.
- **Joins in a segment's three spare measures** (`f.yzw`): the direction of the segment before `a`
  and after `b`, as angles, and a number past a turn for an end with none; the join's kind. Only
  a segment is joined -- a polyline and a polygon's rings are segments -- and a segment has three
  floats spare, so a solid shape's record stays 48 bytes. A round join with no dash writes nothing,
  being two round ends, so a solid polyline's records are unchanged. A box's join is its spare point
  (`p.w`). The angles are mirrored with the view, as the points are.
- **A third store a layer**, `CANVAS_DASHES`, beside `CANVAS_SHAPES` and `CANVAS_FILLS`, of 64-byte
  records:

  ```c
  // A dashed shape's record: a shape record and its pattern, in units.
  typedef struct CanvasDashRecord {
      CanvasRecord record;
      float dash[4]; // length, gap, its place in the pattern at the record's a, the cap
  } CanvasDashRecord;
  ```

  The pattern's lengths are the stretched ones on a closed shape, and the place is reduced by the
  period in double on the edit's thread, so it is a float under one period however long the path.
- **Its head says so**: `CANVAS_FLAG_DASHED`, the record carries a pattern, and
  `CANVAS_FLAG_DASH_BACK`, the pattern runs from `b`: a clockwise arc, whose ends the edit swapped.
  The vertex stage reads the pattern only under the first, so the shape store's vertex array leaves
  attribute 3 disabled and nothing reads it.
- **The edit**: `_segment` and `_polyline` take the distance along the path at their first point and
  each point's neighbours; a piece of a cut segment adds its share of the length, a piece of a cut
  arc its share of the arc's, and a closed ring sums its length before its first segment so its
  pattern can be stretched. Fills and edges take none of it.
- **`canvas_apply`** puts a piece flagged dashed into the dashed store and the rest where they go
  now. A dashed record's `u` is where a shape record's is (`CANVAS_ITEM_U`), so the tile bound, the
  object index, removal by object, the clear and the tags work unchanged.
- **The uploads are a tile's three runs**, each a range of its layer's store, uploaded alone.
- **A draw a pass for a layer that holds dashes**, with the shape program and the dashed store's
  vertex array. The order within a layer is not defined (`canvas.h:93-98`).
- **The vertex stage** turns the pattern into pixels with `uPixelsPerUnit`, as it does every length,
  and hands the fragment stage `flat out vec4 vDash`: the dash, the period, the place at `a`, in
  pixels, and the cap; a period of 0 for solid, which a solid record and a pattern under
  `uDashMinPx` both give. A quad reaches a square cap's half width past each end, and a mitre's
  reach past a joined one.
- **The fragment stage**:
  - **ends**: a square end is a butt end moved out half the width;
  - **joins**: a joined end is the segment's body run on to the corner's bisector, cut there; a mitre
    past its limit is cut again square across the bisector at half the width; a round join is the
    round end it is now;
  - **dashes**: the place along the shape, as the first design measured it -- a segment's own
    `along`, an arc's turn times its radius and past an end the tangent, a circle's turn from its
    rightmost point, a box's rounded outline from the middle of its bottom side -- held at a joined
    end to the corner itself, so a corner inside a dash is the join's and one in a gap is empty.
    The nearest dash's two ends then cut the stroke with the cap, butt, round or square, measured
    along the path and across from the centreline, on a curve at the fragment's own radius;
  - **a cut end**: the place runs on through the half pixel of overlap, so both pieces give a pixel
    there the same coverage, which the layer's max takes once.
- **What it costs**: 16 bytes a dashed shape on the GPU and in each upload, nothing for a solid one;
  16 bytes a piece in an edit, solid pieces too; a draw a pass for each layer holding dashes; one
  flat varying and one uniform in the shape program; no sampler, no table and no new program. The
  fill program is untouched. Attribute 3 is the canvas's own, on its own vertex arrays.
- **A shape drawn today draws as it does now**: the same record, and a distance the new branches
  leave as it was. If the compiler lowers the changed shader so a pixel moves, the difference is
  shown, not worked round with a variant.

### Rings

A tag's outline ring is drawn round each dash and each mitre, as it is round any shape, since the
distance it is measured from is the stroke's: nothing in the ring pass changes.

## Tests

Probe lines from `apps/canvas` under `--canvas-probe`, read by arms in the `canvas` gate group
(`run_canvas_gate`, its docstring listing each), built as 14.0's are: `_canvas_run`,
`_canvas_coverage`, `_canvas_cell`, `compare` and `_rgb_near`; `probe_cell` and `white_paint` in the
app.

**The app gains:**
- **`--sheet dashes`**, white on black, an 8 mm view: a cell a case, each printing its box, its area
  and its edge's length in pixels (`canvas dashes NAME box x0 y0 x1 y1 area A edge E`), a pixel and
  its colour (`canvas dashes-at NAME x y want RRGGBB`), or a box alone for a cell that must be empty
  (`canvas dashes-empty`) or a string (`canvas dashes-text`);
- **`--sheet strokes`**, the same, for caps and joins with no dash;
- **`--dash LENGTH,GAP`**, in millimetres, and **`--cap round|butt|square`** and **`--join
  round|mitre|bevel`**: every paint a sheet makes takes them, its fills and text among them, which
  must ignore them. `--sheet symbol --dash 0.5,0.5 --cap butt --join mitre` draws the symbol as
  pcb's ghost would;
- **`--dash-min-px PX`**, written to `Canvas.dash_min_px`.

**Arms**:

- **`canvas-caps`**: on the strokes sheet, a segment and an arc each butt, round and square, their
  areas in closed form within 0.03 px per pixel of edge, as `canvas-areas`.
- **`canvas-joins`**: polylines of two legs at a right angle and at 30 degrees, and a stroked box
  with no corner radius, each round, mitre and bevel, their areas in closed form; the 30-degree
  mitre past four widths drawn as its bevel; a pixel just inside a mitre's point and just outside
  a bevel's cut, each within 2 codes of white or black.
- **`canvas-dash-areas`**: each cell's coverage summed against its closed form:
  - `bar`: a butt segment whose length is a whole number of periods less a gap;
  - `round-bar`: the same round, each dash a capsule;
  - `carried`: an open butt polyline of two legs at a right angle, the corner in the middle of a gap
    and the far end in the middle of a dash: its dashes and that half dash. The legs' lengths are
    chosen so the pattern restarted at the corner covers more than the tolerance more;
  - `corner`: the same with the corner in the middle of a dash and a mitre join: the dash turning
    the corner square;
  - `ring`: a stroked butt circle: its ring times the dash's share of the period, which stretching
    keeps;
  - `arc`: a butt arc whose length is a whole number of periods less a gap;
  - `box`: a stroked butt box whose sides are whole periods with its corners in the middle of gaps;
  - `polygon`: a stroked butt square polygon of the box's size, the same area;
  - `fill-circle`, `fill-box` and `fill-polygon`: filled shapes in a dashed paint, each its solid
    closed form.
- **`canvas-dash-places`**: pixels at stated places, each within 2 codes of white or black: inside
  the first dash and the first gap of the bar, of the arc, of a clockwise arc from its own `a`, of
  the circle from its rightmost point, of the box from the middle of its bottom side, and of the
  polygon from its first point.
- **`canvas-dash-world`**: the dashes sheet at 8 mm and at 4 mm across: along the bar's middle row,
  the same count of dashes, each run within a pixel of its length times the pixels a unit. Dashes
  are lengths in units, not pixels.
- **`canvas-dash-twins`**: the dashes sheet 2^40 units from the origin against it at the origin,
  0 px; mirrored against it flipped, within a code; with 65 um tiles against the default, within a
  code, every segment and arc cut at the grid carrying its place across the cuts. And a string in a
  dashed paint against the same string in a plain one a whole number of pixels along, 0 px.
- **`canvas-dash-solid`**: the symbol sheet under `--dash 0.5,0.5` against it without, at a width
  across where every dash and gap spans under two pixels: 0 px. Nearer, and there under
  `--dash-min-px 0`, the two differ, so the solid picture is the setting's doing.
- **`canvas-dash-tags`**: a dashed segment whose object's tag colours it, its first dash that
  colour within 2 codes; and one added and removed by its object in one edit, its cell black.
- **`canvas-dash-refused`**: a segment whose gap is negative and an arc whose dash is 2^30 units,
  each refused by name in the log (`_canvas_shot`'s `log`) and its cell black.

Every arm of 14.0's stays as it is: the sheets it draws hold no dash, cap or join but the defaults,
and `flat_ends` becomes `CANVAS_CAP_BUTT` there. `canvas-uploads` holding 48 bytes a record is
what says a solid shape still costs what it did.
