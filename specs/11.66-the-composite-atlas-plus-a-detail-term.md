# 11.66 — D10 stage 1: the composite atlas plus a detail term

## What this review round changed, before anything else

The previously approved draft had two fatal flaws and three serious ones, all found by checking its
claims against the source. The fixes reshape it, so they are stated first:

1. **FATAL — the sampler type contradiction.** The draft specified the physical cache as a
   `GL_TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` (one layer per page, "no border problem") *and* riding the free
   `albedoTex`/`normalTex` declarations — which are `sampler2D`. Two sampler types against one
   image unit is an `INVALID_OPERATION` at draw by this codebase's own documented rule, and a new
   `sampler2DArray` declaration is the 17th sampler `pbr_frag` cannot have. The cache must be plain
   `GL_TEXTURE_2D` pairs, or it cannot ride the free units at all.

2. **FATAL — the camera-keyed ring caches nothing.** The draft priced a 64-page near ring at
   31 mm/texel as "lossless for what it caches". But the cache never holds grain at any resolution
   (that was the draft's own 1.4 GB finding) — it holds the MACRO, whose finest content is the
   splat at ~2 units/texel. A domain-wide 2048² atlas at 0.49 units/texel already resolves that 4x
   over **everywhere**. The near ring was resolution spent on content that is not there. Dropping
   it deletes the ring residency, the strip baking and half the VRAM, and moves paging wholly into
   stage 2 — where it becomes necessary only when the macro itself gets dense content (roads,
   decals, a far denser erosion field).

   Net effect: this lands back on the roadmap's own "baked composite atlas" staging — the earlier
   62–250x-coarser critique was correct about a *plain* atlas, but the answer to it is the detail
   term, not paging. Paging cannot afford grain density either. The user-approved two-spec split
   (sampling first, feedback later) is preserved and simplified.

3. **`layer_fixture` cannot see the cache.** Its generator authors the splat in **UV1 space**
   (`gen_layer_fixture.py:282`: "UV1 is the splat coordinate"), and the cache is world-XZ
   parameterised. So the draft's gate story — `layers-height` as a free invalidation test,
   `layers-triplanar` as "the design's own falsifier", the fixture golden as the identity check —
   was built on a fixture the feature never touches. The gates need a new world-XZ fixture, and
   `.cscn` needs a small parser addition to author one: `splat_space` is settable only from C today
   (`forest.c:684` is the sole writer; `cscene.c` has no key for it).

4. **Bilinearly filtering a layer INDEX is garbage.** The dominant-layer index in the cache's alpha
   interpolates through unrelated indices at layer boundaries (0↔3 passes through 1 and 2) and
   `glGenerateMipmap` averages it into nonsense. Read it with `texelFetch` at mip 0 — unfiltered by
   definition, ten precedents in the corpus (`dof_gather_frag.glsl:46` states this exact reason).

5. **The tap count was undercounted and the detail composition was unspecified.** The detail term
   must carry the surface map too — `layer_fixture_relief`'s lesson is that the normal/roughness
   grain is half the feature (16,468 px when it got its own map) — so the cached path is
   `3 + 2A` taps, not `2 + A`. And "one triplanar detail sample" is not a spec; the composition
   maths is now written down (ratio-to-mean, below), including why the flat-layer case is exact.

## Context

The terrain track is otherwise finished — D6/D7/D8 as 11.59, D9 (material layers) as 11.60, D11
(origin rebasing) as 11.62, D4's CDLOD half as 11.63. D10 comes first among the two remaining XL
entries because it is unblocked (D9 done) while D4-streaming has nothing to stream yet.

The case for D10 is ~3x stronger than the roadmap entry deferring it: the real per-pixel cost of
the layered path is `1 + A*W + A*B` taps (`layers.glsl:148-156`, `:201-230`) — **9 flat, 17 on a
45-degree face, 25 worst case** — against the entry's "eight taps", and `triplanar.glsl:44-46`
names the 45-degree case as "most of an eroded terrain". Pass one cannot be pruned because it
produces the height the cut is computed from. And row 43's third trigger ("a terrain too large for
one atlas") already fired with 11.63's `--terrain-extent`.

The arithmetic that shapes the design: today's ground resolves **3.9 mm/texel** (grass, 4-unit
tiles from 1024² maps; 2.9 mm on gravel). No atlas and no page cache can hold that — matching it
over 1 km takes 524 GiB, and even a 30 m near field takes ~1.4 GB. **A terrain composite cache is
structurally a macro cache at any affordable density.** UE's landscape RVT is the same: decimetre
macro plus a runtime detail term. So:

> **The cache holds the macro — the splat and the blend — losslessly. A runtime detail term holds
> the grain at full tiling density. Neither pretends to do the other's job.**

## The design

### The cache

Per world-XZ-splat layered material: **two plain `GL_TEXTURE_2D` textures**, RGBA8, **linear**
internal format, `GL_CLAMP_TO_EDGE`, full mips via `glGenerateMipmap`, **anisotropic filtering set
explicitly** (the material array sets none, and a kilometre of ground at grazing angles is the
engine's worst moiré case). Coverage = the material's `splatDomain`; addressed at runtime through
the existing `layerSplatUV` (`layers.glsl:79-83`), so an origin shift is a no-op by construction.

Resolution is **derived, not fixed**: `clamp(next_pow2(domain_size / 0.5), 256, 2048)` — half a
world unit per texel, which resolves forest's ~2-unit splat 4x over. Forest gets 2048²
(**44.7 MB** the pair, mipped, at `material_texture_array.c:239`'s own 4/3 factor); the gate
fixture's 4-unit domain gets 256². `--layers-vt-res <n>` overrides (diagnostic, both apps). Checked
against `GL_MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`, which the engine currently never queries — add it to the startup
log at `engine.c:468`.

| target | channels |
|---|---|
| `vt_albedo` | `.rgb` blended albedo in **STORED codes**, `.a` dominant layer index, quantised k/3 |
| `vt_surface` | `.rg` tangent-space normal xy (XZ ground frame), `.b` roughness, `.a` AO |

Stored codes because `pbr_frag.glsl:1013` decodes unconditionally on the layered arm; tangent
normal because two channels cannot express a downward world normal — composed onto the interpolated
geometric normal at runtime (whiteout, the machinery `triplanarBlendNormal` already is).

### The detail term

`.a` of the albedo target names the **dominant layer** k, read with `texelFetch` at mip 0 (never
filtered — finding 4). The runtime samples layer k's own maps from `materialArray`, triplanar at
the layer's own `uv_scale` — full grain density at every distance, layer-count-independent cost.

Composition, chosen so the flat case is **exact**:

- **albedo**: `macro.rgb * detail.rgb / layerMean[k].rgb`, all in stored codes. A flat map equals
  its mean, the ratio is 1, and the macro (which is exact on flat content) passes through
  untouched — which is what lets identity arms assert bytes. On grain it is a gamma-space
  approximation of the same class the blend already accepts (`layers.glsl:192-196`); asserted as
  crossings and amplitude, not bytes.
- **normal**: detail tangent normal whiteout-composed on top of the macro normal.
- **roughness / AO**: additive deviation, `macro + (detail − mean)`, clamped.

`layerMean` comes from the **deepest mip of `materialArray`** — one tiny `glGetTexImage` at bake
time (the 11.49 emissive-quad 1x1-top-mip precedent; `water.c:2153` for the array read), so the
mean is computed from exactly the resampled data the taps read.

**Taps: `3 + 2A`** (2 filtered macro + 1 texelFetch + A detail albedo + A detail surface) — 5 flat,
9 worst — against 9/17/25 today, and independent of layer count, which is what unblocks the
roadmap's layer-count and roads/decals triggers.

### Scope rule

The cache serves `SPLAT_SPACE_WORLD_XZ` materials only. UV1-splat materials — `layer_fixture`,
props, anything with a wall — keep the per-texel path unchanged; it remains the general mechanism.
This is the same limitation the splat already documents ("WORLD_XZ cannot address a vertical
surface", `material.h:296-298`), now load-bearing.

### Sampler ledger: zero new declarations

`pbr_frag` is at 16/16 and the driver counts declarations (`pbr_frag.glsl:299-303`). Units 0 and 1
are **provably unread whenever `layerCount > 0`** — `albedoTex` only at `:1028`/`:1031` inside the
`else if (albedoTexExists > 0)` arm, `normalTex` only at `:1107`/`:1109` inside the arm at
`:1086` — and both are `sampler2D`, matching the cache textures (finding 1). Alias in the house
idiom:

```glsl
#define vtAlbedoTex  albedoTex   // unit 0, unread when layerCount > 0
#define vtSurfaceTex normalTex   // unit 1, unread when layerCount > 0
```

**Bind-order hazard**: a layered material that also authors an albedo texture would have render.c
bind it to unit 0 over the cache. Bind the cache pair **after** the material's texture binds,
inside the existing `layer_count > 0` branch at `render.c:239`.

**Not a `materialArray` tenant**: `MATERIAL_TEXTURE_ARRAY_CAP` 2048 plus the canonical-size rule
(`material_texture_array.c:158-168`) would promote forest's 11 layers to the atlas size. The one
case where D9's tenancy escape is wrong.

### The bake

A fragment pass over the atlas: fragment → splat UV → authored `worldPos = (x, 0, z)`,
`worldNormal = +Y`, and **`#include "layers.glsl"`, calling `sampleLayeredSurface` unchanged** —
the cache is provably a caching of the existing path, not a second implementation (the
`terrain_ground_classes` / `punctualAngular` / IES-folds lesson). Two properties fall out free:

- **Footprint filtering is automatic.** The pass's `dFdx(worldPos)` IS the atlas texel size, so the
  `textureGrad` taps inside `layers.glsl` read the layer maps at exactly the mip that averages one
  atlas texel's footprint. No aliasing, no explicit filter, no code.
- **Baking flat (+Y, A=1) is correct, not a shortcut.** The macro content — splat weights and
  blend — does not depend on the projection; only grain does, and grain is the detail term's job at
  runtime with the real normal.

The dominant index needs `LayerSurface` to grow a `float dominant` field, set where `b[i]` is
already computed — the alternative is the bake recomputing the blend, a second implementation.

Mechanics, all from in-tree precedent:

- Structure per `material_texture_array_build` (`material_texture_array.c:183-233`): transient FBO,
  MRT into the two textures, cached quad VAO, full state save/restore, `glGenerateMipmap`, an MB
  log line with the 4/3 factor.
- **`GL_BLEND` off for the draw**, comment citing `ibl.c:505-508` (undefined source alpha through
  the blend equation made the BRDF LUT differ run to run — a packed target is exactly that shape).
- **Invalidate by VALUE** (`_water_seed_is_current`, `water.c:1080-1091`): layer GL ids, uv_scales,
  both sharpness scalars, splat GL id, `splatDomain`, atlas res.
- **Own the GL names, bypass the texture pool** — `load_texture_from_memory` returns a cached
  Texture by key without reading `pixels` (`texture.h:145-147`), silently discarding a re-bake.
- `glTexStorage2D` is GL 4.2 — `glTexImage2D` + `glGenerateMipmap` (`material_texture_array.c:170`).
- Cadence per `sky_bake_ex`: startup and invalidation, never per frame. Ordered **after**
  `material_texture_array_ensure_built` in the engine loop — it samples the array.

## Gates

The existing five `layers` arms all keep reading the **per-texel path** — `layer_fixture` is
UV1-splat and the cache never touches it. That is correct coverage of a path that stays live, but
it is **zero coverage of the new one**, and per `gate-arm-docs` convention the group docstring must
say so out loud (the `run_origin_gate` "WHAT THIS GROUP CANNOT SEE" format). The group is currently
listless — a single-line docstring — so adding the arm list also opts it into the docs check.

**New fixture: `layer_vt_fixture`** — same painted maps, new geometry and `.cscn`: a floor and a
**45-degree ramp** (the A=2 triplanar leg a floor alone cannot reach), splat authored
`splatSpace: "world"` over a stated domain. At its 4-unit domain the derived 256² atlas is
15.6 mm/texel — flat content is exact there, which is what makes byte-level arms possible; the
coarse-atlas claims are reached through `--layers-vt-res`, and **every density figure is stated in
units/texel derived from the fixture's own half-extent, never in texels** (the fixture-domain trap:
at fixture scale everything reproduces, so an arm that does not force coarseness proves nothing
about a kilometre).

- **`layers-vt-identity`** — one A/B pair (`--no-layers-vt` vs default, `--render-mode 6`):
  flat-layer interior boxes **byte-identical** (ratio term exact on flat maps), and the checker box
  **must differ** by a floor — the built-in anti-vacuity: a flag that does nothing reads 0 diff on
  the checker box and fails. Mutation: halve the bake's `glViewport` — half the atlas is unwritten.
- **`layers-vt-detail`** — checker crossings + amplitude through the cached path, floor **and
  ramp** (A=2). Mutation: stop writing the dominant index — detail collapses to layer 0's flat map
  and the crossings vanish.
- **`layers-vt-macro`** — the central claim. `--layers-vt-res` forced coarse (atlas texel >>
  checker period, >= splat feature): selection boxes still read their painted layers (macro intact)
  while the checker still crosses (grain from detail, not cache). This is the arm a plain baked
  atlas — the roadmap's original stage 1 — fails.
- **`layers-vt-invalidate`** — `cscn_copy` `layerBlend: 0.0` variant through the cached path; the
  transition midpoint must move, which it cannot from a stale atlas. Mutation: drop `layerBlend`
  from the by-value key.
- **`layers-vt-budget`** — parse the bake's MB log against the gate's **own** closed form
  `2 * N² * 4 * (4/3)` under a gate-side ceiling and above a res floor (the `layers-scatter`
  lesson: a bar read from the process under test moves with the thing it checks).

Every arm's mutation is **run**, not reasoned about — two arms shipped inert this cycle.

## Verification

- **The ground looks the same up close** — fixed-camera forest crops before/after at walking
  distance, eye-checked. The one failure that invalidates the design.
- All five existing `layers` arms and the `layer_fixture` golden **unchanged, trivially** — the
  per-texel path is untouched and the fixture never takes the cache. That is a statement about
  scope, not coverage; the new arms carry the coverage.
- 25/25 goldens 0 px or a stated, measured move. A new lit `layer_vt_fixture` golden covers the
  cached path being *shaded* (world normal into lighting, per-layer roughness) — the same gap the
  `layer_fixture` golden was added to close for the per-texel path; `cmd_rebake`'s render-twice
  refusal makes bake nondeterminism loud.
- Perf measured, not asserted: `--profiler` on forest, release build, A/B/A/B interleaved, opaque
  pass GPU ms with `--no-layers-vt` as B.
- Same-build determinism floor measured first on any pixel comparison, per standing rule.

**Stated risk and fallback.** The ratio detail term is an approximation on grain and loses the
mm-scale height-blend interlock inside layer-transition bands (a few units wide, at every layer
boundary). If the forest crops read badly, the fallback is caching the resolved blend **weights**
instead of the finished surface — every layer keeps its own maps and triplanar exactly, tap win
drops to ~2x, and roads/decals stay blocked. The retreat, not the plan.
