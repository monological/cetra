# A defined working space: view exposure as a first-class engine contract

## Context

Specs 9.9 and 10.0 made lights photometric — inverse-square falloff, intensity in
candela, glTF values passed through instead of rescaled. Both are implemented and
verified on `import-photometric-lights`. Both are also, on their own, a mistake in
sequencing: `light_import_fixture` renders a grey cube where master renders red.

The proximate cause is `pbr_frag.glsl:1201` clamping each light to `vec3(10.0)`,
so a 522-nit red channel and a 33-nit green one both arrive as 10 and the hue is
destroyed before exposure applies. But that clamp is a symptom.

**The real defect: nothing in this engine defines what `1.0` means in the scene
HDR buffer.** Every stage invented a constant against an unwritten assumption that
"about 1.0 is white" — `vec3(10.0)` and `vec3(60000.0)` in pbr_frag, `min(2.0)` in
ssr_frag, `GI_MAX_RADIANCE = 4.0` in gtao_frag, `min(500.0)` in the froxel pair,
bloom's threshold/knee/max, the `autoKey` floor in lum_measure, and the `1.0 + luma`
pivot in taa_resolve. None is wrong alone. They are wrong collectively, because the
invariant they share is not stated anywhere — so invalidating it broke nothing
loudly and everything quietly.

Re-tuning those constants preserves the defect. The fix is to define the contract
and make every consumer express itself relative to it.

**This is one feature, not three.** Photometric units, a physical camera and
pre-exposure ship together in Frostbite (Lagarde & de Rousiers, *Moving Frostbite to
PBR*) and in UE (`View.PreExposure`, inseparable from its photometric light units)
for exactly this reason. Shipping units first is what produced the grey cube.

## The contract

Three spaces, kept distinct and written down:

| space | units | range | inhabitants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scene radiance** | photometric (cd / lux / nits) | unbounded; a sun is 1e5 | lights, materials, import |
| **Working space** | pre-exposed | ~0–10, **1.0 = diffuse white at current exposure** | scene HDR buffer, all post |
| **Display** | tonemapped | 0–1 | after tonemap, GUI, overlays |

Everything downstream of the scene passes lives in working space, so every
threshold is expressible in **stops over white** and is scale-free by construction.

## Status

- **Phase 0 — DONE** (`view: declare the working-space contract and wire ViewParams`).
- **Phase 2 — DONE** (`view: move the pipeline into working space`). The grey cube is
  fixed; `light_import_fixture` renders red again, matching master. Seven goldens 0 px.
- **Phase 1 — NOT DONE, and deliberately skipped for now.** `exposure.c/h` was never
  extracted; Phase 2 uses `postfx_exposure_multiplier` directly. Functional, but
  exposure ownership is still spread across postfx, render.c and the tonemap. The
  auto-exposure adaptation gain also still applies at tonemap rather than being
  pre-exposed from the previous frame -- total exposure is unchanged either way, which
  is why goldens hold, but the previous-frame feedback described below is unbuilt.
- **Phase 3a — DONE** (`view: put the ambient floor in working space`). Scale
  invariance holds on surfaces at 2 px, down from 353950.
- **Phase 3b — DONE** (`view: name the working-space ceilings`). The five scattered
  clamps are `WS_*` constants in `view.glsl`. One was not a rename: the metering
  clamp ran after the conversion back to radiance, so it capped a scene at 60000 cd
  and a noon sun would have metered as overcast; it clamps in working space now.
  The `1.0 +` pivots in `taa_resolve` and `ssgi_accum` stay absolute, deliberately --
  in working space that IS white, which is where firefly weighting should hinge.
- **Phase 4 — DONE.** The attenuation triple is deleted (`light: delete the
  fixed-function attenuation triple`), the GUI addresses one selected light instead
  of broadcasting (`gui: select which light the Intensity and Range sliders
  address`), and `LightUnits` is built (`light: give intensity a stated unit`).

  One design note on the enum, since it deviates from the sketch above: it records
  the AUTHORED unit, not the canonical one. Storing the canonical unit would be
  redundant with `type` and could desync from it. `intensity` still always holds the
  canonical unit, so nothing downstream of `Light` changed -- no shader, no UBO. What
  the enum buys is that a value reads back as it was written: `cornell_point.cscn`
  authors 30 lumens and the GUI now says `30.0 lm` where it used to say `2.4 cd`.
  That is the same class of mismatch as the .cscn-vs-slider bug from 10.0.

- **Probe capture — DONE** (`view: bake reflection probes at unity`). Found while
  naming the SSR clamp, and worse than it first looked: `reflection_probe_capture`
  reuses `render_current_scene`, so the cubemap was baked in working space, and
  then every consumer converted it AGAIN -- `pbr_frag` folds the probe prefilter
  into `ambient`, which the composite multiplies, and the debug background runs
  through `skybox_frag`, which multiplies too. Exposure squared, not merely stale.

  The resolution comes from what the probe IS: its prefiltered cubemap is bound
  over the IBL environment prefilter's own sampler unit and read by the same shader
  path. That prefilter is absolute radiance (HDR file or sky bake, neither
  pre-exposed), so the probe has to be as well, or the substitution is unsound.
  Captures render at unity now. `ssr_frag` was then the one consumer NOT converting
  -- it composites into the working-space reflection buffer -- so it gained the
  multiply the others already had.

  Identity at exposure 1, which is why nothing caught it: every golden pins there.
  Measured on a probe scene, `-E 1.0` is 0 px and `-E 4.0` is 2095690 px (25%).

One correction to the Phase 2 design, recorded because it is the whole trick and is
not obvious: pre-exposure belongs on the per-light `radiance`, NOT on the final
write. The `vec3(10.0)` clamp sits between them. Applied at `FragColor` it is too
late -- the clamp still sees scene radiance and still flattens channel ratios, which
is the grey cube. Everything downstream of that (`AccumOut`, `DiffuseOut`) is then
already converted and must not multiply again.

Also learned: a physical camera and auto-exposure are alternative exposure methods,
not stacking ones. Left combined, metering compares absolute radiance against an
absolute 0.18 key and darkens an already-correct frame by up to the 1/64 clamp.

## Approach

Staged so each phase is independently verifiable and the risky one lands with the
goldens already proving it.

### Phase 0 — Declare the contract (no behaviour change)

- `cetra/shaders/include/view.glsl`: a new shared include declaring the working-space
  contract in prose and the `ViewParams` block below. This is the authority, the way
  `lights_ubo.glsl` is for the light layout.
- `cetra/src/ubo.h` / `ubo.c`: add a `ViewParams` uniform block alongside the existing
  clustered-light blocks. Reuse `ubo_wire_light_blocks`' wiring pattern (rename to
  `ubo_wire_blocks`) — **this is why the UBO and not per-program uploads**: the audit
  found no broadcast helper exists, and `time`/wind reach only the scene-traversal
  programs, missing skybox, sky background, particles, catcher, overlays and text.
  Eight hand-maintained upload sites is how a ninth pass gets added wrong.

```glsl
layout(std140) uniform ViewParams {
    float preExposure;        // multiply scene radiance by this before writing
    float oneOverPreExposure; // for passes that must recover absolute radiance
    float exposureEV100;      // debug / GUI readout
    float _pad;
};
```

- Document the contract in `CLAUDE.md` beside the G-buffer layout. The reason this
  bug existed is that the invariant lived only in people's heads.

*Gate: everything wired and uploaded, `preExposure` forced to 1.0. All nine goldens
0 px — the block exists and changes nothing.*

### Phase 1 — One owner for exposure

Today exposure is scattered: a linear multiplier on `PostFX`, an EV100 path added by
10.0, auto-exposure metering in two shaders, and `postfx_exposure_multiplier()`.
Consolidate into one module that owns the camera and publishes `ViewParams` — the
analogue of UE's view-exposure ownership.

- `cetra/src/exposure.c/h`: aperture/shutter/ISO, EV100 conversion (reuse
  `postfx_ev100` / `postfx_exposure_multiplier` from `postfx.c:902`), the EV bias, the
  auto-exposure adaptation state, and the per-frame publish.
- Auto-exposure resolves its circularity with the **previous frame's** adapted value
  (standard; the lag is far below the adaptation time constant). Pinned exposure has
  no feedback and stays bit-deterministic, which is what every golden runs.

### Phase 2 — Move the pipeline into working space

- **Pre-expose every writer of scene radiance**: `pbr_frag.glsl` (`FragColor`,
  `AccumOut`, and `DiffuseOut` — the SSS fold at `postfx.c:931` reads that attachment,
  so missing it desyncs skin), `skybox_frag.glsl`, `sky_background_frag.glsl`,
  `particle_frag.glsl`, and `froxel_composite_frag.glsl`.
  The fog composite is the subtle one: its in-scatter is **independently computed**,
  not derived from the HDR buffer, so it does not self-scale.
- **Leave alone, they self-scale**: SSGI composite, SSR upsample, OIT resolve — all
  derived from `hdr_texture`, so the factor cancels.
- **Leave alone, no radiance**: `catcher_frag.glsl` writes RGB 0 under SRC_ALPHA; it
  multiplies toward black and is automatically consistent.
- **Must NOT be pre-exposed** (they are display-ready and do land in the scene FBO):
  `bone_frag`, `xyz_frag`, `shape_frag`, `text_frag`.
- **Tonemap** stops applying exposure (`tonemap_frag.glsl:195,201`); it keeps only the
  residual EV bias.
- **Metering** multiplies by `oneOverPreExposure` (`lum_measure_frag.glsl`, which
  samples `hdr_texture` or `dof_texture` per `postfx.c:2123-2138`). Without this the
  meter reads its own output and exposure ratchets into the clamp. This is the single
  line that makes or breaks the phase.

*Gate: all nine goldens 0 px. They pin exposure, so pre-exposure is a constant factor
divided back out — algebraically identity. Any movement means a writer was missed or
the tonemap double-applies.*

### Phase 3 — Re-express the thresholds

Replace absolute constants with named, contract-relative ones in `view.glsl`
(`STOPS_OVER_WHITE(n)` or equivalent). Same numeric values initially, so the change is
provably inert — the win is that each now states its meaning and stays true at any
light magnitude.

Covers: `pbr_frag.glsl:1060,1201,1370`, `ssr_frag.glsl:82,351`, `gtao_frag.glsl:37`,
`froxel_inject_frag.glsl:223`, `froxel_integrate_frag.glsl:68`, bloom
(`postfx.c:262-264`, `bloom_bright_frag.glsl:15`), `lum_measure_frag.glsl:17,27`.

**One that is not a clamp and needs judgement:** `taa_resolve_frag.glsl:96-97` and
`ssgi_accum_frag.glsl:63-64` weight history by `feedback / (1.0 + luma)`. That `1.0`
is an absolute pivot governing firefly rejection — it will not error, it will filter
differently. Decide deliberately whether it becomes relative.

Sky LUTs (`sky_view_frag.glsl:99`, `aerial_lut_frag.glsl:89`) bake before the HDR
buffer exists and set the absolute scale the sky background inherits; they are
upstream of pre-exposure, not subject to it.

### Phase 4 — Light units as an authoring API

With working space defined, this becomes small. A `LightUnits` enum on `Light`
(candela / lumens / lux / nits), converted once at set time — the analogue of UE's
`ELightUnits` — so `Light.intensity` carries a stated unit instead of a convention
asserted in a `.cscn` parser, a GUI tooltip and a shader comment separately.

Retires the two items still outstanding from 10.0: the inert
`constant/linear/quadratic` triple (`light.h:37-39`, `set_light_attenuation`, the
`import.c` sanitise block, the `.cscn` key), and per-light GUI selection so Intensity
and Range stop broadcasting to every light.

## Verification

**The property, not the pixels.** Goldens confirm nothing regressed; they cannot show
the contract holds. The test that does:

1. **Scale invariance — THE gate, and it currently FAILS.** Multiply every light by
   1000x, drop exposure by the same factor. The image must be pixel-identical.

   Use `cornell_point`: it is already the right fixture (one point light, and
   `build_point_room` omits the emissive panel), so no purpose-built asset is needed.
   `cornell_leak` is NOT usable — its glTF emissive panel is a second emitter that
   does not scale with the `.cscn` light, and it fails for that reason alone.

   ```
   render -m assets/cornell_point.gltf -x -f 30 --no-auto-exposure -E 1.0 \
       -W 400 -H 300 -S a.ppm
   # edit cornell_point.cscn: intensity 30 -> 30000
   render -m assets/cornell_point.gltf -x -f 30 --no-auto-exposure -E 0.001 \
       -W 400 -H 300 -S b.ppm
   magick compare -metric AE a.ppm b.ppm null:      # must be 0
   ```

   **Measured after Phase 2: 353950 px (73.7%).** The two renders are structurally
   identical -- same shadows, same geometry -- but the red and green walls are much
   more saturated at 1000x. That is the signature of an ADDITIVE TERM THAT DOES NOT
   SCALE: at 1x it washes wall colour toward white, at 1/1000 exposure it is crushed
   and the pure light colour dominates.

   `--ibl-intensity 0` returns a byte-identical 353950, so that flag is not reaching
   whatever the term is. The `ambient` path in `pbr_frag` (converted at the final
   composite, but sourced independently of scene lights) is the first suspect.

   Re-run this after every Phase 3 change; it localises the culprit by where the
   difference appears, which no golden can do.

   **Status: PASSES for surfaces (2 px, fp32 rounding on a 1000x/1000 round trip).
   FAILS with `--fog` at 458632 px (95.5%).**

   The fog culprit is isolated: `froxel_inject_frag.glsl:165`, `vec3 S =
   ambientColor;`. Zeroing it takes the fog case to **2 px**, the same floor as
   surfaces -- so it is the only remaining term, not one of several.

   **RESOLVED: the code is correct; the TEST is what needs fixing.**

   It looks like the 0.03 floor, and it is not. `fog_ambient` is declared as
   in-scatter RADIANCE (`postfx.h:227`), and `sky.c:316` fills it from the sky's
   zenith radiance whenever a sky publishes. It is a real second emitter. The
   hardcoded `{0.05, 0.05, 0.05}` default (`postfx.c:330`) is a stand-in for
   skylight when no sky exists -- a made-up radiance, but radiance.

   So fog ambient legitimately does NOT scale with a punctual lamp, exactly as
   `cornell_leak`'s glTF emissive panel does not. Pre-exposing it would make skylit
   fog track a lamp's intensity, which is physically backwards, and the scale test
   would go GREEN while the engine got more wrong. That is the trap here.

   The scale test's real precondition, which the surface case satisfied only by
   accident: **every emitter in the scene must scale together, or be zero.** For
   the fog case that means zeroing `fog_ambient` for the fixture (verified: gives
   2 px) or scaling it alongside the lights. Neither is currently expressible from
   a `.cscn` -- the fog block carries density and anisotropy but not ambient, which
   is the actual gap to close before the fog case can be a standing gate.
2. **The failing fixture.** `light_import_fixture` renders red again, matching master,
   with SSS on and off. That is the regression this exists to fix.
3. **Nine goldens 0 px** at the Phase 0, 1 and 3 gates. Phase 2 is the only phase
   permitted to be non-trivial, and even it should be identity under pinned exposure.
4. **fp16 headroom.** A ~50,000 cd light close to a surface must not produce Inf or
   NaN in the G-buffer. Check via a debug render mode — an Inf survives every
   downstream clamp and is invisible at thumbnail scale.
5. **No metering feedback.** Interactive, static camera: exposure settles rather than
   drifting. Goldens pin exposure and structurally cannot catch this (already noted in
   `specs/9.9`), so this one is interactive.
6. **`spores`** — the only spot exercising fog in-scatter — beam and lit surfaces stay
   consistent with each other.

Measure the same-build noise floor before trusting any comparison, and never pipe
`./build.sh` through `head`.
