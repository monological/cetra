# 8.2 Wind-Blown Grass

## Context

The tree stands on bare dirt. The ground is the largest surface in frame and
carries no life at all, so the scene reads as a model on a plinth rather than a
place — and the canopy's dappled shadow, which is the best thing the lighting
does, falls on nothing that can show it off.

This adds patches of grass that sway with the same wind field as the tree.

The engine needs no changes. Grass reuses the vegetation wind mode added in 8.0
and the merged-mesh/vertex-colour approach from 8.1; everything here is app code.

## Design

### 1. Blade geometry

A blade is a tapered strip of 3 quads (4 rows x 2 columns = 8 vertices, 6
triangles) that narrows to a point and **arcs over** along its length. The arc
is the whole reason it reads as grass rather than as spikes: straight blades
look like a bed of nails, and a single bend gives the silhouette its character.

Per blade, randomised: height, width, bend amount, bend direction, and a slight
twist so the strip is not perfectly edge-on from every angle.

Blades are one-sided but the material is `doubleSided`, which is cheaper than
emitting back faces and is what the leaf cards already do.

No texture. The blade's shape is geometry and its colour is vertex colour — an
alpha-cutout texture would buy nothing here and would cost the same
shadow-pass and GTAO exclusions that foliage pays (see `material.h`).

### 2. Patches, not a lawn

Uniform coverage looks like carpet. Placement is a jittered grid, thresholded by
a low-frequency noise field so grass gathers into patches with bare earth
between them, then a second, finer noise varies density within a patch.

Blades are placed in **tufts**: each placement drops 5-9 blades in a small
radius with a shared clump phase, splaying outward from a common root. Grass
grows in tufts, and it also means neighbouring blades sway together, which is
what makes wind read as wind rather than as noise.

Excluded: a radius around the trunk (grass does not grow through wood), and
anything beyond `GRASS_RADIUS`.

### 3. Sitting on the ground

The ground is a shallow dome, so a blade's root must be placed at its surface
height rather than at y = 0. `generate_island_mesh`'s dome formula moves into a
shared `ground_height_at(x, z)` so the mesh and the grass cannot disagree.

### 4. Wind — reusing the vegetation contract

Grass uses `wind_mode = 2` (vegetation leaf) unchanged, because the existing
contract already describes exactly what a blade needs:

- `UV1.x` = per-tuft phase, so a tuft moves as a unit and neighbouring tufts
  are out of step.
- `UV1.y` = flex, `t^1.3` along the blade: zero at the root so it stays planted,
  one at the tip.
- `UV0.y` = `t` along the blade, which mode 2 uses as the flutter pivot — the
  same role the leaf card's stem plays.

`wind_response` is set above the tree's (grass is far more mobile than wood).

**Constraint this imposes on the radius.** The vegetation modes take their
height envelope from the *mesh AABB* (`uWindMaskMinY/MaxY`, `pbr_vert.glsl`).
For one merged grass mesh, that AABB spans the whole field, so if the field is
wide enough for the ground dome to drop appreciably across it, the envelope
starts measuring terrain elevation instead of blade height and grass in
hollows stops moving. At `GRASS_RADIUS` 130 the dome falls about 0.4 units
against a blade height of ~5, so terrain is ~8% of the range and the envelope
still means what it should. Widening the field materially would require
per-vertex height instead, so the radius is a real constraint, not a taste
choice.

### 5. Colour and whimsy

All vertex colour, multiplied into the material albedo:

- **Along the blade**: dark at the base where blades shade each other, bright
  toward the tip. This fake occlusion matters for the same reason it did in the
  canopy — grass is dense enough to self-shadow, and screen-space AO will not
  do it at this scale.
- **Per blade**: hue jitter across fresh green, olive and straw, so a patch is
  not one flat colour.
- **Per patch**: a slow field shifts whole areas greener or drier.

Whimsy, kept sparse enough to stay charming rather than busy:

- **Seed heads** on a small fraction of blades: a taller, thinner stalk with a
  little drooping tip, which breaks the height silhouette.
- **Flowers**: tiny cross-quad blooms (two intersecting quads so they read from
  any angle) scattered at low density in a few warm colours, riding the same
  wind as the blade they sit on.

### 6. Material

One material, one merged mesh, one draw call for the entire field:
roughness ~0.75, opaque, `doubleSided`, `wind_response` ~1.6, `wind_mode = 2`,
subtle `subsurface` so blades glow when backlit like the leaves do.

Opaque means grass both casts and receives shadows with no special handling —
the canopy dapple lands on it, which is the point.

### 7. Parameters

A `GrassParams` struct compared by `memcmp`, mirroring `TreeParams`, so the GUI
can rebuild the field live:

`seed`, `density`, `patchiness`, `height`, `blade_width`, `bend`,
`flower_amount`, `seed_head_amount`, `radius`.

GUI section "Grass" alongside the existing panels.

### 8. Budget

At default density roughly 3000 tufts x ~7 blades = ~21k blades, 8 vertices
each = **~170k vertices / ~126k triangles** in one draw call. Comparable to the
tree itself and generated once at startup, not per frame. Hard-capped like the
tree's growth budget so a slider drag cannot stall a frame.

## Files

- `apps/tree/src/grass.c` / `grass.h` — new. Blade/tuft/flower generation and
  the merged mesh build. Deliberately its own translation unit: `tree.c` is
  already ~1500 lines and the code review flagged it for decomposition.
- `apps/tree/src/tree.c` — `ground_height_at`, grass material, node creation,
  GUI section, params.
- The `MeshBuilder` in `tree_gen.c` is exactly what this needs. Rather than
  copy it, promote it to `apps/tree/src/mesh_builder.h/.c` so both generators
  share one copy — the reuse review already flagged that it wants a second
  caller.

## Verification

```bash
./build.sh
# hero: grass patches, canopy shadow falling across them
./out/bin/tree -x -f 240 -S <scratch>/grass_hero.ppm
# clear, high sun: check patch shape, tuft clustering, colour variation
./out/bin/tree -x -f 240 --no-fog --sun-elevation 40 -S <scratch>/grass_clear.ppm
# motion: tufts should sway together and out of step with their neighbours,
# roots planted, tips travelling
./out/bin/tree -x -f 480 --screenshot-every 80 -S <scratch>/grass_wind.ppm
# backlit: blade translucency
./out/bin/tree -x -f 240 --no-fog --sun-elevation 8 --sun-azimuth 30 -S <scratch>/grass_backlit.ppm
```

Check: patches read as patches with bare ground between, not as carpet; no
grass through the trunk; blade roots stay planted while tips move; no visible
grid in the placement; flowers sparse enough to be a surprise.
