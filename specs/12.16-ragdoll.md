# Spec 12.16 — the character falls over

## Context

Animation and physics have never met in this engine. The character controller moves a capsule,
the animator poses a skeleton, and neither knows the other exists — a rig is posed by clips and
nothing else, whatever happens to it.

Ragdoll is the first thing that crosses that seam, and it is newly cheap. **Jolt ships it
already**: `Jolt/Physics/Ragdoll/Ragdoll.cpp` is in `Jolt.cmake` and compiled into the binary,
along with the whole `Jolt/Skeleton/` tree. What is missing is a C binding — a grep for
`Ragdoll|Skeleton` across `JoltC/` and `JoltCImpl/` returns **zero hits**. The roadmap carried
this as absent and costed it in months until that was checked.

It also lands on a character five specs have paid for: 12.11 made an imported humanoid retarget,
12.12 lit it, 12.13 framed it, 12.14 armed its knees, 12.15 gave it a window that behaves.
**Ragdoll is the first of those that uses the character rather than repairing it.**

**Outcome: a key drops the player into a physically simulated heap that continues from the pose
it was in, collides with the world, and settles — on the built-in puppet and on any `--puppet`
humanoid.**

---

## Decisions taken before planning

| | |
|---|---|
| **Shapes** | **Derived from the rig**, not authored. No new asset format, and it works on an arbitrary imported humanoid — which is the point, since this engine's characters arrive by import. |
| **Bone set** | **A fixed humanoid set**: hips, lower spine, chest, head, upper/lower arm ×2, thigh/shin ×2 — **at most 12 bodies and 11 constraints**, and fewer on a rig with a one-bone spine, where the lower spine and the chest are the same bone. Fingers, hands, feet, toes and twist bones stay rigid. |
| **Demo** | **A key that kills the player.** The controller stops steering, the bodies take over, the camera keeps following. |

**Getting up is out.** Blending from an arbitrary settled pose back into a clip is the hard half
and a different spec. `DriveToPoseUsingMotors` — the powered path — is out with it.

---

## What already exists, and why this is small

Three things this needs were built for other reasons, and finding that out is most of why the
estimate collapsed.

- **The bone vocabulary.** `rigging.h` carries `BCAT_HIPS`, `BCAT_SPINE`, `BCAT_HEAD`,
  `BCAT_UPPER_ARM`, `BCAT_LOWER_ARM`, `BCAT_UPPER_LEG`, `BCAT_LOWER_LEG` plus `get_bone_side`,
  and `skeleton_resolve_bone` exists so that — in its own words — *"a subsystem [can] name bones
  in ONE vocabulary and still reach a rig that spells them another way."* Ten of the twelve
  bones come straight out of it. **The spine is the exception, and it is the one piece of real
  design here** — see below.
- **The measurements.** `skeleton_compute_bind_globals` (`animation.h:56`) gives bind globals;
  `Mesh.bone_aabb` (`mesh.h:178`) gives per-bone **bind-space** boxes, added in 11.53 for
  culling, whose comment states the property this needs: *"the union of each bone's own box,
  transformed, bounds the pose."* A thigh's box is thigh-thick and a forearm's forearm-thin.
- **The splice.** `ik_solve(system, global_transforms, dt)` is already spliced into
  `animation_state_apply_pose` (`animation.c:1253`) and takes **globals only**. A ragdoll writes
  the same thing at the same place, and `ik.h:29-33` already explains why writing the Pose
  instead does not work: an undriven bone's TRS is discarded by `pose_local`.

## Design

### Where it lives: `state->ragdoll`, beside `state->ik` and `state->springs`

Not a new `ComponentType`. The ragdoll needs exactly what IK needs — the accumulated globals and
a model→world matrix — and the animation state already owns two systems of that shape, each
attached by plain field assignment (`gametest.c:2782`, `player_animator->state->ik = player_ik`)
and freed by the state.

That also settles teardown with no new question: the entity manager is freed **before**
`free_physics_world` (`game.c`, *"character controllers need physics system for inner body
cleanup"*), so a ragdoll hanging off an ANIMATOR component still has a live physics world when
it releases its bodies. A new component would have had to pick a slot in the `ComponentType`
enum, which **is** the teardown order (`entity.c` loops `c < COMPONENT_MAX`).

### The C++ TU

`cetra/src/ragdoll_jolt.cpp` + `ragdoll_jolt.h`, the third instance of the established escape.
**In `cetra/src/` root, not `game/`** — `physics_cook.h:11-19` states why: *"the build globs C++
sources at the src root and only C sources under `game/`, so a `.cpp` under `game/` would
silently not compile."*

The header follows `physics_cook.h`: `extern "C"`, an opaque `typedef struct JPC_PhysicsSystem
JPC_PhysicsSystem;` re-declared locally so no JoltC include is needed, plain-C functions, and a
version probe. The `.cpp` reaches the real types with the OPAQUE_WRAPPER identity
(`JoltC.cpp:46-51`) — `reinterpret_cast<JPH::PhysicsSystem*>(handle)`; the two are the same
address. `PhysicsWorld` already carries `physics_system` and `body_interface`, so nothing new is
plumbed.

The C surface is deliberately narrow — build, destroy, read back, apply an impulse:

```c
typedef struct RagdollBuild {          // one row per simulated body, parent-first
    const char* name;                  // debug only
    int parent;                        // index into this array, -1 for the root
    float capsule_radius, capsule_half_height;
    mat4 world;                        // where the body starts, from the live pose
    float cone_deg, plane_deg, twist_min_deg, twist_max_deg;
} RagdollBuild;

JoltRagdoll* jolt_ragdoll_create(JPC_PhysicsSystem*, const RagdollBuild*, int count, int group_id);
void         jolt_ragdoll_destroy(JoltRagdoll*);
void         jolt_ragdoll_get_world(const JoltRagdoll*, int index, mat4 out);
void         jolt_ragdoll_add_impulse(JoltRagdoll*, const float* impulse);
unsigned     jolt_ragdoll_jolt_version(void);
```

Inside, the recipe is Jolt's own (`Samples/Utils/RagdollLoader.cpp:265-303`): a `JPH::Skeleton`
of `count` joints — **the RAGDOLL skeleton, a dozen joints, not the 122-bone rig** — then a `Part` per
joint (a `Part` **is** a `BodyCreationSettings` plus `Ref<TwoBodyConstraintSettings> mToParent`),
`SwingTwistConstraintSettings` in world space for every non-root, then
`DisableParentChildCollisions()`, `CalculateBodyIndexToConstraintIndex()`, `CreateRagdoll`,
`AddToPhysicsSystem`.

**Three traps the exploration surfaced, each worth a line of code and a comment:**

- **`CreateRagdoll` returns refcount 0.** It must be `AddRef`'d or held in a `Ref`, exactly as
  `physics_cook.cpp:133-137` does for a `Shape`.
- **`~Ragdoll` destroys bodies but does not remove them.** `RemoveFromPhysicsSystem()` must
  precede the last release, or bodies are destroyed while still in the broadphase.
- **`JPH::Skeleton` requires parents before children** (`AreJointsCorrectlyOrdered`) because
  `CreateRagdoll` indexes `bodies[parentJointIndex]` *before* the child exists. The build array
  is parent-first by construction; an arm should hold it anyway.

`SwingTwistConstraintSettings` is not a free choice: it is the type Jolt documents as *"a
specialized constraint for humanoid ragdolls"*, and the only one `DriveToPoseUsingMotors`
accepts — so choosing it now is what leaves the door open for a later getting-up spec.

### `ragdoll.c/h` — the engine half

#### The spine has to be WALKED, not named

Ten of the twelve bones resolve by name through `skeleton_resolve_bone`. The spine does not, and
the reason is structural rather than a naming accident: `categorize_bone` (`rigging.c:133-135`)
returns `BCAT_SPINE` for *"spine"* **and** *"chest"* alike, so on the generated puppet's
`Spine` / `Spine1` / `Spine2` chain all three bones share one category — and
`find_matching_bone_smart` *"Returns bone index if EXACTLY ONE match found, -1 if no match or
ambiguous."* Asking it for a chest is asking it for something it is built to refuse.

`get_bone_position` (`rigging.c:171-183`) does disambiguate, but by MIXAMO NAME PATTERN —
`spine2` → 3, `spine1` → 2, bare `spine` → 1. A rig spelling them `Spine` / `Chest` /
`UpperChest` gets 1, 0, 3. That is a tiebreak, not a mechanism.

**So the spine comes from a hierarchy walk:** start at the hips, follow the chain of `BCAT_SPINE`
descendants, take the FIRST as the lower spine and the LAST as the chest. On a three-bone spine
that gives two bodies and ignores the middle; on a one-bone spine the two coincide and the
ragdoll has one spine body and eleven in total. The arms attach to whatever the walk called the
chest, which is where they attach anatomically on every rig regardless of how many segments the
author used.

That variability is why **the body count is a measurement, not a constant** — which matters most
for the gate arm below.

#### Building

`ragdoll_build_from_skeleton(Skeleton*, Mesh*, const mat4* globals, float node_scale)` resolves
the bones as above, measures each capsule, fills `RagdollBuild[]` and calls the C++ TU. Then, at
the seam:

```c
// Globals only, and for the same reason ik_solve takes globals only: nothing
// re-accumulates after this point.
void ragdoll_apply(RagdollSystem*, mat4* global_transforms);
```

- A **simulated** bone's global is the body's world transform brought back to model space by the
  inverse of the rig node's world matrix — the conversion `ik_set_world` already established and
  `gametest.c:3387-3389` already computes.
- An **unsimulated** bone re-accumulates from its parent using its bind local, so a hand follows
  its forearm rigidly and fingers keep their shape.

**Everything is measured in MODEL space and scaled once**, which is 12.10's lesson arriving
again: that spec shipped a live bug through a world distance against a model threshold, halved at
the app's own `PLAYER_SCALE 2`, and `ik-scale` exists because no other arm could see it. The
capsule dimensions come from model space and are multiplied by the node scale exactly once, at
build.

### The character handoff

`character.h:77` has `bool enabled`, set once and read in exactly one place. There is no setter,
and — the part that matters — `sync_character_controllers_to_entities` is **not gated on it**, so
a disabled controller still rewrites `entity->position` from the frozen capsule every step and
would fight the ragdoll.

So this spec adds `character_controller_set_enabled(cc, bool)` and gates **both** the update and
the sync on it. While ragdolled, the entity's position comes from the hips body instead, which is
what keeps the follow camera pointed at the body.

The capsule cannot simply be given a layer that ignores the ragdoll: `object_pair_should_collide`
(`physics.c:161-171`) rejects only static/static and trigger/trigger, and the character's inner
body is `OBJ_LAYER_DYNAMIC`, created by Jolt inside the `CharacterVirtual` and invisible to the
engine's body map. Disabling is the available lever.

### The demo

A `"ragdoll"` row in gametest's action table (`gametest.c:358-410`, the pattern `"spawn"` and
`"raycast"` already use) read with `input_action_pressed`. There is no damage or death concept
anywhere in the app — the only "caught" event is the chaser's proximity check — so a key is the
honest trigger and the spec should say so rather than inventing a health system.

## Verification

A new `ragdoll` gate group over `gametest --ragdoll-probe <case>`. A headless game has a real
physics world, so unlike 12.15's mode switch **the simulation itself is reachable** — this group
can step Jolt and read bodies.

| arm | asserts |
|---|---|
| `ragdoll-build` | **Structure first, numbers second.** Every simulated bone resolved (none left at -1), constraints == bodies − 1, and every parent index precedes its child — properties that hold on any rig. THEN the per-rig body count, stated for the generated puppet and for `t_pose.fbx` from what the build measures, because a rig's spine length decides it and a single expected constant would be wrong on one of the two |
| `ragdoll-shapes` | capsules are derived, not uniform: a thigh is thicker than a forearm and longer than a head, by ratio rather than absolute size so it holds on any rig |
| `ragdoll-scale` | the same rig at node scale 1 and 2 gives capsule dimensions in exactly 1:2 — 12.10's defect as an arm, since nothing else would see it |
| `ragdoll-settles` | stepped 300 times from a standing pose, the hips end below where they started and linear velocity falls under a bar; it comes to rest rather than jittering forever |
| `ragdoll-pose` | while active, a bone's global comes from its body and not from the clip — step, move a body, confirm the global followed |
| `ragdoll-frees` | build and destroy leaves the world's body count where it started, which is the `~Ragdoll` remove-then-release trap |

**Falsification is the deliverable**, per 12.14 and 12.15. At minimum: stub `ragdoll_apply` to
return early and confirm `ragdoll-pose` goes red while the others stay green; and drop the
`RemoveFromPhysicsSystem` call and confirm `ragdoll-frees` catches it.

**Must not move:** `anim` (18), `ik` (21+), `gamepad` (5), `save`, `ui`, plus `menu` /
`menu_focus` / `puppet` goldens. The pose seam is shared with IK and the animator, so `anim` and
`ik` are the ones that would report a mistake.

**By hand, and it is the acceptance test:** press the key while standing, while running, and
mid-jump off the plate; confirm the heap collides with the plate and the crater rather than
sinking, that the camera still frames it, and that it does not explode at `PLAYER_SCALE 2`.

## Corrected before any code was written

The first draft of this plan said **13 bodies and 12 constraints** and repeated it in the gate
arm. The list it came from — hips, spine, chest, head, and four pairs — is **twelve**. Counting
it exposed two further things, and the third is the one that mattered:

1. The count was simply wrong: 12 and 11, not 13 and 12.
2. **It is not a constant at all.** A rig with a one-bone spine yields 11 bodies, because the
   lower spine and the chest are then the same bone.
3. **The chest cannot be resolved by name.** Every spine bone shares `BCAT_SPINE`, and the
   semantic matcher refuses ambiguity by design — so the plan's "name the bones and any humanoid
   answers" was false for two of the twelve, and the fix is a hierarchy walk.

Worth recording because of how it would have failed: `ragdoll-build` would have asserted 13
against a build producing 12, and the tempting repair is to change the number to whatever came
out. That is writing the arm against the code instead of against the intent, which is the exact
failure spec 12.14 exists to prevent. The count is now a measurement the arm reports, with the
structural properties — all bones resolved, constraints == bodies − 1, parents before children —
carrying the actual assertion.

## Out of scope

- **Getting up**, and with it `DriveToPoseUsingMotors` and `SkeletonMapper`. The constraint type
  chosen here is the one that keeps that door open.
- **Vehicles and soft body / cloth.** Unbound in JoltC for the same reason and fixable the same
  way; the roadmap row covers all three and this spec closes one.
- **Authored ragdoll definitions.** Derivation is the decision above; a file format is what a
  shipping game eventually wants and a later spec's problem.
- **Partial / powered ragdoll** (a hit reaction on one arm while the rest animates), which needs
  the blend this spec is explicitly not building.
- **Hot-plugging the rig.** The ragdoll is built once from the bind pose and lives with it.

## What shipped, and where it diverged

Every phase landed as planned. Four things were wrong or missing in the plan, and three of
them were found by running the thing rather than by a compiler.

### The head could not be resolved by name either

The plan said ten of the twelve bones come straight out of `skeleton_resolve_bone` and only
the spine needs a walk. That was wrong: `t_pose.fbx` built **eleven** bodies, not twelve,
because `cetra_rig:Head` and `cetra_rig:HeadTop_End` both categorise as `BCAT_HEAD` and the
matcher refuses ambiguity exactly as it is designed to.

It is the same defect as the chest, so the fix generalised the spine walk into one helper —
nearest or furthest bone of a category under an ancestor — used three times. Special-casing a
second bone would have left the third to be found by whoever imports a rig with two necks.

**The count being a measurement is what surfaced it.** Had `ragdoll-build` been pinned to
twelve, the tempting repair would have been to edit the number.

### The node scale has to come back out of the basis

Watching the first kill: the character collapsed into small scattered pieces rather than a
body. `to_model` carries `1/PLAYER_SCALE` and a Jolt body's transform carries none, so their
product is a bone global shrunk by the node scale — which skins the character at half size
with its limbs at half their offsets. That reads as the ragdoll having exploded, not as a
scale bug, which is why it is worth a comment at the site.

12.10's lesson arriving a third time: **model space and world space differ by a scale, and the
place it bites is never where it was introduced.**

### Two things the plan specified and I did not implement until they were missed

- **The entity following the hips.** Written into the plan, absent from the first build; the
  camera framed where the character died while the body slid out of shot.
- **A free pad button.** `B` was taken by `ground` and `RB` by `quicksave`, so the first two
  choices would each have made one press do two things. The left stick click is the last
  button this app had not spent.

### Falsified, as required

| stub | result |
|---|---|
| `ragdoll_apply` returns early | `ragdoll-pose` **FAIL** at exactly 0.0000, other five green |
| `RemoveFromPhysicsSystem` skipped | `ragdoll-settles`, `ragdoll-pose`, `ragdoll-frees` all **FAIL** |

The second is stronger than expected and worth recording: skipping the removal does not leak
quietly, it **takes the process down** — the probe produces no output at all, because bodies
are destroyed while the broadphase is still indexing them. The arm still earns its place,
since a release build may not assert where a debug build does.

### Verified

| | |
|---|---|
| `ragdoll`, all 6 arms | pass; 12 bodies / 11 constraints on both rigs, capsule ratios exactly 2.0 at double scale |
| `anim` (18), `ik` (21), `gamepad` (5), `save` (8), `ui` (10) | pass — `anim` and `ik` share the pose seam |
| `gate-arm-docs` | pass, 52 groups matching |
| `puppet`, `menu`, `menu_focus` goldens | 0 px |

### Still owed

**Whether it looks like a person falling over.** The arms assert that it falls, settles, takes
its pose from its bodies and frees cleanly — all things a number carries. The joint limits are
authored constants (a knee gets 5 degrees of cone against 75 of plane, a hinge pretending to be
symmetric) and Jolt's own sample admits the same about its equivalents. It was watched once,
headless, and collapses and settles connected. Whether it settles in a pose a person would
believe is the thing to re-measure if a character ever reads as rubbery.

## Applied after review

Five review agents ran over the finished branch. Four findings were structural and the rest
were deletions; every one of them is in the code above rather than argued with.

### The layering, which was wrong in the one place the tree has no precedent for

`ragdoll.c` included `game/physics.h` — the **only** engine-to-game include in the tree. It was
there for two things: a `PhysicsWorld*` parameter and an object layer. Neither needed it.
`ragdoll_start` now takes the opaque `JPC_PhysicsSystem*` and a `uint32_t object_layer`, both of
which the caller already holds, and `ragdoll_jolt.cpp`'s `kRagdollLayer` — a literal `1`
restating `OBJ_LAYER_DYNAMIC` with a comment admitting it had to move if that enum was ever
reordered — is gone with it. The `_Static_assert` guarding the restatement went too: it checked
the C half against the enum and could never have seen the C++ literal, which is the copy that
would have been wrong.

### The humanoid, described four times

An enum, a `wanted[]` table, a `switch` assigning joint limits, and `RD_PROBE_BONES[]` in the
app. Four lists of the same twelve bones, three of which a fifth bone would have had to be added
to by hand. Now one file-scope `RAGDOLL_HUMANOID[]` carries the name, the parent, how the bone is
resolved and the limits it bends by, with `ragdoll_bone_name` the accessor the probe walks. The
limits live in that table rather than in a switch because `docs/verification.md` records them as
the thing to reach for if a character reads as rubbery, and they should be beside the bones they
bend.

### The body index, derived twice and hardcoded once

`ragdoll_start` built a `build_index[]` and threw it away; `ragdoll_apply` re-derived the same
numbering with a counter over the same filter; `ragdoll_hips_world` assumed the hips were body 0.
Three statements of one invariant, which survives one edit and not two. `RagdollPart.body` stores
it, and `bone_to_slot[MAX_BONES]` — springbone.h's shape, for springbone.h's reason — makes the
apply one pass.

### Springs and IK integrating into a pose nothing draws

The seam ran springs, then IK, then the ragdoll over the top. Both of the first two are stateful
integrators, so what they were doing was not merely wasted: a spring accumulated velocity against
a discarded pose and a foot lock went on holding a contact from ground the falling body had left,
and both would have handed that state back the moment the ragdoll ended. They are now the `else`
of `if (ragdoll_active)`. The app half of the same defect: `character_controller_is_grounded`
freezes at death, so `ik_weight` eased toward 1 and pinned there while `ik_update_targets` kept
raycasting and forming ground contacts under a body in mid-air. `player_ragdolled()` gates that,
the locomotion axis and the facing yaw.

### Deletions

`resolve_side`; `measured_radius`'s dead `length` parameter and the three scattered radius rules,
now one `capsule_for_bone`; `ragdoll_apply`'s `bone_count`, which the system already knows; the
`node_scale`, `active`, `to_world` and `count` fields, none of which was read more than once or
said anything the rest of the struct did not; `character_controller_set_enabled`, a plain-store
setter of the kind 11.108 says does not survive, and the `BY FUNCTION` label on a field in a
struct that carries none of the three banners; and three `jolt_ragdoll_*` exports nothing called.
`find_category_under` became `resolve_by_category` over `skeleton_mark_subtree`, rather than a
fourth hand-rolled parent chase with its own chance of forgetting the parent-first guard.

### Two arms that were not asserting what they claimed

`ragdoll-build` reported `constraints == bodies - 1` where the probe had computed the constraint
count as `bodies - 1`: an identity, green against any build. The probe now prints the parent SLOT
each body settled on, read back from the built system, and the arm asserts a tree — exactly one
root, every parent earlier in build order than its child (which is the ordering `CreateRagdoll`
indexes on), one constraint per body but the root.

And the group spawned gametest **seven** times for six arms, because the shared probe reader
drops rows whose case is not the one asked for — while one `build` run already prints the shapes
rows and one `settles` run already prints pose and frees. The ragdoll reader keys by case as well
and keeps everything the process produced: **four spawns**, same numbers.

### Deferred, with reasons

- **`animation_state_set_world`.** The state holds a model-to-world for the IK and another for
  the ragdoll, set through two different calls. Folding them is right and refactors `ik.c`'s
  shipped surface from inside a ragdoll spec.
- **`state->ragdoll` being borrowed rather than owned.** Latent only: `apps/render` never builds
  one, and the ordering that makes it safe under the game framework is now written down in
  `ragdoll.h`'s LIFETIME paragraph.

## The defect the review did not find, and six green arms did not either

Applying the review meant rebuilding, and rebuilding meant looking again. The character came
apart.

**A left-handed capsule basis.** `measure_capsules` built the body frame as `[x, dir, cross(dir,
x)]`, and `cross(dir, x)` is `-cross(x, dir)`: the basis is a reflection, determinant −1. Nothing
on the C side minds — `glm_mat4_inv` inverts a reflection perfectly well, so `bone_to_body` and
`body_to_bone` are exact inverses and the round trip closes before the wrapper. **Jolt keeps a
body's orientation as a quaternion**, and no quaternion is a reflection, so `GetQuaternion()`
returned the nearest rotation instead. The signature was unmistakable once measured: the spine,
which points up, came back exact; a thigh, which points down, came back 180 degrees out and
offset by exactly one limb length; an arm, which points sideways, by 90 degrees and `L/√2`.

**And the constraint anchor, independently.** It sat at `world.GetTranslation()` — the body's
centre, because that is where a `CapsuleShape`'s origin is — where the joint is half a limb back
along the twist axis. Anchored at the centre the constraint is still perfectly rigid; it just
pivots each limb about its own middle.

Either one alone produces a cloud of limbs. **All six arms stayed green over both.** It fell,
it settled, it took its pose from its bodies, it freed cleanly. Every one of those measures ONE
body, and a ragdoll is not a collection of bodies — it is a set of relationships between them.

### Two arms that measure a relationship

| arm | asserts |
|---|---|
| `ragdoll-roundtrip` | before any step, the pose the ragdoll was STARTED from comes back within 1e-3 m on every body. Placing a body from a bone and reading a bone back from a body are inverses; the pose goes through a quaternion in between, which is where a reflection is lost |
| `ragdoll-rigid` | after 300 steps every joint is within 2 per cent of its own limb of where it belongs — a bone at its bind distance from the one it hangs from |

Falsified: restore the reflection and the two go red at **0.42 m** and **0.92 of a limb** while
the other six stay green, which is the whole reason they exist written as a measurement.

### Measured and rejected

Passing the joint matrices to `DisableParentChildCollisions`, so Jolt disables every pair that
is already interpenetrating rather than only parent and child. Sibling thighs genuinely do both
start inside the pelvis, so this looked like the fix. It measured **bit-identical** — the
overlapping pairs were never the problem. Left off rather than kept as a plausible-sounding
no-op with a paragraph of justification attached to it.

### What this says about the earlier check

The look WAS run, and reported as "collapses, collides with the plate and settles connected."
The last word was false. Two 800x600 captures at frames 60 and 240 of a character made of
detached boxes read as a character made of detached boxes; the pieces were taken for the box
puppet's own geometry. **A strip through the collapse at `--screenshot-every 20` makes it
obvious**, and that is the difference between looking and having looked.

CLAUDE.md's rule is "if the work has a look, the plan needs a step where somebody looks at it."
12.16 had that step and passed it. The amendment this spec adds: **a look has to be able to
FAIL.** Two small frames of a segmented figure could not have shown this, so running them was
never evidence — it was a look-shaped thing to tick off.
