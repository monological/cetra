# Spec 11.34 — a projected grid, and an island that has a seabed

## Context

The water surface reaches 5.1° short of the horizon, and `apps/tree:412` claims it does
not. That comment — "Past the dome's rim, so the sea reaches the horizon rather than
ending in a visible edge" — was asserted, never measured. Measured: eye height over the
water is ~97 units, the clipmap is camera-centred with half-extent 1100, so its far edge
sits at `atan(97/1100)` = 5.1° below eye level while the horizon is at 0°. That is ~290 px
of an 1800 px frame showing skybox where there should be sea.

**The reach cap is structural, and it is the same decision 11.33 was proudest of.** Rings
tile exactly only because every level snaps to the *coarsest* cell, and that cell is
`extent·2/gridRes`. So reach and near-field stability are welded together: pushing extent
toward the horizon (~318,000 units at this eye height) makes the finest cell 311 units and
the 90-unit swell disappears. Adding ring levels does not help — the snap granularity
tracks the outermost extent either way.

**The clipmap was not a wrong fix; it was a fix aimed by a procedural goal.** 11.32 shipped
a uniform world-space grid, which put vertices where the world is rather than where the
pixels are — a real, measured defect. The clipmap fixed it and the fix worked. But 11.33 was
framed as "close every gap 11.32 left open", so it built the deferral that had a *name*.
Nobody wrote down how far the water must reach, so nothing tested the choice against it. A
projected grid solves the same density problem *and* the reach problem, and the clipmap's
central mechanism — rings as windows into a mip pyramid of streamed data — buys nothing for
a surface that is procedural and evaluable at any point.

**Intended outcome:** the water reaches the horizon, `apps/tree` reads as a domed island
with a real seabed under the whole sea, and no claim about reach survives unmeasured.

## Non-goals

- **Shore breakers, swash, a nearshore SWE solver.** Still a different simulation.
- **Jolt buoyancy.** `water_surface_at` is the seam; wiring bodies to it is not in scope.
- **Spherical-earth curvature.** The horizon will be an infinite-plane horizon. Bending the
  far field to a planet radius is a look decision worth its own change.
- **Keeping the clipmap.** One surface path. A projected grid handles bounded water too, by
  clamping the ray/plane solve; a `--water-mesh` mode would be two vertex paths, two sets of
  arms, and the kind of "temporary" branch that becomes permanent.

## What makes this cheap: the varyings are placement-agnostic

`water_vert.glsl` outputs seven varyings and **all of them derive from one world-space XZ
point** via `oceanShoal(p)` / `oceanEvaluateAt(p, …)` / `oceanPreviousWorldAt(p, …)`:
`WorldPos`, `ViewPos`, `Normal`, `CurrClip`, `PrevClip`, `Jacobian`, `Shoal`. Nothing
downstream of `water_vert.glsl:64` knows how `p` was chosen. `Jacobian` and `Shoal` have no
clipmap dependency at all.

So this replaces **how `p` is produced** and nothing else. The whole of `water_frag.glsl`,
`ocean.glsl`'s evaluation, the cascades, absorption, caustics, the medium and the motion
vectors are untouched. The `aGrid` attribute (`location 0`, `vec2`, stride 8) is kept and
reinterpreted from "grid coordinate in [-0.5, 0.5]²" to "lattice coordinate in NDC", so the
VAO/VBO layout does not change either.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — The projected grid

Replace `water_ensure_grid`'s two index sets with one screen-space lattice, and
`clipmapPosition` with a ray/plane solve.

**Geometry — keep the vertex buffer, delete the annulus.** `water_ensure_grid`'s VBO is
already a unit-square parameter lattice with a `vec2`-only attribute (`water.c:510-516`,
`location 0`, stride 8). That is exactly a projected grid's input. So the VBO and VAO survive
untouched and only the *interpretation* changes, plus the second index set goes: one index
set, one `glDrawElements`, no instancing, no rings, no snap. Resolution becomes a
*pixels-per-cell* quality knob rather than a world size.

**Placement needs ZERO new uniforms, using this codebase's own ray idiom.** The house style
is not to upload an inverse — it is the focal-reciprocal reconstruction, and
`cloud_march_frag.glsl:49-51` is the purest existing form:

```glsl
vec2 ndc = TexCoords * 2.0 - 1.0;
vec3 viewDir = normalize(vec3(ndc * invFocal, -1.0));
vec3 rd = normalize(mat3(invView) * viewDir);
```

`invFocal` is `1.0 / vec2(projection[0][0], projection[1][1])`, computed *in the shader* from
the forward projection — which `water_vert.glsl` already has. And view→world rotation is
`mat3(transpose(view))`, the trick `water_frag.glsl:286` already uses for its reflection
lookup. So the ray is built from uniforms the pass uploads today, and `waterCamPos` (already
uploaded) is the origin. Intersect the water plane, take world XZ as `p`, and the existing
evaluation runs unchanged.

Note there is **no ray-plane intersection helper** anywhere — CPU or GPU. `intersect.h` has
`ray_aabb_intersection` and `ray_point_at_distance` only. This is ten lines written fresh,
placed in `ocean.glsl` beside the surface it feeds. `create_fullscreen_quad_vao` is a
4-vertex triangle strip and is *not* reusable as a lattice.

**The far plane does not cap this, and that is the part worth getting right.** Camera far
planes here are 2000–3000 (`tree.c:920`, `forest.c:748`), so a vertex at true horizon
distance would be clipped. `skybox_vert.glsl` already solves exactly this with
`gl_Position = pos.xyww` — pin z to w and the vertex lands at the far plane in NDC while
keeping its screen position. So: rays whose plane intersection is beyond a cap (or which are
near-parallel to the plane) get their clip-space z pinned, which puts them precisely on the
vanishing line. The horizon row *is* the horizon, at any far plane.

**Dead code this deletes:** the T-junction stitch (`water_vert.glsl:72-112`) exists purely to
reconcile ring boundaries, and it currently costs two extra full `oceanEvaluateAt` +
`oceanPreviousWorldAt` evaluations on one vertex row per patch. The five ring uniforms go with
it, along with `water.h:26-40`'s snap rationale block and `ring_index_count`. The stitch's
*idea* is not lost — Phase 4 hands it to D4, where it fixes a gap the roadmap has open.

**Degenerate cases the clipmap got for free and this must handle explicitly:**
- Camera exactly on the plane, or within a wave height of it — rays are parallel, every
  intersection diverges. Clamp against a minimum ray-plane angle.
- Camera below the plane looking up — the surface is overhead; the intersection sign flips.
  `water_frag` already handles submerged shading (`cameraSubmerged`), so only placement needs
  the sign case.
- Displacement pushes edge vertices off-screen, leaving gaps at the frame border. Over-scan
  the lattice slightly beyond NDC ±1.

**Arms.** `water-draws` re-anchors from `2 / 5 / 131072` to the projected grid's own
integers — 1 draw, 1 instance, a fixed lattice triangle count. Note the constraint the
counter block records at `water.c:1042`: `meshes_seen` must equal `instances + culled` or the
profiler's own sum check fires. `water-crack` survives untouched — its comment is explicit
that it detects a *hole* (a pixel that equals the no-water frame), not a T-junction, so it
covers horizon and near-plane tearing just as well.

**New arm, `water-horizon` — the one that would have caught this bug.** On a fixture framed
so the horizon is in shot, assert there is no band of sky-coloured pixels between the
waterline and the horizon: walk a column from the shore upward and require the last water
pixel to be within N pixels of the horizon row. The current build fails this by ~290 px.

### Phase 2 — Wave-field mips, and the far field as a BRDF

A projected grid's far vertices each cover kilometres, and **the cascade fields have no
mipmaps** — `_water_make_field` sets `GL_LINEAR` with no `glGenerateMipmap` (`water.c:598`).
Sampling LOD 0 there is pure aliasing.

- Generate mips on the two displacing cascades after the spectral transform completes, and
  switch `MIN_FILTER` to `GL_LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR`. `GL_REPEAT` and mips coexist fine, and the
  fields are FBO render targets so the regeneration has to be sequenced after the ping-pong
  ends (`water.c:781-796`). **Costs no sampler unit** — water declares 13 of a hard 16 with
  units 11, 14 and 15 free, so mipping textures it already binds is free where a separate
  coarse field would not be.
- Select LOD per vertex from the lattice cell's world footprint against each band's
  `length_scale` (240 / 64 / 12 m, `water.c:43-45`), with `textureLod` in the vertex stage.
  **No precedent exists** — every `textureLod` in the tree is in a fragment shader, and every
  current vertex-stage fetch is plain `texture()`, i.e. implicitly LOD 0.

**Mipped displacement converges to the mean, which is the still plane** — so the far field
flattens *by construction* rather than by a special case, and the energy it loses is exactly
what the existing geometry-to-BRDF handover picks up: `WATER_SHORT_NEAR/FAR` already fades the
short cascade's slope into roughness past 118 m. Phase 2 extends that mechanism to the long and
medium bands rather than inventing a second one.

**This is the riskiest phase, and there is a recorded failure mode to avoid.**
`water_frag.glsl:153-157` documents that driving roughness from the *faded* slope instead of
from the energy the fade removed made distant water go toward the calm value in both channels
— so the horizon got smoother as its waves went sub-pixel, which is backwards. Read that
comment before writing the selector: the mip must hand energy *over*, not dim both ends.
Verify by measuring surface roughness in a far-field box, not by looking at it.

### Phase 3 — Precision at horizon distances

`oceanCascadeUv` does `fract(p / cascadeLength + 0.5)`, which bounds the *lookup* but not `p`.
At 300k units, `p / 240` is ~1250 and `fract` of that has already spent the mantissa the near
field needs.

Sample the cascades relative to a camera-snapped origin: subtract a world offset snapped to
the cascade period before dividing, so the argument stays small and the field stays anchored
(`fract` is periodic, so a period-aligned offset is a no-op on the result).

**Genuinely new ground.** There is no camera-relative rendering, no world-origin rebasing and
no fp32-precision discussion anywhere in the tree — `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md` has zero
matches for "precision", and everything is absolute world space in fp32. At 10 km a 240 m tile
leaves very few mantissa bits inside the tile, so this is a real requirement of reaching the
horizon rather than a tidy-up. No pattern to copy.

### Phase 4 — `extent` stops meaning two things

`extent` currently conflates two meanings. The geometry bound dies with the rings; **the bed
field domain survives and becomes the only meaning** (`water.c:417-423`, `ocean.glsl:83`,
`water_waves.c:80`, `water.h:215`). `ocean.glsl:84` already clamps out-of-domain reads to the
nearest edge texel, so a surface reaching past `extent` is already safe — it reads open water.

Corrections this forces, each currently stating the old meaning:
- `cscene.h:174` — "half-size of the drawn surface" → the shoaling bed's domain.
- `render.c:151` — `--water-extent` help text, same.
- `apps/forest/src/forest.c:699` — the comment promises the water stops where the terrain
  does. It will not; re-argue or drop the promise.
- `apps/tree/src/tree.c:415` and `:1120-1124` — the extent comment becomes obsolete, and the
  wavelength comment reasons from the clipmap's cell size, which no longer exists.
- `AGENTS.md:602-605` — "The grid is a camera-snapped clipmap … `--water-extent` is a size
  control again". Both halves become false.
- `specs/11.33`'s Phase 1 and its `# As built` section, and `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md`
  D3 (`:848-872`) and the priority table (`:1200`). **Supersede narrowly and honestly: the
  clipmap works, and nothing recorded about it was false.** The argument is that a projected
  grid is better — density exact rather than in 2× steps, no T-junctions, one draw, and reach
  decoupled from near-field detail. Do not cite 11.32's `:450-457` deferral as support; 11.33
  already established it was wrong.

**What the deleted clipmap leaves behind for D4 (terrain).** Worth two sentences in the
roadmap rather than dead code in the tree, because terrain is the item a clipmap was actually
invented for — `docs/aaa-rendering-roadmap.md:886` lists Asirvatham & Hoppe as D4's *first*
reference. The part water never used is the part terrain needs: rings as windows into a mip
pyramid of streamed height data. So:

- Record in the D4 entry that the full clipmap implementation — the snap arithmetic and the
  T-junction stitch — is at commit `8d04658`, as D4's reference implementation. Git is the
  archive; keeping the code live for a hypothetical consumer is the "temporary branch that
  becomes permanent" the review round flagged.
- **Point D4's own open T-junction gap at the stitch, because it is a better fix than the one
  the roadmap currently proposes.** `:976-980` and `:1265-1266` admit that `lod.c` passes
  options 0, so only `meshopt_SimplifyLockBorder` would lock a border, and *"two neighbours at
  different LOD levels can T-junction … None has been observed at any framing tried, which is
  large tiles and near-straight borders rather than a guarantee."* `LockBorder` fixes it by
  never simplifying the border, which keeps full border density forever. The water stitch lets
  both sides simplify and makes the finer side *agree* — evaluate the two even neighbours and
  average, which is by definition the straight line the coarser edge draws. That requires a
  surface evaluable at arbitrary points, which `terrain_height_at` is by construction
  (`procedural/terrain.h:42-46`, "a pure function of (params, x, z)").
- Do **not** pre-shape any of this into a shared primitive now. Terrain today is a different
  architecture — fixed tiles with meshoptimizer LOD chains (`procedural/terrain.c`) rather than
  rings over a resampled grid — and D4 cites both Hoppe and Strugar's CDLOD, so it has not
  chosen. Extracting a "shared" clipmap primitive would be guessing at that choice.

### Phase 5 — `apps/tree`: a dome that reads as a dome

Today: `GROUND_RADIUS 900`, `GROUND_HEIGHT 20` — a slope of 0.044, **2.5° at its steepest**,
never below `y = -20`. No rim, no flank, no mass below the water.

- Give the dome a profile that reads as an island from the app's framing (camera 600 back,
  eye 95, canopy 250). Raise the rise-to-radius ratio and/or shrink the radius.
- **Re-derive `TREE_WATER_LEVEL`.** It is a closed form off the paraboloid —
  `-GROUND_HEIGHT · t²` puts the shoreline at `t · GROUND_RADIUS` (`tree.c:399-411`). Any
  profile change invalidates it. Keep the *derivation* rather than hard-coding a new number.
- **Check the bed bake against the steeper ramp.** `WATER_BED_RES 256` over `2 × extent` is
  8.6 units/texel here. The shoal window spans 2.56 units of depth, which on today's 0.044
  slope is ~58 world units — about 7 texels. A steeper dome shrinks that run, and at some
  slope the bake under-resolves the shoreline. Measure it; raise `WATER_BED_RES` or the bake
  domain if it bites.
- Factor `generate_island_mesh` (currently `static` in `tree.c:267-378`) and
  `ground_height_at` into the `ground.c` that `ground.h` has always implied. `apps/CMakeLists.txt`
  globs `src/*.c`, so no CMake edit — only a re-configure.
- **Fix two stale things while in here.** `ground.h:9-12` says "Flat (0) beyond the rim"; the
  code returns `-GROUND_HEIGHT`. And the falling-leaves particle module collides against a
  **hardcoded plane at y = 0** (`tree.c:859-863`) instead of `ground_height_at` — already
  wrong, and a deeper dome makes it visibly wrong.

### Phase 6 — `apps/tree`: a seabed under the whole sea

There is no geometry past `r = 900` while water is drawn well beyond it. Today the depth
buffer there holds the far plane, so `path` saturates at `WATER_MAX_PATH = 64`
(`water_frag.glsl:85`) and the open water reads as uniformly maximally-absorbed body colour.
**A real seabed changes the appearance of the entire open-water region** — which is why this
phase belongs with the grid rather than after it.

- Build the seabed with `mesh_builder.h` (`mb_init` / `mb_vertex` / `mb_tri` / `mb_transfer`
  — it writes tangent `w = +1` and computes the AABB), following `create_island`'s node
  pattern (`tree.c:433-449`): file-static node, uploaded once, kept out of the tree-rebuild
  path for the reason `tree.c:456-461` records.
- **One height authority.** `ground_height_at` must remain the single function the island
  mesh, the grass roots, the seabed mesh and `tree_bed_height` all read, or the shoreline
  drifts from the ground it meets. Extend it with the seabed profile rather than giving the
  seabed its own.
- Steal `terrain_normal_at`'s approach (`procedural/terrain.h:48-50`) — central differences on
  the height function — instead of hand-deriving the normal the way `generate_island_mesh`
  does at `tree.c:314-316`.
- Reuse `island_albedo_tex` / `island_normal_tex` with a darker `material->albedo`; a third
  procedural generator only if that reads badly. Opaque, single-sided, no wind, no subsurface.
- Caustics currently have nothing to land on out there and will start showing.
- Check the shadow ortho fit: `ortho_size = GROUND_RADIUS`, `far_plane = 6000` with a 14-line
  derivation at `tree.c:985-997`. A seabed past 900 pushes on it.

```bash
./build.sh
# the horizon claim, measured rather than asserted
python3 scripts/gates.py --only water
python3 scripts/goldens.py      # water_fixture WILL move; re-bake and record the number
# cross-build, exposure pinned, on a scene with no water at all
./out/bin/render -m my_models/raiden/source/raiden_textured_rigged.glb \
    -t my_models/raiden/textures -e my_models/studio_small_03_8k.hdr \
    -a my_models/animations/strut_walk.fbx -s my_models/animations/T-Pose.fbx \
    -x -f 120 --no-springs --no-auto-exposure -E 1.0 -S after.ppm
```

Per phase: the full gate suite and all goldens. **Measure the noise floor first** — and note
`apps/tree` has no gate arm and no golden at all, and its own run-to-run floor is ~20k px
(`glfwGetTime` drag damping, `tree.c:1209`), so tree is verified by eye plus the deterministic
fixture arms, not by pixels.

**The horizon claim gets a number, not a comment.** The bug being fixed is a comment that
asserted reach without measuring it; the fix is not done until `water-horizon` prints the
remaining gap in pixels.
