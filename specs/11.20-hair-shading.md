# 11.20 — Hair shading (roadmap B8): the map shipped, the lobes did not

**Outcome: the strand map ships and drives the engine's anisotropic GGX. The
Karis/Marschner fibre lobes were built, rejected on look at every setting, and
DELETED.**

`Material.anisotropy` defaults to 0 and no committed asset binds a map, so the
default look is unchanged. This spec records what was built, what was measured,
why the lobes lost, and why the surviving half ended up on a channel that was
already in the engine.

The split matters and is the whole lesson: the *data* (which way the hair runs
at each texel) was correct and verifiable. The *model* built to consume it was
neither, and was replaced by one that already existed.

## What started it

Spec 11.19 fixed the coverage/translucency conflation and closed B8's stated
gate (B3.1 rejected on measurement, B3.2 shipped). Phase 6 of that plan was the
shading half of B8: Karis/Marschner fibre lobes replacing the isotropic GGX
highlight on hair cards.

The first implementation was rejected on look, twice. The cause was not the
lobes. It was their input.

## The stub, and why it failed

`hairStrandJitter` invented per-strand variation from the texel coordinate:

```glsl
float strand = floor(uv.x * 180.0);
return fract(sin(strand * 12.9898) * 43758.5453) * 2.0 - 1.0;
```

That assumes hair is 180 evenly-spaced vertical bands in UV space. Reading the
actual atlas (`Image_20_14.png`, 1024^2) refutes it: ~8 card regions whose
strands curve and lean up to 30 degrees off vertical at irregular spacing.
Constant-`u` bands follow them nowhere, so the jitter was noise laid over hair
rather than per-strand variation.

**The reusable lesson is not "the hash was wrong".** It is that the comment
beside it argued *for* the shortcut — "the information is the same" — instead of
flagging it as a stand-in. A procedural stand-in is a legitimate way to test the
lobe math before paying for asset plumbing. Presenting its output for a look
verdict without saying so is not, and it cost two review rounds judging a broken
input as though it were the technique.

## What was built

### The tool (`tools/gen_hair_flow.py`)

Derives a strand map from a hair atlas offline. The engine consumes an ordinary
authored texture, so a DCC-baked map would drop in with no engine change.

- **Orientation** from summed structure tensors of alpha and luminance. Tensors
  add and each scales with its own gradient energy, so whichever channel carries
  the strands dominates with no weighting constant. Measured on raiden: alpha
  carries twice luminance's cross-strand energy (mean |d/dx| 0.066 vs 0.034).
  The fixture is the other way round, so both paths are exercised.
- **Identity** by line integral convolution. LIC's defining property is the one
  needed: constant along a streamline, uncorrelated across them. Nothing simpler
  has it.
- **Tensor window sigma 4.0, by measurement.** A card's cut edge is not a strand
  but is a strong straight gradient. Over the 8 texels below each card's first
  opaque row: at sigma 2.0, 57% of confident texels read horizontal against 5.1%
  in the body. At 4.0 that falls to 39.7% over half as many texels. Costs
  nothing elsewhere — the two agree to 0.91 degrees median, orientation spread
  drops 3%. Residue ~0.24% of strand texels; eroding the outline would fix it
  and delete the strand tips, which matter more.

### The encoding

One RGBA8 `mask_array` layer: `.rg` a coherence-weighted doubled-angle
orientation, `.b` a strand identity.

**Doubled angle is load-bearing.** A strand has no head or tail, and
`mask_array_build` resamples every layer through a linear filter and then mips
it. Raw directions average with their own negations to zero — exactly where hair
is minified. Doubling makes antiparallel directions identical, so every average
is defined.

**Coherence as the vector's length** rather than a fourth channel: averaging
coherence-weighted orientation vectors is the correct way to downsample an
orientation field, so the mip chain reports its own uncertainty and the shader
falls back toward the card tangent instead of trusting an average of nothing.

### Plumbing

The map binds to `Material.anisotropy_tex` and rides `anisotropy_layer` — the
mask slot that already existed. `Material.anisotropy` scales it; 0 leaves the
isotropic highlight untouched.

An interim revision added a *second* direction channel (`hair_flow_tex`,
`hair_flow_layer`, `MASKS_PER_MATERIAL` 6 -> 7) beside the unused one. That was
the mistake this spec exists to stop being repeated; see "Where it ended up".

Scene files gained a texture vocabulary (`CSceneMaterialTexture`) whose paths
deliberately do **not** resolve against the scene file: the texture pool already
resolves against its own directory like every other texture, and two resolvers
on one path means the loser is silent.

## The gate (`run_hair_flow_gate`)

One quad, one material, one draw, one tangent. The atlas paints strands along
the card tangent in the left half and across it in the right, with the key off
to the side. Geometry, normal, material, light and tangent are identical either
side of the seam, so nothing but the map can split the halves.

| arm | ratio | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| strand map | **1.31x** | the shader reads per-texel orientation |
| no map | 1.05x | direction alone splits nothing |
| GGX | 1.05x | baseline: the scene is symmetric |

The two control arms are what make the split attributable, and they are the
reason a thin margin is still a real assertion: 1.05x is what this fixture
measures when nothing reads a direction, so 1.31x is a signal and not a
tolerance.

**The margin is thin because of the model, not the gate.** Anisotropic GGX
reshapes an existing lobe, so a per-texel direction redistributes a fixed amount
of light. The fibre lobes keyed their whole magnitude on the strand angle and
split the same fixture **7.21x**. That modesty is the feature — it is why this
version cannot blow out — but it leaves less headroom, so the render size is
pinned (minification mips the orientation away and the split collapses) and the
assertion is DIRECTION-AGNOSTIC: the halves must differ, not differ a particular
way. Anisotropic GGX stretches along the grain where a fibre lobe brightened
across it, so the sign is a property of the model and asserting it would be
asserting the wrong thing.

**Known gap, stated because an earlier draft of this spec overclaimed it.** The
gate falsifies a hashed ORIENTATION — a hash keyed on the texel coordinate
varies within each half but has the same distribution in both, so it leaves the
ratio at 1. It never falsified a hashed strand IDENTITY, which is what the stub
actually was. That arm has since been removed for a different reason (below),
so the gap is now moot rather than closed.

Second gap, still open: nothing runs `gen_hair_flow.py` in CI. The committed
`hair_fixture_flow.png` is frozen output, so the gate would keep passing after
the tool regressed. The tool's own ground-truth check (0.1 degrees against the
painted angles) only fires when a human regenerates the fixture.

The fixture also checks the tool: it knows the angles it painted and asserts the
derived orientation against them — 0.1 degrees in both halves.

## Why the lobes were deleted

Two reasons, neither fixable by tuning.

### 1. The substitution is not energy-paired (structural)

`pbr_frag.glsl` builds the microfacet term as
`numerator / (4 * NdotV * NdotL) * energyComp`, then the hair branch replaces it
with a bare normalised Kajiya-Kay lobe carrying **neither the denominator nor
the energy compensation**. The two are therefore on different scales by
construction, which reads as blown white highlights on a white-albedo surface
where the diffuse is already bright.

**No slider fixes this** — it is a units mismatch, not a magnitude. The lobe was
normalised against Blinn-Phong rather than against the quantity it stands in for.

**Pairing the scales is necessary and NOT sufficient**, which an earlier draft
of this spec got wrong. After the substitution the fibre lobe still flows
through the rest of a *surface* integrand:

1. it is foreshortened by `NdotL` on the CARD normal, where a fibre's projected
   area goes as `sin(T,L)` — and the card normal is the one quantity the strand
   map exists to say is wrong;
2. it is attenuated by `sheenScale` and `(1 - coatAtten)`, a surface-layering
   stack a strand is not part of;
3. it is paired with a Lambert diffuse whose `kD = 1 - F(H)` derives from a
   half-vector that, by this model's own argument, does not exist for a fibre.

So the unit of substitution was wrong, not merely its scale, and the blend
weight that selected it was a shading-MODEL selector wearing a float. The
right-altitude fix, if anyone rebuilds this, is a shading-model
branch yielding `(diffuse, specular)` per light — surface path untouched, hair
path supplying its own geometric factor and its own diffuse — composited once.
Start there rather than scaling the lobe and concluding the model is at fault a
third time.

### 2. Card hair is art-driven, and the model is not (categorical)

- **A card is flat; a fibre is round.** The model assumes a cylinder, where the
  surface tilts through an arc across one hair. The cards carry **no normal
  map** (verified: `Hair` and `Hair_1` declare `baseColorTexture` only), so
  every texel shares the quad normal and all strands light together. The map
  supplies per-texel *direction*; per-texel *facing* is simply not in the asset.
- **The atlas already has hair shading painted into it.** GGX adds one broad
  sheen and lets that detail read through. The fibre lobes generate their own
  high-frequency structure that competes with it — two sets of strands at
  different frequencies, stacked.
- **White hair hides the payoff.** R is uncoloured and TRT is tinted; on white
  hair with a near-white tint both read the same. A dark-albedo override of the
  same close-up shows the tinted lobe is genuinely there; the file lives beside
  the rig under `my_models/`, which is untracked, so it is not reproducible from
  a fresh clone.

Marschner/Karis is built for hair rendered as actual strand tubes. Applied to
flat cards it is being asked to reconstruct geometry that is not present, and
correctness in the map does not supply it.

### Where it ended up: the channel that was already there

The resolution, and the finding that should have come before any of the code.

`pbr_frag.glsl` has an anisotropic-GGX path keyed on a per-texel `anisotropyMap`
layer. It takes a direction, and — unlike the hair branch — it substitutes only
the NDF, so it flows through the shared `/(4·NdotV·NdotL) * energyComp`: it is
already on the correct side of the fault above. `.rg` direction plus a third
channel is also the KHR_materials_anisotropy texture layout, so the strand map
is that texture with a better encoding.

**`set_material_anisotropy_tex` had zero callers**, so the slot was unreachable
— and the first version of this work built a *second* per-texel-direction
channel beside it rather than filling it. Nobody looked before building. That is
the cheapest lesson in this document.

**What shipped:** the map binds to `anisotropy_tex`, the layer's read widens
from a scalar to the doubled-angle vector it already stored, and the resolved
tangent goes to `distributionGGXAnisotropic`. That deleted `hair.glsl`, five of
six material fields, `hair_flow_tex`/`hair_flow_layer`, and the seventh mask
slot — and left the engine with one live direction channel instead of two dead
ones. The gate transferred: "the highlight follows per-texel direction, not the
card tangent" is the same claim either way.

**Two costs, measured.** The fixture split falls 7.21x -> 1.31x (see the gate
section). And the per-strand identity channel lost its consumer: a rotation
jitter on the anisotropic frame measured **inert** — 0.0034 rms with 0.0% of
texels moved at maximum strength, with the uniform verified as reaching the
shader — because an in-plane tangent rotation barely moves the anisotropic NDF
where it strongly moved a fibre lobe's shift. The knob was dropped rather than
shipped doing nothing. `gen_hair_flow.py` still writes `.b`; nothing reads it.

**What this does NOT fix is the categorical problem above**, which is why hair
on cards stays a hard case regardless of which lobe consumes the map.

## Verdict, tested

Every hair parameter swept low and high in all combinations, in the GUI material
editor built for exactly this. The fibre lobes looked worse than GGX at every
setting, so they are gone rather than merely defaulted off — the argument for
keeping dead code is that someone will want it back, and this document plus git
history serve that better than 135 lines nobody compiles against.

**Do not re-open the fibre lobes on this asset.** What that would need: the
energy pairing above fixed AND the surface-integrand problem solved (they are
different faults), a per-texel normal bent around the strand, and a dark-haired
subject to judge against. Absent all four, raiden's groom is better served by
the highlight it already has.

**What IS live and worth using:** binding an anisotropy map stretches the
ordinary GGX highlight along whatever direction the map paints. That is a
general surface feature — brushed metal, vinyl, satin — that hair happened to
motivate. It cannot blow out, because it substitutes only the NDF.

## Cost when off

Nothing. `Material.anisotropy` defaults to 0, no committed asset binds a map,
and every material compiles the same specular expression it always did. All 18
goldens and 35 gates hold, and the mask array only grows a layer if a material
actually binds one.
