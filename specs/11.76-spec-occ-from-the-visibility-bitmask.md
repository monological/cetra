# Spec 11.76 — specular occlusion from the visibility bitmask

## Context

The user reported a doughnut-and-ring shadow pattern on `cornell_rooms`' floor that
disappears if either GTAO or the normals G-buffer is turned off. It has two causes.

**The first is fixed and merged (11.75).** `specOcclusionCone` guarded its horizon
reference with `open > 1e-4 ? visible / open : 1.0`, which reads as a divide-by-zero guard
and behaves as a cliff: `visible` reaches exactly 0 while `open` is still finite, so a band
returns 0.0, and the moment `open` slips under the epsilon the next band returns 1.0.
Measured down one column: 1.000 over y 1292–1310 against 0.000 over y 1316–1352.
Concentric, because those bands are concentric around an occluder. Replaced with a fade.

**The second is what remains, and it is architectural.** `gtao_frag.glsl` implements
Therrien et al. 2023 (visibility bitmask): 32 angular sectors per slice in a `uint`. It then
discards that to one direction plus one scalar, and `spec_occ.glsl` runs Klehm et al. 2011
cone arithmetic on the remains. The ring is manufactured downstream, by a reconstruction,
out of information the sweep already had exactly.

## What the papers actually support — and what they do not

Both papers are in `docs/` (`50c523a`, `050c325`). A close read changes the justification
for this work, so it is recorded before the design rather than after.

**Therrien 2023 contains no specular method.** `specular`, `glossy`, `BRDF` and `roughness`
do not occur in the paper. Its three demonstrated applications are AO, directional AO and
indirect diffuse. **This design is an extension of that paper, not an implementation of
it**, and the spec must say so — the previous draft of this plan claimed §3.2 prescribed
testing a reflection lobe against the mask, which is false.

What §3.2 *does* give is the template. For **diffuse** ambient light it divides the
hemisphere into K equal subregions, generates a direction at each subregion's centre, and
weights that sample by its own unoccluded sector count. This design applies the same move
to **one** subregion — the reflection lobe — instead of K uniform ones. One difference
worth carrying: Therrien's weights use `N_b` as the denominator, so the K weights sum to
the pixel's visibility; a per-lobe *ratio* needs the lobe's own sector count underneath,
which is what a multiplier on ambient specular wants.

**What the papers do provide is stronger than a prescription — they indict the current
code.** Therrien, §4.2, on the collapse this engine performs: *"If the ambient color had
been simply averaged out and then multiplied by ambient occlusion, a lot of the color
variation in the lighting would have been lost."* Therrien cites Klehm exactly once, in
§3.2, as superseded, naming two deficiencies: *"limited to a single ambient direction per
pixel and does not handle thickness properly"* — both being properties of collapsing 32
bits to a mean. And Klehm's own §3.2 states the design rule this engine breaks:

> Most importantly, instead of using the cone as a visibility approximation, we only
> restrict the directions from which we gather light. We still rely on AO to estimate the
> average visibility… Thus, the cone might cover incorrect directions, but AO accounts for
> a plausible darkening.

Klehm's construction fails **bright**, by design, degrading to bent-normal lighting.
`specOcclusionCone` uses cone overlap as an occlusion gate, which fails **dark** — and a
ring is a dark failure. Neither paper names the artifact; Klehm names the rule whose
violation produces it.

**Confirmed against the paper, for the design below:** sectors are uniform in *angle*
(§3.1 — *"we must convert the angles from cosine space to angular space"*), span the full
180° of the slice, and are **centred on the projected normal**, which is what
`hemiStart = n - HALF_PI` already does. 32 sectors is the paper's own recommendation
(§4.1: below it, banding around thin objects; 128 costs 5–10% for no visible gain).

**A correction to Phase 1, already committed.** `520c726`'s message cites "the paper's
round criterion". The paper says round twice — §3.1 prose and Figure 5's caption — but the
authors' own published reference shader uses `floor` for the start and `ceil` for the
width, i.e. what this engine had before. So the paper's text and the authors' code
disagree, and the engine now follows the text. That is still the right call, because the
bias argument stands on its own — marking every *touched* sector adds about half a sector
of phantom width per slab, per slice, which is a systematic darkening no denoiser removes —
and it was measured: heavily-occluded regions lightened ~1.3%, fully open regions
bit-identical. But the commit and `docs/therrien-…md`'s header both state this as
paper-conformance without noting the discrepancy, and both want fixing.

## What this plan supersedes

The previous plan's Phase 2 — derive the cone aperture from the **length** of the mean bent
normal (Klehm's von Mises–Fisher concentration measure) — was implemented, measured, and
reverted.

| bent-normal mean length | measured in-scene | Klehm's reference | that plan's derivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| unoccluded | **0.490** | 0.5 | 0.637 |
| deep contact | **0.675** (max 0.710) | → 1.0 | → 1.0 |

Note the measurement vindicates *Klehm's* 1/2 and falsifies that plan's own 2/π
derivation. It did not help: the usable span is 0.185 against a formula whose domain is
[0.5, 1.0], so `C = (1 − max(0, 2|N| − 1))·π/2` hands a deep contact a **58°** cone where
the AO-derived aperture gives 33°. Wider cone, less occlusion. Measured: the raw mean
removed the ring (0 reversals) but lightened deep contacts **+13.75%**; view-normalising
cut that to **+9.53%** and brought a ring reversal back. The stop condition written before
implementation was "contact shadows must NOT lighten materially". It did. Reverted.

So it is not a constant wanting adjustment — the stored length is too weak a signal in this
2-slice-plus-blur pipeline to set an angle, and no downstream reconstruction beats its
input. The fix has to stop reconstructing.

## Correcting what I told the user

I said the real fix was blocked because "the buffer is full". That is true of **channels**,
and I let it stand as if it were true of **memory**, which it is not.

`AoOut = vec4(1.0 - occlusion, bentN * 0.5 + 0.5)` — four of four channels live, and an
inventory of every reader found **no dead `.gba` consumer**: the blur, the temporal
accumulation and the upsample transport it, the split composite and the tonemap's `bent`
mode consume it, two debug views show it, and the roadmap books it as the foundation for
SSGI directionality, SSR occlusion and DDGI sampling. So the bent normal stays.

But a second render target is **under 10 MB at 1080p** plus two half-res draws, against the
244 MB of fp32 moment targets this engine already allocates for OIT. Cost was never the
blocker. Not having checked was.

There is also a genuine structural fact underneath it, from the paper: Therrien's mask is a
**loop-local register**, built and consumed inside the per-slice loop, and nothing
directional is ever written out. There is no later pass at which a bitmask could be tested,
which is exactly why the term must be computed in the sweep.

## The design

Compute the term where the mask lives, and store the estimator's **sums** rather than its
quotient.

**Every input is already in `gtao_frag`, unread.** Roughness is `linDepthTex.w` — the aux
attachment is `(motion.xy, viewZ, perceptualRoughness)` per `include/velocity.glsl`, and
the sweep reads only `.z`. `V`, `N` and therefore `reflect(-V, N)` exist at `:123–141`. The
per-slice `tangent`, `hemiStart` and `bitfield` are right there.

The lobe becomes sectors through the same machinery the occluders use — project `R` into
the slice plane, take its signed angle from `V` exactly as an occluder's is taken, widen by
the GGX half-angle, map to `[0,1]` across the hemisphere:

```glsl
uint lobe = lobeSectors((aR - lobeHalf - hemiStart) / PI,
                        (aR + lobeHalf - hemiStart) / PI);
specVis += projRLen * float(popCount(lobe & ~bitfield));
specTot += projRLen * float(popCount(lobe));
```

Four things there are load-bearing rather than incidental:

- **The `[0,1]` clamp inside `lobeSectors` IS the horizon reference.** `specOcclusionCone`
  runs a second `coneOverlap` against an open hemisphere because a reflection lobe always
  hangs partly below its own horizon and the BRDF has already zeroed that half. Clamping
  the sector range to the hemisphere does the same thing exactly, for free — and it was the
  *approximation* in that reference, not the reference itself, that `TRUST_OPEN` papered
  over.
- **The lobe uses TOUCH semantics where occluders use ROUND**, and the asymmetry is the
  point: an occluder is asked "do you cover this sector", a lobe "do you reach it". A
  mirror lobe is narrower than one sector; rounded it would round to nothing, `specTot`
  would be 0, and the surface would report itself unoccluded whatever stood in front of it.
  `lobeSectors` guarantees at least one bit whenever the lobe meets the hemisphere.
- **`projRLen` weights the slice**, the way `projNLen` already governs whether a slice
  contributes to AO at all. A reflection direction perpendicular to the slice plane
  projects to nothing and that slice has no opinion about it.
- **Store the sums, divide at the consumer.** The blur, the accumulation and the bilateral
  upsample would otherwise average a quotient whose denominator varies per pixel. Averaging
  `(visible, total)` and dividing last is the right way to denoise a ratio estimator, and
  it hands the early-outs a true neutral: a sky texel writes `(0,0)`, contributes nothing
  to a neighbour's sums, and leaves that neighbour's ratio unmoved — where a ratio
  representation must invent a value and drag the neighbour toward it.

**Storage.** `SpecOccOut` on `layout(location = 2)`, attachment 2 of `ssao_fbo[0]`, RG16F at
half render res, lazily allocated when split spec-occ is live — mirroring
`postfx_ensure_ssgi_targets`, which owns attachment 1 the same way. Then its own blur
target and its own `PingPong` history, because `ssao_blur_program` is shared with the
full-res contact-shadow denoise, and giving a shared program a second output and a second
sampler to save one half-res draw is the wrong trade. Four half-res RG16F textures.

**Consumption.** `specOccSplitAt` keeps its name and its role as the one function both
consumers call, but its body becomes the guard plus the divide — no `invFocal`, no view
ray, no cone. `coneOverlap` and `specOcclusionCone` stay, still reached by `--spec-occ
bent`, so the old term stays live and comparable rather than deleted. The roughness →
lobe-cosine mapping moves to a one-function include shared by both, so the two modes cannot
disagree about what a lobe is for a reason that is not the mode.

**Unchanged:** `off`, `legacy`, `bent`; `--no-ssao` still takes the composite's
`aoActive == 0` path; the bent normal's encoding, blur, accumulation and both debug views.

## Phases

Each phase states its predicted effect **and its scale** before implementation, plus a stop
condition. 11.75 shipped a phase built on a premise its own first measurement had already
disproved; 11.76's Phase 2 was caught only because the bar was written down first.

### Phase 1 — done, committed (`520c726`)

The round hit criterion. Amend: the commit message and `docs/therrien-…md`'s header both
claim paper-conformance without recording that the authors' reference shader disagrees.

### Phase 2 — the term, computed and stored

`gtao_frag.glsl` gains `lobeSectors`, the lobe accumulation and `SpecOccOut`; `postfx.c`
gains the lazy target, the blur draw and the accumulation call; the composite and debug
view 7 read it.

**Predictions.**
1. Open floor far from any occluder reads **exactly 1.0** — the bitmask is empty, so
   `lobe & ~0 == lobe`. A discrete claim: if debug view 7 is not byte 255 there, the
   horizon clamp is wrong.
2. The ring's residual flattens. 11.75 leaves a 0.974 → 0.956 dip; predict the reversal
   goes to zero and the remaining variation is under 0.005.
3. **Contact shadows must not lighten materially** — under 5% in the final frame. The
   bitmask term is better informed than the cone, so a corner whose lobe is genuinely
   blocked must still go to 0.
4. `--no-ssao` and `--spec-occ off|legacy|bent` are **0 px**.
5. Two half-res draws plus ~15 ALU per slice (the paper's own figure for its sample-loop
   overhead). Under 0.15 ms at the suite's resolution; over 0.5 ms is a signal, not a
   result.

**Stop conditions.** 3 fails → the diagnosis is wrong; revert and report, as with the last
Phase 2. 1 fails → a clamping bug; fix before reading anything else. 4 fails → the change
has leaked outside the split path.

### Phase 3 — coverage

An `ao-ring` arm on `cornell_rooms` through `--spec-occ-debug` (view 7) — **the first
executable coverage of any specular-occlusion mode**; today the whole feature is pinned by
goldens alone, and no arm exercises `legacy`, `bent`, or the composite's blend algebra.
Three assertions, because two are individually vacuous:

- the term **dips** near the contact (a build computing nothing reads a flat 1.0 and passes
  a monotonicity test perfectly),
- it recovers **monotonically outward**, no reversal (the defect),
- it is **exactly 1.0** far from any occluder (which the cone cannot satisfy, and which a
  constant-1.0 bug satisfies — hence the first).

Falsified by hand by reverting Phase 2.

### Phase 4 — goldens

**20+ of the 27 will move**, which is the real cost here: split is the default and every
recipe with an environment renders through the composite. The three LTC recipes carry no
environment so their ambient specular is black; `contact_debug` stores the contact term.
Protocol of `24b8051`: check before overwriting, inspect the largest movers 1:1, **crops to
the user before committing**, one revertable commit.

## Risks, named up front

- **Roughness drops to half res**, since the term is computed where the mask is. A
  roughness discontinuity at constant depth is exactly what a depth-bilateral upsample
  cannot help with, and would show as half-res blockiness. `layer_fixture` has per-layer
  roughness on one flat floor and is the check. Fallback: two lobes stored, interpolated by
  full-res roughness at the consumer.
- **Two slices sample the hemisphere along two great circles**, where the cone method tests
  a full 3D direction. Per frame this is directionally coarser; `projRLen`, the per-pixel
  slice rotation, the 4×4 blur and (under TAA) the per-frame golden-ratio turn convert that
  into a smooth aggregate — the same mechanism the AO term already depends on. A genuine
  trade, not a strict improvement, and to be recorded as one.
- **The ratio is a quotient of small integers**, so it can band — the paper's own reason for
  choosing 32 sectors. If it bands, the fallback is a half-sector floor on the lobe width,
  which is what `VIS_EDGE_SOFTNESS` was doing downstream, moved upstream where it belongs.
- `postfx_ensure_ssgi_targets` re-attaches attachment 1 with **no
  `glCheckFramebufferStatus`**. The new lazy attachment should check, and the existing
  omission is worth fixing while adjacent.

## Verification

Per phase: `./build.sh`; `--no-ssao` and `--spec-occ off|legacy|bent` **0 px** against the
same-build baseline; `--only ao,contact` green; determinism ×2 on `cornell_rooms`. Each
prediction measured against its stated scale before moving on — a change that moves the
wrong amount is a wrong diagnosis, which is precisely what 11.75 shipped. Perf interleaved
A/B/A/B at 150 frames (the warm-up window, per the `prepass-crossover` finding). Full suite
and goldens at the end, on a quiet machine, no other GPU work running.

**Independent of this plan:** the full suite on the 11.75 rebake came back 2 failures of 56,
both `water-fft-*`, both because `--water-fft-probe` printed no header at all — an
instrument failure, not a shading one, in a subsystem entirely upstream of the AO chain,
and passing 32/32 in isolation. Filed as contention pending a quiet-machine `--only water`;
not a blocker here.
