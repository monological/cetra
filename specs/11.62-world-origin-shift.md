# Spec 11.62 — D11: the world stops being measured from one point

## Context

Positions in this engine are 32-bit floats, and float precision is **relative**: the gap between
representable values is about `value × 1.2e-7`. Near the origin that is a micrometre; at 250,000
units it is 3 centimetres. Everything downstream inherits it — the camera stops moving smoothly,
shadow-map fitting shimmers, contacts quantise, and motion vectors (a *difference* of two large
nearly-equal numbers) lose most of what is left.

**This is not a hypothetical here, and the codebase already measured it.** `ocean.glsl:595-616`
records spec 11.35 hitting exactly this, fixing half of it inside water with a camera-snapped
lattice, and naming the other half as out of water's reach:

> *"`p` ITSELF is quantised … the camera term dominates — **0.03 units at a quarter-million**.
> Removing the division's share moved a **translation-invariance comparison by 3%**. Fixing the rest
> means carrying the lattice position camera-RELATIVE … **that is an engine-wide change rather than
> water's: absolute fp32 world coordinates are what every mesh in such a world is built from.**"*

So D11 has a named in-tree consumer already waiting on it, with a measured number attached.

And it carries a **live defect that has nothing to do with world size**: `shadow.c:1250` declares
`vec3 scene_center = {0.0f, 0.0f, 0.0f}`, never writes it, and feeds it to the outermost cascade's
look-at. That cascade is the camera-independent fallback everything else clips into
(`shadow.c:1405-1410`), so when it misses, **there is no fallback left and no diagnostic**. Two
separate files contort themselves around it: `terrain.h:125-127` centres the terrain on the origin
"deliberately", and `forest.c:1077-1079` sizes its shadows to match "which is why the terrain is
centred there".

**Intended outcome:** a world origin the engine can move, so coordinates stay small near the camera;
and a scene that genuinely needs it, so the capability is measured rather than asserted.

---

## The two rules the whole thing rests on

### Rule 1 — a shift must preserve DIFFERENCES

Every absolute position gets the same delta subtracted, *including its `prev_` twin*, in the same
instant. Then `curr - prev` is unchanged, so motion vectors, TAA reprojection and the fog's own
accumulator see nothing; relative geometry is unchanged, so the picture is identical; and magnitudes
drop, so precision returns.

### Rule 2 — anything that HASHES or TILES on world position must be handed the absolute back

Rule 1 covers everything linear. It does not cover anything that treats a world coordinate as an
*identity* rather than a location, and the inventory found several:

- **Wind phase.** `pbr_vert.glsl:90` takes `windOrigin = mModel[3].xyz` into
  `windObjectPhase` → `hash13(origin, ...)` (`wind.glsl:39-44`). `wind.glsl:29-35` says the world
  position was chosen *because* it is "stable under everything that reshuffles batches". A shift
  breaks exactly that, and because a hash is nonlinear, subtracting the delta cannot repair it —
  **every tree in `apps/forest` re-phases in one frame.**
- **Terrain layer triplanar** (`layers.glsl:121-141`) tiles on `worldPos` at per-layer periods of 3,
  4 and 6 units.
- **Cloud shadow** (`cloud_shadow.glsl:40`), whose map `sky_clouds.c:266` calls "world-locked at the
  origin".
- **Water's cascade lattice** (`ocean.glsl:617-621`), already half-snapped.

Snapping the shift to a power-of-two lattice does *not* save these — a 6-unit tile period is not a
power of two. The fix is one uniform, and **its reference point is the decision that makes or breaks
the verification**:

**`uWorldOrigin` is the ACCUMULATED shift since the world was authored — NOT the origin's absolute
location.** It starts at zero and takes `+delta` on every shift, so `local + uWorldOrigin`
reconstructs the position in *authoring* space. `windOrigin` becomes `mModel[3].xyz + uWorldOrigin`,
and the same for the tiling lookups.

The distinction matters because the two things Rule 2 must deliver pull apart under the other
semantics. Absolute reconstruction is stable across a mid-run shift, but it makes a world *authored
at* offset N hash differently from the same world at offset 0 — different absolute positions,
different `hash13`, every tree swaying on a different beat — which makes the `origin-identity` arm's
0 px claim **unpassable by construction**. Authoring-space reconstruction gives both: identical hash
inputs across configurations, and (since `local -= delta` pairs with `uWorldOrigin += delta`) an
unchanged sum across a mid-run shift.

**One hardening the hash consumers need and the tiling consumers do not.** Far from the new origin,
`local -= delta` rounds (see the Sterbenz note in Phase 5), so the reconstructed sum can move by an
LSB. A *tiling* lookup shifts by that LSB — invisible. A *hash* is discontinuous: an LSB flips the
phase entirely, so a mid-run shift would visibly re-phase distant wind objects. So `windObjectPhase`
snaps its input to a coarse grid (1/16 unit) before hashing — object origins are metres apart, so
the snap changes no phase identity, and it absorbs the LSB. The tiling lookups take the sum raw.

Every bug in this feature is a place that got the delta once, twice, not at all, or should not have
got it. That is why the verification below is not an afterthought: it is the only thing that can find
them.

## Phase 1 — the cascade fix, alone

Independently useful, independently verifiable, and it removes one variable from everything after.

`compute_directional_light_space_matrix` (`shadow.c:299-319`) centres on `scene_center`, and the
volume it covers is `±ortho_size` laterally **and `±far_plane/2` along the light** (`shadow.c:305`) —
so distance from the origin eats the depth budget too, which is the half `apps/tree` already
documents at `tree.c:1207-1214`.

Replace the hardcoded local with a **shadow-system field** (`ShadowSystem.scene_center`, default
`{0,0,0}` so every existing frame is byte-identical), set by the app or, later, by the origin shift.
`compute_scene_bounds` already exists (`scene.c:1023`) and is used for framing, probes and GI — it is
the obvious default source, but wiring it automatically is a behaviour change, so it stays opt-in.

Also fix here, while the file is open:

- **The texel-snap lattice is anchored at the world origin** (`shadow.c:350-356`).
  `floor(center_ls/texel)*texel` stops being exact arithmetic at large `center_ls`, so cascades
  degrade far out *independently* of `scene_center`. Snap relative to the scene centre.
- The roadmap cites `terrain.h:36-38` for the centring warning; it is at `terrain.h:125-127`.

**Two things the arm needs that the field alone does not provide.** First, a lever: nothing can set
`scene_center` from outside the process, so Phase 1 also adds `--shadow-center <x,y,z|auto>` to the
render app (`auto` = `compute_scene_bounds` centre), which is the opt-in wiring. Second, the right
failure mode: with `cascade_count > 1` the inner cascades are **camera-frustum-fitted**
(`shadow.c:1400-1418`) and follow an offset camera perfectly well — only the outermost fallback map
is `scene_center`-anchored, so a shadow resolved by an inner cascade survives the offset and the arm
reads green against a broken build. The arm therefore passes **`--shadow-cascades 1`**
(`render_args.h:72`, already exists), which takes the classic single-map path where the anchoring is
the whole story.

**Gate arm `shadow-offset`**, and the mechanism is not obvious, so it is named here.
`assets/dir_shadow_fixture.gltf` is the corpus's dedicated cascade fixture (`goldens.py:158`) and
its four nodes carry **no `translation`** — `gen_dir_shadow_fixture.py:194` bakes positions into the
vertex buffer. It is plain-text JSON with a base64 buffer, so an arm can add `nodes[i].translation`
and move the whole scene **without touching the buffer at all**. `gates.py` already reads node
translations out of a fixture's glTF (`_lut_patch_points`, `gates.py:2755-2767`), so this is an
existing idiom rather than a new one.

It has to be done that way because **a `.cscn` cannot translate geometry**: `cscene.c:92-105` reads
`models[].path` and nothing else, so `cscn_copy` can move the camera, the lights and the fog volumes
but not the meshes. Spec 11.35 hit this exact wall (`11.35:520-522`) and dropped its arm over it.

The arm renders at the origin and at an offset with `scene_center` following, and asserts the shadow
survives; today it vanishes. Falsified by pinning `scene_center` back to `{0,0,0}`.

## Phase 2 — a world that needs it

Nothing in the corpus lives far enough out to feel this. The furthest *geometry* is `apps/tree`'s
~2,800-unit seabed; the furthest anything gets is `aerial_fixture`'s camera, at y=400 aiming −19,500
(`aerial_fixture.cscn:11-12`) — and that is still two orders of magnitude short of where fp32 bites.
So build the need before the fix, and **measure** rather than predict.

**`apps/forest --world-offset <units>`** places the entire world — terrain domain, scatter, water,
lights, camera, physics — at that distance from the origin. Nothing else changes.

**The flag has two modes, and the identity claim is only achievable in one of them.**

- **Degrade mode** (Phase 2, before the shift exists): the offset is *materialised* — added to every
  position in fp32. At 262144 that addition rounds each coordinate to a 1/32-unit lattice, which is
  the degradation itself: this is the measurement, captured and recorded in the spec before Phase 3.
- **Origin mode** (Phase 3 onward): the world is *built in origin-relative coordinates* — the same
  floats as offset 0, bit for bit — with the scene's world origin set to the offset. The offset
  never touches a position; it exists only in the origin bookkeeping and `uWorldOrigin`.

The order of operations is the point. Materialising `authored + 262144` and then shifting back
recovers `round(authored + 262144) − 262144` — the subtraction is exact, but the *authoring
addition* already lost up to 1/64 unit per coordinate, so a "shift the degraded world" formulation
can never reach 0 px and would report the authoring quantisation as an implementation bug. Origin
mode sidesteps the addition entirely, which is what makes **bit-identical to offset 0** an
achievable bar — and it is also the honest architecture: "place the world at N" *means* "set the
origin to N".

`262144` is chosen as an exact power of two so that in origin mode every arithmetic step that does
involve the offset (the Rule 2 sums, the physics absolute bookkeeping) is as clean as fp32 allows.

`terrain.h`'s domain is `[-extent, +extent]` with no centre field, so `TerrainParams` gains one —
which is also what lets `terrain.h:125`'s workaround comment be deleted rather than reworded.

### What it measured, and the finding that reorders the rest of this spec

`--render-mode 6`, 800x450 (1,440,000 px on this display), 20 frames, seed 1337, the camera moved
with the world so framing is identical and the only variable is the magnitude of the coordinates.
Floor measured first: **0 px** across two runs at this framing.

**The dominant cost of moving a world is not precision. It is the wind phase hash, and it is already
fully paid at TWELVE units out.**

| offset | as the app ships | wind phase hash neutralised | worst code |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 621,597 px (43.2%) | **1,501 px (0.10%)** | 15 |
| 120 | — | 2,109 px (0.15%) | 18 |
| 1,200 | 617,654 px (42.9%) | 10,526 px (0.73%) | 25 |
| 12,000 | 595,398 px (41.4%) | 56,895 px (3.95%) | 67 |
| 122,880 | — | 517,317 px (35.9%) | 95 |
| 262,140 | 780,233 px (54.2%) | 647,470 px (45.0%) | 95 |

Read the left column first: it is **flat at ~43% from 12 units to 12,000**, which is the signature of
something that is not a precision effect at all. `windObjectPhase` hashes `mModel[3].xyz`, and a hash
is discontinuous — twelve units is as good as a quarter of a million, so every one of forest's 2,000
trees sways on a different beat the instant the world leaves the origin. Every offset in this table
is a multiple of 12, the LCM of forest's four layer periods (4, 6, 4, 3 world units per tile), so the
ground's world-space tiling is held at one phase throughout; without that alignment the figures are
about 19% worse again (770,757 against 627,201 channel-differences at ~12,288).

The right column is the real precision curve, and it is orderly: monotonic, crossing 1% of the frame
near 1,200 units and 4% at 12,000. Underneath it the height function alone — probed through
`--terrain-height-probe`, which touches neither the scatter nor the renderer — degrades exactly as
fp32's relative precision predicts, **quadrupling as the offset quadruples**: 0.000036 units at
1,024 out, 0.000275 at 16,384, 0.004448 at 262,144, against 53 units of relief.

Two things this rules out, both checked rather than assumed. The **scatter does not diverge**: its
rejection sampler consumes a variable number of RNG draws, so a perturbed candidate could have
reshuffled every tree after it, but `--scatter-probe` reads bit-identical aggregates at offset 0 and
1,024. And the **splat and the macro tint do not move**, because both are sampled through the domain
conversion and so are translation-invariant by construction.

**The consequence for Phase 3:** Rule 2 is not a footnote to Rule 1, it is the larger half, and it is
what the identity arm will actually be measuring. Rule 1's failures are one-frame velocity spikes;
Rule 2's failure is a permanent 43% of the frame, reachable from twelve units away.

## Phase 3 — the origin shift

```c
// scene.h
void scene_set_world_origin(Scene* scene, const vec3 new_origin);
```

Subtracts `new_origin - scene->world_origin` from every absolute position the engine owns, records
the new origin, and accumulates the delta into the authoring-space offset `uWorldOrigin` publishes
for Rule 2.

**The call SCHEDULES; the frame top APPLIES.** A shift executed wherever the app happens to call
from would run mid-frame, after some passes have consumed positions and before others — exactly the
half-shifted world the rules forbid. So `scene_set_world_origin` records a pending origin and the
engine applies it at the frame top, the same idiom the render-scale switch already uses
(`engine_run`'s resolution sync applies the *scheduled* scale change at the frame top, never
mid-frame).

**There is no engine-owned frame-top hook today, and that is the first thing to build.**
`apply_transform_to_nodes` (`scene.c:838-919`) is the single root-to-leaf walk, and it is called by
**every app**, never by the engine (`forest.c:1269`, `tree.c:739`, `render.c:1516`, and three more),
seeded with `engine->model_matrix`. That seed is the closest existing precedent — `apps/render`
already shifts its whole graph through it (`model_recenter_offset`, `render.c:1505`, applied
`:1510-1517`) as a load-time one-shot. So the mechanism exists; what does not is an engine-owned
moment to run it.

**Where that moment is, exactly.** `engine_run` orders the frame `gi_volume_update` (`engine.c:2361`)
→ `render_shadow_depth_pass` (`engine.c:2371`) → clear → **app callback** (`engine.c:2454`, which is
where the graph walk lives) → present. So the two passes that consume world positions run *before*
the app's walk — a one-frame lag `render.c:1102-1105` already documents. The shift therefore belongs
at the very **top of `engine_run`'s body, above `gi_volume_update`**, not merely "before the walk".
Anywhere later and the first shifted frame has its shadows and its GI fitted to the old origin.

One cost, not a defect: `scene.c:873` uses `memcmp(global_transform, prev_global_transform)` as its
"did this node move" test for recomputing `normal_matrix`. On a shift frame that is true for **every**
node, so the whole graph takes a `glm_mat4_inv`. A one-frame spike, worth knowing before it is
misread as a leak.

**What moves.** `SceneNode.original_transform` is *local*, so only **root-level** children need
translating — the walk carries the rest. `global_transform` and `prev_global_transform` are cached
absolutes and take the delta too (recomputing loses the `prev` and breaks Rule 1).

Then: the camera's position **and** `look_at`, `GIVolume.grid_min`,
`ReflectionProbe.position`/`box_min`/`box_max`, `Scene.fog_volumes[].center`, and particle pool
positions. Lights are **not** on this list: `Light.global_position` is recomputed from the owning
node on every walk (`scene.c:880-893`), so the graph shift carries them — the two out-of-walk
writers (`import.c:1321`, load-time; `emissive_light.c:518`, re-placed per frame) are both upstream
of or downstream of the shift and safe. Verify at implementation that no light exists without a
node; if one can, it joins the list.

PostFX holds three world values of its own. `local_fog_center_density[]` and `water_level_y` are
republished every frame (`scene_publish_fog_volumes_to_postfx`, `water_publish_to_postfx`) and so
follow for free — but `probe_pos` / `probe_box_min` / `probe_box_max` (`postfx.h:304-306`, SSR's
parallax proxy) are written at probe **capture** time (`probe.c:173-175`) and do not.

**Five that need more than a subtraction**, all found in the inventory:

- **Terrain bakes absolute world coordinates into the VBO.** `terrain_build_tile` (`terrain.c:650`)
  writes `x0 = -extent + tile_span*tx` into the vertex buffer, and `apps/forest` attaches those
  meshes to nodes with **identity** transforms (`forest.c:673-684`). The world position lives in the
  buffer, not a matrix — so a shift either rebuilds the tiles or gives the nodes a transform. Giving
  them a transform is the cheap one and it makes `TerrainParams`' new centre field honest.
- **The GI atlas and the probe cubemap are baked AT a world position.** Their *contents* are wrong
  after a shift, not just their bounds. `gi_volume_mark_dirty` (`gi_volume.c:101`) already exists and
  re-arms every probe; the probe needs an equivalent re-capture.
- **Water's bed re-bake guard has no origin term** (`water.c:750-751` checks extent, level and
  units-per-metre only), so a shift would silently keep a bed baked about the old origin. Also
  `ShoreChain.origin[]` and its per-column run-up history (`shore_chain.h:63-79`) are world-anchored
  and persist across frames.
- **The particle TF backend keeps positions in GPU buffers** (`particle_sim_tf.c:29`), so the CPU
  subtraction does not reach them — they need a re-upload or a TF pass.
- **The shadow catcher plane is hardcoded at the world origin** with no centre uniform
  (`catcher_vert.glsl:13`), as is the sky ground projection (`scene.h:210-211`).

**The `prev_` checklist for Rule 1 is exactly three entries, and two of them are MATRICES.**

- `SceneNode.prev_global_transform` (`scene.c:864`) — a vector subtraction on the translation column.
- `Engine.prev_view_proj` (`render.c:1603`) and `fx->froxel_prev_view`/`froxel_prev_proj`
  (`postfx.h:348-349`) — **not** a subtraction. We need `M_new · p_new == M_old · p_old` with
  `p_old = p_new + delta`, so the correction is a right-multiply: `M_new = M_old · T(+delta)`.
  Subtracting the delta from the matrix's own translation column is the wrong operation and gives the
  wrong answer under any rotation.

Everything else velocity-driven is downstream of those. `run_temporal_accum`'s seven consumers (SSR,
GTAO, SSGI, SSS, contact shadows, the fog layer, TAA) all reproject as `histUv = TexCoords -
velocity` with no world term — they amplify a mistake here but cannot make one. A `prev_` that misses
the delta is a one-frame velocity spike across the entire screen, and the Phase 2 instrument is
sharpest at exactly that.

**Verified IMMUNE — do not add these to the checklist.** The inventory chased each and found it safe,
which is worth recording so the next reader does not re-chase them:

- The **cloud march's** `prev_view` zeroes its translation columns (`sky_clouds.c:400-402`) and the
  shader uses `mat3(prevView)` on a ray *direction*.
- `prev_bone_rows` (`animation.h:144`) and the **spring-bone Verlet** tips are **model space** — the
  world part rides on `uPrevModel`.
- Water's `oceanPreviousWorld` **recomputes** from this frame's `p` rather than storing one, and
  `cascade_prev_array` holds a displacement field in tile-UV space. Water's exposure is its *lattice
  indexing* (Rule 2), not its history.
- `Light.global_position` is recomputed every walk; derived emissive panels are re-placed from current
  transforms each frame (only the local plane fit is cached); `Mesh.aabb` is local space.

**Two small traps:** `gui.c:1007` clamps the camera's look-at to ±100 world units, and
`MouseDragController` caches `free_start_cam_pos`/`free_start_look_at` for a drag's duration
(`app.h:35-36`), so a shift mid-drag snaps.

## Phase 4 — physics, and the app callback

Jolt bodies hold their own positions, and its warm-start contact impulses carry across frames.
`JoltC` exposes `BaseOffset` only as a *contact-manifold* precision offset, not a world shift, so
this has to be an explicit iterate-and-translate — plus the `CharacterVirtual` controller, whose
position is separate from any body.

**And there is nothing to iterate over yet.** `PhysicsWorld` keeps no body list: `body_entity_map` is
private to `physics.c`, and walking the `EntityManager` instead would miss every body created without
an entity. So Phase 4 starts by giving `PhysicsWorld` an owned body array — small, but it is the
prerequisite, not a detail.

Then a callback, because the engine cannot know about app state:

```c
void scene_set_origin_callback(Scene*, void (*on_shift)(const vec3 delta, void* ctx), void* ctx);
```

`apps/forest` caches 5,000 scatter positions and a player entity; `apps/tree` caches player state.
The callback is the honest admission that "hold nothing in world space" is not a contract this engine
can impose on its apps.

## Phase 5 — automatic on threshold

The engine tracks camera drift from `scene->world_origin` and shifts when it passes
`SCENE_ORIGIN_SHIFT_DISTANCE`. Two constraints that decide where the call site goes:

- **At the frame top**, in the precise sense Phase 3 pins down: above `gi_volume_update`
  (`engine.c:2361`), not merely before the graph walk. No pass may see a half-shifted world.
- **Snapped to a coarse lattice**, not to the camera exactly — shifting to an arbitrary camera
  position makes the delta an arbitrary float and every position takes a fresh rounding. Snapping to
  a power-of-two grid keeps the delta exactly representable, which is the same argument
  `ocean.glsl:617-621` already makes for its own lattice snap.

  **But "delta exactly representable" does not make the shift lossless everywhere, and the plan must
  not claim it does.** `x − d` is exact when `d/2 ≤ x ≤ 2d` (Sterbenz) — the neighbourhood the shift
  is aimed at, which is where every bit matters. Far *outside* that zone the subtraction rounds:
  `0.1 − 262144` lands on a 1/32-unit lattice, so a position much smaller than the delta takes an
  error bounded by its own relative precision *at its new magnitude*. That error is exactly the
  precision the position was always going to have out there — harmless as a location, which is why
  the shift is still correct — but it is an LSB a *hash* would amplify into a phase flip, which is
  what Rule 2's snap-before-hash absorbs. The honest statement: **lossless where it matters, bounded
  by inherent fp32 precision everywhere else.**

## Verification

The instrument is **offset invariance**, and it is unusually strong here: at an exactly representable
offset, a correctly shifted world computes on *the same numbers* as an unshifted one, so the frames
must be **bit-identical**, not merely close. That fits `gates.py`'s existing shape exactly — every
arm there compares two renders of one build against a peak-error bar of `LSB = 1/255`
(`gates.py:70-80`), with no stored reference to drift. The closest template to copy is
`run_sss_invariance_gate` (`gates.py:705-748`), the one existing arm asserting that a world quantity
survives a configuration change.

```bash
./build.sh
python3 scripts/goldens.py                    # every golden 0 px after every phase
python3 scripts/gates.py --only origin,shadow,terrain,layers,water
python3 scripts/gates.py                      # once, at the end
```

| arm | asserts | rejects |
|---|---|---|
| `shadow-offset` | far shadows survive an offset scene | Phase 1's whole point; falsified by pinning `scene_center` to zero |
| `origin-identity` | forest at `--world-offset 262144` (**origin mode**) is **0 px** against offset 0 | any subsystem that missed the delta, or leaked an absolute where authoring space was owed |
| `origin-degrade` | the SAME comparison in **degrade mode** differs, and by how much | proves the test can fail, and is the number that justifies the feature |
| `origin-motion` | a moving camera across a shift frame produces no velocity spike | a `prev_` that took the delta once, not at all, or as a subtraction where a matrix was needed |
| `origin-fog` | the froxel volume across a shift frame, `--fog` **on** | `froxel_prev_view`, which no other arm can see |
| `origin-physics` | the character stays on the terrain across a shift | Phase 4 |
| `origin-lossless` | shift + shift-back is bit-identical **for the world's own geometry** (all of forest at offset 262144 sits in `[d/2, 2d]`, the Sterbenz zone where subtraction is exact) | a delta that is not exactly representable |

Three practical notes the inventory produced, each of which would otherwise cost a debugging session:

- **`origin-fog` must turn fog ON, against the harness's own default.** `_forest_run`
  (`gates.py:3877-3879`) passes `--no-fog` deliberately, to save the froxel volume's per-run cost. But
  the froxel accumulator is *precisely* what a shift breaks, and it is the one history in the engine
  that reprojects through a stored camera rather than the velocity buffer — so the cheap default is
  blind to the sharpest failure. Alternatively run this arm on the `froxel_fog` golden's fixture,
  which carries a CSM and the volume together.
- **`origin-lossless` and `origin-physics` should read numbers, not pixels.** There is one precedent:
  `run_fbx_unit_gate` (`gates.py:8595-8651`) asserts a light's `global_position` by parsing a
  `print_light` log line. A position that survives a shift-and-shift-back is a claim about *bits*, and
  a frame is the wrong instrument for it.
- **Nothing in the tree can place a scene away from the origin today.** No `--translate`,
  `--offset` or `--world-origin` flag exists in any app; `--no-recenter` is derived from scene bounds
  and cannot be set to a value, and `cscene_apply.c:70` forces it on for every `.cscn` anyway. So
  Phase 2's `--world-offset` is not a convenience — it is the only way any of these arms can exist.

**`apps/forest` is not pixel-deterministic** on its ordinary path (the Hillaire sky moves ~35,000 px
run-to-run), so every pixel arm uses `--render-mode 6` (0 px) or `--no-sky` (~4 px), with `--seed`
pinned and the camera given explicitly as `FOREST_CAM` (`gates.py:3857`) does. Measure the floor at
the exact framing first — a pixel count without its floor is not a measurement.

The goldens need no such care: `cmd_rebake` (`goldens.py:296-303`) renders every recipe twice and
**refuses to bake on any drift**, so each one is x2-deterministic by construction. The eight that
carry a shadow — `dir_shadow`, `translucent_shadow`, `ao_fixture`, `cornell_box` / `cornell_leak` /
`cornell_point`, `froxel_fog`, `contact_debug` — are the set Phase 1 must leave untouched, and
`cornell_box` is the only one that exercises the GI volume at all.

`origin-degrade` is the arm that makes the rest honest: it is the only one that fails if the whole
feature is a no-op, and its number is what the spec should quote instead of my 10–20 km estimate.

**Three defects the arms found that no amount of reading would have.** Each was a world-space
absolute that nothing thought of as one:

- **A layered material's splat is anchored to a WORLD rectangle.** Missing it does not degrade the
  ground, it re-addresses it — every texel reads somewhere else and the surface comes back made of
  different materials.
- **`forest --cam-eye` re-pins the camera to an absolute every frame**, silently undoing the shift for
  the camera alone and leaving it looking at where the world used to be. A flag is a coordinate
  somebody typed, which is authoring space, and after a shift that is no longer storage.
- **The follow camera reads the ENTITY's cached position, not the controller's.** After a shift it
  placed the camera a whole delta away, which the automatic threshold read as *more* drift and shifted
  again: the origin went 4096 → 8192 → 4096 → 0 → 4096. No picture shows that, and neither does an
  arm that only asks whether a shift happened — which is why `origin-auto` asserts the COUNT.

**Two things the plan budgeted that were already there.** `PhysicsWorld` needed no new body array: the
body→entity map is the complete set, because `entity_add_rigid_body` is the only site that calls
`CreateBody` and it always registers. And `compute_scene_bounds` was the right source for
`--shadow-center auto` unchanged.

**One claim in the plan that measurement overturned.** `shadow-offset` at the viewer's default of 3
cascades reads 0.0000 *both* ways — the camera-fitted inner cascades resolve a fixture this small
whatever `scene_center` says, so the arm is blind unless it forces `--shadow-cascades 1`. The
single-map path is where the anchoring is the whole story.

**And one bound stated honestly rather than overclaimed.** Reconstructing an authored position from
storage is not exact: the sum rounds at `uWorldOrigin`'s own ulp, and stored positions change on every
shift. `windObjectPhase` snaps to a whole unit first, which makes re-phasing *rare* rather than
impossible — about 3% of objects at a quarter-million units out, 0.05% at 4,096. The exact fix is to
carry the phase rather than derive it, and the reason that was declined is a kilobyte of std140
padding, not principle.

## Out of scope

- **fp64 positions.** UE5's answer, and a far larger change; origin shifting is the fp32-preserving
  one and is what the roadmap booked.
- **World-partition / streaming cells.** That is D4, explicitly downstream.
- **Making forest's terrain physically larger.** At `extent` 25,000 the tile grid gives 130 units per
  vertex — a genuinely large walkable world needs D4. Phase 2 moves the world, it does not grow it.
- **Water's remaining lattice term** (`ocean.glsl:607-613`). It becomes *fixable* once the origin
  moves, but carrying the lattice camera-relative is its own change.
