# cetra 14.37: a polygon of thousands of holes

## Context

pcb 7.6 draws each pour's fill as one `CanvasPolygon`, its outline and its holes. A ground plane on
a real board has a hole for every via and pad of another net: on pcb's speed gate board, four
layers with 2,000 through vias and a BGA's 900 balls, a plane of 1,334 holes and a top of 2,234.
Drawing that fill takes about 2.3 s of a headless run's 4.5 s, where pcb fills it in 0.22 s (pcb's
`specs/7.6-pours-and-planes.md`, phase 9). At -O2 it is no faster, 4.8 s for the run, so the cost is
the method's, not the build's. It runs on pcb's evaluation thread, so the window never stalls, but
every edit on such a board, and a drag's refill, shows that much later. Boards of a few hundred
holes draw at once.

`canvas_edit_polygon` triangulates the polygon itself (`canvas_edit.c`, `_polygon_fill`), with
`triangulate.c`, a port of Mapbox's earcut v2.2 (spec 14.0). `_eliminate_holes` sorts the holes by
their leftmost point and bridges each in turn to the outer ring; for each, `_find_hole_bridge`
walks the whole outer ring twice, once for the nearest edge a ray to the left meets, once for the
point inside the triangle that ray makes that sees the hole at the least angle. Each bridged hole's
points join the ring. So the work is about holes times the ring's points: thousands of holes of
twenty-odd points each, the ring growing to tens of thousands.

That is where 7.6 put the cost, from that product and the -O2 run; no profile has yet said which of
the steps inside `canvas_edit_polygon` takes it. The ear clipping after the bridging already finds
an ear's candidates through a z-order curve past 80 points, and cutting the triangles at the tile
grid took 0.3 s when pcb drew the same fill as trapezoids.

Two ways were put to the user (pcb 7.6, Left for later 1):
- **(A) each hole's bridge found through an index** of the ring, so a hole looks only at the edges
  and points near it. Local to `triangulate.c`: no API changes, pcb changes nothing, and any
  polygon of many holes gains.
- **(B) a shape that takes a triangulation as given**, softened only at its outline, which pcb's
  trapezoids would feed. A new canvas shape and pcb's code to feed it; it steps around earcut for
  pcb alone.

The user chose (A) on 2026-11-14. (B) stays the fallback should (A) fall short once measured.
cetra's own roadmap lists "triangles supplied by the caller" under its Left for later; this spec
leaves that entry as it stands.

Branch and spec: `14.37-a-polygon-of-thousands-of-holes`, off cetra's master (`abfc5266`).

## The design

- **Measured first.** A new sheet in `apps/canvas`, `plane`: a board-sized plate with holes laid as
  pcb's planes have them -- rows of via antipads on a grid, a BGA's ball field, each a ring of the
  points pcb's own circles have (about 22 for a 0.5 mm radius at a 5 um chord) -- in the thousands.
  The time `canvas_edit_polygon` takes on it, and a sampled profile of the run, say which step
  costs what. The design below assumes the bridging; if the profile says otherwise, the phase stops
  and comes to the user with the numbers.
- **An index over the ring's edges**, a uniform grid over the polygon's box, each edge filed in the
  cells its box meets, built once before the first hole and kept as holes join:
  - **the ray's edge**: the cells of the hole's row walked leftward from the hole's leftmost point,
    the walk stopping at the first column holding an edge the ray meets, every edge of that column
    tested, so the nearest is found;
  - **the point of least angle**: the cells meeting the triangle's box, each point in them tested as
    now;
  - **a hole joined**: its edges filed, and the two edges the bridge makes; a point
    `_filter_points` takes out is passed over where it is met, by a mark on its node, never looked
    for in the grid.
- **The same choice as the walk makes.** Where two candidates tie -- in pcb's boards, a hole whose
  leftmost point lies level with a vertex of a hole bridged before it, as every via in a row does --
  the walk takes the first it meets going round the ring from `outer`. The index cannot know that
  order. So the tie is broken by a rule of its own, stated with the code, which gives a valid
  bridge either way. The triangles may then differ from earcut's where ties fall. What is held is
  what is drawn, not the triangles: each triangulation's area against the shoelace formula, its
  count n - 2 + 2h, and the coverage arms.
- **Small polygons keep the walk.** Below a count of holes the grid does not pay for, the code walks
  the ring as now, so every polygon of the existing sheets triangulates exactly as before. The count
  is set from the measurement, not guessed.
- **Nothing outside `triangulate.c` changes.** `triangulate_polygon`'s contract and signature stay;
  the pool's size stays `3 (count + 2 holes) + 16` nodes, the grid allocated beside it. Phase 6
  departs from this at the user's word, on 2026-11-14: it fixes the canvas's drawing of a fill,
  which phase 2 found leaving a pixel uncovered.

## As built

### Phase 1: measured

- **The `plane` sheet** (`apps/canvas`): the speed gate board's top pour, from pcb's
  `write_pour_board`. It is a 100 mm plate with 1,333 via antipads of 0.45 mm, two in three of 2,000
  vias on a 1.9 mm grid round the BGA's square. It also has 675 ball antipads of 0.375 mm, three in
  four of 900 at 1 mm. That makes 2,008 holes and 48,196 points. Each hole is the polygon pcb's
  `polygon_around` makes: the least multiple of eight sides whose corners stray no more than 5 um
  outside the circle, set half a side off the eight directions. Both sizes get 24 sides, where the
  plan said about 22 (a chord's rule, not pcb's). Their edges face the eight directions, so each
  hole's leftmost point is a corner level with the lower right corner of the hole before it in its
  row. That is the plan's tie, at every via.
- Printed under `--canvas-probe` as `polygons` prints, with a `plane-time` line giving the edit's
  and the triangulation's milliseconds. `probe_polygon` now takes the sheet, the box and the arrays,
  for both sheets.
- **Measured** (the Debug build, -O0 -g, M1 Max): the edit 570 ms, its triangulation 520 ms. The area
  equals the shoelace formula's, in 52,210 triangles, n - 2 + 2h.
- **Profiled** (`sample` every 1 ms over one run of the edit): `canvas_edit_polygon` 645 samples.
  `_find_hole_bridge` has 463 of them (72%): 224 in the ray's walk and 239 in the angle's. The ear
  clipping has 130 (20%), and cutting the triangles at the tiles 45 (7%). The bridging is most of
  the time, so the design stands.
- **Seen**, headless, whole and cropped: the plate white, every hole round and black. One pixel in
  the plate is dark, at (3.6, 50.85) mm on the board, between four vias. That is master's
  triangulation; phase 2 looks for it again.

### Phase 2: the index

- **The grid** (`triangulate.c`), as planned: square cells over the box of every point, each edge
  filed by its start node in every cell its box meets, the outer ring before the first hole and
  each hole as it joins. The ray tests its own row's cells leftward and stops past the column whose
  edges put the nearest x right of that column. It starts a column right of the hole's and ends a
  column past that, so an x rounded past its edge's box by an ulp is still found. The angle tests
  the points of the cells its triangle's box meets, by the walk's own test. Each node carries the
  stamp of the latest query that tested it, so a node filed in several cells is tested once.
- **A point the filter takes out**, a departure from the plan's "passed over where it is met". As a
  point it is passed over. As an edge it stands for its nearest predecessor still in the ring,
  reached through its own `prev`, whose edge now runs along the line its own did. Passed over as an
  edge too, a ray meeting the merged edge only in the removed node's cells finds nothing there.
  `_remove` sets the mark, so the ear clipping sets it as well, unread.
- **The tie's rule**, stated over the grid's code: of two edges the ray meets at the same x, the
  one whose node was made first; of two points at the same place and angle, the walk's sector test,
  then the one made first. Nothing in this phase met it: on every polygon tried, the grid's
  triangles are the walk's (below).
- **Set from the measurement**, scratch harnesses timing `triangulate.c` alone, each the least of
  several runs and processes, since one launch can land on a slower core:
  - **The threshold, 32 holes.** On plates of via clearances, the grid and the walk cost the same
    at 32 holes unoptimised, and the grid is ahead from 16 to 24 at -O2. Below that, making the grid
    costs a tenth more than it saves.
  - **16 points a cell.** From 1 to 64 the plate triangulates within a fifth: 132 to 157 ms
    unoptimised, 90 to 94 ms at -O2. 16 was the least in both. The walk takes 495 and 424 ms.
- **The plate**: the triangulation 520 ms to 135 ms, the edit 570 ms to 184 ms (Debug). What is
  left is the ear clipping, not the bridging. Its triangles are the walk's, to the hash.
- **The walk's triangles, everywhere tried.** The polygons sheet's six (the walk, under 32 holes) and
  the board sheet's plane (52 holes, the grid), each 0 px from master's triangulation. A scratch
  fuzz of 800 seeded plates, the grid on every one: vias, square holes in rows whose bridges set the
  filter round the ring, jittered holes, turned polygons of 3 to 12 sides, a round outline, and
  squares with a point mid each upright side. Built against the walk and against the grid, its
  output is the same line for line. The first 500 ran under the address and undefined-behaviour
  sanitizers, which were quiet. The fuzz's six wrong areas are all jittered holes that overlap, and
  wrong under the walk too.
- **Arms**: `canvas-triangulation` with the plane's plate beside the six, 52,210 of 52,210, its
  detail now naming an area that disagrees, where it said "areas agree" whatever it found.
  `canvas-polygon-areas` with the plate at the default tiles, -0.0009 px per pixel of outline. At
  65 um tiles the plate's long triangles cross more than 65,536 tiles and are refused, so it is not
  held there. `canvas-plane-edit`: the edit, the least of three, under 400 ms, 184 to 192 measured.
  The sheet times the two more edits into edits thrown away. `--only canvas`: every arm passes. So
  does `gate-docs`, static.
- **Falsified**:
  - The ray stopped at the first cell holding any edge: the plate came out as 30,318 triangles of
    52,210, its holes left unbridged, and its coverage +0.47 px per pixel of outline. Both arms fail.
  - **The bridge's two edges not filed, which the arms do not catch**, where the plan said the area
    arm would fail. The plate's triangles are still the walk's, to the hash. The angle search tests
    every point in the larger triangle the missed edge leaves, and the bridge's ends are among them.
    In the scratch fuzz, 3 of 300 plates came out with other triangles, 1 with a wrong area. None of
    2,000 seeded plates of turned polygons, 20 by 20, came out wrong. Filed, the grid takes the
    walk's bridges; unfiled, it takes them almost always.
  - A point the filter took out passed over as an edge, my own: not caught by the arms either. It
    breaks 24 of 50 fuzz plates of squares with upright midpoints, thinned at random. A fixed rows
    plate of them, thinned six ways, did not break, so no sheet holds it.
- **Seen**, headless: the plate whole, 0 px from master's, and a crop 12 mm across at the BGA's
  corner, every hole round. Both have the dark pixel of phase 1: two in the whole plate at the
  default tiles, at (127, 295) and (127, 485), and one in the crop. Each is a pixel nothing covers,
  inside a sliver 0.22 px thick at that column that the triangulation covers exactly. It moves with
  the tile size, and at 16.8 mm tiles there are ten. It is the canvas's drawing of the triangles, in
  `canvas_edit.c` or its shaders, not the triangulation's, and on master too. Left for the user, as
  outside this spec.

### Phase 6: the uncovered pixel

**Found.** Three experiments narrowed it:
- At 400 px a millimetre round the first pixel, (127, 295) on the plane sheet, the plate is whole.
  So the hole is not in the triangles.
- With every fill fragment's coverage forced to 1, the two pixels stay at 0. So no fill fragment
  lands there at all; it is not a coverage that falls to 0.
- In the harness, the pixel's centre lies 0.0001 px inside a triangle (17806, 17805, 17266) that is
  0.06 px thick at that column. That triangle spans tiles −12 to −11 and was drawn whole. The
  triangle across its lower edge spans −12 to −10, so the edit cut it at the tile lines, with the
  crossings rounded to the unit. The two sides of the shared edge are then different lines on the
  GPU, and a pixel centre between them is filled by neither. `canvasPixels` adds a corner and its
  tile's offset in integers, so a corner shared by two triangles lands on the same pixel either way;
  the mismatch is the cut alone.

**Fixed** in `_polygon_triangle`: a triangle is drawn whole only inside one tile, and any triangle
crossing the grid is cut at every tile it crosses. `_crossing` already orders an edge's ends, so
two triangles sharing an edge now split it at the same points. `docs/canvas.md` already said that a
fill triangle crossing a tile is cut, its neighbours sharing their corners to the unit; for a
triangle within two tiles it is now true. It held first time.

**Pictures**, against before the fix, with a noise floor of 0 px between two runs of each:
- **The plane at 4 mm tiles**: its 2 pixels filled, and a third by a hole's rim the eight-round test
  had missed, each 0 to 255. Two rim pixels went 195 to 231, where the gap had taken coverage. The
  other 204 pixels changed by a code, along the new cuts.
- **The plane at 16.8 mm tiles**: 10 pixels filled; 63 changed by a code.
- **The plane moved 1.2 mm**: none missing before; 212 pixels changed by a code.
- **The polygons sheet** 4 px and **the board** 2 px, each by a code. The polygons sheet at 65 um
  tiles, where everything was already cut, is unchanged.
- No pixel lost coverage. The plate's coverage went from −0.0009 to −0.0008 px per pixel of
  outline. Its edit went from 178–184 ms to 194 ms, from the extra pieces.

**Arm**: `canvas-fill-whole` counts, on the plane sheet's plate, pixels under half covered while all
eight round them are over 0.8. It runs at 4 mm tiles, at 16.8 mm tiles and moved by 1,234,567
units. All three come out 0.

**Falsified**: with the fix taken back (whole within two tiles), the arm fails with 2 at 4 mm and
10 at 16.8 mm. The moved run finds none either way.

**Run**: `--only canvas,gate-docs` passes every arm. `canvas-tiles` is 1 px apart by a code, and
`canvas-plane-edit` 194 ms.

**Seen**: the plane whole, the crop where (127, 295) was, and the 12 mm crop at the BGA's corner
where phase 2 saw one. None has a missing pixel.

### Phase 7: the reviews of phase 6

`/simplify`'s four and the thermo-nuclear review on phase 6's commit, background agents at effort
high. Nothing was applied until all five were back. The altitude review found the fix at the right
depth: an edge is split if and only if it crosses the grid, so both triangles beside it must be
cut, and no rule keeping a triangle whole across a grid line can hold that. It checked the other
places such a mismatch could live and found none: T-junctions from the triangulation, a tile corner
on a shared edge, the outline's edge pieces, the float positions.

**Applied:**
- **No path draws a triangle whole** (simplification, thermo-nuclear, efficiency). Under phase 6's
  rule, the one-tile branch gave exactly the piece the clip gives, so it is gone. Every fill
  triangle goes through `_clip_triangle`, which now passes over a side the triangle's box lies
  inside: every corner, clipped or not, lies in that box, and such a side would copy them as they
  are. The pieces are the same, and every sheet is 0 px from phase 6's. A triangle in one tile is
  never clipped. The plane's edit went from 194 ms to 187.
- **The rule lives in the polygons' section comment** (simplification, thermo-nuclear, altitude): an
  edge is split where it crosses the grid, so both triangles beside it must be cut there.
  `_polygon_triangle`'s comment is one line, without the measurement or the history.
- **`canvas-fill-whole`** (efficiency, altitude, thermo-nuclear):
  - The 16.8 mm and moved runs no longer ask for the probe. The plate's box comes from the cached
    plane run.
  - The moved run is now 2 mm, not 1.2. With the old rule put back for one build, nine offsets from
    0.25 to 3.5 mm left gaps at 0.25, 0.78 and 2 mm. 2 mm lands on other pixels than the default
    does, so the arm now samples a third configuration that can fail.
  - The neighbour test is a 3x3 window, and the docstring keeps what the arm asserts and the failure
    it catches, leaving the C mechanism to the C code.

**Falsified after**: with the whole-within-two-tiles branch put back, `canvas-fill-whole` fails at
all three: 2 at 4 mm, 10 at 16.8 mm, 2 moved 2 mm.

**Not applied:**
- **A check on the mesh itself** (altitude): every inner directed edge of the fill pieces met by its
  reverse, exact and with no sampling. The pieces live in the edit, which the app reaches only
  through the public API, so it needs a new way out of `canvas_edit.c` for a test. The sampling arm
  fails on the old rule in all three runs.
- **A padded fill footprint** (altitude, offered as an option and not recommended): it changes the
  fill record and the shaders' contract.
- **Splitting each grid line once, strip then cell** (efficiency), so that a crossing is not worked
  out from both tiles: the box test above already took the common case back to one pass.

**Run**: `--only canvas,gate-docs` passes every arm. `canvas-plane-edit` measured 192 ms.

### Phase 8: the grid against the walk, and the fill's mesh closed

Both checks go through internal headers in `cetra/src`, which reach only the in-tree apps as
`cetra/internal/`. Each new header needed a reconfigure for its forwarder. `triangulate.h` and
`canvas.h` are unchanged.

**`triangulate_hooks.h`**: `triangulate_polygon_walked`. `triangulate.c` has one `_triangulate`
taking the hole count from which the grid finds bridges, carried in `EarState`. The public function
passes 32, the hook `SIZE_MAX`. `probe_polygon` triangulates every polygon both ways and prints
`walked 1` when the indices are the same.

- **The plate that catches unfiled bridge edges.** The scratch harness generated turned regular
  polygons of 3 to 12 sides, one a cell, thinned and wound either way at random. In 400 seeds of up
  to 23 cells a side, the mutation changed nothing. Up to 47 cells a side, it changed one in 600:
  seed 137, 573 holes and 4,415 points. The grid gives the walk's triangles there, and the mutation
  does not. `plane_turned` builds it in the plane sheet with the harness's arithmetic, and the probe
  prints it as the ninth seeded plate. Its counts match the harness's: 573 holes, 5,559 triangles.
- **`canvas-grid-walk`**: the plate and the nine seeded plates, each `walked 1`.
- **Falsified**: with the bridge's two edges left unfiled, `canvas-grid-walk` fails on `turned-137`,
  while `canvas-triangulation` passes with every area agreeing. Phase 2 had nothing that caught this.

**`canvas_edit_hooks.h`**: `canvas_edit_fill_count` and `canvas_edit_fill_corners`, each piece's
corners in units. They deliberately avoid reading `CanvasEdit`'s fields from `canvas_record.h`,
which would tie the test to the struct's layout. The probe checks one of the timing edits, the
plate alone:
- every piece's edges, unordered, sorted and counted;
- an edge met once must lie along the outline, both ends within a unit of an outline edge, found
  through outline edges filed by cells of 2^20 units;
- an edge met three times or more counts as open too.
- **`canvas-fill-closed`**: 0 open edges at 4 mm tiles (239,268 pieces) and at 16.8 mm (96,352).
  The pieces the edit drops as rounded onto a line leave no open edge on this plate.
- **Falsified**: with the two-tile rule put back, 6,737 open edges of 194,558 pieces at 4 mm, and
  1,438 of 81,068 at 16.8 mm. `canvas-fill-whole` sees 2 and 10 pixels for the same mismatch.
- **The old rule's count**, from the same run: cutting every crossing triangle made 23% more pieces
  at 4 mm tiles and 19% more at 16.8 mm.

**The arms' runs**: the plane at 16.8 mm tiles is one cached run under the probe, read by
`canvas-fill-whole` for its picture and by `canvas-fill-closed` for its mesh.

**Docs**: `docs/modules.md` names both headers in the canvas's and the triangulation's rows.

**Run**: `--only canvas,gate-docs` passes every arm. `canvas-plane-edit` measured 189 ms with the
walk's triangulation of each plate added to the probe.
