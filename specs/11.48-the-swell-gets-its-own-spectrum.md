# 11.48 — The swell gets its own spectrum

## Context

`apps/tree` renders a large opaque white blob over its shallow shelf that moves with the waves.
Six commits of 11.47 did not touch it, because it is not crest foam.

Traced this session, at one camera, by elimination:

| render | result | conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| `--water-foam-debug 1` | flat green, 2 specks | not crest foam |
| `--no-water-surf` | blob gone, sea glassy | surf-gated → `Breaking` |
| `--gerstner-waves` | **clean parallel surf lines** | `Breaking` itself is correct |
| `--wind-speed 3 --fetch 4000` | blob unchanged | not amplitude — something ignores the wind |
| `--swell 0` | **blob gone, proper surf lines** | it is the swell train |

Frames kept at `_debug/src{1..6}_*.png`.

**Root cause, `water.c:271-273`:** the swell train is seeded from a hardcoded 8.4 m/s wind over
310 km of fetch. It reads neither `sea->wind_speed` nor `sea->fetch`. `WATER_DEFAULT_SWELL` is
0.38 so `sw_weight` is *exactly 1.0* at the default — full strength, always.

`Breaking` (`ocean.glsl:769`) tests `disp.y / (0.39 · depth)`, the depth-limited criterion. Over
tree's 1–3 m shelf that swell genuinely *is* breaking, everywhere. **`Breaking` is reporting the
truth about an absurd sea**, which is why no amount of work on the foam path could fix it.

Two things this corrects:

- **Commit `839c6fd` overclaimed.** "The swell answers the sea state" made the `swell` *knob*
  scale the train, but at the default `sw_weight` is 1.0, so nothing changed for any app that
  does not author `swell`. tree does not.
- **`swell` is overloaded.** `water.c:241` already uses it as the directional-spread sharpener —
  which is what it means in both reference implementations. `839c6fd` added `:273` using it
  *again* as the swell train's amplitude. Turning it down now also broadens the wind sea's
  spread, two unrelated effects on one knob.

**What the references do** (`research/water/`):

- **gpuocean** — not applicable. Scrolling band-limited noise, no spectral model.
- **inkwell** — has our exact bug, because we ported it verbatim: `8.4` / `310_000` / gamma `2.6`
  / `+0.82` rad / spread `+9.0` / gain `0.72`, and our library defaults are its defaults. It does
  not have the *problem*, because its wind sea is hardcoded at 11.5 m/s too. One fixed sea, one
  fixed swell, calibrated against each other. **We broke that when 11.42 made wind and fetch
  authorable — we kept the proportion's numerator and made its denominator a knob.**
- **poseidon** — solves it. The swell is a **complete second parameter set**
  (`params.js:22-44`), and `fillSet` (`spectrum.js:98-107`) derives `alpha` and `peakOmega` from
  **each set's own** `windSpeed` and `fetch`. The two spectra are summed (`:133-140`). Its swell
  runs at wind 2.0 m/s over 300 km — a *low* wind over a *long* fetch, which is what swell is.

Swell as a fraction of wind sea: poseidon **19%**, inkwell **55%**, cetra-in-tree **300%**.

**Outcome:** the swell is a wave train like any other, authored per scene, and a calm sea is
calm. `Breaking` is not touched.

## Design

One type, twice. Today's swell block (`water.c:252-291`) is 40 lines of near-duplicate
JONSWAP + spread math; it collapses into **one density function called twice**, and the
asymmetries between the two trains become *data* rather than divergent code.

```c
// One wave train's spectrum. The wind sea and the swell are the same kind of thing, so
// they are one type twice rather than one type and a hardcoded imitation of it.
typedef struct WaterWaveTrain {
    float wind_speed;       // m/s at the standard 10 m reference height
    float fetch;            // metres of open water the wind has blown across
    float direction;        // radians, RELATIVE to Water.wind_dir
    float scale;            // spectral density weight; 1 is the spectrum as the fetch law gives it
    float peak_enhancement; // JONSWAP gamma
    float focus;            // 0..1 directional narrowness (Horvath's parameter)
    float spread_gain;      // outer factor on the spread power
} WaterWaveTrain;

typedef struct WaterSeaState {
    float sea_depth;        // metres; drives the TMA correction. A property of the WATER,
                            // not of a train, so it is not in WaterWaveTrain.
    WaterWaveTrain wind_sea;
    WaterWaveTrain swell;
} WaterSeaState;
```

Defaults chosen so the sea stays today's:

| | `wind_speed` | `fetch` | `direction` | `scale` | `peak_enhancement` | `focus` | `spread_gain` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wind_sea` | 11.5 | 120 000 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 3.3 | 0.38 | 0.58 |
| `swell` | 8.4 | 310 000 | 0.82 | 1.0 | 2.6 | ~0.86 | 0.72 |

**Everything is bit-exact except the swell's directional spread.** The old swell used a flat
`+9.0` where the wind sea uses `16·tanh(min(ω/ωp,20))·focus²`; unifying the form is what makes
this a parameter set rather than two code paths. `focus` 0.86 is fitted to `+9.0` at the peak
(`tanh(1) = 0.762`, `16·0.762·0.86² ≈ 9.0`) and drifts either side of it. Fit it numerically
across the band before pinning the constant.

**The overload dies:** `focus` is the spread knob (the wind sea's, and the swell's own);
`swell.scale` is the swell's amplitude. `swell.scale = 0` disables the train, where today
`swell = 0` does — so that is a change of meaning, deliberately.

**`secondary_scale` stays per-cascade** (`WATER_CASCADE_CFG`, 0.22/0.08/0). It is a *band*
weighting — which cascades carry the second train at all — not a property of the train, and it is
what keeps the swell out of the 12 m cascade. `swell.scale` multiplies on top of it.

## Phases

### P1 — one density function, two trains

`water.h` / `water.c`. Extract the JONSWAP + directional-spread + short-fade body of
`_water_build_spectrum` (`water.c:174-348`) into `_water_train_density(train, omega, theta_abs,
tma, wind_angle)`; call it twice and sum. `_water_peak_omega` and `_water_jonswap` already take
the pieces they need — pass a `WaterWaveTrain*` rather than a `WaterSeaState*`.

Three traps, all confirmed:

- **`_water_seed_is_current` (`water.c:1032-1039`) is a hand-written field-by-field float
  comparison**, deliberately not a `memcmp` (its comment rejects a dirty flag). Every new field
  must be added or editing it silently never re-seeds. Consider a `_water_train_is_current`
  helper so the two trains cannot drift.
- **`_water_surf_state` (`water.c:1324`) calls `_water_peak_omega(&water->sea)`** → becomes
  `&water->sea.wind_sea`. Behaviour unchanged. *Recorded, not done:* physically the surf should
  follow the longer train (the swell), and at these defaults the swell's period is 8.3 s against
  the wind sea's 6.7 s — but that moves `waterSurfOmega` and every shore frame with it.
- Log lines at `water.c:1092-1096` and `:1120-1122` print wind/fetch; say which train.

### P2 — the scene-file surface

Nested, symmetric:

```json
"water": {
  "waves": "fft", "seaDepth": 54.0,
  "windSea": { "windSpeed": 11.5, "fetch": 120000, "direction": 0.0,
               "scale": 1.0, "peakEnhancement": 3.3, "focus": 0.38, "spreadGain": 0.58 },
  "swell":   { "windSpeed": 8.4,  "fetch": 310000, "direction": 0.82,
               "scale": 1.0, "peakEnhancement": 2.6, "focus": 0.86, "spreadGain": 0.72 }
}
```

Top-level `windSpeed` / `fetch` / `peakEnhancement` / `swell` are **removed**. `parse_water`'s
closed `known[]` block then warns on any scene still using them, which is the loud migration this
wants — and there are exactly two such scenes, both ours.

*Alternative rejected:* flat prefixed keys (`swellWindSpeed`, `swellSpreadGain`, …). It avoids
the gate work below, but gives seven awkward keys and leaves the wind sea's fields unprefixed, so
they read as belonging to the water rather than to a train — which is the exact confusion that
produced this bug.

Files: `cscene.h` (`CSceneWater`), `cscene.c` (`parse_water` + `known[]`),
`cscene_apply.c:354-361`, both fixtures **and both generators**
(`assets/gen_{water,beach}_fixture.py` — the committed `.cscn` is byte-compared against a fresh
regeneration).

**`_cscn_water_key_sets` (`gates.py:4072-4084`) must learn to nest.** Its regex is
`\(water,\s*"([A-Za-z]+)"`, so nested keys are invisible and `water-fixture-roundtrip` fails until
taught. Extend it to the sub-objects rather than loosening it — the point of that gate is that
`parse_water`, its `known[]` list and the fixture cannot drift apart.

Note `_water_cscn_variant` shallow-merges the gate's override dicts, so
`WATER_SEASTATE_CALM` (`gates.py:3470`) must author a whole `windSea` object, not one key.

### P3 — GUI

`gui.c:610-623`. Nothing iterates the struct, so it is hand-written: two `igTreeNode` sections,
seven sliders each, plus the shared depth. Direct pointer binds as today.

### P4 — apps/tree authors its own swell

tree's wind sea is 6 m/s over 15 km → Hs ≈ 0.375 m. The swell it inherits is ~1.1 m effective.
Author a proportionate one (start near `wind_speed 3.0`, `fetch 40000` → ~0.14 m effective, ~38%)
and **tune by eye against the A/B**, not to a target number. Its `--swell` flag becomes the
train's `scale`; `--wind-speed` / `--fetch` address `wind_sea`.

The `--no-water-surf` flag added to `tree.c` while tracing this is the instrument that found it;
it landed on master ahead of this branch rather than inside it.

### P5 — docs

`AGENTS.md:654-661` and `:922` state "21 keys", "authors all 21", "the five sea-state keys". All
go false. `CLAUDE.md` is a symlink to the same file.

## Verification

- **Goldens must stay 0 px.** Both water goldens are Gerstner and never reach the spectral

- **`water-fft-var` will move** and needs re-baselining — it reads measured/predicted variance

  `known[]`, the fixture and the generator all agree.
- **`beach_fixture` is `"waves": "fft"`** and authors a sea state, so it moves. Its `beach-*` arms

- **The A/B is the acceptance test for P4**, at the camera these were taken with:
  `--sun-elevation 35 -W 2000 -H 1280 --fov 31.5 --cam-eye -88.746,31.602,-50.250
  --cam-target -89.642,31.465,-50.673`. Before is `_debug/src1_baseline.png`; the target is

## Risks and falsifiers

- **The `focus` fit is worse than the flat `+9.0` across the band.** The two forms agree at the
  peak by construction and diverge in the tails. *Falsified by:* `water-fft-var`'s height ratio
  moving outside its band, or the swell visibly changing character in the fixture. *Response:*
  keep `spread_flat` as an eighth per-train field (0.0 / 9.0) and be bit-exact — the wart is
  cheaper than a re-baseline nobody can interpret.
- **Removing the top-level keys breaks a scene nobody remembered.** *Falsified by:* grep says two
  fixtures, both ours. The `known[]` warning makes any survivor loud rather than silent.
- **tree still blobs after P4.** Then the swell was not the whole story and `Breaking`'s shape is
  implicated after all. *Response:* the three defects listed for `Breaking` earlier (no
  accumulator, swash opacity, area-not-face) become 11.49 — but do not pre-emptively fix them,
  the Gerstner and `--swell 0` frames both say `Breaking` is correct.
- **`water-seastate` gets weaker, not stronger.** Would mean the wind sea is not what moves those
  20 000 px. Worth knowing either way.

## Results

**24 goldens 0 px. 177 gate arms pass; `beach-shoreline` fails at a line byte-identical to the
one it failed on master before this branch** (7 of 18 azimuths, 2 without the bed), so it is
pre-existing and untouched here.

Four predictions above were wrong, and each is worth more than the phase it belonged to.

- **The struct needed EIGHT fields, not seven.** The wind sea blends a focused lobe with a broad
  `cos²` one at 0.68/0.32 and the old swell used the focused lobe alone — an asymmetry the plan
  did not notice. poseidon settles it: one `directionSpectrum` for both sets, with the blend as
  per-set data (`spreadBlend`, `spectrum.js:50`). So `spread_blend` is a field, and the wind sea
  stays bit-exact because `1.0f - 0.68f == 0.32f` exactly in float32.
- **`water-fft-var` did NOT move and needed no re-baseline** (0.96/0.91/0.80 height,
  0.99/0.97/0.93 slope, identical to 2 dp). The reason is structural and should have been
  predicted: the directional factor is normalised over the circle, so reshaping the lobe moves
  the total variance only through discrete sampling. That also makes it a **weak falsifier for
  the `focus` fit** — it cannot see the change it was nominated to catch. What can is the
  per-cascade probe.
- **`water-seastate` did not get stronger** — 340,557 → 341,222 px, which is noise. The plan
  assumed the swell was diluting the arm; it still is, because the arm lowers `windSea.windSpeed`
  and leaves `swell.scale` at 1. Making the swell authorable does not change an arm that does not
  author it. Neither the predicted improvement nor the falsifier's weakening: it simply does not
  bear on this.
- **`spread_flat` was not needed.** The `focus` fit is 0.833, energy-weighted least squares over
  the 880 modes that actually carry the swell rather than the peak-only 0.859 the plan proposed
  (weighted RMS error in the spread power 0.879 against 1.053). 72% of the swell's energy sits
  within a quarter of its peak. The residual is a lobe half-width of 28-38 degrees where it was
  a flat 32.

**The containment check is the per-cascade probe, not a golden.** Both water goldens are Gerstner
and never reach the seeding, so they prove only that nothing leaked. Cascade 2 has
`secondary_scale` 0, carries no swell in either version, and `--water-fft-probe` reads it
**identical to every printed digit** across the change — which is what pins the wind sea. Cascades
0 and 1 move by 0.016% and 0.005% in predicted height, the swell's reshaped lobe and nothing else.

`apps/tree`: Hs 0.66 m with the authored swell, 0.64 m without it, against a wind sea the plan
estimated at 0.375 m from the fetch law alone — the measured figure is 4σ over the seeded
cascades and is the one to quote. The blob is gone and the surf is a line at the beach.

## Recorded after approval

assets/beach_fixture.gltf --water-bed dome` paints a blown-out white ring over the whole shoal.

not clear that frame**, and the `beach-shoreline` gate arm fails in the opposite direction
(7/18 azimuths, wants ≥13) on Gerstner, which never reaches the spectral seeding at all. Both are
evidence for the "tree still blobs" risk above; neither is in scope here.
