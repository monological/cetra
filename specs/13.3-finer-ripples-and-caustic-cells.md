# Spec 13.3 — Finer ripples and caustic cells

## Context

13.2 shipped the refracted-grid caustics, and up close their cells read as squares. The lattice cell is 9.375 cm because it sits on the short band's texel (12 m / 128). The water surface has nothing finer than that texel either, so a finer lattice alone would only refract smooth interpolation.

Clearwater hides the facets with 1.8 cm cells. The fix is both halves, as 13.0's caustics row and 13.2's as-built already book it:
- **A ripple band.** A fourth spectral band in its own 512² texture, so the surface carries centimetre ripples.
- **A fine caustic level.** A second, finer lattice near the camera that traces through them.

A coarser grid is off the table. This spec only adds detail.

## Design

### Ripple band (spectral water only, like the short band it extends)

- **Size: 6 m at 512², giving a 1.171875 cm texel.**
  - That is exactly 1/8 of the short band's texel, so every short-band texel is a whole number of ripple texels, and the pattern stays commensurate with a 12 m combined period.
  - The Nyquist wavenumber is about 268 rad/m.
- **k window `[24, 260]`.** The short band keeps `[1.22, 24]` unchanged, so bands 0-2 are byte-identical and no energy is counted twice. The existing `short_fade = exp(-0.00016 k²)` decides how much energy survives up there. It stays as it is, because it is the spectrum's own small-wave cutoff.
- **Capillary dispersion.** `ω² = g·k·(1 + (k/370)²)`, applied to every band. It is about 0.2% at k = 24, so bands 0-2 move only by that much. Without it, ripples at k = 200 travel about 20% too slow.
- **Storage:** its own `RGBA16F` array (2 layers × ping-pong), mipped and REPEAT, plus its own 512×9 twiddle table. Sampled on free unit **4** (`WATER_RIPPLE_UNIT`), bound in `_water_bind_ocean`, since every program that includes `ocean.glsl` goes through it. `water_frag` goes to 13 of 16. There is no previous-frame copy (motion vectors stay bands 0 and 1) and no foam (bands 0 and 1).
- **FFT:** `_water_fft_transform` takes `(size, log, twiddle)` instead of the compile-time constants. `_water_build_twiddle` and `_water_build_spectrum` already take a size. The ripple band adds 1 + 18 = 19 passes, so 45 becomes 64. Its viewport is set per band, and the 128 viewport is restored before the foam pass.
- **Shading:**
  - `oceanShadingNormal` gains a footprint argument. The ripple slope is sampled at a LOD derived from the footprint, via a per-band-resolution version of `oceanCascadeLod`.
  - Its unresolved slope variance goes to roughness through `oceanRemovedMss`, the path the short band's view fade already uses. So distant water gets rougher glitter rather than aliasing.
  - `water_frag` passes the pixel footprint. The caustic land pass passes its cell, so the coarse lattice correctly sees the ripples filtered away.
- **Gerstner:** stays ripple-free, because `oceanShadingNormal` returns the mesh normal there, as it does today.

### Fine caustic level (both wave models)

- **Everything scaled by 1/4:**
  - cell 2.34375 cm, which is 2 ripple texels
  - G = 512, a 12 m lattice
  - target 1024² over the inner 10 m
  - snap 5 cells = 12 texels, the same invariant as the coarse level
- **Placement:** the fine window is centred ahead of the camera, like the coarse one, with the reach clamped to its own size.
- **Levels as data:**
  - `water_caustic_constants.glsl` becomes a coarse row plus `WATER_CAUSTIC_FINE_DIV 4`.
  - The vert shader's `GRID_N` and `TARGET_M` become uniforms.
  - `_water_run_caustics` loops over levels, reusing the one 513² land target per level in turn.
  - Both targets live in one `sampler2DArray` on unit 3, with no new unit.
- **Consumer:** `waterCaustics` samples both layers in uniform flow and computes `c = mix(coarse, fine, fineEdgeWeight)`. Both levels average 1, so the blend conserves energy.
- **Probe:** prints one line per level (`level=0`/`level=1`). `_water_caustic_probe` takes a level, and `water-caustic-energy` checks both levels on every leg.

## Risks

- **Cost:** about +19 FFT passes at 512² plus a second 262k-cell lattice. That could roughly double the 1.9 ms caustic cost. It is measured and reported, not reduced.
- **Visible 6 m tiling** of the ripple texture up close. Look for it in phase 2.
- **FFT gates** move slightly from capillary dispersion, and from new roughness on FFT water.
- **Every include of `ocean.glsl`** must bind unit 4, or it samples the cascade array through a 2D sampler. Binding in `_water_bind_ocean` is what covers all of them.

## Verification

- `./build.sh`. Between phases, `python3 scripts/gates.py --only water`; `--only beach` after phase 3.
- **Numbers:** `water-fft-var` holds on all four bands, `water-caustic-energy` holds on both levels, and `water-night-caustic`'s dark leg stays at 0 px.
- **The look, which no arm replaces:** close-up crops at phases 2 and 3 against phase 0, on `water_fixture` FFT and Gerstner and in the tree app.
- **End:** the full `gates.py` and `goldens.py` once.

## As built

### Phase 0: before, and the floor

The close framing is `water_fixture --water-waves fft` at 800x600, `--cam-eye -5,1.5,5 --cam-target
-5,-1,1`. It shows the caustic lines built from visible square steps. Rendered twice, it differs by
0 px, so any change measured at this framing is real.

### Phase 1: the transform takes its size

`_water_fft_transform` and `_water_build_twiddle` take `(size, log2_size)`. That is 0 px on the
close framing, and `--only water` passes.

### Phase 2: the ripple band

- **Storage and pipeline.** `WATER_RIPPLE_CFG` is 6 m, [24, 260] rad/m, amplitude 0.82, with
  nothing displaced. Its sizes live in `water_ripple_constants.glsl`, which both languages read.
  It has its own seed pair, 512×9 twiddle table and two-layer ping-pong array. It runs 19 passes
  after the cascades, then the viewport goes back to 128 for the foam pass, making 64 passes a
  frame in total. It is mipped after the transform and bound on unit 4 in `_water_bind_ocean`.
- **Shading.** `oceanShadingNormal(meshN, xz, shortKeep, footprint)` adds the ripple slope at the
  footprint's mip, at the short band's gain. `water_frag` passes the pixel footprint (from the
  `SurfParam` derivatives); the caustic land pass passes its cell.
- **Roughness.** It takes `rippleSlopeVarLod[0]` minus the level that was read.
  `_water_build_spectrum` predicts each level's variance at seeding. Each 2×2 reduction passes a
  mode at `cos(w d / 2)` per axis, and the prediction is the product of those factors.
- **Capillary dispersion** applies to every band, with its derivative, at km = 370 rad/m.
- **Measured.**
  - The seeded slope variance goes from 0.1010 to 0.1272; the ripple band carries 0.0262.
  - Ripple measured over predicted is 0.97 for height and 0.98 for slope.
  - Per mip level the ratio is 0.94-0.98 through level 6. The new arm `water-ripple-mips` holds
    levels 1-4, the ones carrying at least 1% of the band.
  - The impulse test now also runs at 512 and reports the worse of the two sizes: 1.9e-7, the
    same as before.
  - `--only water` and `--only gate-docs` pass.
- **The look.**
  - **Close up**, the surface carries centimetre ripples: finer refraction breakup and more
    sparkle. The caustics are still stepped, which is phase 3's job.
  - **At the fixture's own framing**, there is no aliasing at the horizon. The mid-distance water
    reads hazier and the sun glint is wider, because the ripples' unresolved slope has gone to
    roughness.
  - The sea's total slope variance is now about twice Cox-Munk's 0.062 at this wind; it was 1.6×
    before. That is for you to judge.

### Phase 3: the fine caustic level (committed as fa2e85a9)

- **Levels as data.** One `R16F` array with a layer per level and a framebuffer per layer. The
  level loop reuses the one land texture, and the lattice vertex stage takes its target side as a
  uniform. The consumer blends each finer level over the coarser ones by its edge weight. The
  probe prints one line per level, and `water-caustic-energy` checks every level.
- **A 13.2 defect, found and fixed.** The lattice reads the landed points by vertex-stage
  `texelFetch`. On this driver that read saw the texture as it was before the pass that had just
  rendered it:
  - with one level, every frame's caustics were the previous frame's trace, which a still frame
    cannot show;
  - with two levels, each level drew the other's.

  A `glFlush` between the trace and the draw fixes both; `glFinish` did too, but also stalls the
  CPU.
- **The lattice shift** is taken at the floor under the window's centre: the bed where there is
  one, else the plane. Level 1 had been shifted for 3 m of water while its window lay over 9 m.
- **Measured.** At the fixture, both levels hold: 0.996/0.997 Gerstner, 0.970/0.989 spectral. On
  the close framing the square steps are gone, and there is no seam in the factor view.
- **Open.** Over the beach's dome, the fine level reads **0.77** where its window lies over 9 m of
  water. The same scene reads 0.98 calm and 1.05 against the plane, so the loss needs both waves
  and depth. Ruled out:
  - the window's edges (the central 40% reads the same);
  - the ceiling (400 instead of 40 changes nothing);
  - sub-texel cells (a 2048 target changes nothing);
  - fp16 accumulation (R32F changes nothing).

  The decision is to use the fine level only in shallow water, where ripple focus exists at all,
  and to hand deep water to the coarse level.

### Review round (thermo-nuclear and /simplify, five reviews)

Applied. The picture is 0 px against fa2e85a9 on the close framing, and the probe numbers are
identical.

- **Shared band plumbing.** The cascades and the ripple band share one set of helpers:
  - `_water_band_seed_build` / `_water_band_seed_upload`
  - `_water_make_twiddle_tex`
  - `_water_attach_band`, also used by the impulse probe's scratch framebuffers
  - `_water_advance_band`, driven by a `WaterBandPass` description

  The ripple band's crest-term arguments now come from its config, like the cascades'.
- **Seeding is build-then-commit.** A failure after the cascades had already been replaced used to
  leave them on the new sea while the log said the old one was kept, and it re-seeded every frame.
  Now nothing on the GPU changes until every band has built.
- **One slope-variance output.** `_water_build_spectrum` returns a single per-level array whose
  length the caller passes (the cascades pass 1), bounded by `WATER_RIPPLE_LODS`. The per-mode
  mip factor is a separable table, about 5k `cosf` per seed instead of about 5M.
- **Derived constants.** `WATER_RIPPLE_RES` and `WATER_RIPPLE_LODS` are derived from
  `WATER_RIPPLE_LOG`.
- **`oceanTileLod`** backs both `oceanCascadeLod` and `oceanRippleLod`.
- **Caustics as a loop.**
  - `waterCaustics` loops over `WATER_CAUSTIC_LEVELS`, which is the same expression as before.
  - Each level's side is a `causticSide[]` uniform, so the shader has no `pow`.
  - A level is identified by its index alone, with the scale in `_water_caustic_level_scale`.
  - The level-invariant trace uniforms and the ocean binding happen once per frame.
  - The CPU floor rule is `_water_caustic_floor`, which states that it mirrors `oceanBedHeight`.
  - The array comes from `create_texture_2d_array_float`.
- **Probe and viewport.**
  - The probe shares `_water_mean_square_slope` and runs the impulse test as a two-row table.
  - The foam pass sets its own viewport.
- **Comments and gates.**
  - Three misplaced comments are back above their functions.
  - Stale pass counts are corrected.
  - The gates select rows by kind, and read the caustic level count from the probe's own header
    instead of a restated constant.

Skipped, with the reason:

- **Transform feedback for the land trace.** Capturing the landed points as vertex attributes
  would remove the vertex texture fetch, the land target and the flush. It is a redesign of the
  pass, not a cleanup.
- **A single-target ripple transform.** The efficiency review's largest item: only the slopes are
  read, and the band is about 7× the cascades' FFT work. It needs shader variants and belongs with
  phase 4's timing.
- **Per-level land textures.** They would allow one flush instead of two: a small gain, and 4 MB.
- **Conditional caustic fetches.** Negligible.
- **Array uniform setters.** Outside this diff.
- **Moving the short band to footprint filtering.** Out of 13.3's scope.
- **Depth-derived overhang and a coverage channel.** These belong to the shallow-water change
  above.
